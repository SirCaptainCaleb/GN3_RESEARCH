# Grossman–Häggkvist–Yeo alternating-cycle theorem

**Summary:** If every vertex sees at least two colors and there is no color-separating cut vertex, then the edge-colored graph contains an alternating cycle.

## Statement

Let G be a c-edge-colored graph, c>=2, such that every vertex is incident with at least two edges of different colors. Then either G has a cut vertex that separates colors, or G contains an alternating (properly colored) cycle. Here a cut vertex v separates colors if no component of G-v is joined to v by edges of at least two different colors.

## Body

Literature theorem. Grossman and Häggkvist proved the result for two edge colors; Yeo extended it to arbitrarily many colors. An alternating/properly colored cycle is a cycle whose consecutive edges have different colors. A cut vertex v separates colors if no component of G-v is joined to v by edges of more than one color. Thus, under color degree at least two at every vertex, absence of an alternating cycle forces a color-separating cut vertex. Potential GN3 relevance: a naturally edge-colored obstruction/transition graph with local color degree at least two can be forced either into an alternating cycle or into a structural separator. The separator alternative is especially attractive because GN3 already has strong decomposition and support-gluing machinery. Sources: J. W. Grossman and R. Häggkvist, Alternating cycles in edge-partitioned graphs, JCTB 34 (1983), 77-81 (two colors); A. Yeo, A Note on Alternating Cycles in Edge-Coloured Graphs, JCTB 69 (1997), 222-225 (general number of colors).
