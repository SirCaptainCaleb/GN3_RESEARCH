# Rank-five parity-peeling theorem (computer-assisted, all dimensions)

**Theorem.** For every binary matrix A of any size satisfying diag(A)=0, A1=1 and rank(A)≤5, there is a legal deletion order i1,...,ik satisfying sum_(s<t) A_(i_t,i_s)=0, with nonsingular remaining principal matrix.

This extends the previously proved rank≤4 theorem in the NORI shared research archive. Together with the nested-core phase-immunity theorem, it forces a red full antipodal geodesic in every prescribed parity fiber of every legal paired-flat coloring with a fixed affine reader matrix of rank at most five, even when the parity-only phase gates are arbitrary nonlinear Boolean functions.

## Proof skeleton

Choose a size-minimal bad matrix of rank≤5. The existing rank≤4 theorem and exhaustive size≤6 base show its rank is five and size m≥7. The existing column compression gives
A_ij=(z_i+z_j)·t_j over F2,
with nonzero t_i in F2^4, sum t_i=0, at most one repeated quotient class, and opposite lifts in the repeated class. Common translation of the z_i leaves A invariant, allowing gauge z_0=0.

Classify quotient multisets up to GL(4,2) generated by elementary transvections. The number of orbits for sizes 7,8,9,10,11 is respectively 2,3,4,4,2.

* m=7: two original-matrix C++17 enumerations cover 4,194,304 and 8,388,608 eligible normalized matrices; no failed peel.
* m=8: the duplicated quotient orbit covers 67,108,864 original matrices; no failed peel. A six-quartet exhaustive certificate handles the distinct orbit and the affine-eight lemma handles the third.
* m=9: for each of the four orbits, a compact original-row-factor search finds a legally peelable subset of size 3,4,6 whose full column sum vanishes. Its deletion preserves odd row sums and contradicts minimality. All four search trees terminate with zero survivors.
* m=10,11: all six quotient orbits contain one of the six-column patterns (1,1,2,3,4,5) or (1,2,3,4,5,6). An independent six-column enumeration covers 524,288 and 1,048,576 normalized assignments, respectively. In every case, a full-column-zero-sum subset of size 3,4, or 6 has a complete legal peel. The six-label subset is essential.
* m=12: the previously checked eleven-quartet eight-row kernel and the distinct-column hyperplane-coset argument force a zero-sum peelable block.
* m=13,14,15: previous projective reductions plus independently exhausted eight-point affine-coset peeling lemmas force a zero-sum peelable block.
* m=16: quotient XOR rules out the multiplicity pattern.

All independent enumerators use only integer bit operations, subset reachability, and GF(2) Gaussian elimination. No SAT UNSAT assertion is used in the final finite proof.

**Limit:** The theorem concerns fixed affine reader matrices of rank at most five; general nonlinear fiber selectors, parity-varying readers and unrestricted NORI1 remain open.
