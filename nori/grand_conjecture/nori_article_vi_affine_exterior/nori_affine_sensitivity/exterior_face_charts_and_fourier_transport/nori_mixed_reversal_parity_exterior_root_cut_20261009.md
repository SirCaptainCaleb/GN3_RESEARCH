# Mixed reversal parity: cut size lower bounded by discordant antipodal pairs and exterior dimension

For fixed ordered three-directions pi in active NORI let m=n-3, let f:Q_m->{-1,1} give its physical face color sign, and E be the number of unordered antipodal exterior vertex pairs {z,bar z} with f(z) != f(bar z), equivalent to same-face reversal parity rho(F_z,pi)=0. Let B be the number of bichromatic EXTERIOR edges for this fixed ordered triple. Then B >= E; if E>0, also B >= m. Proof of B>=E: write f_odd(z)=(f(z)-f(bar z))/2; it is supported at the 2E vertices of discordant antipodal pairs, so its squared L2 norm is E/2^(m-1). Every odd Fourier coefficient contributes degree >=1 to total influence I(f), whence I(f)>=E/2^(m-1), and B=2^(m-1)I(f)>=E. Proof B>=m: one discordant antipodal pair admits m edge-disjoint paths in Q_m; every path must cross bichromatic edge. If E=2^(m-1), all antipodal pairs discord, then B>=2^(m-1) with equality precisely signed coordinate dictators by odd-degree Fourier equality and Boolean linear classification. Conversely for sparse parity-zero pairs no uniform exponential lower bound follows. Color f on an ordered pi and color reverse pi at z by -f(bar z) in signs: hence the reverse orientation is already fully determined, and rho can be any antipodally invariant binary pattern. This mixed theorem prevents extrapolating a single cap parity to global Fourier oddness. No web/literature search.
