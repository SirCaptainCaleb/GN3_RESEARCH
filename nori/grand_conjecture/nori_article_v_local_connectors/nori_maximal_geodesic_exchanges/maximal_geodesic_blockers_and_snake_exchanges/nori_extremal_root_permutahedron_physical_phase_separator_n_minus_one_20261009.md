# Physical root-permutahedron packet has an n−1 phase-separator lower bound

# A physical extremal-path root–permutahedron packet with n−1 vertex connectivity

Assume n>=7 and an arbitrary binary coloring of ordered physical three-faces of Q_n has NO good (k+1)-edge direction-distinct path for some 6<=k<n; good means at most one color change. Fix six pairwise distinct ordered cap directions A=(a_1,a_2,a_3), B=(b_1,b_2,b_3), a disjoint interior direction set U of size t=k−6, and the remaining unused alphabet T=[n]\(A∪U∪B). Fix the starting-root bits on U and leave all r=|[n]\U|=n−t= n−k+6 other root bits free. For every root x of this r-dimensional cube and every permutation σ of U, form the literal k-edge cube geodesic with word (a_1,a_2,a_3,σ(U),b_1,b_2,b_3). These are actual physical paths with supports A∪U∪B, and all endpoint faces are assigned by c on real cube faces.

Define the packet graph K with these root–order states: one edge flips exactly one free root bit, and another edge exchanges two adjacent interior letters. Thus K is the one-skeleton of the convex polytope I^r × Perm(U), a cube times the (t−1)-dimensional permutahedron (taking Perm(U) to be a point for t<=1). For t>=1 its dimension is r+t−1=n−1; for t=0 its dimension is r=n.

**Theorem (extremal root–order packet separation).** Every good packet vertex has a one-switch word and hence a well-defined terminal phase λ∈{0,1}. On every packet edge joining TWO good vertices their terminal phases AGREE. Consequently each connected component of the induced good-state subgraph is monophasic. If both phases occur somewhere in K, at least n−1 packet vertices are bad. When t=0, at least n bad vertices are necessary. This gives a dimension-independent geometric separator with honest physical path states and certified local edge comparisons.

**Proof (phase preservation on cube edges).** Every good k-geodesic has exactly one switch because a monochromatic k-path could append an unused d∈T and remain good, contradicting the assumed k+1 obstruction. If a root bit d among the first three directions A is flipped, the first ordered physical three-face is unchanged, since d is free in that face. The two first colors agree, so the two terminal phases agree by their single-switch property. A root flip in the last three directions B leaves the final ordered physical three-face unchanged and hence preserves terminal phase. For a root flip d∈T, the two appended d windows are the SAME physical ordered face: their endpoints differ by d, which belongs to the cap's free triple {b_2,b_3,d}. If two good words had opposite terminal phases, this shared cap would agree with one terminal phase and give a good (k+1)-edge extension, contradiction. These cases exhaust all free root bits, since [n]\U=A∪B∪T.

**Proof (phase preservation on permutahedron edges).** Adjacent interior permutations have identical first three and last three directions, the same starting root x, and the same overall support. Therefore the first ordered face is identical. Their endpoints coincide, so the last ordered face through that common endpoint, with free directions (b_1,b_2,b_3), is identical as well. Their first and last bits agree; in particular the terminal phases coincide whenever both paths are good.

**Proof (separator size).** Partition the good packet vertices into terminal phases G_0,G_1. The established edge preservation shows that there are no edges joining G_0 and G_1. If both are nonempty, deleting the other (bad) packet vertices disconnects K. The cube and permutahedron are convex polytopes, and their cartesian product is a convex polytope of dimension d=r+max(t−1,0). By the standard polytope graph connectivity theorem (the graph of a d-dimensional convex polytope is d-vertex-connected), every separating vertex set has cardinality at least d. For t>=1, d=n−1, and for t=0, d=n. QED.

**Exact obstruction to a grand extraction.** The physical NORI antipodal-reversal involution sends a good partial path (x,p) with missing set T to (x+T,reverse(p)), with the SAME initial and terminal phase for its one-switch word; it exchanges the ordered endpoint triples A,B and reverses both. Accordingly antipodal reversal maps this packet to a DIFFERENT packet rather than producing an opposite-phase pair inside it. To derive grand closure via packet connectivity, construct an actual certified chain or intersection of packets that forces opposite phase inside ONE packet, or prove a global bound of at most n−2 bad states in such a packet. The theorem itself supplies neither ingredient.

**Relation to the cube-only result.** Fixing one interior permutation recovers the actual r-cube good-root separator. Allowing all interior permutations adds t−1 independent permutahedral dimensions, raising the vertex-cut lower bound from r=n−k+6 to n−1, uniformly for every k>=7. This is a genuine support-growth-oriented topological carrier rather than an abstract root witness.

**Counterexample to automatic good-state supply in a packet.** For every choice of disjoint A=(a_1,a_2,a_3), B=(b_1,b_2,b_3) and nonempty U, there is a VALID reversal-odd coordinate-only coloring making EVERY packet state bad, at EVERY physical root. Set h(a_1,a_2,a_3)=0; set h(a_2,a_3,u)=1 for every u∈U; and set h(b_1,b_2,b_3)=0. These prescribed directed triples lie in distinct reversal-orbits (their underlying coordinate triples differ), so extend h arbitrarily subject to h(c,b,a)=1−h(a,b,c), then use c(F;(a,b,c))=h(a,b,c). Every packet path has first window 0, second window 1, and final window 0, hence at least two switches, regardless of interior permutation and cube root. The separator theorem therefore supplies a VALID conditional topological obstruction, while no unconditional good-state abundance follows from the mere presence of the root–permutahedron carrier. A closure proof must supply a witness-rich packet by independent global reasoning.
