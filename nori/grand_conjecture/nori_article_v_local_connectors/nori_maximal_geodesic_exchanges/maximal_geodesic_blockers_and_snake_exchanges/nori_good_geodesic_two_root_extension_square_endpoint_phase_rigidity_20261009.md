# Two-root extension square and extremal terminal-phase rigidity

Let c be a binary coloring of actual ordered three-faces of Q_n, with or without antipodal oddness. A k-edge direction-distinct path P_x(p) starts at x and traverses p=(p_1,...,p_k), k>=4. Its (k-2)-bit word W_x(p) records colors of its consecutive ordered-three-face windows. A path is good if this word changes at most once.

**Theorem 1 (two-sided one-coordinate extension).** Let d be a direction outside p. Suppose P_x(p) and P_(x+d)(p) are both good. If either is monochromatic, it admits a good extension by appending d and also by prepending d. Otherwise both words have exactly one switch. If their terminal window colors differ, one of the two literal paths P_x(p),P_(x+d)(p) extends to a good (k+1)-path by appending d, and one extends by prepending d. In particular, if no good (k+1)-edge geodesic exists, all adjacent good roots x,x+d of the same p have identical initial and terminal window colors.

**Proof.** Put y=x+sum_{i=1}^k e_(p_i), a=p_(k-1), b=p_k, alpha=p_1, beta=p_2. Appending d to P_x uses as its sole new window c(F_y({a,b,d});(a,b,d)). Appending d to P_(x+d) uses c(F_(y+d)({a,b,d});(a,b,d)), the SAME physical face and ordered directions, since d is free in that face. Denote the common bit A. If the two one-switch words have different terminal bits, A equals exactly one terminal bit, so the corresponding append extension preserves goodness. For prepending, the new window of d+P_x is c(F_x({d,alpha,beta});(d,alpha,beta)), and that of d+P_(x+d) is c(F_(x+d)({d,alpha,beta});(d,alpha,beta)); again these are the SAME physical ordered face. Call the common bit B. Since a one-switch binary word has opposite initial and terminal bits, the two initial bits also differ; B matches exactly one, giving a good prepend extension. A monochromatic word remains good after either one-window extension. All claimed paths flip each coordinate at most once. QED.

**Theorem 2 (terminal-hub intersection and sharp dimension threshold).** Assume there is no good (k+1)-edge geodesic, where 4<=k<n. Fix an endpoint y and an ordered terminal pair (a,b). For any two good k-edge paths P,Q ending at y with last directions (a,b), let T(P),T(Q) denote their missing-coordinate sets. If T(P)∩T(Q) is nonempty then P and Q have the SAME terminal window color, and therefore the same initial window color. Consequently, if k<(n+2)/2, the terminal color is uniquely determined by (y,a,b) for every such good k-edge path, whenever one exists. Dually, if two good k-paths start at the same x with the same ordered first pair (alpha,beta) and overlapping missing sets, their initial colors agree; for k<(n+2)/2 the initial color depends only on (x,alpha,beta).

**Proof.** Every good k-path must have exactly one switch; otherwise any unused direction could be appended. Let P end with color r. For every d in T(P), maximality of k forces the appended window c(F_y({a,b,d});(a,b,d)) to equal 1-r. A path Q with the same endpoint, final pair and missing d has exactly the same appended physical ordered face, so its terminal color is also r. In the n-2 available directions outside {a,b}, the missing sets have equal size m=n-k. If 2m>n-2, any two such sets intersect. This inequality is equivalent to k<(n+2)/2. The initial statement follows by the same argument at the prepend end. QED.

**Corollary (exterior-root pivotal-edge bound).** Under no good (k+1)-path, fix p and d outside its support. Let G be the set of cube roots where p is good, and let L(x) be its final ordered-face window color at root x, defined at ALL roots. If L(x) differs from L(x+d), at least one of x,x+d is outside G. Thus, with normalized Boolean influence Inf_d(L)=Pr_x[L(x) != L(x+d)], one has Pr_x[x∈G] <= 1-Inf_d(L)/2.

**Proof.** Theorem 1 excludes a pivotal d-edge whose two endpoints both belong to G. The d-edges form a matching of 2^(n-1) edges. Each pivotal edge contributes at most one good root, while each nonpivotal edge contributes at most two, giving |G|<=2^n-2^(n-1)Inf_d(L). QED.

**Extraction frontier.** Theorem 2 yields an honest endpoint-memory phase label on the extremal family whenever k<(n+2)/2. At larger k its incidence graph of good paths joined when their missing sets intersect still has constant phase on connected components. To close NORI one must force overlapping endpoint-memory families of opposite phases, or a pivotal root edge whose two paths are both good. Neither follows from the incidence geometry alone; arbitrary face data can disconnect good-root sets. The theorem supplies exact physical cap certificates for any proposed topological overlap.
