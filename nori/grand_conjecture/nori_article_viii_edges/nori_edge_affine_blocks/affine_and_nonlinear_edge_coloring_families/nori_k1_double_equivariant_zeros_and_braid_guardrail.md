# k=1 doubled-root topology: universal odd-map zeros and a three-braid carrier obstruction

Original edge-color k=1 double-equivariant no-go. Let c_i(x_-i) in F2 be antipodally odd. Interpolate the signed colors a_i=(-1)^c_i multilinearly on [0,1]^(n-1), giving a_i(1-x)=-a_i(x) and a_i(1/2,...,1/2)=0. Set Psi_j(r,s)=r_j+s_j-2r_j s_j and F_i(r,s)=a_i(Psi(r,s) excluding coordinate i). Then each F_i changes sign when all root coordinates are complemented, and independently when all progress coordinates are complemented. Nevertheless for any coloring and n>=2, choose r_1=0,s_1=1/2,r_2=1/2,s_2=0 and r_j=s_j=1/2 for j>=3. This lies in the boundary of BOTH root and progress cubes, Psi(r,s)=(1/2,...,1/2), and every F_i is zero. Therefore the natural double-equivariant Borsuk-Ulam zero carries no information about a monochromatic geodesic, even on the product of root and progress boundary spheres. There is also failure of a common discrete carrier across a three-direction braid. In Q4 let c_i(x) be parity of the three exterior coordinates; this satisfies antipodal edge oddness. On the face with direction set {1,2,3}, fourth coordinate 0, and initial physical vertex 0000, every order of those three directions has ordered edge colors 0,1,0. Map each direction order to the color-cube geodesic starting at its direction-indexed color vector and flipping that order. For the cyclic orders 123,231,312, the three sets of color-cube vertices are respectively {010,110,100,101}, {001,011,010,110}, and {100,101,001,011}, with empty triple intersection. Pairwise adjacent-swap compatibility alone does not give a common vertex at a three-direction braid. The global edge conjecture is not refuted: this particular parity coloring has monochromatic geodesics from other roots.
