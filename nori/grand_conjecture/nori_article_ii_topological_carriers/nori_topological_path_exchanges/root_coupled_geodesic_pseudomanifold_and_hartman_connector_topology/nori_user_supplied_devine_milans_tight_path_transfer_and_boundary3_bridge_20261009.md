**EXACT NORI SUBCLASS = BOUNDARY 3-TOURNAMENT.**
The paper's scrapbook defines a boundary r-tournament by the rule that, for each fixed middle (r-2)-tuple, exactly one of two orderings which swap first and last symbols is present. For r=3 these two orderings are (a,b,c) and (c,b,a): FULL REVERSAL. Thus any DIRECTION-ONLY active NORI coloring c(a,b,c) with c(c,b,a)=1-c(a,b,c) defines, separately for q=0,1, a boundary (3,3)-tournament G_q on [n], where (a,b,c) is an oriented 3-edge iff c(a,b,c)=q. Indeed each unordered triple contains exactly three q-oriented triples, one from each reversal pair. Conversely a boundary 3-tournament is exactly such a direction-only NORI coloring (taking membership as color). A MONOCHROMATIC ordered-three-window geodesic is precisely a directed tight path in G_q. A full ≤1-switch NORI geodesic corresponds precisely to a spanning permutation whose consecutive triples are first a tight G_q-run then a tight G_(1-q)-run with the same two-coordinate seam. This is a terminal-pair-compatible TWO-COLOR tight-path problem, not a mere existence of a long monochromatic tight path.

**WHY THIS DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY TRANSFER TO FULL NORI.**
For ACTUAL faces the law is c(bar F,(c,b,a))=1-c(F,(a,b,c)), and c(F,(c,b,a)) can equal OR differ from c(F,(a,b,c)). Thus neither same-face color class is required to be a boundary 3-tournament; a physical face can even have all six ordered triples of one color, with its antipodal mate all six the opposite. The appropriate structure is a TWISTED two-sheet boundary condition across antipodal PHYSICAL faces, not an ordinary tournament on direction labels. Any extension of a manuscript terminal-pair argument must retain root/face exterior bits and antipodal face changes.

**MOST RELEVANT PROOF MECHANISMS.** 
(1) The scrapbook r-boundary tournament proposition uses all cyclic (r−1)-tuples, chooses a maximal tight path ending in each, pigeonholes a common ordered (r−2)-suffix, and compares distinct predecessor directions by a tournament on them. A terminal comparison either extends one maximal witness or forces vertex reuse. In NORI r=3 this is a 1-direction suffix; grand-closure equivalence instead needs SAME ROOT and TWO REVERSED terminal directions. Thus lift via root-coupled directed pair memories, not ordinary suffix quotient.
(2) Paper's proof of the (3,4) lower bound shows, under absence of directed triangles, that maximal tight paths are pairwise intersecting; uses a pairwise splicing/interleaving and longest-path contradiction. Try a NORI counterpart where two monochromatic geodesics share a real physical hub and orientation-compatible last TWO direction memories; arbitrary path intersection is insufficient.
(3) Paper's pattern-shift graph route works by a FINITE symbolic quotient followed by an honest lifting step. The NORI physical fibers create a sharper lifting obligation. This explicitly identifies a potential false shortcut: a PSG walk may be unable to lift to a single direction-injective cube path at one physical root.
(4) Paper §§2–3 'trap' patterns: short directed path within each rank, with a lexicographic rank that must decrease on changing rank. A NORI potential must depend on actual face/root memory and have a verifiable termination rule under antipodal involution, not just ordered direction pattern.

**APPENDIX PROOF REPAIR NOTE (independent of NORI).** The manuscript's appended lemma deriving p_m>=lambda^m from p_m>=p_(m-1)-q p_(m-r) contains a reversed-inequality substitution: a LOWER bound on p_(m-r) cannot be inserted into a negative term as an upper bound. The conclusion nevertheless follows from a STRONGER INDUCTION on conditional survival ratios p_m>=lambda p_(m-1) for all m>=1. Base: p_m=1 for m<r, p_r=1-q>=lambda if q<=lambda^(r-1)(1-lambda). The earlier recurrence and resulting numeric Hamiltonian bound are thus supported by this corrected reasoning; do not copy the appendix's absolute-lower-bound substitution unchanged.
