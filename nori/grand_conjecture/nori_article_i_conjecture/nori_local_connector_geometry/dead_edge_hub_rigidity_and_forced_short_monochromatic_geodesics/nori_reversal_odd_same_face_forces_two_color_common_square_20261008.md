# Every same-face reversal-odd NORI coloring has a physical square certified monochromatically in both colors

THEOREM (IMMEDIATE EDGE-SHADOW ALTERNATIVE A FOR REVERSAL-ODD COLORINGS). Let n>=5 and c be a binary coloring of physical ordered three-dimensional faces of Q_n. Suppose c(F,reverse pi)=1-c(F,pi) for every ordered three-face F,pi (a separate same-physical-face reversal-odd condition). For ANY centered monochromatic directed 4-geodesic P_z(a,b,c,d) of window color q, the centered REVERSED-DIRECTION path P_z(d,c,b,a) is also monochromatic, has window color 1-q, and certifies the IDENTICAL physical middle square Q(z;b,c). Proof: P_z has two ordered face windows F(z;{a,b,c}) in order (a,b,c), and F(z;{b,c,d}) in order (b,c,d), both color q. Its reversed-direction counterpart at the SAME center z uses the SAME two physical faces in reversed orders (d,c,b) and (c,b,a); each color is 1-q by the hypothesis. Its unordered middle pair remains {b,c}, so the square is the same and all four of its physical edges have opposite-color genuine monochromatic certificates.
The centered odd-five-cycle theorem guarantees at least one monochromatic four-geodesic at EVERY hub for arbitrary binary ordered-face coloring. Therefore EVERY hub z has a physical square with two opposite-color mono four-geodesic certificates, and EVERY edge of that square satisfies K(e)={0,1} in the certified-square edge-shadow dichotomy. If c ALSO obeys active NORI antipodal-reversal oddness, the two laws jointly imply c(bar F,pi)=c(F,pi), i.e. antipodally EVEN colors for unchanged ordered triple, and the conclusion still holds. In particular EVERY coordinate-only reversal-odd coloring has alternative (A) of the certified edge-shadow dichotomy; the edge-color shadow alternative (B) can only arise when some physical faces BREAK local reversal oddness.
This theorem gives honest opposite-color square overlap, not a spanning antipodal geodesic. The unresolved extension is to turn the common-square opposite-color connector into global compatible terminal-memory reachability.
