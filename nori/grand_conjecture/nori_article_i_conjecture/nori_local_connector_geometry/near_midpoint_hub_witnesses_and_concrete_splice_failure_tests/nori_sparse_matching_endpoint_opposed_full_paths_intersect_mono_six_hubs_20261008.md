# Endpoint-opposed full geodesics meet monochromatic six-hubs under a sparse certified-edge defect matching

THEOREM (certified hub / opposite-end overlap). Assume active NORI with n>=10 in the globally unique-color certified-square edge-shadow regime. Let M be the matching of cube edges absent from the genuine certified-square 1-skeleton, let B be the bichromatic hub set, and let H6 be the vertices carrying a genuine centered monochromatic six-geodesic. Prior NORI results establish B subset H6 and that every full M-avoiding antipodal cube path meets B.

Put t=floor(n/3) and δ=[C(floor(t/2),2)+C(ceil(t/2),2)]/C(t,2), at least 1/3. The proved disjoint-ordered-triple theorem gives, at EVERY root x, at least δ of its n! full permutations with opposite first/last ordered-three-face colors.

Let ρ=|M|/2^(n-1). Then at least max(0,δ-ρ)·2^n·n! rooted full n-geodesics simultaneously: (1) have opposite initial/final physical ordered-face colors; (2) avoid M entirely; (3) meet an actual mono-six hub. When ρ<δ these yield at least (δ-ρ)·2^n distinct physical roots with such a path. If M is empty, the bound δ n! holds separately at every root.

Proof. Sample uniformly a cube root x and full direction permutation π. Each physical cube edge e is traversed with probability 2^-(n-1); hence expected number of missing edges along the path is ρ, so P(hit M)≤ρ. Independently of any correlation, P(opposite endpoint colors)≥δ from the universal endpoint density theorem. Thus P(both opposite and M-free)≥δ-ρ. Each such genuine M-free geodesic lies in the certified graph and hits B⊂H6. Multiply by 2^n n!. For the distinct root count, at most n! paths can share a root. For M=empty, all δ n! opposite endpoint paths at any prescribed root are certified and hit H6.

This is a substantial ACTUAL-PHYSICAL intersection of a high-index endpoint-opposition carrier with the genuine mono-six hub separator. It does NOT align the direction order of the six-edge witness with the full path, nor force a one-switch path. The condition ρ<δ is not automatic. Grand NORI remains open.
