# Root–endpoint torus has antipodal index one: a sharp symmetry-only no-go

# Antipodal root–endpoint torus: equivariant index one

Let \(T_n=(\partial[0,1]^2)^n\) be the root–endpoint state torus, with the \(i\)-th square boundary cyclically labeled \(00,01,11,10\). Simultaneous complementation of the two endpoints acts by \(\alpha(x,y)=(\bar x,\bar y)\), the half-turn on every square factor. In an antipodally odd **edge** coloring this involution interchanges edge colors.

**Theorem.** For every \(n\ge1\), the \(\alpha\)-action on \(T_n\) is free, and there is a nowhere-zero continuous \(\alpha\)-odd map \(F:T_n\to\mathbb R^2\). The same conclusion holds for any \(\mathbb Z_2\)-space \(X\) with an equivariant projection \(X\to T_n\). In particular, equivariance and the source/target state loci alone cannot force a zero of a 2-component reachability-label map.

**Proof.** Place the first square factor in \(\mathbb R^2\) centered at the origin, with the square vertices \(00,01,11,10\) consecutively on its boundary. The map \(F:T_n\to\mathbb R^2\) projecting onto that square boundary is continuous and nowhere zero. The involution exchanges opposite corners and takes every point on the boundary to its negative, so \(F(\alpha u)=-F(u)\). This also proves freeness. Composition with an equivariant \(X\to T_n\) gives the second assertion. The diagonal vertex states \(D=\{(x,x)\}\) have first-factor states \(00,11\), whose images are \(\pm e_1\), whereas full antipodal endpoint states \(A=\{(x,\bar x)\}\) have states \(01,10\), whose images are \(\pm e_2\), after choosing the square as a diamond. Thus the no-zero example even places the two endpoint types in disjoint orthogonal antipodal label pairs. \(\square\)

**Cohomological sharpness.** The quotient \(T_n/\langle\alpha\rangle\) is a torus, and the double-cover class \(w\in H^1(T_n/\alpha;\mathbb F_2)\) is nonzero. The mod-two cohomology of a torus is the exterior algebra on degree-one classes, so \(w^2=0\). Its standard cohomological \(\mathbb Z_2\)-index is precisely \(1\), independently of \(n\).

**Implication for the Hartman strategy.** Ordinary Borsuk–Ulam arguments on the full endpoint torus cannot establish the edge-geodesic conjecture from antipodal parity alone. Any successful theorem must additionally use the directed color-admissible cover transitions, a genuinely different high-index source, or more restrictive carrier/boundary conditions. Memory-state lifts retaining an equivariant projection to \(T_n\) inherit this obstruction. For three-face NORI, the color-changing involution also reverses the direction order, so equivariance of the corresponding memory projection requires a separate check. This is a restriction on a proposed proof strategy, not a counterexample to either conjecture.
