# The antipodal-reversal-odd geodesic conjecture

Let n≥4. An ordered three-face of Q_n is a three-dimensional face F together with an order π=(i,j,k) of its free coordinates. Define antipodal reversal (F,π)↦(bar F,(k,j,i)), where bar F is obtained by complementing all cube vertices. Let c be a binary coloring of ordered three-faces with c(bar F,rev π)=1−c(F,π). Each length-three subgeodesic of an antipodal geodesic determines the face that contains it and its traversal coordinate order. CONJECTURE: Every such coloring admits an antipodal geodesic whose consecutive ordered-three-face colors change at most once. The color may depend on F but not on which of its corners starts a traversal in the fixed direction order. This includes reversal-odd coordinate-triple colorings as a restricted case. It is distinct from arbitrary colorings of directed length-three cube geodesics, for which rank parity gives a counterexample. Its one-face analogue is Norine's antipodal edge-coloring setting.
