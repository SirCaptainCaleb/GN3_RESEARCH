# Square rigidity at triple-alternating seams of a globally minimum edge-geodesic cycle

Let c be an antipodally odd binary EDGE coloring of Q_n, and let P=(x,p) globally minimize the odd cyclic seam index m among all root/order chambers. Suppose the 2n-edge antiperiodic cycle associated with P has four successive edge colors (L,a,b,R) that alternate, i.e. L=b and a=R=1-L. Suppose the central two edges correspond to consecutive coordinate directions i,j and hence form the two-edge route through one square. Exchange those two directions at the same root, using the opposite two-edge route in the square with colors (a',b'). Then necessarily (a',b')=(a,b). Proof: only the three seam bits immediately around the exchanged two-edge subpath change in the full 2n-cycle, together with their antipodal copies. The original local seam cost is 3; the new cost is odd (the endpoints L,R are opposite), hence either 1 or 3. If it were 1, the total m would decrease by 2, contradicting global minimality. Thus the new local cost is 3, which for fixed alternating outer colors L,R uniquely forces the inner pair a'=1-L=a and b'=L=b. Geometric consequence: for every triple-alternating portion of a minimum-m cycle, the corresponding two outgoing edges of the square from its entrance have identical colors, and its two incoming edges into the exit have the complementary identical colors. This is exactly the rank-layer parity pattern on that square. Separately, if (L,a,b,R) has exactly two changes, minimality forbids the alternate route from making the entire four-edge word monochromatic, because that would again reduce m by 2. These are dimension-independent necessary local rigidity conditions for a counterexample or a nonminimal-value plateau. They are insufficient alone to force m=1: colorings can contain rank-parity rigid squares and only root-coupled moves can escape them.
