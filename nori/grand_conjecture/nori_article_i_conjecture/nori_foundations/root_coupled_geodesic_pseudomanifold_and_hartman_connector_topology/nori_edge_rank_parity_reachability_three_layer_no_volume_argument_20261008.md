# Rank-parity odd coloring makes every rooted reachability set smaller than half the cube in all large even dimensions

For even n color each edge between Hamming ranks r and r+1 by c(e)=r mod 2. This is antipodally odd, because an antipodal edge has lower rank n-r-1 of the opposite parity. Fix any root x of Hamming rank k and let U(x) comprise all targets reachable via monochromatic geodesics of either color (empty path included). Then EXACTLY U(x)={z: ||z|-k|<=1}. Proof: edges of a fixed color join disconnected pairs of consecutive Hamming layers. A monochromatic path from rank k stays within either levels k-1,k or k,k+1, so the stated inequality is necessary. For sufficiency, write desired changes as d flips of x's 1-bits downward and u flips of x's 0-bits upward. If |d-u|<=1, alternate down/up or up/down as appropriate; this is a geodesic using each required coordinate once and stays in one adjacent layer pair, so all its edges have the same color. Thus |U(x)|=binom(n,k-1)+binom(n,k)+binom(n,k+1), with out-of-range coefficients zero. This is maximized at k=n/2 for even n. At n=20 its maximum is 167960+184756+167960=520676<524288=2^19. For every even n>=20 the maximal three-layer binomial proportion is nonincreasing as n increases by 2: the Bin(n+2,1/2) law is the convolution of Bin(n,1/2) and Bin(2,1/2), so every three-consecutive-layer probability is an average of such probabilities for n and cannot exceed the previous maximum. Therefore |U(x)|<2^(n-1) for EVERY root x in these odd colorings. Nevertheless roots of middle rank n/2 admit monochromatic antipodal geodesics by alternating upward and downward steps. This proves that the pigeonhole condition |U(x)|>half, even optimized over all roots, is too weak as a universal method. A successful topological argument must exploit the placement and interactions of reachable targets rather than their raw cardinality.
