# Two differently directed dead edges force full monochromatic geodesics in Q8–Q9 and one-switch closure in Q10

TWO-DEAD-HUB WINDOW-GLUING LEMMA (DIRECTLY ACTIVE NORI). Let n>=8, and suppose there are dead physical cube edges in DISTINCT coordinate directions i,j. Choose endpoints z of the i-edge and w of the j-edge such that their i,j coordinate bits MATCH and the Hamming difference set T={k outside{i,j}:z_k!=w_k} has size d>=3. (Always possible: both dead edges permit flipping their own free coordinate.) Their local rigidity bits are t_i and t_j. Choose a direction permutation with first prefix A of length p, then the d directions of T, then suffix B of length ell=n-d-p, where A,B partition [n] minus T, j belongs to A, and i belongs to B. Start at x=z xor A, traverse A to reach hub z at vertex index p, traverse T to reach w at index p+d, and traverse B to reach bar x. For EVERY length-three ordered window containing z, its three directions avoid i (since i appears after d>=3 further changes), so its physical face color is the CONSTANT t_i. Likewise every three-window containing w avoids j, so has color t_j.
Each window of a full geodesic is covered by z when its first vertex index s lies in [p-3,p], and by w when s lies in [p+d-3,p+d]. For 1<=p,ell<=3, their union contains EVERY s=0,...,n-3 whenever d<=4, because the two index intervals have no gap. Therefore IF d=4 and n-d<=6, then the full n-edge ordered-three-face word consists of one contiguous t_i block followed by one contiguous t_j block, and there is AT MOST ONE color change: grand closure follows regardless of whether the two local bits agree. If d=3, z and w lie on one physical three-face with free directions T avoiding i,j, so t_i=t_j. Therefore IF d=3 and n-d<=6, the full geodesic is MONOCHROMATIC. Moreover if d=3 and n-d=7, choose p=4,ell=3: only the FIRST three-window fails to contain either hub, while EVERY later window contains z or w and hence has color t_i=t_j, yielding at most ONE switch. These deductions are true for arbitrary ordered-three-face colorings whenever the asserted local dead-edge patterns hold; antipodal oddness is used next to ensure a short gap.
ANTIPODAL DISTANCE FOR TWO DEAD DIRECTIONS. Under active NORI, the antipodal partner of the j-edge is dead with flipped local bit. The minimum endpoint distance between e_i and e_j is d, while that between e_i and the antipodal j-edge is (n-2)-d. The proven interdirection dead-edge separation says BOTH are >=3, hence 3<=d<=n-5. Replace the j-edge by its antipodal mate as needed to attain d'=min(d,n-2-d), still >=3. Thus n=8 forces d'=3, n=9 forces d'=3, and n=10 forces d' in {3,4}. Apply the two-hub lemma: in Q8,Q9, d'=3 with n-3<=6, so a FULL MONOCHROMATIC antipodal n-geodesic exists. In Q10, d'=3 gives n-3=7 and at most one switch; d'=4 gives n-4=6 and at most one switch. Hence ANY ACTIVE NORI coloring in dimensions 8<=n<=10 that has dead edges in at least two coordinate directions satisfies the GRAND one-switch conjecture.
This theorem is dimension-independent as a splicing mechanism but does not settle any full dimensional case, since colorings with no dead edges or all dead edges parallel exist. It substantially sharpens dead-edge matching rigidity by extracting actual near-spanning and spanning monochromatic paths across two distant hubs. For larger n, the same construction yields mono chunks of length up to d+6 but does not cover every window when n>d+7, so new long-range transport remains necessary.
