# High-index obstruction rules out global equivariant single-window reachability selectors

Let n>=5. Let X_n be the punctured all-root antipodal-geodesic complex |K_n| with the 2^(n-1) antipodal-edge midpoints removed. It contains at each deleted midpoint an invariant linking sphere S^(n-1) on which cube antipodality acts antipodally. Let H_n^(3) be the genuine ordered-three-face window-shift graph, with its free involution tau(F,pi)=(bar F,reverse pi). Then there exists NO continuous equivariant map X_n -> |H_n^(3)|. Indeed, the previous connected-graph/index-one theorem constructs a nowhere-zero tau-odd map |H_n^(3)| -> S^1 subset R^2. Composing a hypothetical equivariant selector with this map and restricting to any linking S^(n-1) would yield a nowhere-zero odd map S^(n-1)->R^2, contrary to Borsuk-Ulam (n-1>=2). Thus a topological label that continuously picks a SINGLE ordered-three-face or its location in the 1-dimensional window shift graph cannot consistently cover the all-root geodesic pseudomanifold. This obstruction is COLOR-INDEPENDENT: it does not prove NORI, and it cannot by itself extract a one-switch path. A viable reachability-based closure argument must either use a higher-dimensional, equivariantly indexed space of JOINTLY realizable window/branch configurations, or allow singularities of an attempted window selector and prove that those singularities furnish the exact forward/co-reachable monochromatic branches and two admissible seam-window colors needed by the existing extraction lemma. The theorem precisely formalizes why instantaneous single-window color labels and ordinary graph-level Borsuk-Ulam cannot suffice.
