# A single dead edge forces an antipodal geodesic with at most n−6 ordered-three-face color changes

THEOREM (UNIVERSAL SINGLE-DEAD-HUB SWITCH BOUND). Let n>=7, and let c be ANY binary coloring of actual physical ordered three-faces of Q_n. If one physical cube edge in direction i is traversed by NO monochromatic four-edge geodesic (a DEAD edge), then there exists a FULL antipodal n-edge cube geodesic with at most n-6 ordered-three-face color changes. In particular n=7 gives the desired <=1 switch grand conclusion, without requiring antipodal oddness. In n=8 it gives at most two changes; n=9 at most three.
PROOF. Choose any endpoint z of the dead edge and any six distinct coordinate directions p1,...,p6 outside i. The dead-edge local rigidity theorem makes EVERY ordered-three-face through z omitting i have the same color t. Let x=z xor {p1,p2,p3}. The first six edges of the full directed n-edge geodesic obtained by traversing p1,...,p6 and then ANY order of the remaining n-6 coordinates form a mono six-edge path centered at z (which is reached after the first three flips). Consequently the FIRST FOUR colors of the full n-edge ordered-three-face window word equal t. The word has n-2 windows total; the remaining n-6 window colors are arbitrary. Any binary word beginning with four equal symbols has at most n-6 changes, since there are only n-6 inter-window boundaries thereafter. QED.
For active NORI c(bar F,reverse pi)=1-c(F,pi), its antipodal dead partner gives the color-complemented mirrored certificate. The more powerful two-distinct-dead-direction theorem yields the improved n-9 bound when TWO nonparallel dead edges exist in n>=10. No general one-switch closure is obtained if the dead set is empty or all edges are parallel in n>=8.
