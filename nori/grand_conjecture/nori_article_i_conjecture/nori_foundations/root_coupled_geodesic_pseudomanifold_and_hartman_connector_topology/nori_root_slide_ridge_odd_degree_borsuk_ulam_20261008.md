# Every odd cube-vertex labeling forces balanced root-slide ridges of odd degree at each antipodal root

# Root-slide ridge Borsuk–Ulam parity theorem

Fix \(n\ge2\). Let \(\ell:Q_n\to\mathbb R^{n-1}\) be any map with \(\ell(\bar v)=-\ell(v)\). A **root-slide ridge** is the \(n\)-vertex set of an \((n-1)\)-edge cube geodesic. It has exactly two full antipodal-geodesic completions, obtained by attaching its one unused coordinate at either end, and these completions have distinct antipodal endpoint pairs.

**Theorem (balanced root-slide certificate).** For every antipodal endpoint pair \(e=\{x,\bar x\}\), there exists a root-slide ridge \(R\) whose vertex set contains \(x\) or \(\bar x\) and satisfies
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}\{\ell(v):v\in R\}.
\]
For generic \(\ell\), the set of such root-slide ridges (quotiented by simultaneous cube antipodality) forms a multigraph \(G_\ell\) on the \(2^{n-1}\) antipodal endpoint pairs: connect the endpoint pairs of the two full completions of each balanced ridge orbit. **Every vertex of \(G_\ell\) has odd degree.** In particular, \(G_\ell\) is a spanning odd-degree submultigraph of the folded cube's adjacency graph.

**Proof.** Let \(K_e\) be the subcomplex of the all-root geodesic complex generated by complete paths with antipodal endpoints \(e\). It is the join
\[
K_e=e*\operatorname{sd}(\partial\Delta^{n-1}),
\]
an \(n\)-ball. Its boundary
\[
\partial K_e=\partial e*\operatorname{sd}(\partial\Delta^{n-1})\cong S^{n-1}
\]
is a free antipodal sphere: complementing every cube vertex exchanges the two points of \(\partial e\) and complements the barycentric-coordinate subset vertices. The top simplices of \(\partial K_e\) are exactly the root-slide ridges containing one endpoint from \(e\). Extend \(\ell\) affinely over this triangulation. The extension is antipodally odd. Borsuk–Ulam for \(S^{n-1}\to\mathbb R^{n-1}\) gives a zero; its supporting simplex lies in a root-slide ridge, proving the first assertion.

For generic odd \(\ell\), zero avoids the \((n-2)\)-skeleton and meets each balanced \((n-1)\)-simplex in one interior point. In the free antipodal quotient \(\partial K_e/\tau\cong\mathbb RP^{n-1}\), this map is a transverse section of \(\gamma^{\oplus(n-1)}\). Its number of zero orbits is odd, because its top Stiefel–Whitney class is \(a^{n-1}\) with \(\langle a^{n-1},[\mathbb RP^{n-1}]\rangle=1\). Thus each root pair \(e\) is incident to an odd number of balanced ridge orbits. Every root-slide ridge has precisely two antipodal-geodesic completions, belonging to distinct rooted balls \(K_e,K_{e'}\), where \(e'\) differs from \(e\) by one cube coordinate. Its antipodal image has the same two endpoint pairs, so each ridge orbit contributes one edge incident to both vertices. Hence every vertex has odd degree. \(\square\)

**NORI extraction criterion.** This is a direct finite-dimensional, root-coupled Tucker/Borsuk–Ulam statement with a concrete carrier: an actual \((n-1)\)-edge geodesic, shared by exactly two full root choices. Grand closure would follow if, assuming no good NORI geodesic, one could define an odd reachability/defect label \(\ell:Q_n\to\mathbb R^{n-1}\) for which *every* root-slide ridge has origin outside the convex hull of its labels. This is the precise outstanding coloring-dependent ingredient. Equivalently, it suffices to arrange that a balanced ridge forces a monochromatically compatible two-ended repair or a one-switch full completion. Ordinary abstract odd labels alone give no such extraction, as the theorem applies to every odd labeling.

**Quantitative consequence.** The odd-degree multigraph has \(2^{n-1}\) vertices and therefore at least \(2^{n-2}\) edges, counting multiplicity. For generic odd \(\ell\), at least \(2^{n-2}\) antipodal orbits of root-slide ridges—and hence at least \(2^{n-1}\) individual \((n-1)\)-geodesic ridges—have origin in the convex hull of their labels. This abundance is available before any reachability labeling is chosen.

**Exact two-completion color obstruction.** For a root-slide ridge \(R=(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1})\), let \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_{n-3})\) be its ordered-three-face window colors (take \(n\ge5\)), and let \(u\) and \(v\) be the *new* window colors on the full paths obtained by prepending, respectively appending, the one missing coordinate. The full color words are \((u,w)\) and \((w,v)\); the existing windows refer to the same physical ordered faces. Consequently:
- if \(w\) is constant, **both** completions are good;
- if \(w\) has exactly one switch, **both** completions are bad exactly when \(u\ne w_1\) and \(v\ne w_{n-3}\);
- if \(w\) has at least two switches, both completions are bad.

Thus a coloring-dependent odd label realizing the proposed extraction criterion needs to force, on some balanced ridge, either a constant interior word or a one-switch interior word with a matching outward extension. This identifies the precise local event the topological certificate must detect.

**Odd-cut parity for collective root sets.** For a generic odd labeling \(\ell\), let \(G_\ell\) be the balanced-ridge root-pair multigraph. If \(S\) is any subset of antipodal root pairs, the number \(b_\ell(S)\) of balanced ridge orbits with one completion rooted in S and the other rooted outside S satisfies
\[
b_\ell(S)\equiv |S|\pmod 2.
\]
Indeed the sum of the odd degrees at vertices of S equals \(2|E(G_\ell[S])|+b_\ell(S)\). Thus every odd-sized collective root set has at least one balanced boundary ridge. This is the appropriate relative connector statement if a coloring-dependent argument supplies an odd *region* of roots together with a boundary-extraction rule. It remains insufficient without that rule: the one-direction coordinate-projection example localizes all balanced edges to a perfect matching while obeying every odd-cut parity identity.
