# No antipodally odd edge coloring has globally constant cyclic seam index greater than one

THEOREM (GLOBAL NONFLATNESS). Let n>=5, and c be any antipodally odd binary EDGE coloring on Q_n. If the odd seam index m(x,p) is independent of every root x and permutation p, then m(x,p)=1, and c admits a monochromatic antipodal geodesic. More precisely, a globally constant m forces every vertex to have monochromatic incident edges except possibly one.
PROOF. Put s_i(v)=(-1)^{c({v,v xor e_i})}. Fix any vertex v, distinct square directions i,j, and distinct exterior directions a,b not in {i,j}. Choose a full geodesic whose directions begin (a,i,j,b,...) and whose first a-step ends at v. Let u=v xor e_i xor e_j. The four successive edge signs are (L,A,B,R) with L=s_a(v), A=s_i(v), B=s_j(u), R=s_b(u). Swapping i,j produces (L,C,D,R), where C=s_j(v),D=s_i(u). The only changed seam bits are the three local boundaries; the difference in their sum is half the difference of the two adjacent-product sums. If m is globally constant, for every ordered distinct exterior a,b,
 L(A-C)+(AB-CD)+R(B-D)=0.
Suppose A!=C, so C=-A. If B=D, the displayed identity reduces to (A-C)(L+B)=0, forcing L=-B independently of a. If B!=D, D=-B and AB-CD=0; the identity reduces to L A+R B=0. Fixing b, L is independent of a!=b. Since there are at least 3 exterior directions (n>=5), varying b shows L is independent of *every* exterior direction a. In either case, all n-2 edges incident to v in directions outside {i,j} have the same color. Since i,j have opposite colors at v, exactly one of the n incident edges has the minority color. If no pair i,j has opposite colors at v, all incident edges at v have the same color. Thus, for every v, k(v) in {0,1,n-1,n}. The previously proved exact uniform-chamber average is E[m]=2/(n-1) E[k(v)(n-k(v))], and therefore E[m]<=2. Since every m is positive odd and is assumed constant, m must equal 1. QED.
This proves an exact rigidity obstruction for any putative globally flat nonminimal chamber-energy landscape; it does NOT prove that every nonminimal local/plateau minimum can descend.
