# Every vertex admits an antipodal geodesic avoiding any nonperfect cube matching; application to all monochromatic four-path witnesses

GENERAL THEOREM (WEIGHTED MATCHING-CHAIN INEQUALITY). Let M be ANY matching of cube edges in Q_n, n>=2. Fix ANY root x. If M is not perfect, there is a full Hamming-shortest antipodal path from x to bar x using NO edge in M. More precisely, for a random uniform permutation p of all n coordinate directions,
 P(P(x,p) avoids M)>=sum_(v unmatched by M) 1/((n+1)*binom(n,d(x,v))) >0.
PROOF. An edge e={u,v} with d(x,u)=k and d(x,v)=k+1 is traversed by a uniformly random directed full geodesic from x to bar x with probability w_k=k!(n-k-1)!/n!=1/[n*binom(n-1,k)]. Define alpha_k=k!(n-k)!/(n+1)!=1/[(n+1)*binom(n,k)], which is strictly positive. Then EXACTLY w_k=alpha_k+alpha_(k+1). Because M is a matching, no vertex contributes to two selected edges, so
 E[# of M-edges along random path]=sum_(e={u,v} in M)(alpha_rank(u)+alpha_rank(v)) =sum_(v covered by M) alpha_rank(v).
But sum_(all cube vertices v) alpha_rank(v)=sum_(k=0)^n binom(n,k)/[(n+1)binom(n,k)]=1. Therefore the expectation is 1-sum_(unmatched v)alpha_rank(v). Since indicator(path hits M)<=number of M-edges on path, P(path avoids M)>=sum_(unmatched v)alpha_rank(v)>0 if M is not perfect. QED. This is an exact dimension-independent Lubell-style fractional vertex-matching argument, not a SAT result.
APPLICATION TO ACTIVE NORI. Let M(c) consist of physical cube edges not traversed by ANY genuinely monochromatic 4-edge geodesic of ordered-three-face windows. The already proved dead-edge matching theorem makes M(c) a matching, and the antipodal-reversal symmetry together with its two-step interdirection separation and cap-rigidity forbids M(c) from being perfect. Hence for EVERY cube vertex x, some full antipodal geodesic P from x to bar x has ALL its n physical edges covered by ACTUAL monochromatic 4-edge witness geodesics (not necessarily by one common witness/color). This strengthens the earlier theorem which only guaranteed >=4 suitable ROOTS for each fixed coordinate order: now EVERY ROOT has at least one suitable order. Quantitatively the probability of success is at least the displayed sum of positive weights at uncovered matching vertices. The antipodal-invariant dead matching has >=4 unmatched cube vertices, and hence the uniform coarse bound P(success)>=4/[(n+1)*binom(n,floor(n/2))].
LIMITATION. The separate four-edge witnesses of edges along the selected full geodesic need not be compatible, rooted on that full path, or monochromatic in one common color. So even universal root-by-root local coverage does not yet establish NORI one-switch closure. It supplies a strong geometric starting point for a root-fixed connector-repair/fixed-point argument, with no missing-edge obstruction.

SHARP CLASSIFICATION OF ALL MATCHING BLOCKERS (STRONGER). For any matching M in Q_n, n>=2, and ANY specified root x, the following are equivalent: (a) EVERY full antipodal geodesic from x to bar x meets M; (b) M is the FULL SET of 2^(n-1) cube edges in one fixed coordinate direction. Proof: the nonperfect case is excluded by the weighted expectation argument above. If M is perfect, the vertex-weight identity gives expected number of M-edges on a uniformly random rooted antipodal geodesic EXACTLY one. If every such path meets M, this integer count must equal one ON EVERY path. Fix any square in Q_n spanned by distinct directions i,j. Complete either of its two monotone routes from its x-nearest corner to its x-farthest corner into a full root-to-antipode geodesic using the other directions before and after the square. Both full paths have exactly one M-edge and agree outside the square, so the two two-edge routes have the SAME number of selected matching edges. Since M is a matching, the selected edges in the square are either none, one, or two opposite parallel edges; a single selected edge gives route counts one versus zero and is forbidden. Therefore if any i-edge e is in M, every square containing e has its opposite parallel i-edge also in M. Propagation through all other coordinate flips forces ALL parallel i-edges into M, yielding (b). Conversely, every antipodal geodesic crosses direction i exactly once and therefore meets the full parallel i-matching. QED. This classification removes the nonperfect hypothesis in the general path-avoidance theorem: a matching admits an avoiding antipodal geodesic FROM EVERY root unless it is exactly a full parallel coordinate matching. The active NORI dead-edge set cannot be that obstruction by antipodal oddness; thus the application remains valid.

CONSTRUCTIVE ROOT-FIXED METHOD: For active NORI the dead-edge matching is nonperfect, so the expected number of dead edges along a uniformly permuted full geodesic from ANY prescribed root is strictly below one. Choose the next coordinate greedily to MINIMIZE the conditional expected number of dead edges in a random completion. This conditional expectation never increases, remains <1, and at the final step is the integer number of dead edges actually traversed, hence zero. The future conditional expectation from current vertex v with m unused directions is the sum over dead edges e INSIDE the remaining m-face of k_e!(m-k_e-1)!/m!, where k_e is the Hamming distance from v to the nearer endpoint of e. Choosing the minimum among m choices at each stage is constructive and retains the starting root. It does not align the separate four-edge monochromatic witnesses into a good one-switch path.
