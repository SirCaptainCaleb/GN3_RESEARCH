# In each coordinate, monochromatic four-geodesic coverage hits every antipodal geodesic of the parallel-edge cube

Let n>=5 and c be active NORI antipodal-reversal-odd binary ordered-three-face coloring. Fix direction i. Identify the 2^(n-1) physical parallel i-edges with Q_(n-1), using their bits outside i. Write D_i for projected positions whose i-edge is DEAD (is traversed by NO monochromatic directed four-edge geodesic), and L_i=Q_(n-1) minus D_i for the LIVE i-edges. The proved dead-edge local rigidity assigns a canonical bit t(v) to each v in D_i: at either endpoint hub of the dead i-edge, ordered 3-faces with i in the middle (or absent) have color t(v), and those with i first or last have color 1-t(v).
THEOREM. (1) If v,w belong to D_i and Hamming distance(v,w)<=2, then t(v)=t(w). (2) If v,bar v belong to D_i, then t(bar v)=1-t(v). (3) EVERY antipodal cube geodesic in Q_(n-1) meets L_i. In particular |L_i| >=ceil(2^(n-1)/n), a positive fraction >=1/n of all i-parallel edges. (4) Dead edges of DISTINCT directions in the original Q_n must be separated by physical endpoint-set distance at least THREE.
PROOF. For two dead parallel i-edges at projected distance 1 or2, choose one physical ordered-three-face whose free directions include i and all differing exterior directions (and fill up to three free directions if needed). Both dead-edge endpoint hubs lie on this physical face. Put i as the middle direction of its ordered triple; its common color equals both t(v),t(w). For the antipodal statement apply the NORI law to a face with i FIRST: it has color 1-t(v), while its antipodal-reversed ordered face has i LAST and color 1-t(bar v). The law gives (1-t(bar v))=1-(1-t(v))=t(v), so t(bar v)=1-t(v). A projected antipodal geodesic whose every vertex belonged to D_i would connect antipodal vertices using adjacent dead positions, making t constant along it by (1), contradicting (2). Hence every such geodesic meets L_i. Choose uniformly a starting vertex v and coordinate permutation of Q_(n-1); the n vertices along the resulting antipodal geodesic are each uniformly distributed in Q_(n-1). Its expected number of visited live positions is n|L_i|/2^(n-1), which must be >=1, yielding the quantitative bound.
For (4), suppose dead edges e_i,e_j of different directions i,j have nearest endpoints z,w at distance 1 or2, and let S be their set of differing coordinates. The minimality of the endpoint choice means i,j do not belong to S, or flipping an endpoint along its dead edge would shorten the distance. Choose a three-element free-coordinate set T0 containing S but excluding i,j, possible because n>=5. The actual physical ordered face through z and w with T0 has the same color from both dead-edge hub patterns, and both i,j are absent; thus their local bits t_i=t_j. Now choose a triple T1 containing S and i but excluding j, and put i FIRST. This same physical face has color 1-t_i by the i-dead pattern and t_j by the j-dead pattern, contradiction. Thus two differently directed dead edges have minimum endpoint distance at least3.
This is a new GEODESIC-HITTING / HEX-type necessary structure satisfied by every active NORI coloring, not a proof of one-switch full antipodal closure. It strengthens a mere matching constraint to a coordinatewise antipodal transversal and a spatial separation law.
