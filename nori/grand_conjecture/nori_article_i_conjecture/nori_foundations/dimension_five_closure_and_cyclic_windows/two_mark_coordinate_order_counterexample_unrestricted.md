# Sharpness of the unrestricted five-dimensional theorem: two marked directions

For any n≥6, the 4+2 partition when n=6 gives the particularly small obstruction. The oddness hypothesis of the full NORI conjecture is essential already in dimension six; this family deliberately violates oddness, and no claim against NORI follows. It also cautions against trying to extend the unconditional Q_5 theorem by induction. The construction is coordinate-only and uses no dependence on fixed exterior face bits.

**Statement**

For every n≥6, partition the coordinate directions into A and B with |B|=2. Color every ordered three-face (F,(a,b,c)) by h(a,b,c)=1 precisely when b∈A and (a∈B or c∈B), independently of F. Every full antipodal geodesic has at least two color changes. Thus the dimension-five one-change theorem for arbitrary ordered-three-face colorings cannot extend to any n≥6 without additional hypotheses. The coloring is reversal-even, h(c,b,a)=h(a,b,c), and hence does not satisfy NORI's antipodal-reversal oddness.

**Proof**

Fix any permutation p_1,...,p_n and mark the two positions occupied by B. The window whose middle position is j (2≤j≤n-1) has color 1 exactly when p_j∈A and at least one of p_{j-1},p_{j+1} lies in B. Call the resulting length-(n-2) word w. It contains a 1: if the first B-position is i≥3, position i−1 is an eligible marked-neighbor A center; if i=2, position 3 is such a center unless the second B is at 3, in which case position 4 works; if i=1, position 2 works unless the second B is at 2, in which case position 3 works. All these centers are within [2,n−1] because n≥6. Suppose w begins with 1. Then position 2 is A and B occurs at position 1 or 3. If position 3 is B, then w_2=0; position 4 is A and w_3=1 unless the other B lies at position 4, in which case position 5 is A and w_4=1. If position 3 is A, the first B is at position 1 and the second B lies at some k≥4. For k=4, the consecutive colors w_2,w_3,w_4 equal 1,0,1. For k≥5, w_2=0 and w_{k−2}=1 (center k−1 is A adjacent to B at k). Thus whenever w begins with 1 it exhibits a later 0 followed by a later 1, giving at least two switches. By reversal symmetry, the same conclusion holds when w ends with 1. In the remaining case w begins and ends with 0, and its established occurrence of 1 gives at least two switches. Since all windows of the geodesic are those of the permutation p, their colors are independent of the starting vertex and the conclusion holds for every antipodal geodesic. Finally reversal exchanges a,c and preserves the disjunction of their membership in B, so h is reversal-even.
