# Research judgment, publication restraint, and the present closure frontier

Prepared 19 October 2026 in session session_nori_r11785_3, at the user's request for a new assessment and research programme. This is a strategic assessment, not a closure theorem. NORI1 and the full boundary one-change Hamiltonian problem remain open.

## 1. The change in research model

The objective is to understand and eventually prove the unrestricted conjectures. Publication is an occasional consequence of that work. A run that explores seriously, finds no reliable advance, and publishes nothing is an acceptable and often preferable outcome. There is no expectation of a discovery, note, counterexample, new definition, or manuscript addition per continuation.

The recent record shows a recurring substitution: a difficult universal implication is identified; a tractable family satisfying its desired hypotheses is studied; a theorem about that family is published; then the next investigation enlarges the family. The proofs can be correct and the scope statements careful while the research programme makes little movement toward the missing implication. Repeated publication also changes the apparent norm for subsequent researchers, who imitate the accessible endpoint rather than return to the difficult question.

This assessment includes the present author's nonlinear nested-return and odd-singular-core results. Those theorems exhibit useful mechanisms and retain nonlinear dependence in parts of the system. No argument derives their decompositions at an arbitrary critical boundary. Extending those families again is therefore a poor default next step. The same judgment applies to further bounded terminal-component classifications and finite rank ceilings.

The replacement model is sustained investigation of an unresolved mechanism, with publication restraint:

* Choose a full-class implication or a missing hypothesis-production theorem. Write its quantifiers and explain the path from that statement to closure.
* Spend substantial effort on competing proof ideas, unsuccessful constructions, and attempts to break the proposed lemma. A promising route can deserve several continuations without a theorem.
* Use exploratory restrictions as temporary laboratories. Return to the unrestricted obstruction after learning the mechanism; avoid allowing the laboratory to become the objective.
* Keep routine calculations, unsuccessful guesses, and uninformative examples out of the shared manuscript and research-note stream. A short conversational report can say that no result was established.
* Persist a negative result when it decisively changes a live central route, with its precise scope and the hypothesis it invalidates. Another variant of an already understood limitation usually needs no separate record.
* Publish a restricted theorem when its mechanism supplies a credible missing step, or when its independent mathematical significance warrants separate attention. Clearly distinguish those reasons.
* Continue or change direction on mathematical evidence. The recommendations here impose no quota, fixed experiment count, certificate-size bound, or compulsory output.

These principles should reduce administrative and publication activity as well as mathematical noise. They are not a request to replace theorem production with a stream of strategy reports. This article records the requested change once; future revisions should respond to consequential evidence.

Several recent obstructions materially change priorities. The acyclic-orientation ceiling applies even to nonlinear strongly connected systems with large feedback number. Independent products cannot repair failed sweep-rank or Frobenius-mixing certificates. More decisively, a legal successful dense-dependency nonlinear family has unavoidable safe curvature while all safe one-per-pair midpoint collisions vanish in a chosen parity fiber. Thus shared gates plus local safe curvature still do not force the desired selected-fiber collision.

These are scoped method limitations. They do not invalidate root changes, other parity fibers, unsafe orders transported across the switch, or complementary halfwords with other multiplicities. An argument must explain which of those additional features it uses. Adding dependency density to a failed hypothesis is particularly unhelpful when invisible parity rows can create arbitrary extra dependencies without changing the tested trajectories.

The full-gate 2-CNF and the common-prefix cocycle are stronger starting points than another solved family because they apply to arbitrary nonlinear tables and preserve relations among many paths. Their publication still leaves a universal forcing obligation. We should resist treating every faithful representation as progress of the same order as a theorem eliminating a possible counterexample.

## 3. A single hypothetical boundary, with its quantifiers preserved

Let G be a legal paired-flat coloring of Q_(2m), with pair gates f_i(p,q) independent of their own p_i,q_i and satisfying
\[
f_i(p,q+\mathbf1)=1+f_i(p,q).
\]
Let S be one legal antipodally paired alternating-square support and let B=T_SG. The boundary target is
\[
G\text{ admits a red full antipodal geodesic}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B\text{ admits a red full antipodal geodesic}.
\]
This must hold for every m for which the model is defined, every such G, and every legal S. The established connected switch space and paired-flat lift make the universal target sufficient for unrestricted NORI1.

Assume a hypothetical failure. On this same pair (G,B,S), the established normalization gives perfect balance and the strict one-internal-hit property for every red full geodesic of G. The zero- and two-hit alternatives are already excluded. The following additional statements can be used together where their hypotheses apply:

* Every square-safe complementary-halfword collision vanishes, since its witness would survive in B.
* Every fixed-parity square-safe sweep family obeys the rank ceiling and the bidirectional support exclusions.
* A switched step in a red witness cannot commute with an adjacent step from another pair, since that exchange would supply an avoiding witness.
* Fixing all gates except the switched pair gives a whole-gate 2-CNF whose clauses arise from geometric full paths. The assignment defining B satisfies it; the neighboring assignment defining G does not.
* Prefix perturbations arise from conjugates of the same local two-transposition switch. They cannot be independently specified for different orders.

These constraints have not been proved jointly impossible. Their co-occurrence is the research problem. Witness existence at another coloring, another independently chosen root, or another reduction cannot be silently inserted into this list. Nor does a necessary condition for a successful special family become a necessary condition for badness.

For a proposed reduction, check only the preservation properties that its argument uses: legality, antipodal oddness, success or failure, the selected boundary, shared gate values, root freedom, and direction multiplicity. An unknown preservation claim is a mathematical gap. Frozen faces need not inherit antipodal oddness.

## 4. What merits recording

A substantial result should identify the new implication it supplies. A universal hypothesis-production theorem, a repair operation with a proved termination rule, a usable estimate at the required asymptotic scale, or a decisive refutation of a central live lemma warrants durable attention. An equivalent encoding warrants attention when it enables such a mechanism; the encoding alone should not start an indefinite publication chain.

Correctness, unrestricted applicability, and relevance to closure are separate questions. A theorem valid in all dimensions can still concern a thin subclass. An unrestricted necessary condition can still be too weak to advance the proof. A finite computation can be decisive when it refutes the intended lemma, while a much larger computation can add no useful information.

No publication should claim grand-conjecture progress merely because its terminology applies to graphs or matroids. The boundary implication requires its own full-strength witness and a decoding that preserves all original vertices. The following Subsection retains this as an active mathematical line.
