# Article I — Antipodal-reversal-odd geodesic colorings of the cube

Let n≥4. An ordered three-face (F,π) of Q_n consists of a three-dimensional cube face F and an order π=(i,j,k) of its free coordinate directions. Let bar F be the antipodal image of F and rev π=(k,j,i). Consider any binary coloring c(F,π) satisfying c(bar F,rev π)=1-c(F,π). The color may vary between faces but is independent of the initial traversal corner of F. Each length-three window of an antipodal geodesic determines an ordered three-face. CONJECTURE: An antipodal geodesic exists for which the sequence of consecutive ordered-three-face colors has at most one change. Coordinate-only reversal-odd triple colorings are the subclass whose colors depend only on π. The one-dimensional analogue colors antipodal edges oppositely, as in the original Norine conjecture. Unrestricted based-window colorings form a larger class for which rank parity supplies counterexamples; they are not the current problem.
