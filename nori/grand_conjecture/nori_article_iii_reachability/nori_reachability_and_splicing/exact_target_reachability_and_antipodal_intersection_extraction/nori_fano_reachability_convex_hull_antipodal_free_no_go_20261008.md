# Fano reachable-region counterexample: cube center in convex hull without antipodal overlap

# A Fano-plane obstruction to naive convexification of uncolored monochromatic reachability

Let \(V=\{1,\ldots,7\}\) and consider the seven lines
\[
\mathcal L=\{123,\ 145,\ 167,\ 246,\ 257,\ 347,\ 356\}.
\]
Each line has three points, each point belongs to exactly three lines, and any two lines meet. Let
\[
\mathcal F=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal L}2^{V\setminus L},
\]
the downset generated by the seven four-element complements of the lines.

**Theorem.** The family \(\mathcal F\) contains the empty set and every singleton, is downward closed, and has **no complementary pair** \(S,V\setminus S\). Nevertheless the center \(m=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)\) lies in \(\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_S:S\in\mathcal F\}\). In fact \(\mathcal F\) is exactly the *color-free monochromatic-geodesic reachability family* of the zero root in a binary edge coloring of \(Q_7\) (with antipodal oddness NOT required).

**Proof (anticomplementarity).** If \(S\subseteq V\setminus L\) and \(V\setminus S\subseteq V\setminus M\), then \(S\supseteq M\), so \(M\cap L=\varnothing\), impossible since Fano lines intersect. Hence \(\mathcal F\) contains no complementary pair. Downward closure and inclusion of singletons are immediate.

**Proof (center).** Each point lies outside precisely four of the seven lines. Therefore
\[
\frac17\sum_{L\in\mathcal L}\mathbf1_{V\setminus L}
=(4/7,\ldots,4/7).
\]
Consequently
\[
m=\frac18\mathbf1_{\varnothing}
+\frac18\sum_{L\in\mathcal L}\mathbf1_{V\setminus L},
\]
a convex combination of the empty-set vertex and the seven maximal members of \(\mathcal F\). 

**Proof (reachability realization).** For an edge of the Boolean cube going from subset S to S∪{i}, color it 0 if \(S\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal F\) and 1 otherwise. Every edge from the empty root is color 0 because all singletons lie in \(\mathcal F\), so a nontrivial monochromatic geodesic starting at the empty set must be color 0. Every prefix of a color-0 root geodesic lies in \(\mathcal F\). Conversely every \(T\in\mathcal F\) is reached by a color-0 geodesic enumerating its elements, because downward closure ensures all prefixes lie in \(\mathcal F\). Thus \(R(0)=\mathcal F\). \(\square\)

**Sharp scope.** This refutes the tempting statement \(m\in\operatorname{conv} R(x)\Rightarrow R(x)\cap\overline{R(x)}\ne\varnothing\) for general binary edge colorings, *even when R(x) is a genuine monochromatic-geodesic reachability region, is a downset, and contains all singleton targets.* The constructed coloring is not assumed to satisfy NORI/edge antipodal oddness; the corresponding implication for antipodally odd colorings remains a separate question. More generally, convex-hull coincidences of abstract antipode-free accessible sets are insufficient without exploiting the coloring law.

**Check.** The displayed coefficients are correct: one empty vertex has weight 1/8 and each of the seven four-set vertices weight 1/8, total eight weights summing to one; each coordinate occurs in four of the seven four-sets, yielding 4/8=1/2.

## Dimension-independent strengthening: projective binary hyperplanes

For every integer d>=3, set n=2^d-1 and index the cube coordinates by the nonzero vectors v of F_2^d. For each nonzero a in F_2^d define A_a={v !=0 : a·v=1}, and let F_d be the downset generated by these n sets A_a.

**Theorem.** (1) F_d contains the empty set and every singleton, and it has no antipodal-complementary pair. (2) The center of the n-cube lies in conv{1_S:S in F_d}; indeed
\[
(1/2,\ldots,1/2)=2^{-d}\left(\mathbf1_{\varnothing}+\sum_{a\ne0}\mathbf1_{A_a}\right).
\]
(3) For every S,T in F_d, **their union misses at least \(2^{d-2}-1\) coordinates**. In particular, for d>=4 the family contains NO two disjoint supports covering all but one coordinate; as d grows, it avoids complementary and near-complementary pairs by an unbounded gap. (4) F_d is again realizable as the uncolored monochromatic-geodesic reachability family of one root in some binary edge coloring of Q_n without imposing antipodal oddness.

**Proof.** Each nonzero v satisfies a·v=1 for exactly 2^{d-1} choices of a, proving the displayed convex combination: there are exactly 2^d equally weighted vertices, including the empty set, and each coordinate occurs in exactly half of them. The complementary hyperplane H_a={v !=0:a·v=0} has 2^{d-1}-1 coordinates. Two distinct H_a,H_b intersect in the nonzero vectors of a codimension-two subspace, of size 2^{d-2}-1; for a=b their intersection is even larger. If S subset A_a and T subset A_b then V\\(S union T) contains H_a intersect H_b. This proves the gap, anticompementarity, and the absence of near-partitions when d>=4. Downward closure and inclusion of all singletons hold since for each v there exists a with a·v=1. The same 'red iff upper endpoint in F_d' coloring realizes R(0)=F_d as above. QED.

**Implication.** Any argument that extracts antipodal or near-antipodal monochromatic connectors solely from convex-hull centrality, downward closure, or accessibility fails by an arbitrarily large support deficit. To exploit such convex geometry for antipodally odd EDGE coloring, one must prove an additional COLOR-SPECIFIC restriction on the reachability family. This no-go concerns arbitrary colored cubes and does not refute the odd-coloring conjecture.
