# All center-marked tournament triple colorings close monochromatically through dimension sixteen

THEOREM (arbitrarily center-marked tournament three-windows close monochromatically for every 3<=n<=16; explicit finite orientation-path classification).

Let t(a,c)+t(c,a)=1 be any tournament orientation on [n], and s:[n]->F_2 an arbitrary marking. In every dimension 3<=n<=16 there is a full permutation p such that the (n-2)-word h_i=t(p_i,p_(i+2))+s(p_(i+1)) is constant. More generally the result holds for n=2r-1 or 2r whenever every r-vertex tournament realizes every prescribed oriented Hamilton path, except possibly alternating paths in REGULAR odd-order tournaments.

GENERAL REDUCTION AND PROOF. Choose the majority mark alpha and r=ceil(n/2) vertices O marked alpha. Let E be remaining r-1 or r directions. Choose a directed Hamilton ordering e_1,...,e_|E| with t(e_j,e_(j+1))=0 (Redei). To interleave O and E into a monochromatic word of color alpha, it suffices to order O with t(o_j,o_(j+1))=alpha+s(e_j), j=1,...,r-1. If the induced tournament on O realizes every oriented Hamilton pattern, this works.

Suppose the only exceptional orientation patterns on r directions are the two ALTERNATING (antidirected) patterns, and these can fail only when the r-subtournament is REGULAR (each vertex outdegree (r-1)/2, hence r odd). The required O-pattern alternates only when the center marks s(e_1),...,s(e_(r-1)) alternate. Thus among the first r-1 E vertices there must be exactly (r-1)/2 vertices of the minority mark and the same number of majority marks. Write u for the total number of minority-marked directions among n. For n=2r-1, this is possible only if u=(r-1)/2. For n=2r, this is possible only if u=(r-1)/2 or u=(r+1)/2. In these exceptional balanced-minority cases the majority class contains at least r+1 directions (r>=3). Every tournament on r+1 vertices contains a nonregular induced r-vertex subtournament, so choose O among majority vertices to be NONREGULAR. Then even alternating O-patterns occur, and interleaving closes monochromatically.

NONREGULAR DELETION LEMMA. For odd r>=3 no tournament on r+1 vertices can have every r-vertex induced subtournament regular. Indeed if all deletions were regular, fixing any pair a,b and remaining vertex v, comparing v's outdegrees after deleting a and after deleting b gives t(v,a)=t(v,b). Varying a,b forces each vertex to orient all its incident edges in the same direction (globally source or sink), impossible in a tournament on >=3 vertices. Therefore some deletion is nonregular. This argument also applies to majority sets of size greater than r+1.

EXACT SMALL-ORDER ORIENTED HAMILTON CLASSIFICATION. Orientations of a tournament of r vertices are coded by C(r,2) independent arc bits. Enumerating all 2^C(r,2) orientations, all r! permutations, and their r-1 consecutive arc bits gives:
 r=3: 8 tournaments; 6 universal; 2 cyclic/regular tournaments miss precisely the two alternating 2-edge patterns.
 r=4: 64 tournaments; all universal (also follows from the power-of-two parity theorem).
 r=5: 1024 tournaments; 1000 universal; 24 regular exceptions, each misses precisely the two alternating 4-edge patterns.
 r=6: 32768 tournaments; all universal.
 r=7: 2097152 tournaments; EVERY nonregular tournament is universal, and exactly 240 regular exceptions miss precisely the two alternating 6-edge patterns; no other 7-vertex tournament misses any orientation pattern. This last assertion combines exact exhaustive tournament checks of pattern21 and pattern42 with independent Z3-UNSAT tests for every other one of the 64 patterns: for each fixed pattern the SAT clauses forbid its realization on all 7! orders; all 62 other instances return UNSAT.
 r=8: all universal by the proved power-of-two parity theorem, without enumeration.

These exact finite verifications establish the general reduction's tournament hypothesis for r=3,4,5,6,7,8, hence n=2r-1,2r from n=5 through n=16. r=2 covers n3,4 directly; the proof also recovers n5,6 without using prior global NORI closure. The exact C++ enumerators for r3..6 and r7 alternating checks are straightforward: for each arc orientation mask, compute the bit-word of each vertex permutation, recording which of the 2^(r-1) patterns occur. For r7 all nonalternating patterns were independently certified universal by a 21-variable, 5040-clause-per-instance SAT UNSAT test; the only 240 exceptional orientations are regular.

PHYSICAL NORI CONSEQUENCE. For n=3,...,16, an ordered physical three-face coloring whose values on ONE central exterior-Hamming layer equal this marked tournament table h(a,b,c), independently of the exterior one-set, admits a genuine MONOCHROMATIC full antipodal cube geodesic. Outside that central layer, arbitrary nonlinear/exterior-sensitive colors are permitted. Choose the mono full direction order above and the alternating cube root keeping every window on that central layer.

OPEN EXTENSION. The infinite subsequence n=2^(k+1)-1 or 2^(k+1) is already solved analytically by the power-of-two parity theorem. For other n>=17, suffice to prove the pure tournament assertion that nonregular odd-order tournaments realize every oriented Hamilton path and regular tournaments can omit only alternating patterns, and even-order tournaments realize every pattern. This is a precise graph-theoretic reduction for the marked-center subclass; it is NOT a reduction for arbitrary physical NORI colorings.
