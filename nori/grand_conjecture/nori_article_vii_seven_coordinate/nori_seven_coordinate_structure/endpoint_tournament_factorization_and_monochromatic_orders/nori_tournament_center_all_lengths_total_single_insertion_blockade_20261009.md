# Arbitrarily long monochromatic tournament paths reject every one-vertex insertion

Proposition (all k>=5). For every k>=5 there is a tournament orientation t on V={v_1,...,v_k,x} and a constant center label s=0 such that the reversal-odd ordered-triple coloring h(a,b,c)=t(a,c)+s(b) makes P=(v_1,...,v_k) monochromatic 0, but none of the k+1 permutations obtained by inserting x into P is monochromatic. The same coloring nevertheless has a monochromatic spanning order, by the tournament endpoint-factorization theorem.

Proof. Define t(u,v)+t(v,u)=1 for distinct u,v. Prescribe t(v_i,v_{i+2})=0 for 1<=i<=k-2, t(v_i,v_{i+1})=1 for 1<=i<=k-1, t(x,v_2)=1, and t(v_{k-1},x)=1. The prescribed unordered tournament edges are all distinct, so extend orientations arbitrarily. Each old P-window (v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}) has color t(v_i,v_{i+2})=0. Inserting x between v_i and v_{i+1} creates the window (v_i,x,v_{i+1}) colored 1; it destroys at most two of the k-2 old windows, leaving at least one 0 because k>=5. Prepending x creates (x,v_1,v_2) colored 1, and appending x creates (v_{k-1},v_k,x) colored 1; all old 0-windows survive. Each one-vertex insertion therefore has at least one 0 and one 1. The constant-center global tournament theorem yields a different monochromatic full order. QED.

Scope. This explicit single-path extension obstruction holds for arbitrarily long paths within the elementary tournament-factorized subclass. Any general enlargement proof must permit rerouting already-used directions or exploiting a family of witnesses. It concerns monochromatic insertion, not one-switch insertion.
