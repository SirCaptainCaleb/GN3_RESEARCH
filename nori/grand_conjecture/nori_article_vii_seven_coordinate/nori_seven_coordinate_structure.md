# Seven-coordinate endpoint constraints and tournament structure

**Coupled chronological edge-order potentials and the full-boundary defect target.** For any balanced or nearly balanced bipartition, the full alternating *increasing* Hamilton-path conditions are exactly the conditions that the triangular edge-rank potential \(F(A,B)=\sum_{j}\sum_{i\le j}w(a_i,b_j)\) be an adjacent-swap local **minimum** in the \(A\)-permutation and local **maximum** in the \(B\)-permutation. This proves the precise joint compatibility requirement left by the transitive chronological theorem. Checkerboard-free edge-order matrices are equivalently those whose every rank prefix is a chain graph; each such matrix, including each induced submatrix, has a row-maximum/column-minimum pivot and therefore local increasing three-edge connectors. Spanning compatibility remains an **open conjecture** even for chain-prefix matrices, although exhaustive small cases hold. A first-moment bound shows that a random complete-graph edge order typically has no checkerboard-free \(K_{k,k}\) with \(k>(8/\log(3/2)+o(1))\log n\), so this special class cannot be extracted at polynomial scale in arbitrary orders. An explicit \(K_5\) edge ordering has increasing Hamilton paths in every \(K_4\) restriction but none spanning all five vertices, refuting four-local Hamilton completion. Finally, for all **ordinary** boundary tournaments, an \(N\)-vertex spanning permutation with \(D\) bad windows contains a positive tight path of order at least \(2+\lceil(N-2-D)/(D+1)\rceil\) when a positive window exists, and the known hereditary square-root bound supplies \(D=O(\sqrt N)\). Any full-class theorem reducing \(D\) to \(o(\sqrt N)\) would improve the established square-root guarantee. Proofs, executable finite certificate, sharp hypotheses and limitations: *Coupled chronological saddle potentials and checkerboard obstructions*, Subsection composition v2.

**Chronological transitivity and the exact parity-coupling gap (new).** For any prescribed sequence of transitive tournaments \(T_1,\ldots,T_{m-1}\) on \(m\) vertices, there is a spanning directed path whose edge in slot \(i\) belongs to \(T_i\): minimize \(\Phi(p)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=j}^{m-1}\rho_i(p_j)\) for increasing rank functions \(\rho_i\). Each inverted adjacent pair admits a strictly decreasing swap. In an ordinary boundary tournament this orders one parity class so that *all* windows centered on any fixed ordered complementary class are positive, provided those middle-local tournaments are transitive. The two interleavings cannot in general be made simultaneously positive: ordering the three perfect matchings of \(K_4\) in successive levels gives a globally edge-ordered boundary tournament with every middle-local tournament transitive yet no monochromatic spanning tight path (every simple four-vertex graph path has equal first and last edge levels). By contrast arbitrary cyclic time-indexed tournaments can fail to support even a prescribed three-vertex Hamilton path. Complete proofs and limits: *Endpoint-tournament factorization and monochromatic orders*, composition v6. These barriers retain the full open ordinary boundary-tournament long-path problem.

**Small-order completion.** The arbitrary-middle-sign spanning theorem now holds for **every \(n\ge3\)**. Havet–Thomassé's only exceptional oriented Hamilton paths are antidirected in three regular tournaments of orders \(3,5,7\). If the majority sign class has extra vertices, choose a nonregular induced majority subtournament of the requisite order; otherwise the opposite-sign parity sequence is constant and requires only an ordinary Hamilton path. The complete elementary elimination appears in *Endpoint-tournament factorization and monochromatic orders*, composition v5.

**New linear ordinary-boundary theorem for two arbitrary middle comparison tournaments.** If every local middle tournament is one of *two arbitrary tournaments* \(T_0,T_1\), independently of whether either admits a common edge order, a boundary 3-tournament on \(n\) directions has a positive vertex-simple tight path of length/order at least \(\lceil2n/3\rceil\); when the two middle-type classes differ in size by at most one, a positive Hamilton tight path exists. The proof interleaves directed Hamilton paths in the cross-type tournaments and, for unbalanced classes, takes the larger of an entire one-type path and a balanced two-type subpath. This theorem and the complementary arbitrary-sign spanning theorem are integrated in *Endpoint-tournament factorization and monochromatic orders*, composition v5. Both results remain structural subclasses; the fully arbitrary boundary case is open.

**New spanning boundary-tournament theorem (arbitrary middle-sign twists).** For any tournament \(T\) on \(n\ge3\) vertices and any function \(s:V(T)\to\mathbb F_2\), the direction-only reversal-odd triple coloring \(b(a,v,c)=t_T(a,c)\oplus s(v)\) has a vertex-simple **spanning positive tight path**, hence a genuinely full monochromatic cube geodesic from every root. Split vertices into the \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) majority-sign odd positions and the remaining even positions. A directed Hamilton path (or its reverse) on the latter fixes one parity of triple comparisons; the Havet–Thomassé universal oriented-Hamilton-path theorem realizes the prescribed arbitrary orientation word on the former. This removes the one-exception restriction of the elementary factorization theorem below and introduces an oriented-path universality mechanism. The unrestricted boundary class remains open because its different middle tournaments need not be global reversals of one tournament. See *Endpoint-tournament factorization and monochromatic orders*, version 4.

Acyclic boundary-tournament transfer (all dimensions): a direction-only reversal-odd triple coloring whose comparison orientation of L(K_n) is acyclic inherits an edge order on K_n. The Bucić-Kwan-Pokrovskiy-Sudakov-Tran-Wagner increasing-path theorem then yields a monochromatic coordinate geodesic of length n/2^{O(sqrt(log n loglog n))}. The graph path is original-vertex-simple, so every cube direction is distinct. This exact transfer is proved in the new Subsection on nearly-linear monochromatic geodesics. Exterior-dependent comparison orders require a separate coherence principle.

# Seven-coordinate endpoint constraints and tournament orders

A directed geodesic with direction word \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\) produces a binary word \(w_1,\ldots,w_{m-2}\) of colors of its consecutive physical ordered three-faces. The dependence of \(w_i\) on the initial cube vertex is confined to the coordinates exterior to that face. We exploit this locality to separate endpoint choices from an invariant middle block.

**Lemma 1 (shared-pivot formula).** For a seven-move order \(p=(a,b,c,d,e,f,g)\), fixing all initial bits other than \(t=x_d\) makes its five colors have the form
\[
(A(t),M_1,M_2,M_3,E(t)).
\]
For the analogous six-move order, two independent root bits vary the two endpoint colors, giving \((A(u),M_1,M_2,E(v))\).

**Proof.** In the seven-move order the coordinate \(d\) belongs to each of the three interior free triples \(bcd,cde,def\), while it is exterior to the first \(abc\) and last \(efg\). Hence all three middle colors are independent of \(x_d\). In the six-move order, the intersections of free sets of interior windows contain the two central directions, whereas the first and last faces omit exactly one of those directions each. \(\square\)

If the fixed middle block \(M\) has \(q\) changes, a one-switch choice of root exists exactly when some pivot satisfies
\[
q+[A(t)\ne M_1]+[E(t)\ne M_{\rm last}]\le1.
\]
For an invariant middle block with \(q=0\), failure for both values of \(t\) forces both endpoints always to disagree with its common color. For \(q=1\) and endpoints that each toggle, existence is governed by whether their XOR matches the XOR of the middle endpoints. These are exact finite-memory conditions on the two physical end windows.

**Lemma 2 (independent seven-coordinate wings).** Fix the initial bits in directions \(a,b,d,f,g\) and vary \(u=x_e\), \(v=x_c\). Then
\[
(w_1,\ldots,w_5)=(A(u),B(u),M,D(v),E(v)).
\]
Writing \(\ell(u)=[A(u)\ne B(u)]+[B(u)\ne M]\) and \(r(v)=[M\ne D(v)]+[D(v)\ne E(v)]\), a successful root exists among the four choices exactly when
\[
\min_u\ell(u)+\min_v r(v)\le1.
\]
**Proof.** The direction \(e\) is exterior exactly to the first two ordered windows and free in the middle; the direction \(c\) is exterior exactly to the last two and free in the middle. Their effects on the left and right wing colors are independent. Every switch is internal to one wing, so the count is exactly \(\ell(u)+r(v)\), which minimizes separately. \(\square\)

An additional bit \(t=x_d\) affects only the first and last windows, refining the pattern to
\[
(P(u,t),L(u),M,R(v),S(v,t)).
\]
When \(L\) and \(R\) each toggle, choose their unique inputs \(u_*,v_*\) making the three central windows equal to \(M\). If either \(P(u_*,t)\) or \(S(v_*,t)\) can equal \(M\), the full path has at most one change. Thus failure at both values of \(t\) forces
\[
P(u_*,0)=P(u_*,1)=S(v_*,0)=S(v_*,1)=1-M.
\]
This rigid endpoint obstruction is local to the seven-direction order.

**Theorem 3 (tournament-factorized monochromatic subclass).** Let \(T\) be a tournament on the \(n\) directions, let \(t(a,c)=0\) if \(a\to c\) and \(1\) otherwise, and let \(s:V\to\mathbb F_2\) be constant outside one distinguished vertex \(v\). The position-independent ordered-face coloring
\[
c(F,(a,b,c'))=t(a,c')+s(b)
\]
is antipodal-reversal odd and admits a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic.

**Proof.** Reverse the free order: the tournament orientation bit changes by one, while the middle bit is unchanged. This is exactly NORI oddness. Every tournament admits a directed Hamilton path, obtained inductively by inserting a new vertex immediately before the first path vertex it dominates. Choose such a path through the distinguished direction \(v\), and extract from one side of \(v\) a directed subpath of length \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) (one side is long enough). Put its directions into every other position of the desired permutation, with \(v\) at the corresponding endpoint; put a directed Hamilton path on the remaining directions into the other parity positions. Thus \(p_i\to p_{i+2}\) for every \(i\) and \(v\) is an endpoint. Along this full order, every window has color \(s(p_{i+1})\), the same constant since no interior \(p_{i+1}\) equals \(v\). \(\square\)

The local pivot and wing theorems are exact necessary restrictions for bad seven-coordinate orders. The tournament theorem gives an independent all-dimensional positive subclass. Synchronizing the local restrictions between different orders and roots remains open for arbitrary NORI colorings.

## Seven-coordinate absorption as a compatibility problem

The factorization \(w=(P(u,t),L(u),M,R(v),S(v,t))\) exhibits three genuinely independent roles: a central physical window \(M\), the two separately adjustable wing bits \(u,v\), and a common endpoint pivot \(t\). When \(L\) and \(R\) are sensitive, choose \(u,v\) to force the central three-window block to be monochromatic. A bad order then requires *both* endpoints to remain opposite to \(M\) for every pivot value, producing the rigid bit pattern from the wing subsection.

The one-flipper and flat-bridge lemmas handle special ways to carry this block into larger cubes. They are not a general seven-dimensional closure theorem, since the exterior-bit colors on the two newly formed seam windows can still obstruct extension. The separate tournament-factorization construction is all-dimensional, but assumes its explicit coordinate-only outer-tournament plus middle-bit representation.

## Polynomial paths under sparse triplewise exterior dependence (new)

A natural intermediate hypothesis between direction-only boundary tournaments and arbitrary physical-face colorings gives a **polynomial** monochromatic-geodesic lower bound. Suppose colors satisfy **separate same-face reversal oddness** \(c(F,(a,b,c))=1-c(F,(c,b,a))\). For each *unordered* triple \(T\), allow its six ordered-face colors to depend on an arbitrary triple-specific set \(S_T\) of at most \(d\) exterior coordinate bits; unlike a common-support hypothesis, the support may vary with \(T\). Build a 4-uniform dependency hypergraph with edges \(T\cup\{u\}\) for \(u\in S_T\). Its at most \(d\binom n3\) hyperedges admit, by elementary random selection and deleting one vertex per induced hyperedge, an independent direction set \(D\) of cardinality \(\Omega((n/d)^{1/3})\). For every triple \(T\subseteq D\), all coordinates capable of affecting the corresponding physical-face color lie **outside** \(D\). Fixing one cube root therefore produces a genuine direction-only boundary 3-tournament on \(D\), with no inconsistently transported faces. Applying the Devine–Milans snake theorem to that tournament yields a monochromatic cube geodesic of length \(\Omega((n/d)^{1/6})\). If there is instead one *common* support set \(S\) with \(|S|=t\), take \(D=V\setminus S\) immediately and obtain the sharper bound \(1+\sqrt{(n-t-1)/2}\). The unrestricted boundary-compatible case with \(d\asymp n\) remains open. Full hypotheses, constants, and proof are given in *Polynomial monochromatic geodesics from sparse triplewise exterior support* (Subsection 5); no antipodal oddness is needed beyond same-face reversal oddness for this particular extraction lemma.


**Strengthening (robust partial boundary snake).** The sparse per-triple exterior-support Subsection has now been strengthened from \(\Omega((n/d)^{1/6})\) to \(\boxed{\Omega((n/d)^{1/4})}\) for \(d=o(n)\), with *unchanged hypotheses*. The essential new lemma says a partial boundary 3-tournament on \(N\) coordinates with \(B\) missing unordered triples has maximal simple directed tight-path order \(L\) satisfying
\[
(N-1)/4\le(L-1)^2+(L-1)\sqrt{24B/N}.
\]
Thus \(B=O(N^2)\) still forces \(L=\Omega(\sqrt N)\). Sampling directions at probability \(p=1/\sqrt{dn}\) retains \(N=\Omega(\sqrt{n/d})\) coordinates and only \(O(N^2)\) triples whose designated influential exterior bits enter the sampled set. The good triples form a partial direction-only boundary tournament in *one fixed cube root*, and its robust snake path is a true monochromatic cube geodesic. The previous independent-set proof and its exponent \(1/6\) remain as a weaker historical route, not the best current bound. The unrestricted \(d=\Theta(n)\) boundary-compatible case remains unresolved.

**Current strongest sparse-dependence theorem (improved to exponent \(1/3\)).** Under same-face reversal oddness and at most \(d\) exterior-bit dependencies per unordered triple, with *no common support* required, the polynomial monochromatic-geodesic guarantee is now \(\boxed{\Omega((n/d)^{1/3})}\) for \(d=o(n)\). The prior \(1/6\) and \(1/4\) exponents are superseded. The new proof samples \(N\asymp(n/d)^{2/3}\) directions, retaining only triples whose exterior supports avoid the sampled set. It then discards terminal **pairs** with too many missing triples, rather than deleting entire directions or requiring low defects at one entire vertex. The robust snake lemma is \(\alpha(N-1)/2\le(L-1)^2+b(L-1)\), where \(\alpha\) is the fraction of surviving terminal pairs with at most \(b\) missing triple extensions. Sampling yields \(\alpha\ge5/8\) and \(b=O(\sqrt N)\), hence a positive simple tight path of order \(\Omega(\sqrt N)\), realized in one fixed physical cube fiber as a monochromatic geodesic. See the revised full proof in Subsection 5. ## Directed-pair comparison rigidity (new, Subsection 4)

There is an exact collapse from asymmetric *ordered-pair* ranking models to the already-treated globally edge-orderable class. Let \(\lambda(a,b)\) be any scores in a totally ordered set and define \(h(a,b,c)=\mathbf1\{\lambda(a,b)<\lambda(b,c)\}\). If \(h(c,b,a)=1-h(a,b,c)\) for every triple, then normalize the finitely many directed scores to integer ranks \(\rho(a,b)\) and assign to each undirected edge
\[
s(\{a,b\})=\rho(a,b)+\rho(b,a).
\]
Reversal oddness implies \(h(a,b,c)=1\) precisely when \(s(\{a,b\})<s(\{b,c\})\): the forward strict directed comparison combines with the reverse weak comparison, and conversely. Thus the adjacent-edge orientations have an acyclic global potential, a single edge order realizes every triple, and the preceding nearly-linear increasing-path transfer applies with original-vertex simplicity.

The quantitative obstruction is sharp at the level of forced symmetric-score collisions. If \(\lambda\) assumes \(m\) distinct scores, let \(B\) count unordered triples having a reversal violation at any middle vertex. Among the \(n-1\) edges at a fixed vertex, symmetric ranks occupy at most \(2m-1\) integer values. Each collision forces a reversal violation at that middle vertex. Cauchy–Schwarz and the three possible middle vertices per unordered triple give
\[
B\ge \frac n6\left(\frac{(n-1)^2}{2m-1}-(n-1)\right)_+.
\]
Consequently exact boundary reversal oddness forces \(m\ge\lceil n/2\rceil\), and a logarithmic-height directed-pair comparison model has \(\Omega(n^3/\log n)\) defective unordered triples. These results forbid an entire low-rank potential representation of general boundary tournaments; they leave cyclic comparison orientations and unrestricted physical NORI1 outside their hypotheses. Complete proofs are in the revised acyclic-boundary Subsection 4.


## Probabilistic generic full-geodesic theorems (new)

**Uniform random nonlinear physical boundary-compatible NORI3:** For any fixed binary face-color word w of length n-2, the probability that no full antipodal geodesic realizes w is at most \(2^{-\varphi(n)/2}\) for n>=20, and a Janson sharpening gives
\[
\Pr(\text{no geodesic of word }w)
\le\exp\{-\varphi(n)[1-\tfrac14(\binom n1+\binom n2+\binom n3)2^{6-n}]\}.
\]
Thus a random legal BC coloring with arbitrary nonlinear exterior dependence almost surely has monochromatic FULL geodesics. For prime n, the union bound over all \(2^{n-2}\) window words shows that every word occurs on some full geodesic whp. The core proof uses \(K=\varphi(n)/2\) arithmetic-progression direction orders with pairwise disjoint unordered consecutive triples, hence genuinely independent sets of physical-face random variables. For each fixed order, two antipodal starting roots determine the same color word; the 2^{n-1} root orbits have expected two realizations of each prescribed word, and the physical-face overlap graph has only O(n^3) near neighbors per root, yielding second-moment/Janson bounds.

**Affine smoothing of ANY adversarial base boundary tournament:** Independently add uniform even-parity affine exterior-vector corrections to each ordered-triple reversal orbit of an arbitrary prechosen b(a,b,c). The correction preserves both separate reversal oddness and antipodal face invariance. For n>=15, with probability at least \(1-0.55^{\varphi(n)/2}\), some single full direction order has full-rank root-to-color-word map, realizing all \(2^{n-2}\) words via exactly four cube roots per word. This follows from independent random affine coefficient rows constrained to vanish on their own free triples and have even parity: with probability >0.45 an (n-2)-row matrix is full rank, and the arithmetic-progression orders provide independent trials. The exact affine change-vector/syndrome criterion itself was established previously and is credited rather than republished as new. The two new probabilistic results do NOT prove the universal square-root bound for adversarial fully physical boundary-compatible colorings.
