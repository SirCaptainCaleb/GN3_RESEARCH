# Folded-cube cycle-space presentation and antipodal girth

For \(n\ge3\), let \(Q_n\) denote the binary cube with antipodal map \(\tau\), and put \(G_n=Q_n/\langle\tau\rangle\) and \(H_n=L(Q_n)/\langle\tau\rangle\), where \(L\) denotes the line graph. The quotient \(G_n\) is a simple \(n\)-regular graph with \(2^{n-1}\) vertices and \(n2^{n-2}\) edges: two different coordinate directions cannot connect the same antipodal vertex classes for \(n\ge3\).

**Proposition 6 (the folded-cube line graph).** There is a canonical isomorphism
\[
H_n\cong L(G_n).
\tag{1}
\]
Under this isomorphism the two-sheeted covering \(L(Q_n)\to H_n\) is induced by \(Q_n\to G_n\).

*Proof.* Vertices on both sides are pairs of antipodal cube edges. Two pairs are joined precisely when their representatives can meet at a vertex. In \(G_n\) this is incidence of its corresponding edges; simplicity ensures a unique center of incidence. Every turn downstairs lifts uniquely once its first cube edge is chosen, proving the covering assertion. \(\square\)

A star triangle is a triangle formed by three edges incident with one vertex of \(G_n\). A square turn cycle consists of the four successive edges of a square face of \(Q_n\), regarded as four vertices of \(H_n\). Let \(T\) and \(S\) be their respective binary spans in \(Z_1(H_n;\mathbb F_2)\). Write \(\alpha\) for the covering monodromy \(Z_1(H_n;\mathbb F_2)\to\mathbb F_2\).

**Theorem 7 (local generation of even monodromy).** For every \(n\ge3\),
\[
\boxed{\ker\alpha=T+S.}
\tag{2}
\]
The dimensions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dim Z_1(H_n)&=n(n-2)2^{n-2}+1,\\
\dim\ker\alpha&=n(n-2)2^{n-2},\\
\dim T&=(n-1)(n-2)2^{n-2},\\
\dim(T+S)/T&=(n-2)2^{n-2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, star triangles, square turn cycles, and one antipodal-geodesic cycle span \(Z_1(H_n;\mathbb F_2)\).

*Proof.* Let \(I(G_n)\) be the subdivision graph of \(G_n\), whose vertices are \(V(G_n)\sqcup E(G_n)\). To a turn \(t=\{e,f\}\) at \(v\), assign the incidence chain
\[
\varphi(t)=(e,v)+(v,f).
\tag{4}
\]
This preserves boundaries and induces a linear map \(\varphi:Z_1(H_n)\to Z_1(I(G_n))\cong Z_1(G_n)\). It is onto: every cycle of \(G_n\) lifts to the turn cycle of its successive edges. Its kernel is \(T\). Indeed, different vertex stars use disjoint turn edges, and on each star \(\varphi(z)=0\) means that the turn chain has even degree at every vertex of the clique \(K_n\) of incident edges. The cycle space of a complete graph is generated by its triangles. We therefore have
\[
0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow Z_1(H_n)
 \xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }Z_1(G_n)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{5}
\]
with image equal to the whole final space and kernel equal to \(T\).

Let \(\beta:Z_1(G_n)\to\mathbb F_2\) be the monodromy of the cube covering \(Q_n\to G_n\). Tracing contact vertices in a lifted turn walk yields
\(\alpha=\beta\circ\varphi\).

Let \(F\) be the span in \(Z_1(G_n)\) of projected square boundaries of \(Q_n\). We prove \(\ker\beta=F\). Every projected square lifts closed, so \(F\subseteq\ker\beta\). Conversely, express \(z\in\ker\beta\) as a disjoint union of closed trails and connect them by paths used twice to obtain one closed walk \(W\) with incidence vector \(z\) modulo two. Since \(\beta(W)=0\), the walk lifts closed to \(Q_n\). Its binary edge chain lies in the cycle space of the cube, which is spanned by square boundaries: adjacent coordinate steps may be interchanged across squares and immediate backtracks canceled until any closed walk is reduced. Projection gives \(z\in F\). As the turn cycle of a cube square maps under \(\varphi\) to its projected square boundary, \(\varphi(S)=F\). Together with (5), this proves (2).

The \(2^{n-1}\) star cliques are edge-disjoint copies of \(K_n\), each with cycle-space dimension \(\binom{n-1}{2}\). Hence
\(\dim T=2^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{2}\).
The connected graph \(H_n=L(G_n)\) has
\[
|V(H_n)|=n2^{n-2},\qquad
|E(H_n)|=2^{n-1}\binom n2,
\]
giving the first line of (3). A full antipodal geodesic projects to a closed turn cycle whose lift ends at the antipode of its initial edge, so \(\alpha\ne0\). Its kernel consequently has codimension one, giving the remaining lines of (3). The same geodesic cycle adds a generator outside \(\ker\alpha\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 8 (local cochain criterion).** A function \(d:E(H_n)\to\mathbb F_2\) is induced by an antipodally odd binary coloring of cube edges if and only if its sum vanishes on every star triangle and square turn cycle, and its sum equals one on any one specified antipodal-geodesic cycle. The coloring is unique up to global complementation.

*Proof.* Such a coloring makes turn sums equal to covering monodromy, proving necessity. Conversely, Theorem 7 makes the stated conditions equivalent to \(d(z)=\alpha(z)\) for every binary cycle \(z\). The pullback of \(d\) to \(L(Q_n)\) then sums to zero on all cycles and equals the coboundary of a vertex function \(c\), unique up to a constant because \(L(Q_n)\) is connected. Its vertices are cube edges. Invariance of \(d\) under \(\tau\) makes \(c(e)+c(\tau e)\) constant on cube edges; summing around the specified monodromy-one cycle identifies that constant as one. Thus \(c\) is antipodally odd and induces \(d\). \(\square\)

**Theorem 9 (antipodal girth and stability).** If \(C\) is a simple cycle of \(H_n\) with \(\alpha(C)=1\), then
\[
\boxed{|C|\ge n.}
\tag{6}
\]
Equality holds if and only if \(C\) is the cyclic turn cycle of a full antipodal geodesic of \(Q_n\). More quantitatively, let \(|C|=r\), choose a lift of its cyclic edge sequence
\[
e_1,\ldots,e_r,e_{r+1}=\tau e_1,
\]
and let \(u_i\) be the common cube vertex of \(e_i,e_{i+1}\), with \(u_0=\tau u_r\). Write \(s\) for the number of indices with \(u_i=u_{i-1}\), and \(m_j\) for the number of changes \(u_{i-1}\ne u_i\) made in coordinate \(j\). Every \(m_j\) is a positive odd integer, and
\[
\boxed{r-n=s+2\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{m_j-1}{2}.}
\tag{7}
\]

*Proof.* Both \(u_{i-1}\) and \(u_i\) belong to the cube edge \(e_i\). They either coincide or differ only in its direction. Since \(u_r=\tau u_0\), the sequence of contact vertices changes each coordinate an odd number \(m_j\) of times. Therefore \(r=s+\sum_jm_j\), giving (7) and (6). When \(r=n\), the identity forces \(s=0\) and \(m_j=1\) for all \(j\). Thus the lifted edges traverse every direction once from \(u_0\) to \(\tau u_0\), yielding an antipodal geodesic. The converse follows directly by cyclic completion of its turn sequence. \(\square\)

**Corollary 10 (minimal-cycle enumeration).** Both the monodromy-one cycles of length \(n\) and the monodromy-zero direction-rainbow star cycles of length \(n\) number
\[
\boxed{2^{n-2}(n-1)!}
\tag{8}
\]
when cycles are unoriented.

*Proof.* There are \(2^nn!\) directed antipodal geodesics with specified root. Each projected cycle has \(n\) choices of its initial edge-pair, two cyclic orientations, and two lifts, hence \(4n\) preimages. Division gives (8). Independently, every vertex of \(G_n\) supports \((n-1)!/2\) unoriented cyclic orders of its \(n\) incident direction-distinct edges, and \(G_n\) has \(2^{n-1}\) vertices. These star cycles are distinct, giving the same count. \(\square\)

Theorem 9 identifies full antipodal geodesics intrinsically as the shortest cycles with nontrivial covering monodromy. Theorem 7 supplies generators for its monodromy kernel using local triangles and squares. Consequently the turn-weight optimization of Article XI can be formulated over the shortest monodromy-one cycles, with no independent direction-rainbow condition. The support-weight bound needed for unrestricted NORI1 remains open.

## Half-rank rainbow palette rigidity

THEOREM (palette rigidity at the half-rank threshold). Let R be a spanning subgraph of the binary cube Q_(2m) with red degree m at every vertex. Give each edge its cube-direction label. Let L_R be the maximum length of a red direction-rainbow path. Then L_R>=m. Moreover, L_R=m if and only if EVERY red connected component is a full m-dimensional cube face. If at least one red component is not a full m-face, a red direction-rainbow path of m+1 edges exists.

Proof. At each vertex x define the red palette S(x)={i : x to x+e_i is red}, of size m. Starting at any root, a red rainbow path with t<m used labels can always extend by an unused red label, as S(x) has size m. Thus it can attain m steps. Suppose there is no red rainbow path of length m+1. Take any red rainbow m-path P beginning at x, with used label set U(P). Reversing P, any red incident label at x not already in U(P) could be prepended (or appended to the reversed path), making a rainbow (m+1)-path; hence S(x) subset U(P). Both have size m, so S(x)=U(P).

Now take a red edge in direction i from x to y=x+e_i. If S(y) contained some j not in S(x), begin a red rainbow walk from x with the two moves i,j; these labels are distinct because i belongs to S(x). Continue greedily to length m: at every stage t<m the terminal point has m red directions and at most t previously used labels. The resulting rainbow m-path from x includes j not in S(x), contradicting S(x)=U(P). Hence S(y) subset S(x); equality holds by cardinality. Therefore red palettes are constant along every red edge and every red component. At each vertex of such a component, precisely its m palette directions are red; traversing all the corresponding face edges shows the component is the entire m-dimensional cube face generated by that palette. Conversely, in a union of m-faces no red direction-rainbow path can exceed m edges. QED.

COROLLARY (unconditional antipodal improvement). Every antipodally odd, vertex-balanced coloring of Q_(2m) contains a red direction-rainbow path of length at least m+1, and by antipodal reflection also a blue one. Proof: if all red components were m-faces, choose a red A-face R, |A|=m, and put B=[2m] minus A. On the antipodal face tau R every A-direction edge is blue by oddness, hence every B-direction edge at tau R is red by balance. Because all red components are m-faces, through each vertex of tau R passes an entire red B-face. As tau R contains one vertex for every possible assignment of A-bits, these B-faces cover the entire cube. Therefore every B-edge of the entire cube is red, contradicting the existence of the initial A-face of red edges (which has all its B-edges blue). Thus at least one red component is not an m-face and the theorem applies.

STABILITY EXTENSION TEST. Let P be any red direction-rainbow path of length m+k, with endpoints x,y and direction set U. If |S(x) symmetric-difference S(y)|>2k, then P extends by at least one red edge at an endpoint. Indeed |S(x) union S(y)|=m+|S(x) symmetric-difference S(y)|/2 >m+k=|U|, so at least one endpoint has a red incident direction not yet used. Appending to one endpoint, or prepending and reversing at the other, produces a longer red rainbow path. Consequently every globally longest red rainbow path with length m+k has endpoint palettes differing in at most 2k directions. For k=0 the palette rigidity theorem upgrades this endpoint constraint to constancy across every red edge, by constructing m-paths beginning with an arbitrary red two-step prefix.

Scope: this is an unrestricted structural rainbow-path result, not a proof of NORI1; m+1 is far from the n-1 target sufficient for NORI1. The stability inequality highlights a quantitative avenue: sufficient palette disagreement at the ends of long rainbow paths forces improvement without assuming a bound on nonlinear direction rules.

The bound is sharp for balanced unary examples: choose signed direction rules paired as +chi_k,-chi_k for reader bits k other than their own bits.

EQUALITY CLASSIFICATION. Equality of the motion inequality requires total influence of every signed Boolean rule s_j to be one. In its Fourier expansion this forces all Walsh coefficients beyond degree one to vanish; a real-valued Boolean sign function supported on the first Walsh level must be a single signed character s_j=+chi_k or -chi_k. Edge legality requires k!=j. Conversely such unary rules have unit total influence, so equality holds. Pointwise balance further imposes cancellation between the positive and negative copies of every reader character chi_k. Thus the globally smallest red-palette motion characterizes the balanced unary family, rather than restricting the theorem to few nonlinear rules.

QUANTITATIVE ABUNDANCE. For vertex-balanced n=2m, along any red i-edge the two endpoint palettes both contain i, and both have cardinality m. Hence D_i<=n-2 and is even. Let C_R denote the number of undirected red edges across which the palette changes. The motion lower bound implies sum_(ordered red edge endpoints) D_i >=n*2^(n-1). Each changing undirected edge contributes at most 2(n-2), hence C_R>=n*2^(n-2)/(n-2). The same holds for the blue color by antipodal translation. This is a global exponential-size supply of potential rainbow-path exchanges, not yet a guarantee that enough exchanges can be joined into a near-spanning path.

ENDPOINT PALETTE STABILITY. If a red direction-rainbow path has m+k edges and endpoints x,y, and |S(x) symmetric-difference S(y)|>2k, then at least one of its endpoints has a red incident edge in an unused direction. Therefore the path extends by one red rainbow step at that end. In any longest red rainbow path of length m+k, the endpoint palette disagreement is at most 2k. This gives a quantitative target for pushing the half-rank theorem: exploit many palette-changing red edges to force an extremal long rainbow path to have endpoint disagreement exceeding twice its surplus over m.

## Antipodal half-volume bound for face components

HALF-VOLUME STRUCTURAL THEOREM. In a vertex-balanced antipodally odd binary coloring of Q_(2m), m>=2, let F_R be the vertices in red connected components that are full m-dimensional cube faces, and F_B the analogous blue-face components. Antipodal translation identifies red-face components with blue-face components, so F_B=tau(F_R). Then F_R and F_B are disjoint. Proof: if x lay in both, the red m-face A through x would have free direction set A and the blue m-face through x would have complementary free direction set B=[2m] minus A. Apply antipodal translation to the blue B-face: it becomes a red B-face through tau x. Any A-dimensional face and any B-dimensional face with A,B complementary have a nonempty intersection (fix the A-bits according to the B-face and the B-bits according to the A-face). At this intersection both the A-direction edges and B-direction edges would be red, giving red degree 2m rather than m, contradiction. Thus F_R and tau F_R are disjoint, implying |F_R|=|F_B|<=2^(2m-1). At least half the vertices belong to red components that are not full m-faces, and the same holds for blue. At each vertex, at least one of its red and blue components is nonface. Each nonface component contains a monochromatic direction-rainbow path of length at least m+1, by the palette rigidity theorem. This is stronger than the mere existence of one such path and is dimension uniform. It does not imply that each such vertex itself starts an m+1 path, only that its connected component contains one.

## Polynomial root abundance beyond half dimension

ROOT-ABUNDANCE THEOREM FOR MONOCHROMATIC RAINBOW PATHS. Let n=2m>=4, and let c be any antipodally odd, vertex-balanced coloring of Q_n. Let G_R={x: a red direction-rainbow path of m+1 edges starts at x}. Then |G_R| >= 2^(n+1)/(n(n-2)), hence Pr_uniform[x in G_R]>=2/[n(n-2)]. The same bound holds for blue. Proof: red palette S(x) has size m; let D_i(x)=|S(x) symmetric difference S(x+e_i)|. From antipodal oddness and the Boolean Poincare inequality, the palette-motion identity gives sum_x sum_(i in S(x)) D_i(x)>=n 2^(n-1). On any red i-edge, both endpoint palettes contain i and have size m, so D_i(x)<=n-2. Therefore the set X of vertices incident with at least one red edge across which S changes satisfies |X| m(n-2)>=n 2^(n-1); hence |X|>=2^n/(n-2).
For each x in X select a red i-edge x->y=x+e_i on which S changes. Equal palette cardinalities imply an index j in S(y) minus S(x). In particular j!=i since i is red at both endpoints. Begin a red rainbow path at x with i then j. At every current path of length t<m, its endpoint has m red incident directions, of which at most t have been used, so the path can be extended to length m. Let U be its m-element used direction set; since j is in U but not in S(x), choose q in S(x) minus U. The edge x+e_q -> x is red and direction q is unused by the constructed path. Prepend it to obtain a red rainbow path of length m+1 starting at z=x+e_q. Every x in X supplies one z in G_R, and each z is a red neighbor of at most m vertices x. Hence |G_R|>=|X|/m>=2^(n+1)/(n(n-2)). The same result for blue follows by antipodal reflection.
FINITE AUDIT: Exhaustive enumeration of all 48 balanced antipodally odd colorings of Q4 (16 independent antipodal edge-orbit bits with balance imposed) shows that eight of the sixteen roots in each coloring have a red rainbow path of length at least three. The theorem supplies only four; its polynomial density is not claimed sharp.
The implication is dimension-uniform and independent of nonlinear-rule count. It guarantees polynomial root density for paths just beyond half rank, stronger in multiplicity than mere existence, but it is not a near-spanning theorem and does not by itself imply NORI1.
