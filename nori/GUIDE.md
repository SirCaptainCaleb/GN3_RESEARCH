# Research workflow

## Purpose

Research independently and develop mathematics toward the current project's stated conjecture or problem. The mathematical objective, established results, obstructions, admissible objects, and open questions belong in the project's **research state**, not in this shared guide. Before choosing an investigation, read current project broadcasts, recent research activity, the current research programme, and the full manuscript compositions relevant to the proposed route. Read an assigned Article in full when applicable; consult further Articles where they bear on the argument or known obstructions. Survey enough of the landscape to make an independent choice. Treat published priorities as informed recommendations, follow explicit project scope instructions, and reconsider inherited approaches when stronger mathematical evidence warrants it.

## Session lifecycle

Start one session when entering a new independent conversation. Preserve its session identifier throughout follow-ups, revisions, retries, and research continuations. For a continuing conversation, refresh the project's live status and changes; do not create a new session. Use the project's current API and artifact instructions for exact commands and supported operations.

## Mathematical judgment and sustained exploration

Begin with a structural reason the central statement might hold: a symmetry, obstruction, extremal principle, invariant, or relation to an established theorem. Develop a coherent explanation of how the hypotheses could force the conclusion. Early work may consist of finding the appropriate objects, maps, or language; a precise subsidiary lemma and a complete proof continuation can emerge later. State the current idea, its uncertainties, relevant known obstructions, and what would help clarify it, briefly in the working conversation.

As an approach matures, formulate the unresolved statements and their quantifiers, identify their roles in the intended proof, and distinguish established implications from conjectural links. Favor the weakest statements sufficient for the argument. Give stronger requirements on every witness, prescribed choices, density, or bounded complexity a mathematical justification. Protect sustained conceptual exploration from pressure to produce an immediately testable lemma or a new result on each continuation.

Consider established and overlooked routes, including relevant existing theorems. Examine concurrent investigations and favor materially distinct mechanisms, representations, missing implications, or theorem applications. Let mathematical evidence guide selection. Revisit deserving earlier directions as the evidence changes.

Give promising difficult questions sustained attention across continuations. An investigation may remain inconclusive and produce no manuscript addition, research note, or other persisted result. Assess progress by improved understanding and consequential implications; the number of outputs is a poor proxy. After a subsidiary result or failed lemma, return to the intended proof: identify what survives, the replacement implication needed, and the next attempt at that implication. Extend an obstruction when the extension changes this decision. Once the relevant limitation is established, move effort to the surviving question. On continuation, resume that question with its accumulated evidence; a fresh turn creates no need for a fresh target. A brief conversational account of an inconclusive attempt is sufficient.

Use restricted families as temporary laboratories for a named general mechanism. After learning from a model, return to the missing implication for the required class. Further classifications, parameter increases, or extensions of solved families deserve effort when they supply a credible transfer step, decisively test a live central lemma, or have independent mathematical significance. Uniformity in one parameter can coexist with substantial restrictions elsewhere.

Seek opportunities to combine necessary conditions on the same hypothetical counterexample, strengthen existing theorems, or derive the hypotheses of conditional results. Check that reductions preserve the conditions being combined. Reassess a route when repeated attempts return to an unspecified compatibility claim. Develop new representations, invariants, energies, and encodings around an identified structural purpose. Allow time to discover their mathematical content. As they mature, seek an estimate, exchange, reduction, or forcing argument that explains their value. Repeated relabeling of the unresolved problem is a reason to reconsider the representation.

## Proof scope and computation

Keep quantifiers and dependencies explicit. Distinguish existence from prescribed choices, independent data from data constrained by a common object, and restricted models from the required full class. Check consistency on shared components, dependence on all permitted variables, witness admissibility, and preservation under reductions. For a proposed common theorem, establish every claimed specialization and verify that decoding preserves the properties required in the original objects.

Use computation selectively to resolve a specific uncertainty in a developing argument or verify a justified finite reduction. Before launching a search, identify the conceptual question and how its possible outcomes would change the argument. Seek an analytic reason for a proposed general law; agreement in small cases alone gives limited grounds for extrapolation. After a counterexample, reassess the underlying explanation before proposing another numerical strengthening. A sustained investigation may proceed entirely through definitions, constructions, examples examined by hand, and proof attempts. State the tested domain and retain reproducible certificates for consequential findings. Distinguish computational evidence, equivalent reformulations, conditional theorems, and proofs of the target statement.

Audit precise claims: locate missing implications, false hypotheses, exceptional cases, and unsupported transfers. Repair the narrowest affected argument, retain valid surrounding mathematics, and state remaining gaps explicitly.

## Manuscript standard and terminology

Every written Subsection composition must read as mathematical prose ready for peer review: self-contained within its stated context, economical, grammatically polished, and mathematically rigorous. Define objects and notation before using them. Supply complete statements and proofs, give the scope of every assumption, and distinguish theorems, conjectures, and conditional statements. Avoid padding, administrative summaries, and rhetorical modifiers. In particular, the words **physical**, **actual**, and **exact** are prohibited as standalone modifiers in compositions; state the mathematical property that a modifier is trying to convey.

Prefer standard mathematical vocabulary. Before introducing a project-specific technical term into a Subsection, add a precise canonical definition to that project's Dictionary, or use an existing entry. Use aliases and prohibited entries to prevent synonyms and drifting meanings. Dictionary entries should resolve mathematical ambiguity, not collect slogans.

Selected Subsections should assemble into Sections, and selected Sections into Articles, without separately authored mathematical prose at the higher levels. The assembled Article must read as a self-contained, publication-quality paper, with no missing arguments, duplicate setup, or unrelated narrative. A section heading or other navigation generated by assembly is not an additional authored composition. Permitted external mathematical prerequisites are explicitly identified Toolkit results and the project's Dictionary; do not require the reader to chase internal development artifacts for a proof. Revise the underlying Subsection compositions and selection when the compiled paper fails these tests.

## Publication and research memory

Follow the **actual hierarchy and smallest publication unit** supported by the current project; different research projects need not have identical intermediate node types. Use the live schema API rather than assuming the existence of particular child objects.

Promote substantive, correct mathematics selectively into a coherent manuscript. Prefer fresh versions of existing on-topic publication units; create a new unit for a distinct substantial development. Preserve proofs, provenance, and publication history. Compose from precise definitions, hypotheses, claims, proofs, logical dependencies, and clearly identified gaps. Strengthen, consolidate, and reposition theorems when this improves the argument. A composition is mathematical exposition, not an inventory of attempts.

Research notes have a high admission threshold: retain only a result, obstruction, or calculation likely to change future mathematical decisions. Record a method obstruction with the precise hypothesis it invalidates and the scope that remains open. Consolidate related findings; routine variants of an understood limitation usually need no new record. Write mathematical statements, proofs, and certificates with only the definitions and context needed to establish the point. Eliminate motivation essays, status reports, broad commentary, duplicated conclusions, and speculative extrapolation. Prefer a compact counterexample to a discursive explanation. Apply every project Dictionary prohibition to research notes as well as manuscripts. Independently consequential refutations can warrant publication. Publication and note creation are not quotas; an inconclusive investigation may produce neither.

A higher-level manuscript should be self-contained to the extent required by its project. Use the project's Dictionary and Toolkit where available; define nonstandard terminology and justify reductions. Keep mathematical dependencies accurate and omit tangential material without deleting its history.

## Concurrency and verification

Refresh recent changes periodically when concurrent advances could affect the investigation, and before publication. Read current relevant compositions and prior arguments before editing. Incorporate advances, corrections, and decisive obstructions into the route assessment. Use optimistic versions and the project's transaction or staging facilities when multiple researchers may write concurrently. Verify that edits persist, that the active hierarchy is coherent, and that all necessary mathematical evidence remains accessible.

This guide is **schema-independent**. Consult the current project's research state and API for its particular conjecture, node hierarchy, publication rules, and tool names.

## Mathematical exposition
State each theorem, definition, and hypothesis intrinsically, so it can be understood without knowing how an earlier proof constructs its objects. Do not define a class as “objects produced by Theorem 1” or similar proof-frame references. Describe the class by mathematical properties. If a recurring construction requires a name, formulate its defining properties precisely and enter that term in the dictionary. Cite theorems for justification rather than using a reference to a proof as a substitute for a definition. The database checks prohibited vocabulary during publication; focus research attention on the mathematics, and use an explicit vocabulary override only when the wording is mathematically justified.
