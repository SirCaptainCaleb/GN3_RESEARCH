# Exponential lower bound for coloring-blind universal direction-order hitting

For a legal antipodally odd coloring c of Q_n call a direction permutation pi successful if some root has a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic using that order. A fixed family F of permutations is universal if every legal c has a successful order in F. All families below are chosen independently of the particular coloring. A universally successful family need not be known to exist in unrestricted dimensions.

THEOREM 1 (dimension-uniform exponential lower bound). Let n>=3, a=floor((n-1)/2), b=n-1-a. Any universal coloring-blind direction-order family has at least
H(n)=n!/(2 a! b!)=(n/2)*binom(n-1,a)
orders. In fact, for every given family F, among a distribution of explicitly relabeled legal colorings the probability F contains any successful order is at most |F|/H(n). By Stirling H(n) is asymptotic to 2^n sqrt(n)/(2 sqrt(2 pi)). Thus for any polynomial-size predetermined list, almost every such random relabeling defeats every listed order.

PROOF. Choose a hub h and partition the other n-1 directions into A,B of sizes a,b. Choose any leaf j. Let c_h(x)=x_j, c_i(x)=x_h for i in A, and c_i(x)=1+x_h for i in B. Every rule ignores its own direction bit and flips color under complementing all vertex bits, so c is legal. For any red full path in root x, all A directions must occur before h and all B after h if x_h=0, or all B before h and all A after h if x_h=1. Conversely every such order admits a red root: the root bit x_j is free to satisfy the hub edge, while x_h is chosen for the required phase. There are precisely 2 a! b! allowed permutations. Reversing a red path from its antipodal endpoint is another red path, and antipodally translating it gives a blue path in the same order; hence blue and red successful orders coincide. Uniformly randomly relabel all directions. Every predetermined pi is successful with probability 2 a! b!/n!. Apply the union bound over F. If |F|<H(n), positive probability remains that no member succeeds. This proves the unconditional order-hitting obstruction, without assuming NORI1.

THEOREM 2 (sharp Q4 universal order-hitting number). For n=4 the minimum size of a universal family is 12. Index the 16 antipodal orbits of undirected Q4 edges by scanning undirected edges (u,u+e_i), u<u+e_i, increasing u then i, introducing each orbit on first encounter. Assign mask bits on the first edge of each orbit and complementary bits on opposite edges. The legal mask 0x0536 has successful orders only (0,3,2,1) and (1,2,3,0), which are reversals. Relabeling directions realizes every reversal pair as the sole successful pair, so a universal family must meet each of the 12 reversal pairs. Conversely exhaustive enumeration of all 65536 legal Q4 colorings gives at least two successful orders in each case; every successful order comes with its reversal. Thus selecting one permutation from each reversal pair is universal, attaining 12. Among the 65536 colorings, 48 have only two successful orders. Their complete histogram appears in the accompanying manuscript.

THEOREM 3 (rigid block amplification). For each n>=4, every fixed family of fewer than 12^floor(n/4) permutations can be defeated by a legal coloring. Take r=floor(n/4) autonomous Q4 copies of mask 0x0536, and for residual directions assign c_i(x)=x_0 reading a bit of the first block. Randomize each four-block by an independently uniform relabeling. Any global monochromatic full path restricts to a monochromatic full path in every four-block. For one prescribed global order, each restriction is uniform over 24 possible local permutations and succeeds with probability 2/24=1/12, independently across the r blocks. Thus the global success probability is at most 12^(-r). Union bound proves the statement.

There is also an explicit Q5 legal mask 0x1cbf6d2423, with edge orbits indexed by the same scan rule, whose only successful direction orders are (0,1,2,3,4), (0,1,2,4,3) and their reversals (3,4,2,1,0), (4,3,2,1,0). This was checked by enumerating all 120 orders and 32 roots, using the independently defined orbit index. Parallel five-block products and random independent relabelings give another lower bound 30^floor(n/5). More generally for any integers s,t>=0 with 4s+5t<=n, one may place s four-blocks and t five-blocks with residual reader directions, yielding an unconditional family-size lower bound 12^s 30^t.

COMBINED BOUND. When the universal order-hitting number is finite, it obeys H_n >= max{ (n/2)binom(n-1,floor((n-1)/2)), max_(4s+5t<=n) 12^s30^t }. Dimension four has equality H_4=12; in dimension five the explicit example shows H_5>=30, without an assertion of optimality.

SCOPE. This is a theorem about coloring-independent, predetermined direction-order families. It does not constrain adaptive algorithms choosing order permutations after reading the coloring, and does not settle unrestricted NORI1. Its significance is a dimension-uniform worst-case obstruction to arguments testing only polynomially many fixed orderings. It complements the earlier dihedral transport result note_fd1bec3611385a3cdf60a079 and sharply contrasts with near-sure random-coloring success for a small cyclic order list.

## Stronger coupled two-hub theorem, cluster geometry, and failure-only search complexity

Let n>=4 and partition the direction set into A,{h,k},B with a=|A|>=1, b=|B|>=1, a+b=n-2. Define a legal antipodally odd coloring by c_h=1+x_k, c_k=1+x_h, c_i=x_h for i in A, and c_j=x_k for j in B. Its rules ignore their own direction bits and flip under antipodal vertex complementation.

**THEOREM 1.** The successful direction permutations are precisely the a!b! permutations of block order A,h,k,B and the a!b! permutations of B,k,h,A. For every successful order there are 2^(n-2) red roots and 2^(n-2) blue roots. The total number of rooted red full geodesics is 2^(n-1)a!b!, and the same number are blue. The roots admitting monochromatic full geodesics are those with x_h+x_k=1; every such root admits both colors when the order is allowed to vary.

Proof. If h precedes k, the red hub-edge conditions force x_h=0,x_k=1. Then every A direction must precede h and every B direction must follow k, and conversely any such order is red from every root with those two bits. If k precedes h, the conditions force x_h=1,x_k=0 and the opposite block order. Antipodal translation swaps red and blue for any fixed order, and reversing order exchanges the two blocks for each successful root. All counts follow.

**THEOREM 2 (strengthening of the earlier order-hitting theorem).** For a= floor((n-2)/2), b=ceil((n-2)/2), every coloring-independent direction-order family that succeeds for all legal colorings has at least
K_n = n!/(2a!b!) = n(n-1)/2 * binom(n-2,a)
members. This improves the previous balanced one-hub bound by floor(n/2), and K_n grows as 2^n n^(3/2)/(4 sqrt(2 pi)) asymptotically. Values: K_4=12 (the established optimum), K_5=30, K_6=90, K_7=210, K_8=560, K_10=3150, K_20=9237800.

Proof. Uniformly randomly relabel all direction names of the displayed legal coloring. Any prescribed permutation succeeds with probability p=2a!b!/n!, by transitivity of the full symmetric group on orders. A union bound over any proposed family F shows that if |F|<1/p then a relabeling defeats all its members. The central-binomial asymptotic follows from Stirling.

**THEOREM 3 (quadratic witness-cluster gap).** In the adjacent-transposition Cayley graph of S_n, the successful order set induces two disjoint components, each isomorphic to the Cartesian product Perm(a) square Perm(b). Every successful order has precisely three adjacent-swap neighbors that fail. The total number of success/failure boundary edges is 6a!b!. The minimum Kendall-tau distance between the two components is
delta=ab+2a+2b+1=n^2/4+O(n)
for balanced a,b. Therefore every shortest adjacent-swap path from a successful order of the first component to one in the second passes through at least delta-1 successive unsuccessful orders.

Proof. Internal adjacent swaps permute A and B without changing their block placement, while the three boundary swaps cross A|h, h|k, or k|B and invalidate the required order. Between the two components, every relative comparison across two different blocks reverses, giving ab+2a+2b+1 unavoidable inversions. Choosing matching internal A and B permutations in both components realizes that distance.

**THEOREM 4 (failure-only order-oracle lower bound).** Consider an algorithm that queries a direction permutation and learns only whether that order supports at least one monochromatic full geodesic. Assume no additional access to coloring data; allow internal randomness and adaptation based on query responses. For the distribution obtained by uniformly relabeling the coupled two-hub coloring, the chance the algorithm has seen a successful order after q queries is at most q/K_n. Consequently any algorithm guaranteed to find a successful order on every legal coloring has worst-case expected query count at least (K_n+1)/2.

Proof. Condition on internal random bits. Until the first successful query, every oracle response is failure; therefore the query sequence along that branch is predetermined independently of the sampled relabeling. Apply the previous one-order probability and a union bound to its first q queries. Average over internal randomness. If T is the first successful query, sum Pr(T>q)>=1-q/K_n over 0<=q<K_n to obtain E T>=(K_n+1)/2. This result concerns the failure-only order oracle, not procedures that inspect individual edge colors or full profile tables.

The results are negative theorems for coloring-blind order choice and failure-only order oracles, not a counterexample to unrestricted NORI1. They also exhibit a family whose two successful permutation clusters are quadratically separated, constraining proposed local order-exchange forcing strategies.


## Tensor amplification of successful-order sparsity beyond base two

**Theorem 5 (tensor product principle).** For any legal antipodally odd colorings c^(1),...,c^(r) on mutually disjoint direction blocks of sizes m_1,...,m_r, take their Cartesian product coloring C on Q_n, n=sum m_j, in which every block rule depends only on the vertex bits of its own block. Write S(c) for the set of direction permutations admitting a monochromatic full geodesic from some root, and s(c)=|S(c)|. Then
  s(C)/n! = Prod_(j=1)^r s(c^(j))/m_j!.
More precisely, a global permutation lies in S(C) if and only if each block-restricted permutation lies in S(c^(j)). For any chosen successful block-restricted permutations, the full product coloring has a monochromatic path using every interleaving of them.

**Proof.** If a full path of one color exists, the subsequence of its edges in each block is a full geodesic in that factor with the same color. Conversely, fix any global order whose restriction in every block is successful. Choose a red starting root in each block for the corresponding restricted order. Existence of a red root follows from existence of a monochromatic root because antipodal translation flips colors while preserving the direction order. Concatenate these roots to form one global root. Since no block rule reads any other block bits, interleaving the block steps preserves every red edge. The number of global shuffles of given restricted permutations is n!/Prod m_j!, giving the displayed formula.

**Corollary 6 (multiplicative worst-case universal-order obstruction).** Let q_m(c)=m!/s(c) for a legal coloring with s(c)>0. For any fixed block factors as above, uniformly and independently relabel the directions within each block. For a prescribed global order pi the probability pi is successful is Prod_j 1/q_(m_j)(c^(j)). A union bound implies every coloring-blind universal order family has size at least Prod_j q_(m_j)(c^(j)). If n exceeds the sum of the chosen block sizes, remaining directions may be padded with legal reader rules c_i(x)=x_0 where 0 is a direction of the first block; the projection argument gives the same lower bound. In the failure-only oracle model of Theorem 4, this block distribution forces worst-case expected query count at least (1+Prod_j q_(m_j))/2.

**Corollary 7 (base greater than two).** Use the balanced coupled two-hub coloring of dimension 14, with six leaf directions on each side. It has 2(6!)^2 successful permutations out of 14!, so q_14=84084. For all n>=14 any universal family satisfies
  |F| >= 84084^floor(n/14)
and therefore
  liminf_(n->infinity) |F|^(1/n) >= 84084^(1/14) = 2.247839198... .
The same conclusion holds for worst-case order-only query complexity up to the constant factor one-half. This is a strictly stronger exponential base than the single-block 2^n polynomial-factor estimate or the older Q4 product base 12^(1/4)=1.86121.

**General optimized construction.** For each m>=4 write
  K_m=m!/[2 floor((m-2)/2)! ceil((m-2)/2)!].
For every integer n and every finite set of sizes m_1,...,m_r>=4 with sum<=n,
  |F| >= Prod_j K_(m_j).
Consequently one may optimize over block partitions to improve finite-dimensional lower bounds. In dimensions 28,42,56 the fourteen-block construction yields respectively
  7,070,119,056;
  594,483,890,704,704;
  49,986,583,466,014,331,136.
These counts concern predetermined direction-order families; they are not numbers of monochromatic paths in a coloring.
