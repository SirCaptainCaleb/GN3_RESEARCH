## Research note

**Top-cell parity theorem.** Let n>=5, let X be the dual type-A Coxeter cellulation of the sphere of Hamilton orders on n vertices, with its antipodal order-reversal involution J, and H a real (n-2)-dimensional space carrying the negation involution. Given any odd map f:X^(n-3)->S(H)=S^{n-3}, let D run over one representative from each J-pair of top (n-2)-cells. Orient the attaching spheres and define d(D) in Z to be the degree of f restricted to the attaching boundary of D. Then
\[
\boxed{\sum_{D/J}d(D)\equiv1\pmod2.}
\]
In particular some full-dimensional dual cell has odd obstruction degree. The top cells are indexed by ordered partitions A|A^c into two nonempty blocks, and J interchanges the complementary block orders.

*Proof.* The target is the unit sphere of the sign representation H of rank n-2. Its associated bundle over X/J=RP^{n-2} is the Whitney sum of n-2 copies of the canonical real line bundle. The mod-two Euler obstruction to extending the equivariant map across the top-dimensional cells is its top Stiefel-Whitney class alpha^{n-2}. Evaluating this class on the mod-two fundamental class of RP^{n-2} gives one. Cellular obstruction theory identifies that evaluation with the parity of the local attaching degrees d(D), one per quotient top cell. Signs of cell orientations do not matter modulo two. Thus the displayed sum is odd. QED.

**Interpretation.** For the critical-dimensional contraction of nonzero rational switch bivectors, the absence of favorable Hamilton orders gives a specified odd labeling of all complete flags and a compatible map on the one-skeleton. By sphere connectivity this extends across the (n-3)-skeleton. The parity theorem then forces an odd attaching degree on some two-block residue, regardless of the coloring. To prove the boundary one-switch conjecture using this target, the missing step is a coloring-sensitive construction forcing even (preferably zero) attaching degree on every two-block cell. The theorem localizes the obstruction but does not establish that cancellation.
