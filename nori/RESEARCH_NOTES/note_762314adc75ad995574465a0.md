## Research note

**Proposition (linear zero-switch rainbow path).** Let G be a finite simple graph with a proper edge coloring λ and minimum degree k≥1. Suppose an involutive graph automorphism τ and a binary edge labeling χ satisfy χ(τ e)=1−χ(e) on every edge. Then G contains a vertex-simple λ-rainbow path of length at least ⌈k/8⌉ whose edges all have the same χ-label. No assumption λ(τ e)=λ(e) or connectedness is needed.

**Proof.** The involution pairs each χ=0 edge with a χ=1 edge, so each label class has |E(G)|/2 edges. Let H be the spanning graph of χ=0 edges. Since |E(G)|≥k|V(G)|/2, its average degree is d(H)=|E(G)|/|V(G)|≥k/2. Every finite graph of average degree d has a nonempty (induced) subgraph H' of minimum degree at least d/2: iteratively deleting vertices of degree below d/2 would, if all vertices were deleted, remove fewer than d|V(H)|/2=|E(H)| edges, a contradiction. Thus δ(H')≥k/4. Take a λ-rainbow simple path P in H' maximal by length, with ℓ edges, and let v be an endpoint. Every edge from v to a vertex outside P has a color appearing on P, otherwise P extends. Properness makes the incident colors at v pairwise distinct. At most ℓ incident edges meet vertices already on P, and at most ℓ further incident edges have colors on P. Hence δ(H')≤deg_H'(v)≤2ℓ, so ℓ≥k/8. All edges of P lie in H and have label zero. Since ℓ is integer, ℓ≥⌈k/8⌉. □

The same assertion holds choosing χ=1. It applies to the general Article XIII problem under proper resource coloring and provides a quantitative baseline for the proposed weakening from a k-edge one-switch path to a linear-length path with logarithmically many switches. The estimate is not claimed sharp.
