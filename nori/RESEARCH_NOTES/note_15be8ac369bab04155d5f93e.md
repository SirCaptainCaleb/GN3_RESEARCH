## Research note

**Theorem (exponential fragmentation of perfectly balanced monochromatic cubes).** Let \(F\) be the antipodally odd edge coloring of \(Q_4\) whose vertex bits are \((x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1)\), with direction rules
\[
c_0=x_1,\qquad c_1=x_0,\qquad c_2=1-x_1,\qquad c_3=1-x_0.
\]
Every vertex has two incident edges of each color. The red graph is \(C_{12}\sqcup C_4\). For each \(h\ge1\), the Cartesian product coloring \(F^{\boxtimes h}\) of \(Q_{4h}\) is legal, antipodally odd, and perfectly balanced. Its red graph has \(2^h\) components: for each \(0\le k\le h\), there are \(\binom hk\) components isomorphic to the Cartesian product of \(k\) copies of \(C_{12}\) and \(h-k\) copies of \(C_4\), containing \(12^k4^{h-k}\) vertices. The largest red component contains a fraction \((3/4)^h\) of the \(16^h\) vertices. Its blue graph has the same component-size distribution. Nevertheless, there exists a red full antipodal direction-simple path of length \(4h\).

*Proof.* The two pairs \((c_0,c_2)\) and \((c_1,c_3)\) are complementary, proving balance; antipodal complementation reverses all color rules, and each rule ignores its own direction bit. A red step in either \(x\)-direction cannot enter \(x=(1,1)\) from another \(x\)-state, since at \(x=(1,1)\) both original-direction rules have color one. In the fiber \(x=(1,1)\), both \(y\)-direction edges are red, producing a \(C_4\). Among the three remaining \(x\)-states, red \(x\)-steps join \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\) to \((0,0)\); at \(x=(0,1)\) the \(y_0\)-direction is red, while at \(x=(1,0)\) the \(y_1\)-direction is red. The corresponding 12-vertex red subgraph is connected because one can move between the three \(x\)-states and toggle either \(y\)-bit. It is two-regular, hence \(C_{12}\).

The red subgraph of a Cartesian product coloring is the Cartesian graph product of the factor red graphs, whose connected components are products of factor components. This establishes the component census. A red full antipodal path in \(F\) starts at integer vertex 1, with bit 0 least significant, and follows the direction word \((3,0,1,2)\): all four traversed edges have color zero. Concatenate copies of this path across the \(h\) factors.

**Corollary (failure of macroscopic red-edge mixing).** The red graph of \(F^{\boxtimes h}\) admits a red-edge-free cut \((A_h,B_h)\) with \(|A_h|,|B_h|=(\frac12+o(1))16^h\). To see this, choose a union of red components whose vertex count is within \(12^h=o(16^h)\) of half the total. At its red density \(p=2h/(16^h-1)\), a mixing estimate would predict \(p|A_h||B_h|\sim(h/2)16^h\) crossing edges, while the observed count is zero. Thus these legal balanced graphs violate macroscopic sparse-pseudorandom edge mixing despite admitting full red geodesics.

*Research consequence.* Ordinary dense-graph regularity is vacuous for any subgraph of \(Q_n\), whose edge density is \(O(n2^{-n})\). Sparse regularity arguments that impose host-level macroscopic jumbledness on each monochromatic graph exclude this infinite family. Existing counterexample-preserving pseudorandom padding furthermore shows that independently fair fixed-order incident-color marginals do not simplify the underlying unrestricted conjecture.
