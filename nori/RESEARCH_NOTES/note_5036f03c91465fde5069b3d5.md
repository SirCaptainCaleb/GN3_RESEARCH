## Research note

**Theorem (binomial root defects and exponential scarcity in balanced cubes).** Let \(n=2h\) and define a legal odd coloring \(c\) of \(Q_n\) by \(c_{2a}(x)=x_{2a+1}\) and \(c_{2a+1}(x)=x_{2a}\) for \(0\le a<h\). For any doubled-direction word \(w\), let \(r(w)\) count those \(h\) reciprocal-reader pairs whose two occurrences alternate. Then
\[
\boxed{\sum_{x\in Q_n}t^{d_H(U_w(x),\bar x)}=(1+t)^{2r(w)}(4t^2)^{h-r(w)}.}\tag{1}
\]
Every successful word has \(r(w)=h\), and its defect distribution is binomial: there is one successful root among \(2^n\) roots.

Take the paired balanced lift \(C\) on \(Q_N\), where \(N=2n=4h\), defined by \(C_i(x,y)=c_i(x)\) and \(C_{n+i}(x,y)=1-c_i(x)\). Then **every** doubled-direction red holonomy word \(w\) on the lifted \(N\) directions has either zero antipodal-return roots or \(2^n\) antipodal-return roots. Thus every successful word has root-success fraction
\[
\boxed{2^{-n}=2^{-N/2}.}\tag{2}
\]
Some simple-bigon word is successful for \(C\).

*Proof.* In each two-bit factor of \(c\), swaps from other factors act trivially. A nonalternating four-letter subword reduces to the identity, leaving defect two at all four roots, so its defect polynomial is \(4t^2\). An alternating subword is \(U_jU_iU_jU_i\) or its inverse. The two swaps are transpositions along adjacent red edges of a square, and their product squared is a 3-cycle with one fixed vertex. The four root defects are \(0,1,1,2\), giving \((1+t)^2\). Factor independence proves (1).

For \(C\), the original-direction swaps act on \(x\) as the base swaps \(U_i\), independently of \(y\). Dummy swaps change their own \(y_i\) bits according to the current \(x\) and do not inspect \(y\). Any doubled word in all \(N\) directions restricts on the original \(n\) directions to a doubled word of \(c\). By (1), at most one original state \(x\) yields antipodal original-bit return. At this state, the dummy flip mask is independent of the initial \(y\). Accordingly, the full word has either no successful roots or all \(2^n\) roots \((x,y)\) with that \(x\). This proves (2).

Each reciprocal-reader square has a red antipodal geodesic starting from mixed bits. Concatenating the factor paths gives a red full geodesic for \(c\); its paired lift has one too, and the simple-bigon normal form gives a successful simple-bigon word for \(C\).

*Scope and consequence.* This shows that even balanced legal colorings with NORI1 witnesses can have an exponentially small successful-root fraction for every word. Hence no universal lower bound bounded away from zero can hold for the successful-root fraction of an individually chosen word. The theorem says nothing about the number of distinct successful words.
