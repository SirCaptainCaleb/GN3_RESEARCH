## Research note

**Theorem (classification of constant-weight full-geodesic roots).** Let c be any legal antipodally odd undirected-edge coloring of Q_n. Define H_x(pi) to be the number of color-one edges on the full geodesic from x in direction order pi. Call x weight-flat when H_x is constant over S_n. Let Gamma(c) be the undirected interaction graph: ij is an edge when either partial_j c_i or partial_i c_j is nonzero on some cube edge/square. If Gamma has q connected components, the set F(c) of weight-flat roots is either empty or a coset of the binary space spanned by the indicator vectors of its connected components. In particular |F(c)|=0 or 2^q. Whenever F is nonempty, n and each interaction-component size are even, and H_x(pi)=n/2 at every flat root. If Gamma is connected, at most one antipodal pair of roots is weight-flat.

**Proof.** Reversing a full direction order while keeping its starting root fixed replaces every direction's edge by its antipodal mate; the red count becomes n-H_x(pi), proving the n/2 assertion. Two permutations are connected by neighboring transpositions. On an oriented square with edge colors a,b,d,e in the two increasing routes, the swap leaves the red count unchanged precisely when a+b=d+e over the integers. Every square occurs as a consecutive two-step segment of rooted full geodesics, with orientation determined by the root. Thus x is flat precisely when all these oriented-square identities hold.

If x and y=x xor 1_T are flat, consider any square in directions i in T and j outside T. With the same face, the x-oriented identity is a+b=d+e; reversing its orientation only in i produces the y-oriented identity a+d=b+e. Subtraction gives a=e,b=d, so both cross-derivatives partial_j c_i and partial_i c_j vanish on this face. Because the face is arbitrary, Gamma has no edge crossing T and its complement. Hence T is a union of connected components of Gamma.

Conversely the absence of cross-component interactions makes c a Cartesian product of antipodally odd factor colorings. H_x(pi) is the sum of factor weights, and x is flat iff each factor root is flat. Antipodal complementation within a factor takes its red counts to complementary counts and preserves flatness. Thus independently complementing entire interaction components of one flat root gives all flat roots. Every flat factor has even dimension. This establishes the coset formula.

**Signed-square feasibility criterion.** For every pair i<j, anchor each i,j square at its 00 corner and denote its four colors a,b,d,e, with two routes having totals a+b and d+e. The parity x_i xor x_j of a flat root must equal 0 if a+b=d+e, or 1 if a+d=b+e. Intersect these permitted signs over all faces of each direction pair. A flat root exists iff the resulting pairwise XOR constraints are jointly consistent; solving the signed graph constraints takes O(n^2 2^n) square checks. When both signs are permitted on all squares for a pair, opposite edge colors on every such face agree, so that direction pair is noninteracting. Whenever some square has nonzero mod-two curvature a+b+d+e=1 mod2, neither equality holds, and F(c) is empty.

**Four-path strict-majority forcing.** At any root x not in F(c), some oriented square has unequal integer totals along its two increasing routes. Extend these two length-two routes to full geodesics using a common prefix and suffix. Their red-edge totals differ. Reversing the two orders at the same root complements their counts. Consequently among at most four full geodesics rooted at x, one has strictly more than n/2 red edges and one strictly more than n/2 blue edges. If Gamma is connected, this holds at every root except possibly one antipodal pair. If any square has nonzero mod-two curvature, it holds at every root.

**Self-dual integer rank characterization.** At the zero root, a weight-flat coloring has zero integer diamond defect on all oriented squares. Define rho(A) as the sum of color-one edges along any increasing path from the empty set to A. It is path-independent; rho(empty)=0; and each increment rho(A+i)-rho(A) lies in {0,1}. Antipodal oddness implies rho(A)=|A|+rho(E\A)-rho(E) for every A subset E=[n]. Indeed rho*(A)=|A|+rho(E\A)-rho(E) has the same single-element increments as rho, since each antipodal edge has opposite color, and both vanish at empty. Conversely any integer-valued rho satisfying normalization, zero-one increments, and this self-duality formula defines a legal coloring by its increments and makes the zero root flat. The formula forces rho(E)=n/2. Submodularity is not required.

**Sharp complete-interaction example.** For even n=2r, take rho(A)=min(|A|,r), the rank of the self-dual uniform matroid U_(r,2r). Equivalently c_i(x)=1 iff fewer than r other vertex bits are one. Every direction interacts with every other, so Gamma is complete. Both the zero and all-one roots are flat and by the theorem no other roots are. Nonetheless a red full antipodal geodesic exists: start with r one-bits and r zero-bits, alternate flipping a previously one bit to zero and a previously zero bit to one. Every step is red and every direction appears once. Thus the connected-case bound of two flat roots is attained for all even n.

**Reproducibility and boundary.** The independent Python verifier nori_flat_root_rigidity_verify.py compares all rooted full-path red counts with the signed-square criterion in random legal Q3,Q4,Q5 colorings and checks the component-coset formula, uniform matroid examples through Q10 and reciprocal-reader product examples through Q8. The proof holds in all dimensions. The strict-majority witnesses are not necessarily monochromatic and do not settle NORI1.

**Uniform rooted full-profile size theorem (proved).** Let c be any legal antipodally odd coloring of Q_n, n>=2. For every root x, let W_x be the set of direction-indexed color words of all rooted full geodesics. Then |W_x|>=2ceil(n/2). For n>=5 the stronger bound |W_x|>=2ceil(n/2)+2 holds. Equality in the first inequality is possible only in dimensions 2,3,4, where the coloring factors into reciprocal two-direction reader blocks, plus one unpaired odd reader if n is odd.

**Proof of first bound.** Form the graph G_x on W_x, joining profiles realized by permutations differing by one adjacent swap. It is connected because the permutation gallery is connected, and each edge of G_x changes at most two profile bits. Reversing the direction order at the same root complements every direction color and preserves gallery adjacency, so W_x has a free complement involution. Choose a shortest G_x path P=(w_0,...,w_L=bar w_0) between any antipodal pair of profiles. Necessarily L>=ceil(n/2). Its complement path bar P meets P only at its endpoints: a further meeting w_i=bar w_j would give an antipodal subpath of length |i-j|<L. Thus P union bar P has 2L vertices, establishing |W_x|>=2ceil(n/2).

**Equality classification.** Assume |W_x|=2k, k=ceil(n/2). Then L=k and W_x=P union bar P. Each step changes at most two bits. For even n=2k the changed-bit sets of P partition directions into k disjoint pairs. For odd n=2k-1, parity and the upper bound 2k on total changed bits force the same disjoint partition into k-1 pairs and one singleton h. The 2k profiles form a cycle with these disjoint-block transitions. Any nonneighboring pair on this cycle differs in at least three bits (four for even n), so every active gallery adjacent swap changes one of the displayed blocks. Every cube square in directions i,j can be reached after a suitable prefix of a full path from x, so any interaction of color rules for directions in different paired blocks would generate a gallery swap changing an impermissible bit set. Thus the only dependencies of a paired direction are on its partner; in odd dimension the paired rules also ignore h. Antipodal oddness then forces c_a(v)=v_b XOR alpha_a and c_b(v)=v_a XOR alpha_b for each pair. Interleaving does not affect pair colors, and each pair's two possible direction orders produce two complementary two-bit profiles. In odd dimension, inserting h before or after any such paired ordering produces opposite h colors while leaving pair colors fixed, by antipodal oddness of its rule. Hence W_x consists of 2^k profiles. Equality requires 2^k=2k, whose only positive integer solutions are k=1,2, yielding n=2,3,4. Since W_x is complement-invariant and its cardinality even, the lower bound improves by two for every n>=5.

**Examples and audit.** The coloring c_(2j)(v)=v_(2j+1), c_(2j+1)(v)=v_(2j) for each paired block, together with c_h(v)=v_0 when n is odd, has 2^ceil(n/2) profiles at each root and attains the theorem's lower bound in n=2 through n=6. The standard-library script nori_root_profile_holonomy_verify.py exhausts all 64 legal Q3 colorings and 65,536 legal Q4 colorings, finding respectively 48 and 48 with the smallest root profile size. Every one of the 48 Q4 extremizers is a reciprocal pairing: 3 matchings of four directions and 16 choices of direction constants. Further samples verify arbitrary nonlinear legal colorings through Q7 and the paired examples through Q8. The manuscript nori_rooted_profile_antipodal_gallery_theorem.md has the complete proof. This is a dimension-uniform profile diversity result, not a proof of NORI1.

## Rooted affine-hull transport and source-SCC parity theorem (new dimension-uniform strengthening)

Let W_x subset F2^n be the direction-indexed profiles of full antipodal geodesics from x. For every two-face F in directions i,j define alpha_F=partial_j c_i(F), beta_F=partial_i c_j(F), and its swap vector Delta_F=alpha_F e_i + beta_F e_j. Put L=span_F Delta_F and K=L^perp.

THEOREM A (root-independent direction and diagonal transport). For EVERY root x, aff(W_x)=x+z+L, where z is any fixed element of W_0. Proof: every adjacent permutation swap changes the profile by a square vector, and every square vector is realized at any root by arranging its two directions consecutively after the appropriate prefix. Thus the difference span is L independent of root. Sliding a first direction i to the end changes the root by e_i and changes just profile bit i. The parallel affine hulls therefore differ by e_i, proving the displayed transport formula over all roots. In particular the affine hulls always admit the bijection x -> x+z; that profile need not be realized by any path.

THEOREM B (classification by flat source SCCs). Form directed influence arcs j->i whenever partial_j c_i is nonzero somewhere. Call a source strongly connected component S flat if partial_j c_i=partial_i c_j on EVERY i,j square with i,j in S. If S_1,...,S_k are precisely these flat source SCCs, then K=span{1_(S_1),...,1_(S_k)}. All S_i are disjoint, nonempty, and EVEN in size; thus k<=floor(n/2) and dim aff(W_x)=n-k.

Proof: y in K is equivalent to y_i partial_j c_i = y_j partial_i c_j on each face. Let T=supp(y). Every i in T ignores all bits outside T (no incoming influence), and all internal interactions are pointwise reciprocal, so the connected components of the internal interaction graph are flat source SCCs in the full directed graph. Conversely each flat source SCC indicator satisfies every square equation, by incoming independence and internal flatness. Complements of rooted full direction orders complement ALL color-profile bits, so every affine parity constraint y has even support. Every block therefore has even size.

THEOREM C (rank-profile inequality). If |W_x|=2m, write its elements as m antipodal profile pairs. The binary affine difference span has dimension at most m: it is generated by the all-one vector and m-1 differences of pair representatives. Hence |W_x|>=2(n-k). If the directed influence graph is strongly connected, the only possible nonzero parity constraint has support ALL n directions, requiring n even. Therefore every odd-dimensional strongly directed-connected legal coloring has full rooted affine span and at least 2n profiles at EVERY root. For even n under strong directed connectivity, the rooted affine dimension is n or n-1 according as the global square system is nonflat or flat.

ROOT PARITY FORMULA. For each flat source block S, a constant lambda_S exists with sum_(i in S) w_x(pi)_i = sum_(i in S) x_i + lambda_S mod2 for ALL roots and permutations. These k equations describe the entire rooted affine hull. All 2^k cosets occur across roots, with 2^(n-k) roots per coset.

TWO-DIRECTION RIGIDITY. For any fixed root and any distinct directions i,j, the profile projection on i,j has 4 patterns unless {i,j} is itself a flat source SCC. In the exceptional case it has 2 complementary patterns, and necessarily c_i(v)=v_j+alpha_i and c_j(v)=v_i+alpha_j, independent of all other bits. Thus any requested two-direction color pattern is realizable at EVERY prescribed root unless the pair is a separate reciprocal-reader subsystem. Such exceptional pairs cannot overlap. Every three directions containing no exceptional pair realize at least six of eight projected patterns at each root.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: strong DIRECTED connectivity is essential for full rooted span in odd dimension. Weak undirected interaction connectivity does not suffice, as shown by the legal rules c_0=x_1,c_1=x_0,c_2=x_0 in Q3: the undirected interaction graph is connected but {0,1} is a flat source SCC and W_0 has only four profiles.

FINITE AUDITS AND LIMITS. Independent files nori_rooted_affine_span_verify.py and nori_rooted_affine_scc_verify.py test swap spans, source SCC decomposition, affine diagonal translations, and pairwise rigidity: all 64 legal Q3 and 65,536 legal Q4 colorings pass, as do sampled nonlinear Q5-Q7 colorings. Q4 rank-dimension distribution is 59232 with rank4, 6256 with rank3, and 48 with rank2. An independent exhaustive enumeration of all 32768 zero-constant legal affine Q5 coloring matrices, in nori_affine_q5_exhaustive.cpp, found minimum 24 rooted profiles under strong directed connectivity. Nonlinear orbit mask 403118304493, however, has strong directed connectivity and only 12 rooted profiles, independently checked by full permutation enumeration. This illustrates that full affine span does not imply profile-space saturation. The unrestricted NORI1 conjecture remains open.
