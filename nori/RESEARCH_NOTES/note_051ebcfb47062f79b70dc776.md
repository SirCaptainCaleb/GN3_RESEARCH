## Research note

**Proposition (word-only antipodal labels fail for unrestricted boundary colorings).** Let \(n\ge6\) be even and \(m=n-2\). In the ordinary reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament problem the opposition on complete Hamilton flags reverses the vertex order, and transforms every color word by
\[
J_{\rm word}(w_1,\ldots,w_m)=(1-w_m,\ldots,1-w_1).
\]
There exist admissible colorings and a complete Hamilton flag \(\mathcal F\) whose word \(w=\mathsf w_2(\mathcal F)\) has \(m-1\ge3\) switches but is fixed by \(J_{\rm word}\).

*Proof.* Take \(w=(0,1,0,1,\ldots,0,1)\) of even length \(m\). Then reversing and complementing gives the same word. Assign these bits to the ordered triples in an arbitrary Hamilton ordering \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\). None of its consecutive oriented triples is the reversal of another such triple, since the consecutive triples advance along the fixed order and the reversed triple travels backward. The assignments therefore extend to an unrestricted reversal-odd coloring of all ordered triples. The opposite flag \(J\mathcal F\) has the same word \(w\). Its switch count is \(m-1\). \(\square\)

**Corollary.** Suppose \(Y\) has a free involution \(A:Y\to Y\) (in particular, \(Y\) could be an antipodal sphere). There is no function \(L\) on the set of complete Hamilton flags which (i) depends only on the binary color word and (ii) is \(J\)-equivariant into \(Y\) for all unrestricted reversal-odd boundary colorings. Indeed the opposite flags \(\mathcal F,J\mathcal F\) of the construction have the same word, so condition (i) gives equal labels while (ii) gives \(L(J\mathcal F)=A L(\mathcal F)\), impossible because \(A\) is free.

This obstructs *all word-only* odd labels, not merely the first-signed-second-switch label. The simplex's complement-reversal opposition can fix an even-length binary word with many switches, whereas the cube's central antipode complements each word position and therefore never fixes any nonempty word. Consequently a universal Borsuk–Ulam-style chain proof cannot obtain equivariant target labels from the switch pattern alone; it must carry additional face-flag geometry, orientation, or incidence information to distinguish opposed flags.

**Additional 1-skeleton obstruction.** On the 5-vertex simplex, consider adjacent Hamilton orders \(\pi=(0,1,2,3,4)\) and \(\pi'=(0,1,3,2,4)\), differing by a single rank flip. Assign the three consecutive triple bits of \(\pi\) as \((0,1,0)\), and those of \(\pi'\) as \((1,0,1)\). The six oriented triples involved lie in distinct reversal pairs, so the assignments extend to an unrestricted legal boundary coloring. Both words have second switch at index two, but their initial bits differ. Therefore the simple label \(\ell(\mathcal F)=(-1)^{w_1(\mathcal F)}e_{s_2(\mathcal F)}\) takes opposite values on adjacent flags, so it cannot be extended to their dual 1-cell in the crosspolytope boundary by the canonical short edge. This rules out copying the cubical second-switch 1-skeleton map verbatim for boundary flags.
