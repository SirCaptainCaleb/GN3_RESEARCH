## Research note

Let \(n=2h\). For each factor \(a\in[h]\), take the legal odd two-cube coloring \(c_{2a-1}(x)=x_{2a}\), \(c_{2a}(x)=x_{2a-1}\), and form their independent Cartesian product \(c\).

**Theorem (exponential root scarcity for all doubled words).** For every word \(w\) in which each of the \(2h\) directions occurs twice,
\[
\#\{x\in Q_{2h}:U_w(x)=\bar x\}\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Some simple antipodal-bigon words attain one witness, so the maximum fraction of successful uniform roots over all doubled words is \(2^{-n}\). For every such word,
\[
2^{-n}\sum_x d_H(x,U_w(x))\le n/2,
\]
with equality when each two-cube factor's two direction chords interlace.

*Proof.* On the two-dimensional factor with bits \((u,v)\), the swaps \(U_u,U_v\) are transpositions of the two edges from \(00\), so they generate \(S_3\) on \(\{00,01,10\}\) and fix \(11\). A four-letter word containing each generator twice is the identity unless the occurrences alternate. If they alternate, it equals \((U_uU_v)^2\) or its inverse, a three-cycle on these three vertices. This three-cycle sends one of \(01,10\) to its antipode, and no other root to its antipode. Its mean Hamming displacement is \((1+1+2+0)/4=1\).

Swaps from distinct factors commute. Restricting the doubled word to each two-dimensional block gives one of the four-letter words above. The full return is the Cartesian product of these block returns. An antipodal root exists precisely when all factor restrictions interlace, in which case each block has one admissible root, giving one root in the entire \(2h\)-cube. The mean displacement is the sum of the block means and therefore at most \(h=n/2\). Choose any permutation \(\rho\). The doubled word \(w=\rho\rho\) interlaces the two directions in every block and gives one antipodal root. To represent the same word as a simple bigon, factor \(\rho=pq\) into two nonempty consecutive blocks, set \(\pi=qp\), and let \(A\) consist of the terminal block \(p\) in \(\pi\). Then \(W(\pi,A)=p(qp)q=\rho\rho\), and the first and last membership conditions hold. \(\square\)

**Consequence.** Uniform-root averaging cannot force a return displacement exceeding \(n/2\) for every successful legal coloring, and successful roots of doubled holonomy words can occupy only a \(2^{-n}\) fraction of the fiber even when NORI1 holds. Root selection, rather than a positive-density antipodal-return claim, is essential.
