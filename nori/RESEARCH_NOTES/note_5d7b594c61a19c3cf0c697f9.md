## Research note

Let \((G,K,t,\rho,m)\) be either canonical Coxeter-coset system of Article XIV, with \(f:G/K\to\mathbb F_2\) reversal-odd. Choose a random such \(f\) by assigning independent unbiased bits to representatives of the free \(\rho\)-orbits of \(G/K\), and complementary bits to their partners.

**Proposition 1 (first-moment obstruction).** For each complete flag \(g\),
\[
\mathbb E\,\operatorname{sw}(f(g),f(gt),\ldots,f(gt^{m-1}))=(m-1)/2.
\]
If \(D(g)\) denotes the circular Coxeter-orbit switch count (periodic in type A; antiperiodic after \(n\) steps in type B), then \(\mathbb E D(g)=n/2\). Consequently there are admissible colorings for which the mean of \(D(g)\) over flags is at least \(n/2\). In particular no universal small upper bound on the mean can establish the conjectured existence of a cycle with one change.

*Proof.* Distinct consecutive local incidences \(gt^jK\) and \(gt^{j+1}K\) belong to distinct \(\rho\)-orbits. In type A they are distinct ordered triples, with neither obtained from the other by reversal of its outside entries; in type B their underlying undirected cube edges have different dimensions, also after antipodal complementation. Thus their random bits are independent, and each neighboring pair disagrees with probability \(1/2\). The same property holds across the circular seam, interpreted using \(f(gt^n)=1-f(g)\) in type B. Summing the expectations proves the statements. \(\square\)

**Proposition 2 (Coxeter sign characters do not descend).** For \(n\ge5\) in type A and \(n\ge3\) in type B, no nonconstant one-dimensional character \(\chi:G\to\{\pm1\}\) is trivial on the prescribed parabolic subgroup \(K\). Therefore no multiplicative sign-character coloring on the chambers can satisfy both right-\(K\)-invariance and right-\(\rho\)-oddness in these ranges.

*Proof.* In \(S_n\), the only nontrivial real one-dimensional character is permutation sign. Since \(K_A\cong S_{n-3}\) contains the transposition \((4\,5)\) for \(n\ge5\), sign is nontrivial on \(K_A\). For \(B_n=\mathbb F_2^n\rtimes S_n\), the abelianization is \(\mathbb Z_2^2\): its characters are products of translation parity and permutation sign. The subgroup \(K_B\) contains the first-dimension sign flip, detected by translation parity, and a transposition among the remaining dimensions, detected by permutation sign when \(n\ge3\). Therefore any character trivial on \(K_B\) is trivial on both generators of the abelianization, hence is the trivial character. The trivial character is even under \(\rho\), contrary to the coloring oddness condition. \(\square\)

The paired alternating-word constructions from the preceding subsection are thus explained algebraically: they exploit Coxeter sign parity *on the chamber group* but fail the invariance under the corresponding local-incidence parabolic subgroup. A proof of the full one-switch conjecture must use inter-cycle relations imposed by that subgroup; global mean variation and chamber-character parity leave the hard issue unresolved.
