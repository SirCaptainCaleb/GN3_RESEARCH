## Research note

For n>=4 let b(a,h,k) be ANY reversal-odd binary coloring of ordered distinct vertex triples, b(k,h,a)=1-b(a,h,k). Let H be Hamilton vertex orders modulo reversal (|H|=n!/2). Define sw(P) as the number of changes between consecutive triple colors in P. Let Q be the reversal orbits of ordered distinct quadruples (a,h,k,d), and let delta(Q)=[b(a,h,k) != b(h,k,d)], well defined under reversal. Put T_b=sum_Q delta(Q). Each quadruple-reversal orbit occurs in (n-3)! Hamilton-reversal orbits as a consecutive window: n-3 possible positions and (n-4)! permutations of the remaining vertices. Consequently sum_{P in H} sw(P) = (n-3)! T_b. Since every Hamilton order with more than one switch contributes at least 2, the number of Hamilton orders with at most one switch is >= (n! - (n-3)! T_b)/2. Thus T_b < n(n-1)(n-2) guarantees the ordinary boundary 3-tournament goal for the full unrestricted reversal-odd coloring class.

Structural comparison: for legal antipodally odd cube-edge coloring of Q_n, each projective two-edge turn extends to (n-2)! Petrie cycles. With N_c=sum_[v]s_v(n-s_v), one has sum_C (2h(C)+1)=(n-2)! N_c, and at least max(0,(3*2^(n-2)*(n-1)!-(n-2)!N_c)/2) grade-zero cycles. Both formulas follow from the same intrinsic regular-local-turn-incidence counting lemma; no family of graphs is hardcoded into a new conjecture. This only gives sufficient density conditions; it is NOT a proposed common conjecture and cannot resolve high-disagreement unrestricted instances without higher incidence or topology.
