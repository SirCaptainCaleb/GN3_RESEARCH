## Research note

For odd ell>=5 and m>=1, let tilde G=C_(2ell) Cartesian Q_m and G=C_ell Cartesian Q_m, with deck tau(i,u)=(i+ell,u). The covering graph is bipartite, tau swaps its bipartition, and G has degree and vertex connectivity m+2 (Cartesian product connectivity formula). Give a ring edge from layer i to i+1 color i mod2, and any Q_m fiber edge in layer i color i mod2. Deck shift by odd ell complements every color. Deck displacement is ell; every shortest deck path uses ell consecutive ring edges with no fiber steps. The colors alternate even at the cyclic antipodal seam. Hence each minimum port cocircuit Y has size ell+1 and intersects the admissible color cycle X in ell+1 elements, so P4 fails. At each base star the lifted colors split into m+1 versus one, giving polar rank 2floor(m/2); summing over ell*2^m stars yields unbounded quadratic rank. The cycle space Z(G), modulo the span of its Cartesian squares, is one-dimensional, generated by a ring cycle: attaching square faces to C_ell Cartesian Q_m gives a complex homotopy equivalent to a circle. Covering monodromy evaluates one on the ring class and zero on every square. Thus its monodromy kernel is generated by squares, and the corresponding line-graph turn kernel is generated by star triangles plus projected squares, matching the type of homology presentation proved for folded cubes in Article XI. Consequently bipartiteness of the covering graph, high connectivity, large local quadratic rank, and square-generated monodromy kernel cannot together imply P4. This is not a hypercube counterexample: its long ring coordinate is not a commuting involution. The missing property must constrain involutive independent resource directions.
