## Research note

For a uniform rooted full geodesic of any binary cube-edge coloring, let X count its color-one edges and let a_i(S) be the Walsh coefficient of direction i. If k_s=1/(s+1) for even s and 1/(s+2) for odd s, then Var(X)=sum_{S nonempty}[(1-k_s)sum_{i not in S}a_i(S)^2+k_s(sum_{i not in S}a_i(S))^2]. Thus Var(X)>=(2/3)sum_i p_i(1-p_i), where p_i is direction-i color-one density. For antipodally odd colorings Var(X)>=n/6, sharp in dimensions divisible by four. Equality forces each edge color to read one other bit, with equal positive and negative readers per input. All equality colorings satisfy NORI1 by the functional-cycle theorem. Proof: expand each f_i at the vertex reached before its permutation step; after root averaging the two-sign covariance is k_s, obtained by counting how many S steps lie between two specified steps. Applying Parseval gives the bound and equality classification. Verified by rational arithmetic against every rooted geodesic in random Q3-Q5 colorings. Details/code: nori_geodesic_variance_audit.py.

## Sharp n/6 color-count variance in all even dimensions n>=4

Previously the bound Var(X)>=n/6 was shown sharp for n divisible by four. It is attained for EVERY even n>=4.

Set c_1(x)=x_0 and c_2(x)=1+x_0. Put J={0,3,...,n-1}, whose size n-2 is even, and define c_i(x)=x_1+alpha_i for i in J, with half of the alpha_i zero and half one. Every rule is a signed unary reader, independent of its own bit and antipodally odd.

In the existing Walsh variance formula all nonzero Fourier coefficients belong to singleton supports {0} and {1}. At support {0} the coefficients of c_1 and c_2 cancel. At support {1} those of the directions in J cancel. Thus every cross-coherence square vanishes, while the singleton variance factor is 1-k_1=2/3. Parseval gives
 Var(X)=(1/4)(2/3) n=n/6.
This includes n=6,10,14, and all other even dimensions at least four. The existing equality classification remains valid.

For odd n>=3, the signed-star family c_0=x_1 and c_i=x_0+alpha_i (i>=1), with (n-1)/2 positive and (n-1)/2 negative reader signs, gives Var(X)=n/6+1/12. Hence the leading n/6 bound is asymptotically sharp across both parities.

Independent check: /mnt/data/nori_unrestricted_fourier_variance_verifier.py compared the existing spectral identity with brute-force root/order variance in 146 legal colorings in Q2--Q7. Separate full enumeration verified equality in dimensions 4,6,8. Manuscript: /mnt/data/nori_unrestricted_defect_tensor_and_spectral_audit.md. This is a sharpness refinement of the prior archive theorem, not a rediscovery.
