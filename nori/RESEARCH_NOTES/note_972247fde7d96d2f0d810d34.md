## Research note

**Theorem (two-transitive parallel classes in matroids with small circuits).** Let \(M\) be a finite loopless matroid whose circuits have cardinality at most three. Assume \(M\) has at least three parallel classes, and its automorphism group acts two-transitively on the set of parallel classes. Then one of the following holds:
(1) \(M\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}U_{1,q}\), with \(d\ge3\) and \(q\ge1\);
(2) \(M\) is obtained by replacing each element of \(U_{2,d}\), \(d\ge3\), by a parallel class of the same size \(q\ge1\).
Conversely, both kinds have two-transitive action on their parallel classes. More generally, without the group assumption, every such matroid is a direct sum of rank-one parallel classes and parallel extensions of simple rank-two uniform matroids.

**Proof.** Simplify \(M\), retaining one representative from each parallel class, to obtain a simple matroid \(N\). All circuits of \(N\) have size three. Suppose two distinct rank-two flats \(L_1,L_2\) containing triangles meet in an element \(x\). Since two rank-two flats sharing two points are equal, we can choose triangles \(\{x,a,b\}\subset L_1\) and \(\{x,c,d\}\subset L_2\), with \(a,b,c,d\) distinct. Circuit elimination at \(x\) produces a circuit contained in \(\{a,b,c,d\}\). This circuit cannot have size three: any triple from this four-element set contains two points of one line and one of the other, forcing the latter point into the first line by closure, contrary to the distinctness of \(L_1,L_2\). Therefore the eliminated circuit has size four, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence all rank-two flats supporting triangles are disjoint. All circuits of \(N\) lie within these flats, and elements outside them lie in no circuit. Circuit connectivity now gives
\[
N\cong\left(\bigoplus_j U_{2,d_j}\right)\oplus U_{1,1}^{\oplus s},
\]
with every \(d_j\ge3\). Restoring the parallel classes of \(M\) yields the general decomposition.

The relation 'two parallel classes belong to the same connected matroid component' is preserved by automorphisms. If some rank-two component exists and any other component exists, a pair from the rank-two component and a pair from different components belong to different automorphism orbits, violating two-transitivity. Thus the existence of any rank-two component forces it to be the sole component. All its parallel classes have equal cardinality by transitivity. If no rank-two component exists, each connected component is one parallel class, and transitivity again forces all sizes equal. Conversely, arbitrary permutations of the \(d\) parallel components of \(\bigoplus_iU_{1,q}\), and arbitrary permutations of the \(d\) equal-sized parallel classes of the rank-two extension, induce full symmetric groups on the class sets. \(\square\)

**Consequence for Article XIII.** Two-transitivity is a severe structural restriction: within the small-circuit class it leaves only Hamming-type products and rank-two multipartite extensions. It therefore supplies a classification, not evidence of a minimal universal one-switch hypothesis. The boundary \(U_{2,d}\) and cubical \(\bigoplus_i U_{1,2}\) constructions are included, but the one-switch conclusion for general parallel extensions of \(U_{2,d}\) remains unproved. A weaker circuit-pair condition suffices for the same decomposition: among pairs of distinct parallel classes, either every pair belongs to a three-element circuit after simplification or no pair does. Two-transitivity is unnecessary to enforce that dichotomy.
