# A balanced six-cube with no directed square at the root

For i=0,...,5, define a binary rule c_i(x) on Q6 as follows. Delete bit i from x and pack the remaining bits in ascending order into t in {0,...,31}; let c_i(x) be bit t, with bit zero least significant, of the hexadecimal word H_i, where
\[
(H_0,\ldots,H_5)=(\texttt{7CFD40C1},\texttt{D5155754},
\texttt{08A8EAEF},\texttt{6E58E589},
\texttt{D1271B74},\texttt{8A9B26AE}).
\]

**Verified finite obstruction.** For all 64 vertices x, the sum of the six incident edge-color bits equals three, and c_i(x xor 63)=1-c_i(x) for each direction i. Thus the coloring is perfectly balanced and antipodally odd. For each of the 15 square faces containing 000000, the cyclic four-edge color word has precisely two switches. There is no alternating red-blue square, equivalently no directed four-cycle of the canonical Eulerian orientation, through the root 000000.

Counting all square faces in Q6 gives 62 alternating squares and 14 monochromatic squares; the difference 48 agrees with the dimension-uniform square-turn identity. Exhausting the 720 full-geodesic direction orders rooted at zero finds three all-red and three all-blue geodesics, so this is not a NORI1 counterexample. It rules out the universal assertion that at every vertex of an antipodally odd balanced cube, a directed-square flip can be performed locally. The first possible even dimension is six: every one of the 48 balanced legal Q4 colorings has two directed squares at every vertex.

Finite verification recipe: calculate c_i(x) by bit extraction; test balance and oddness at every x; and for each pair i<j calculate cyclic square colors (c_i(0),c_j(e_i),c_i(e_j),c_j(0)) and compare each neighboring cyclic pair.
