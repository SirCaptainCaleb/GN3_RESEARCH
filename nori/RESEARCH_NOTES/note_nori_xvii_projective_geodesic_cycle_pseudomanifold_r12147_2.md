# Projective geodesic-cycle pseudomanifold

Session session_nori_r12147_2. This is a dimension-uniform topological carrier for unrestricted NORI1, not its proof.

For n>=3, let V=F2^n/<1> denote the 2^(n-1) folded-cube vertices. An antipodal full geodesic P=(x;pi) has n projective vertices [x+prefix_k(pi)] for 0<=k<n; these form an induced n-cycle using every direction once. Cyclic re-rooting, antipodal translation, and reversal preserve this vertex set. Define G_n as the simplicial complex having one (n-1)-simplex for each such projective cycle and all their faces.

**Theorem (pseudomanifold).** Every codimension-one face of G_n lies in precisely two facets, and the facet adjacency graph is connected. Hence H_(n-1)(G_n;F2)=F2, generated by the sum z_n of all facets.

Proof: Deleting one cycle vertex leaves an induced path with n-2 distinct direction labels; the two remaining labels i,j can be reinstated only as ij or ji, giving the two containing facets. Facets containing a fixed folded vertex [x] are connected by adjacent direction swaps rooted at x. Every adjacent pair of folded vertices belongs to some full cycle, and the folded cube graph is connected; therefore the full facet adjacency graph is connected. Over F2 the ridge equations force all top-cycle coefficients to be equal.

**Theorem (vertex links).** Each vertex link in G_n is the quotient of the type-A Coxeter sphere sd(boundary Delta^(n-1)) by the complement involution S -> [n]\S on nonempty proper subsets. It is a PL triangulation of RP^(n-2). Links of all positive-dimensional faces are spheres, so for n>=4 only the vertices are pseudomanifold singularities.

Proof: A cycle containing projective [x] is rooted at x. Its remaining vertices are projective classes of the nonempty proper prefix subsets of a direction order; their faces are the resulting proper subset chains. Identifying each subset with its complement gives the antipodal quotient of the standard Coxeter sphere. The involution acts freely and without simplex self-identification. This is the usual projective triangulation; links of nonempty simplices in it are spheres.

**Theorem (uniform f-vector and Euler characteristic).** With Stirling numbers S(n,k) of the second kind:
f_0=2^(n-1);
f_k=2^(n-2) k! S(n,k+1) for 1<=k<=n-1;
chi(G_n)=2^(n-2).
Proof: The vertex link has (k+1)! S(n,k+1)/2 faces of size k; count incidences of vertices with k-dimensional faces. The Stirling alternating identity follows from exponential generating function sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k (e^z-1)^(k+1)/(k+1)=log(e^z)=z.

Independent F2 boundary-matrix checks: n3 f=(4,6,4), Betti=(1,0,1); n4 f=(8,28,48,24), Betti=(1,0,4,1); n5 f=(16,120,400,480,192), Betti=(1,0,11,5,1). Calculations are not used as proof. The standard Coxeter-quotient projective triangulation is documented in Sage topology's simplicial complex examples, https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/topology/sage/topology/simplicial_complex_examples.html .

## Simple connectivity and lost covering class (session session_nori_r12225_3)

**Theorem.** For every \(n\ge3\), the projective geodesic-cycle pseudomanifold \(\mathcal G_n\) is simply connected. Thus \(H^1(\mathcal G_n;\mathbb F_2)=0\), and the connected antipodal graph cover \(Q_n^{(1)}\to Q_n^{(1)}/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\) cannot extend to a double cover of \(\mathcal G_n\) with that restriction.

*Proof.* Put \(F_n=Q_n^{(1)}/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\) inside the one-skeleton of \(\mathcal G_n\). Each edge of \(\mathcal G_n\) belongs to a facet, a simplex spanned by the vertices of a complete direction-simple (Petrie) cycle in \(F_n\). Within its containing simplex, the edge is path-homotopic relative to endpoints to an arc of that Petrie cycle. Thus \(\pi_1(F_n)\to\pi_1(\mathcal G_n)\) is surjective.

Every Petrie cycle bounds a disk in its corresponding simplex. Every two-direction square in \(F_n\) is also nullhomotopic. To see this, choose either corner \([x]\) of such a square in directions \(i,j\), complete the routes \((i,j)\) and \((j,i)\) using the same ordering of the remaining \(n-2\) directions, and consider their Petrie cycles. Both are nullhomotopic, and they share the suffix from \([x+e_i+e_j]\) back to \([x]\). Cancellation of this common suffix identifies their difference with the square.

Represent any closed walk in \(F_n\) by a word in the commuting involutions \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) of \(V=\mathbb F_2^n/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\). The filled squares allow adjacent letters to commute; immediate reversals cancel \(e_ie_i\). Hence the loop is homotopic to \(e_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots e_n^{\varepsilon_n}\) for bits \(\varepsilon_i\). Closure forces \((\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n)\in\langle\mathbf1\rangle\), so either all bits vanish or all equal one. The former loop is trivial; the latter is a Petrie cycle and contracts inside its facet. All loops in \(\mathcal G_n\) therefore contract. The cohomology and covering statements follow. \(\square\)

**Relation to prior results.** Article XIV, “Dimension-simple Petrie presentation of the full projective cycle space,” previously proved the two-Petrie square-exchange identity and the unfiltered one-cycle generation theorem. The present argument upgrades those relations to nullhomotopies in the new simplicial pseudomanifold.

**Scope and forcing requirement.** This is an uncolored structural result, not NORI1 closure. The nonzero top-dimensional mod-two fundamental cycle and projective-space vertex links of \(\mathcal G_n\) coexist with vanishing \(H^1\). Filling essential folded-cube Petrie loops destroys their odd covering monodromy. A topological forcing strategy on this carrier needs color-sensitive relative data, a twisted carrier with different attachments, or a separate integral exchange theorem linking its classes to a monochromatic direction-simple path.
