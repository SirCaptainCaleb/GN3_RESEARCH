## Research note

# Full color symmetry and square generation still allow constant one-switch length

**Theorem.** For each \(n\ge5\) there exists a connected simple graph \(G_n\) with \(\widehat\delta(G_n,\lambda)=n-1\), a fixed-point-free vertex involutive automorphism \(\tau\), and a binary edge labeling \(\chi\) with \(\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e)\), such that
\[
\boxed{L_1(G_n,\lambda,\chi)=3.}
\]
The entire binary cycle space is generated by four-cycles. Moreover the full symmetric group \(S_n\) acts on the graph by automorphisms commuting with \(\tau\) and inducing its natural action on the \(n\) ordinary colors. Therefore all ordinary color classes are isomorphic and have equal cardinalities. Every color class is a disjoint union of \(n-1\) double-stars whose two centers have \(n-2\) leaves apiece. The graphs necessarily exhibit four-cycles with precisely three ordinary colors.

**Construction.** Take vertices \((u,v,s)\), with \(u\ne v\in[n]\) and \(s\in\mathbb F_2\). For distinct \(a,b,c\), add edges \((a,b,s)(b,c,1-s)\) of ordinary color \(c\) and binary label \(s\). Add the matching edges \((u,v,0)(u,v,1)\), assigning each matching edge ordinary color \(v\). Define \(\tau(u,v,s)=(v,u,s)\). Label each matching edge zero if \(u<v\) and one otherwise; this complements labels under \(\tau\). The old edges also satisfy binary reversal. Each vertex sees old incident colors \([n]\setminus\{u\}\), and the matching edge has already-present color \(v\), so the minimum color degree remains \(n-1\).

For every \(\sigma\in S_n\), the map \((u,v,s)\mapsto(\sigma u,\sigma v,s)\) preserves the underlying graph and sends each ordinary color \(a\) to \(\sigma(a)\), on old and new edges alike. It commutes with \(\tau\). The classes for each ordinary color \(a\) are isomorphic: for every \(u\ne a\), the two vertices \((u,a,0)\) and \((u,a,1)\) are joined by one matching edge of color \(a\); each has \(n-2\) additional old neighbors by edges of color \(a\), and these are disjoint leaves. Thus each class contains \(n-1\) double-stars, each on \(2n-2\) vertices and \(2n-3\) edges. The cycle-space assertion depends only on the underlying graph, and follows from the proof in note \`note_nori_xiii_joint_square_conditions_fail\`: that graph is the matched two-sheet lift of the ordered-pair graph whose cycle space is generated by triangles and squares.

**One-switch proof.** Call an old edge *outgoing* at a vertex \((u,v,s)\) if its ordinary color occurs there once; equivalently its binary label is \(s\). Every other old edge incident with that vertex has ordinary color \(v\), as does the matching edge. Thus a rainbow path can meet a matching edge only through outgoing old edges on both sides.

In the graph of old edges, a rainbow path of length three with at most one switch has binary word \(001,011,100,\) or \(110\) (constant runs of length three repeat ordinary colors). In each case a sheet check shows that its terminal old edges are of repeated color at both endpoints: if the repeated binary bit is \(0\), the two equal bits meet in sheet \(0\); if it is \(1\), they meet in sheet \(1\), and the other vertices follow by alternating sheets. Consequently such a three-old-edge path cannot be extended at either end by a matching edge without repeating its ordinary color.

Next consider a hypothetical four-edge rainbow path with at most one switch. If it uses no matching edge, it contradicts the old-graph bound \(L_1=3\). If it has just one matching edge at an endpoint, it contradicts the preceding three-old-edge observation. If the matching edge is second, the following two old edges must begin with an outgoing edge of binary label \(a\) and then have binary labels \(a,1-a\); otherwise their ordinary colors repeat. The old edge immediately before the matching edge must be outgoing at its endpoint in the opposite sheet and therefore has binary label \(1-a\). The four-edge binary word is
\[
1-a,\ \varepsilon,\ a,\ 1-a,
\]
which has at least two switches regardless of the matching-edge label \(\varepsilon\). The case where the matching edge is third follows by reversing the path.

Two matching edges cannot be adjacent, since they form a matching. They cannot be separated by one old edge, because that old edge has the same ordinary color as a matching edge at one of its endpoints. They cannot be separated by two old edges: each of the two old edges would have to be outgoing at its endpoint adjacent to the matching edge, so both would enter their common middle vertex, carrying its repeated ordinary color and violating the rainbow condition. Thus no four-edge one-switch rainbow path exists.

The original old-edge subgraph has a three-edge one-switch rainbow path: for pairwise distinct indices \(t,u,v,w,z\), use
\[
(u,v,1),\ (t,u,0),\ (u,w,1),\ (w,z,0),
\]
whose ordinary colors are \(v,w,z\) and binary word \(001\). Hence \(L_1(G_n,\lambda,\chi)=3\).

**Failure of the other local law.** Every directed triangle \((a,b)\to(b,c)\to(c,a)\to(a,b)\) in the ordered-pair graph lifts to a four-cycle comprising three old edges and the matching edge at \((a,b)\). Its ordinary-color word consists of \(c,a,b,b\), which uses precisely three colors. Thus these examples satisfy square generation and complete ordinary-color permutation symmetry, while violating the no-three-color-four-cycle condition.

**Research consequence.** Square generation together with color transitivity is insufficient for a linear one-switch rainbow-path bound. Conversely, the companion note constructs a graph satisfying square generation and the no-three-color-square law but with one distinguished color and \(L_1\le7\). Both results narrow, without resolving, the search for a minimal sufficient assumption.

**Uniformity in binary labels on matching edges.** The one-switch upper bound of three is independent of how the new matching edges are labeled, provided the old-edge binary labels remain as defined: in the four-edge argument the matching label \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, and the remaining cases use only ordinary-color repetitions. Thus every assignment of binary labels to the added matching edges, in particular every assignment compatible with \(\tau\)-reversal, still has \(L_1=3\).
