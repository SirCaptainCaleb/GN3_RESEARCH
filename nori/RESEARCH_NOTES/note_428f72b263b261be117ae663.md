## Research note

For any odd antipodal coloring of Q_(2m), fix root x and let U_a act on rank-k subsets by appending unused color-a original edges. Let J map subset S to its complement and L_a be transpose of U_a. Original-edge oddness gives J U_a J = L_(1-a). With v_a(x)=U_a^m [empty], the rooted color-zero full-geodesic count is the nonnegative complementary middle pairing N_0(x)=<v_0(x),Jv_1(x)>; similarly N_1=N_0 at each root. This is an integral positive path count; reduction modulo a fixed modulus cannot serve as a universal nonvanishing obstruction. To show this in all large dimensions, use the legal balanced Q4 coloring c1=x3,c2=1+x3,c3=x1,c4=1+x1, which has a red full path from x=e1 using directions 4,1,3,2. Its k-fold Cartesian power is balanced and legal on Q_(4k). Every rooted monochromatic count factors as (4k)!/(4!)^k times the product of the k individual rooted counts. For any fixed positive modulus M, choose k sufficiently large that M divides this shuffle multinomial, using Legendre valuations. Then ALL rootwise red and blue full-path counts vanish modulo M, although a red full path exists from root with x=e1 in each block. The standard signed (4,4) shuffle sum is 6 as well, versus 70 unsigned shuffles. A path-sensitive chain degree must distinguish shuffle-equivalent witnesses rather than use the raw finite-modulus count. This is a limitation of scalar counting, not a proof of NORI1.
