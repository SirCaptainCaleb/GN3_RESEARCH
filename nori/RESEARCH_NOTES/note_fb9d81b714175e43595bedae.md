## Research note

Let Q_4 have bit positions 0,1,2,3. Give its unoriented direction-i edges the binary colors
\[
 f_0=x_2,\qquad f_2=x_0,\qquad
 f_1=x_3\lor(\neg x_0\land\neg x_2),\qquad
 f_3=(\neg x_1)\land\neg(x_0\land x_2).
\]
Each function ignores its own direction bit. Deleting directions 0 and 1 leaves the acyclic directed influence graph on {2,3}, with possible arc 2->3 and no arc 3->2. Let g_0,g_1 denote the induced Q_3 edge colorings on the two facets x_0=0,1. Direct full-geodesic checking gives
\[
|A_0(f)|=9,\quad |P(f)|=4,\qquad
|A_0(g_0)|=|A_0(g_1)|=7.
\]
Hence the tempting multiplicative facet inequality
\[
|A_0(f)|\ge \tfrac34\bigl(|A_0(g_0)|+|A_0(g_1)|\bigr)
\]
is false even when f has a two-direction feedback vertex set: 9<21/2. It cannot be used to derive the desired nine-sixteenths favorable-root bound from the one-feedback-vertex three-quarters bound.

Reproducible certificate: encode the starting root by the integer r=sum_i 2^i x_i. The f-rising favorable roots are precisely
\[
r\in\{0,1,2,3,4,6,10,11,14\}.
\]
The paired roots are precisely {1,4,11,14}. For each root and each permutation of the relevant directions, compute the traversed word by evaluating f_i(r xor the set of previously used direction bits); accept the root iff at least one resulting word has no 1-to-0 transition. There are 24 permutations per full Q_4 root and six per Q_3 facet root. This also checks the two facet counts of seven.

The example saturates the proposed nine-sixteenths favorable density while invalidating the simplest inductive multiplicative argument. Any two-feedback bound must retain information beyond the cardinalities of its two facet favorable-root sets.
