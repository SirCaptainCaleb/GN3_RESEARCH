## Research note

**Proposition.** For every \(n\ge4\) there is a legal antipodally odd undirected cube-edge coloring with a six-flag terminal braid residue whose every complete dimension-simple geodesic has at least two switches but whose extreme-switch bivectors have arithmetic mean zero.

Fix root \(0^n\). For each \(\sigma\in S_3\), take order \(\pi=(\sigma(1),\sigma(2),\sigma(3),4,\ldots,n)\). In the three-dimensional subcube with all remaining bits zero, color an edge according to its increasing Hamming-weight layer: layers \(0\to1\), \(1\to2\), and \(2\to3\) receive respectively colors \(0,1,0\). Color the common tail edges in dimensions \(4,\ldots,n\) zero. Thus every one of these six complete orders has word \((0,1,0,0,\ldots,0)\), with precisely two switches.

The prescriptions are compatible with antipodal oddness for \(n\ge4\): the prescribed first-three-dimension edges lie in the face where dimension four is zero, and their antipodal edges lie in the opposite face; prescribed tail edges have different dimensions and hence cannot be antipodal to a different prescribed edge. Choose complementary colors on antipodal edge pairs to extend to all of \(Q_n\).

Each flag has extreme-switch bivector \(Z_1(\pi)\wedge Z_2(\pi)\). Averaging over the six permutations of the first three symbols gives zero by the transposition symmetry of the exterior product (equivalently, the large-first-block zero-average identity in the universal convex-carrier theorem with \(s=0\)). Thus zero lies in the convex hull of all six labels. This shows the boundary triple-color rank-two convex-filling theorem has a real dependence on triple reversal oddness. For the cube, a rank-two filling method must also use information across different roots or opposite braid residues.
