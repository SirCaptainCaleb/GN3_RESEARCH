## Research note

**Proposition.** Let \(P=\prod_{i=1}^r\Delta^{q_i-1}\), where \(q_i\ge2\) and \(\dim P\ge3\). Let \(J\) be an involution of the poset of nonempty proper faces which either preserves inclusion globally or reverses inclusion globally. Suppose its induced simplicial involution on the barycentric subdivision of \(\partial P\) is free. In the inclusion-preserving case, \(P\) is a cube and \(J\) is central inversion. In the inclusion-reversing case, \(P\) is a simplex. Thus mixed products of simplices admit no such opposition.

*Proof.* An inclusion-preserving automorphism induces an automorphism of \(G(P)=K_{q_1}\square\cdots\square K_{q_r}\). Uniqueness of the Cartesian prime factorization of connected graphs implies that it permutes equal-size factors and permutes vertices within factors. If the involution swaps two factors, fix a vertex in the first factor and its image in the second, leaving every other factor unrestricted. The resulting nonempty proper product face is invariant under the involution, contradicting freeness. Hence it preserves each factor. Its action on the \(q_i\) vertices of the \(i\)-th factor is an involution. If \(q_i\ge3\), at least one of its orbits (of cardinality one or two) is a nonempty proper subset, hence determines an invariant proper face after taking the product with all the other factors. Therefore every \(q_i=2\). The involution must swap both vertices in every segment factor, since an identity factor also supplies an invariant proper face. This is central inversion, which is free on proper cube faces.

An inclusion-reversing face involution interchanges vertices and facets, so \(\prod_iq_i=\sum_iq_i\). For two factors the difference is \((q_1-1)(q_2-1)-1\), which vanishes only for the two-dimensional square. For \(r\ge3\), \(\prod_iq_i>\sum_iq_i\): at all \(q_i=2\) this says \(2^r>2r\), and increasing a factor cannot decrease the difference. Thus \(r=1\). Complementing nonempty proper vertex subsets supplies the free inclusion-reversing involution for a simplex. \(\square\)

**Consequence and limitation.** Simple polytopes with every two-face triangular or quadrilateral are combinatorial products of simplices. Consequently the proposed local restriction to triangular/quadrilateral rank-two residues, combined with a globally inclusion-preserving or inclusion-reversing face opposition, collapses to the two original extreme families in dimension at least three. It is therefore insufficient for a broad intrinsic unification. This statement leaves open opposition maps on the barycentric face complex that do not preserve or reverse the face order globally, and alternative carrier complexes.
