## Research note

**Theorem (cyclic variation under rooted cube chamber moves).** For any NORI1 edge coloring and complete geodesic P=(x;pi_1,...,pi_n), let w_1,...,w_n be its edge bits and define an antiperiodic extension w_(i+n)=1-w_i. Let d_i=w_i xor w_(i+1), for i modulo n, including the seam comparison between w_n and 1-w_1. Put D(P)=sum_(i=1)^n d_i, an odd integer.

(a) The endpoint root slide R:(x;pi_1,...,pi_n)->(x xor e_(pi_1);pi_2,...,pi_n,pi_1) preserves D. Its binary word is (w_2,...,w_n,1-w_1). This is the known cyclic rerooting identity from Article IX, invoked here.

(b) Swapping two adjacent directions in pi with the root held fixed changes at most the two corresponding consecutive edge bits; all other traversed cube edges are unchanged. Hence it changes at most three cyclic comparison bits d. Since D is odd for every chamber, its increment belongs to {-2,0,+2}. Thus D is a parity-constrained, locally bounded variation function on the all-root chamber graph.

(c) This function can possess arbitrarily high flat local minima. For even n>=6, consider the antipodally odd coloring c_i(x)=sum_(j!=i) x_j (mod 2). For every chamber rooted at 0, its bit word is 0101... with D=n-1. An adjacent-direction swap at root 0 leaves D=n-1; a root slide also leaves D=n-1 by (a). Yet some chambers rooted at a vertex of Hamming weight n/2 have monochromatic words and D=1 (see note_52fd24abbc3549954285b0a5). Therefore all chambers rooted at 0 are stationary flat local minima despite having nonminimal D. Any argument that repeatedly accepts only strictly improving single root-slide or adjacent-swap steps cannot establish NORI1.

**Boundary comparison (cyclic triple variations).** For any cyclic permutation of original symbols in a reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament, the cyclic triple bits t_i define comparisons d_i=t_i xor t_(i+1) and even D. A cyclic rotation preserves D. Swapping adjacent symbols changes at most four consecutive triple bits, and so at most five comparisons; the parity of D remains even and its increment is in {-4,-2,0,2,4}. The path target is not simply D<=2: the sharp criterion is D<=2 or D=4 with three consecutive comparison bits 1 (see note_a31f3ced5651f1da607b547c). The latter exception is indispensable. On a four-symbol set define b(a,b,c) as the inversion parity of the permutation (a,b,c,d), where d is the omitted fourth symbol. Reversing a,b,c flips parity, so b is reversal odd; cycling an ordered quadruple performs a four-cycle and flips parity, so every cyclic symbol ordering has circular triple-bit word 0101 up to complement, giving D=4. Nevertheless every four-symbol Hamilton path has just two triple colors and therefore at most one switch. Thus the tempting stronger boundary condition D<=2 is false already for four vertices.

**Research consequence.** Both problems admit a cyclic variation function preserved by cyclic rotation and altered locally, in parity-constrained steps, by adjacent direction/symbol exchanges. The root-free boundary cyclic order and the rooted cube cyclic orbit have different monodromy, but the same *two-move obstruction strategy* is available: analyze stationary high-defect plateaus under a simultaneous topological selection theorem rather than insisting on a one-step descent. No proof of a universal selection theorem follows here.
