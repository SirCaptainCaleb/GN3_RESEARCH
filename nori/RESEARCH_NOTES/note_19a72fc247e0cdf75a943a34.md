## Research note

Let f be an arbitrary undirected binary edge coloring of Q_n. Define its directed influence digraph D(f) on the n direction indices by j->i iff flipping bit j can change the color of a direction-i edge. Its directed feedback-vertex number tau(D) is the minimum size of a vertex set H for which D-H is acyclic. Let P(f)=A_0(f) intersect A_1(f), where A_0 and A_1 denote starting roots with respective full dimension-simple geodesic color words 0*1* and 1*0*.

**Global multiplicity conjecture.** For every n>=1 and every such f,
\[
\boxed{|P(f)|\ge 2^{\,n-\tau(D(f))}.}\tag{FM}
\]
This is a dimension-uniform assertion over every coloring f. It would prove unrestricted NORI1: every loopless influence digraph has tau<=n-1 (retain one vertex), and the bound would give |P(f)|>=2.

**Proved endpoints.** If tau=0, the acyclic influence theorem gives P(f)=Q_n. If tau=1, the full hub-fiber paired-root selector theorem gives |P(f)|>=2^(n-1). These statements are proved in the existing paired-root manuscript.

**Sharp infinite family.** For every 0<=k<=floor(n/2), choose k disjoint reciprocal-reader pairs, with f_a=x_b and f_b=x_a in each pair, and give every remaining direction a constant color independent of all other bits. Each pair contributes precisely two paired roots on its two-bit block (01 and 10), while each constant direction contributes two roots. Thus |P(f)|=2^(n-k). The influence graph consists of k disjoint directed two-cycles and isolates, so tau(D)=k. This establishes sharpness of (FM) throughout k<=floor(n/2), including the proved k=0,1 cases.

**Inductive obstacle.** Simply bounding the paired-root counts in opposite facets is insufficient. The Q4 coloring documented in note_fb9d81b714175e43595bedae has tau(D)=2 and |P(f)|=4, although each of the two x_0-facets separately has six paired roots; the inequality |P(f)| >= (|P(g_0)|+|P(g_1)|)/2 fails, since 4<6. Any proof of (FM) must exploit path-seam compatibility or a root-matching mechanism rather than counts in the two facets alone. A purported sharp Q6 nonlinear hub-table example was erroneous: independent complete-path re-enumeration gives 32 paired roots, not 16. Sharpness for tau=2 already follows from two disjoint reciprocal-reader pairs. In the strongly connected ordinary-facet example (SC1) of Article XV, n=2m, |P|=2^m and the m vertex-disjoint reciprocal-reader two-cycles force tau>=m; deleting the m even directions makes the remaining graph acyclic, hence tau=m and equality also holds there.

**Finite tests, not a proof.** All 4096 edge colorings of Q3 were enumerated by edge truth tables, with complete direction-order geodesic reachability and a direct minimum-feedback-vertex search, and no violation occurred. Additional independent random-coloring tests and nonlinear two-hub searches in dimensions 4 through 7 found no violation. In one Q6 two-hub family, a search located the table pair f0=0xCCCCCCCC and f1=0x7F7F1010 with nonhub rules f2=x0,f3=1+x0,f4=x1,f5=1+x1; it has tau(D)=2 and 32 paired roots, with 48 rising-favorable roots; two independent full-path computations agree. The former count of 16 was mistaken.

**Closure challenge.** Prove (FM) for tau=2 or derive a dimension-uniform multiplicity-preserving reduction that decreases tau by one. The six-cube fixed-nonhub-fiber obstruction shows that a successful induction cannot insist that every assignment of the nonfeedback bits extend to a paired root by changing only feedback bits. A matching or globally routed exchange may be required. Failure of the conjecture is also meaningful and should be sought before treating it as an invariant.
