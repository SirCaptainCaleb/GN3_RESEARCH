# Common Freudenthal carrier with precise intersection extraction

Let c be any two-coloring of undirected edges of Q_n. Fix antipodal roots u and bar u, and identify each vertex x with its support S_u(x)={k:x_k ≠ u_k}⊆[n]. The order complex Δ(B_n) of the Boolean lattice B_n=2^[n] has a vertex for every S⊆[n] and a simplex for every nested family of supports. Its standard geometric realization, sending S to its 0-1 incidence vector in [0,1]^n, is the ordinary Freudenthal (staircase) triangulation of the n-cube.

For i∈{0,1}, let K_i(u) be the subcomplex generated by all simplices of vertex supports occurring along all-i geodesics STARTING at u. Each such geodesic traverses pairwise distinct coordinates and yields a nested ascending support chain. Define K_j(bar u) using all-j geodesics starting at the opposite corner. Relative to u its support sequences are nested DESCENDING chains. Consequently BOTH are honest simplicial subcomplexes of the SAME fixed Δ(B_n), despite being rooted at opposite corners.

**precise extraction theorem.** For any i,j∈{0,1},

|K_i(u)| ∩ |K_j(bar u)| ≠ ∅
  ⇔ V(K_i(u)) ∩ V(K_j(bar u)) ≠ ∅
  ⇔ R(u;i) ∩ R(bar u;j) ≠ ∅
  ⇔ some geodesic from u to bar u has color word i^a j^(n−a) for some 0≤a≤n.

Proof. The first equivalence holds because the intersection of two subcomplexes of a common geometric simplicial complex is the realization of their common simplicial subcomplex: a point in the relative interiors of two simplices requires the same minimal ambient face, and hence their intersection contains an realized shared vertex. The second equivalence holds by construction: a vertex occurs in K_i(u) precisely when reachable from u by an all-i geodesic, and likewise at bar u. The third is the antipodal-geodesic metric concatenation lemma: for any x, d(u,x)+d(x,bar u)=n, so any such pair of geodesics joins into a full geodesic. Conversely split a two-block full geodesic at its unique color boundary; empty blocks are permitted. For i=j, this is precisely an all-i antipodal geodesic.

**Equivariance.** Under the cube complement involution τ(x)=bar x, the support S_u(τx)=[n]\\S_u(x), an order-reversing automorphism of B_n and therefore a simplicial involution of Δ(B_n). If c is antipodally odd, τ(K_i(u))=K_(1−i)(bar u), with the identical statement for opposite-root complexes. This symmetry is precise at the simplicial level.

**Why this is a useful correction.** Convex hulls of the VERTEX SETS of R(u;i) and R(bar u;j) may intersect at points witnessing no shared cube vertex, even in a legal affine Q4 example (linked note). Their convex hulls include fictitious chords joining mutually incomparable supports. In contrast, the valid flag complexes include ONLY chains of supports jointly witnessed by individual monochromatic geodesics and sit as subcomplexes of ONE Freudenthal triangulation. An intersection theorem proved for these subcomplexes, rather than their arbitrary convex hulls, has fully rigorous geodesic extraction with no further path-history compatibility condition.

**precise remaining gap.** This construction proves the extraction implication, NOT the required intersection. The ambient Freudenthal cube is contractible; antipodal oddness exchanges starting root AND color (K_i(u)↔K_(1−i)(bar u)), whereas the desired NORI1 intersection is K_i(u)∩K_i(bar u). It remains open to find a nontrivial equivariant/index or covering argument over the root-indexed family of these subcomplexes that forces a same-color intersection for some antipodal root pair. This note establishes a correctly witnessed carrier and local extraction, not a universal topological forcing theorem or a boundary-tournament transfer.

**Central-diagonal rigidity (sharpening for the same color).** Write D for the entire Euclidean main diagonal joining u to bar u, with midpoint m=(1/2,...,1/2) in u-relative coordinates. For either i,

|K_i(u)| ∩ |K_i(bar u)| ≠ ∅
⇔ D ⊆ |K_i(u)| ∩ |K_i(bar u)|
⇔ m ∈ |K_i(u)| ∩ |K_i(bar u)|.

Indeed a shared cube vertex splices to an all-i full geodesic P. The entire chain of vertices of P is a simplex belonging to BOTH generated prefix complexes (read P backward at the opposite root). Its face {u,bar u} is an edge of the common Freudenthal triangulation, so the WHOLE main diagonal D belongs to both complexes. Conversely midpoint membership gives a nonempty intersection and hence splicing. Thus for a same-color root pair, intersection is either empty or contains the antipodally fixed center and its entire main diagonal. This structural feature may permit targeting a single distinguished point by a topological forcing argument; no such forcing theorem has been established.
