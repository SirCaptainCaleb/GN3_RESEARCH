# Binomial-square obstruction to every fixed order of global turn moments

**Theorem (unbounded scalar moment-order obstruction).** For every integer \(m\ge2\), put \(n=2m+1\) and \(M=2^{n-2}(n-1)!\). There is a legal maximally balanced coloring of \(Q_n\) whose probabilities at the possible cyclic turn energies \(T=2+4j\), \(0\le j\le m\), are
\[
p_j=\frac{\binom mj^2}{\binom{2m}m}.
\]
There is also a *formal*, nonnegative histogram on the same \(M\) support positions,
\[
\boxed{q_j=\frac{\binom mj^2+(-1)^{j+1}\binom mj}{\binom{2m}m},
\quad0\le j\le m,}
\]
such that \(q_0=0\), \(\sum_jq_j=1\), every \(Mq_j\) is a nonnegative integer, and
\[
\boxed{\sum_{j=0}^m p_j(2+4j)^k
=\sum_{j=0}^m q_j(2+4j)^k
\quad\text{for all integers }0\le k<m.}
\]
In particular, for each fixed integer \(K\), choosing \(m>K\) produces a legal coloring whose first \(K\) global turn-energy moments, balanced-star status, energy congruence, and support count are compatible with a no-monochromatic-support histogram.

**Proof.** Take the legal coloring \(c_0=x_1\), \(c_i=x_0\) for \(i\in A\) and \(c_i=1+x_0\) for \(i\in B\), where \(A\sqcup B=\{1,\ldots,2m\}\), \(|A|=|B|=m\), \(1\in B\). Its red degree is \(r(x)=m+x_1\). As proved by a direct run-composition count in Theorem 8 of the home Subsection, the turn-energy probabilities are \(p_j=\binom mj^2/\binom{2m}m\).

Let \(C=\binom{2m}m\). Since \(\binom mj\ge1\), the numerator \(\binom mj(\binom mj+(-1)^{j+1})\) of \(q_j\) is nonnegative. The \(j=0\) numerator is zero. The alternating binomial sum gives
\[
\sum_{j=0}^m(q_j-p_j)=
-\frac1C\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^j\binom mj=0.
\]
More generally, the \(m\)-th finite difference annihilates every polynomial \(P\) of degree below \(m\):
\[
\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^j\binom mj P(j)=0.
\]
Apply this to \(P(j)=(2+4j)^k\) for \(k<m\), proving all claimed moment identities.

Finally \(M/C=2^{2m-1}(m!)^2\) is an integer, so \(Mq_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). The coloring realizes \(p\); the histogram \(q\) is a formally admissible no-witness distribution, with no claim of realizability by a legal cube coloring. \(\square\)

The first separating scalar moment is order \(m\), for which
\[
\mathbb E_q T^m-\mathbb E_p T^m
=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}4^m m!}{\binom{2m}m}\ne0.
\]
Thus this obstruction holds simultaneously for the maximal possible proper initial segment of scalar moments.

**Examples.** At \(n=7\), \(m=3\), the realizable and formal profiles are
\[
p=(1,9,9,1)/20,\qquad q=(0,12,6,2)/20,
\]
which agree through the second moment. At \(n=9\), \(m=4\),
\[
p=(1,16,36,16,1)/70,\qquad q=(0,20,30,20,0)/70,
\]
which agree through the **third** moment.

This proves a limitation of scalar moment-only forcing methods. Subtype-resolved multi-turn correlations, projective curvature, and compatibility with the original edge colors are additional data and remain potential forcing mechanisms.

# Seven-cube obstruction to forcing from two global turn moments

Let \(Q_7\) have directions \(0,\ldots,6\), with bit vector \(x\). Define a legal antipodally odd edge coloring by
\[
c_0(x)=x_1,\qquad c_1(x)=1+x_0,\qquad
c_i(x)=x_0\quad(i\in\{2,3,5\}),\qquad
c_i(x)=1+x_0\quad(i\in\{4,6\}),
\]
with arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_2\). Each \(c_i\) ignores \(x_i\), and each is complemented when all vertex bits are complemented. Every vertex has red degree \(3+x_1\in\{3,4\}\), hence the coloring is maximally balanced and its mean cyclic turn energy is \(8\).

**Proposition (complete energy histogram).** Among the \(23040\) coherent projective supports, the multiplicities of cyclic turn energies are
\[
\boxed{(a_2,a_6,a_{10},a_{14})=(1152,10368,10368,1152).}
\]
**Proof without computer enumeration.** Root a cyclic direction order at the distinguished dimension \(0\). The other six dimension labels form a uniformly random binary word recording whether the direction lies in \(\{2,3,5\}\) or \(\{1,4,6\}\); precisely three labels lie in each class. Thus each of the \(\binom63=20\) balanced binary words occurs equally often. The color of direction \(0\), relative to the other colors, supplies an independent uniform bit \(b\). Let \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_6)\) be the class word, and \(m(w)=\sum_{h=1}^5\mathbf1_{\{w_h\ne w_{h+1}\}}\). The number of projective color changes is
\[
X=m(w)+\mathbf1_{\{b\ne w_1\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{w_6\ne1-b\}}.
\]
Directly classifying the twenty binary words gives counts \(2,18,18,2\) among the \(40\) equally weighted pairs \((w,b)\), for \(X=1,3,5,7\), respectively. Multiplying by \(23040/40=576\) proves the stated histogram.

It follows that \(\mathbb ET=8\) and \(\operatorname{Var}T=36/5\). Consider now the **formal** distribution, with the same support cardinality and admissible turn-energy congruence,
\[
\boxed{(a'_2,a'_6,a'_{10},a'_{14})=(0,13824,6912,2304).}
\]
It has \(\mathbb E'T=8\) and \(\operatorname{Var}'T=36/5\), although it contains no two-turn support. Indeed its probabilities at \(6,10,14\) are \(3/5,3/10,1/10\); its second moment is \(8^2+36/5\). Consequently the mean, variance, support count, energy congruence, and balanced-star status of this legal coloring do not algebraically distinguish its energy histogram from a no-witness histogram.

This is an obstruction to arguments that depend **only on the values of the first two global turn moments and the preceding universal constraints**. The formal no-witness distribution is not claimed to arise from a legal coloring. Further cube-color compatibility, such as higher-order incidence constraints, remains available and may resolve the conjecture.

For a dimension-seven maximally balanced coloring, the mean is always \(8\). In the notation \(p_k=\Pr(T=2+4k)\), the mean identity gives
\[
p_1=\tfrac12+p_3-2p_0.
\]
Thus any hypothetical counterexample must have \(p_1\ge1/2\); a uniform inequality \(\Pr(T=6)<1/2\) for maximally balanced legal colorings would rule out that entire extremal regime. This proposed inequality remains unproved and is logically equivalent, in this regime, to \(p_3<2p_0\).

**Independent certificate.** The script
https://github.com/SirCaptainCaleb/GN3_RESEARCH/blob/87497835d6985c6abc99429ee18aedb5b3fe3ae1/scripts/nori_turn_higher_moment_verify.py
independently checks legality, balanced stars, the complete support histogram, both moments, and the formal comparison. The same script verifies the nested-prefix factorial formula for all occurring turn subsets of size at most four for \(n=3,4,5,6\), samples incompatible subsets, and checks the four Fourier characteristic polynomials in \(n=7\).

## A lower-density balanced example

A second legal affine seven-cube coloring has an even smaller two-turn density:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
c_0=x_1,&c_1=1+x_0,&c_2=x_0,\\
c_3=x_6,&c_4=1+x_6,&c_5=x_1,\quad c_6=1+x_1.
\end{array}
\tag{A}
\]
Every color is independent of its own dimension and odd under antipodal complementation. Moreover \(r(x)=3+x_1\), so all vertex stars are maximally balanced. Direct counting gives
\[
\boxed{(a_2,a_6,a_{10},a_{14})=(480,11232,10656,672),}
\]
or probabilities
\[
\boxed{(p_2,p_6,p_{10},p_{14})=(1/48,39/80,37/80,7/240).}
\]
In particular \(\Pr(T=6)=39/80\) approaches the necessary counterexample threshold \(1/2\), and \(\Pr(T=2)=1/48\).

This finite computation reduces to \(5040\cdot8=40320\) combinations of direction order and starting bits \(x_0,x_1,x_6\). More explicitly, write \(c_i(x)=b_i+x_{d(i)}\) and let \(\pi\) be a direction order. The color of direction \(i\) along the rooted path is
\[
b_i+x_{d(i)}+\mathbf1_{\{d(i)\text{ precedes }i\text{ in }\pi\}}.
\]
For the coloring (A), \(d=(1,0,0,6,6,1,1)\) and \(b=(0,1,0,0,1,0,1)\). Counting the antiperiodic cyclic transition energies for these \(40320\) choices gives the multiset counts \((840,19656,18648,1176)\); multiplication by \(4/7\), accounting for the \(16\) irrelevant starting bits and \(28\) rooted representatives per support, yields the stated support counts. Independently, direct enumeration of the \(322560\) canonical-rooted direction orders gives the same histogram.

For this coloring \(\mathbb ET=8\) and \(\operatorname{Var}T=28/5\). The formal no-witness histogram \((a'_2,a'_6,a'_{10},a'_{14})=(0,12672,9216,1152)\) matches both moments. This further limits any proof using only those moments.

Among its \(144\) alternating square-orbits available for degree-preserving antipodally symmetric recoloring, none individually increases the six-turn support count above \(11232\). This is a finite local maximality statement for this coloring and this specific family of recolorings, not a global extremal bound.
