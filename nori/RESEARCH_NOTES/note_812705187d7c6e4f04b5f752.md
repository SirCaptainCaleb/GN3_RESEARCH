## Research note

OPEN VERTEX-DELETION CONJECTURE. Let G be a finite connected graph, z a root, and v≠z a vertex for which H=G−v is connected. For all w∈V(H), write D_G(z,w) and D_H(z,w) for the respective maximum lengths of vertex-simple z-to-w paths. Then
\[
\boxed{
D_G(z,v)+\sum_{w\in V(H)\setminus\{z\}}\bigl(D_G(z,w)-D_H(z,w)\bigr)
\ge 2\deg_G(v)-\mathbf1_{zv\in E(G)}.
}\tag{VD}
\]
All the terms in the summation are nonnegative.

THEOREM (rigorous implication). If (VD) holds for every rooted connected graph and every noncut v≠z, then the unrestricted rooted-detour inequality
\[
\sum_w D_G(z,w)\ge 2|E(G)|-\deg_G(z)
\]
holds for every connected graph. Proof: Every nontrivial connected graph has at least two noncut vertices, so we may choose one v≠z and set H=G−v. Induct on |V(G)|; the one-vertex graph is immediate. The induction hypothesis gives S_H(z)≥2|E(H)|−deg_H(z). The left side of (VD) equals S_G(z)−S_H(z), whereas its right side equals [2|E(G)|−deg_G(z)]−[2|E(H)|−deg_H(z)], because |E(G)|−|E(H)|=deg_G(v) and deg_G(z)−deg_H(z)=1_{zv}. Addition yields the desired rooted bound. The weighted-detour and Hamming-product conclusions then follow by the manuscript's established reductions.

PROVED SHARP CASES. If v is a leaf with neighbor u, it cannot be internal to a path with endpoints in H, so D_G(z,w)=D_H(z,w) for all w∈H. Also D_G(z,v)=D_H(z,u)+1. If z=u this is one and the (VD) right side is one; otherwise D_H(z,u)≥1 and the (VD) right side is two. Thus (VD) holds for leaf deletion. For G=K_n, v≠z, each D_G(z,w)=n−1 for w≠z and each D_H(z,w)=n−2 for w∈H−{z}. The left side of (VD) is (n−1)+(n−2)=2n−3 and the right side is 2(n−1)−1=2n−3; equality shows sharpness in the maximally dense case as well.

VERIFICATION / LIMITATION. A direct exhaustive finite check over all connected unlabeled graphs in the standard NetworkX graph atlas of orders at most seven and every eligible pair (z,v) found no violation (35,732 eligible rooted-deletion triples). Additional random trials on graphs of orders eight through ten found no violation; this is only diagnostic evidence. A dimension-independent proof of (VD), especially for deletion of high-degree noncut vertices in two-connected graphs, is unavailable. The conjecture is more stringent than the rooted inequality and could be false even if the rooted inequality is true; a failure of (VD) would not refute the latter.

RESEARCH OPPORTUNITY. For v of degree k, choose a longest z-to-u path in H among neighbors u∈N(v). Adjoining v gives D_G(z,v)≥D_H(z,u)+1. Neighbors outside that chosen path admit root paths enlarged through v; neighbors on the chosen path are candidates for path rotations producing the remaining marginal increase. A rigorous aggregate fan-rotation inequality could close (VD). Graphical circuit dependencies are directly visible in these rotations, providing a promising common basis-exchange mechanism.
