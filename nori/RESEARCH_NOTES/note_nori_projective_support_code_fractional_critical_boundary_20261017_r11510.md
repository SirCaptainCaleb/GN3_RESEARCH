## Research note

Let n>=3, Q_n={0,1}^n, and identify antipodal unoriented-edge orbits. A full antipodal geodesic P determines the n-element set S(P) of edge orbits traversed by P. Its union with its antipodal image is an isometric undirected 2n-cycle C(P).

THEOREM 1 (support-code separation and square-exchange connectivity). If C(P) and C(Q) are different antipodally symmetric isometric 2n-cycles, then |S(P) intersect S(Q)|<=n-2. Thus the incidence vectors of distinct supports form a binary constant-weight-n code of minimum Hamming distance four. This bound is attained by swapping two successive, different directions in a rooted geodesic. The graph of supports joined when their symmetric difference has size four is connected.

PROOF. If two cycles shared n-1 orbits, their undirected cycles would share 2n-2 edges. Removing the omitted antipodal edge pair from the first cycle leaves two disjoint paths of length n-1. Their four endpoints are a,b,bar a,bar b with d_H(a,b)=1, while d_H(a,bar a)=n and d_H(a,bar b)=n-1. To reconnect these two paths into a 2n-cycle using a new antipodal edge pair, the only possible pairing of endpoints by cube edges is (a,b) together with (bar a,bar b): both alternative pairings require edges of Hamming length n or n-1. Thus the cycles coincide, contradiction. If all n orbits were shared, the cycles coincide directly. Exchanging successive directions i,j replaces the two square-route edge orbits by the other two; for n>=3 the new orbits differ from the old ones and the other n-2 direction orbits remain unchanged. This attains distance four. Adjacent transpositions connect all direction orders at any root. To move the root across direction i, first move i to the beginning of the order by transpositions and cyclically rotate the rooted full path; this changes the root by e_i while retaining its support. Repeating reaches every root, proving connectedness of the support-code distance-four graph.

THEOREM 2 (sharp fractional obstruction to first-moment orbit repair). Fix an all-red full antipodal geodesic P under a legal coloring c, and let O be an antipodal edge orbit of P at a strictly internal position. Let c^O flip the colors of both edges in O. Let C_P be the family of all legal completions of the n edge-orbit colors prescribed by P, followed by the flip at O. For EVERY probability distribution mu on rooted full geodesics independent of the unprescribed orbit bits,
sup_(d in C_P) E_(Q~mu)[number of blue edges of Q in d] >= 1.
Equality is attainable, so the minimax value is one.

PROOF. Give every orbit bit outside S(P) an independent unbiased binary value, retaining the n prescribed bits of P, and then flip O. If S(Q) differs from S(P), Theorem 1 says Q uses at least two unprescribed edge orbits, each contributing an expected 1/2 blue edge in the resulting coloring. Thus E B_d(Q)>=1. If S(Q)=S(P), Q lies on the doubled antipodal cycle C(P) (in either direction). Prior to the flip, the cyclic color word around that 2n-cycle is 0^n1^n. Flipping an internal orbit creates a cyclic word whose longest zero-color run has length at most n-2; consequently every n-edge halfcycle uses a blue edge. Thus B_d(Q)>=1 for every completion also in this case. Average first over the random completion and then over Q~mu; some deterministic completion has mean blue count at least one. Conversely the point mass on P has blue count precisely one for every completion after flipping O, showing the minimax value is one.

When O is a first or last edge of P, a cyclic root rotation gives a red full geodesic after flipping O, so the internal-edge hypothesis is essential.

INTERPRETATION. A color-uniform repair certificate of the form E_mu B_d(Q)<1 would force a red full geodesic by integrality. Theorem 2 excludes every certificate of that form using a path distribution chosen solely from the original red witness P and the flipped orbit O, for all n>=3. A successful uniform critical-orbit theorem must impose additional shared-edge information or use nonlinear combinations of path events. The theorem addresses the leading critical-surgery programme in Article XVI; it is a method boundary, not a counterexample to NORI1.

THEOREM 3 (two-square random repair and nonlinear packing). Under the hypotheses of Theorem 2, let P=(x,(i_1,...,i_n)) be red and suppose O is its orbit at position k with 1<k<n. Swap the kth direction with its predecessor to obtain P_-, and swap it with its successor to obtain P_+. Each of P_-,P_+ avoids O and retains precisely n-2 red edges of P. Its other two edge orbits lie outside S(P), so under uniform independent completion of the unprescribed colors each has probability 1/4 of being all red after the flip at O.

For n>=4, the FOUR new orbit bits of these two paths are distinct. Thus the two repair events are independent and their union has probability 1-(3/4)^2=7/16. For n=3 the two new paths share one orbit, visited on opposite antipodal representatives, so their repair events are disjoint and their union has probability 1/2. These are probabilities for the two selected repair paths; additional red geodesics may occur.

PROOF. An adjacent swap changes the two routes across one (i,j)-square, replacing the two old edge orbits by the two transverse edge orbits. With n>=3 these transverse orbits are different from old ones, and the changed paths do not use the pivot orbit. Around an internal pivot, one square is before the pivot and the other after it. The two pairs of transverse orbits have distinct directions except for their common pivot direction. Their two new pivot-direction edges differ in their endpoints by toggling the predecessor and successor directions. They belong to the same antipodal edge orbit precisely when the complement of the pivot direction consists of those two directions, which occurs when n=3. In that case they have opposite colors. In every n>=4 the four new orbit bits are independent. Each event requires its two newly sampled edges to be red and hence has probability 1/4. The claimed union probabilities follow.

THEOREM 4 (multiplicative path-network bound). Let P_1,...,P_t be all-red full geodesics of one legal coloring, each containing the same red orbit O strictly internally. Let R be the union of their antipodal edge-orbit supports. Assume n>=4 and that the four orbit bits introduced by the two adjacent pivot swaps of each P_s all lie outside R, with these 4t bits pairwise distinct. Condition on the edge-orbit colors in R that make all P_s red, choose every other orbit bit independently and uniformly, and then flip O. The probability that NONE of these 2t swap paths is all red equals (9/16)^t. Consequently among completions of the prescribed colors, the fraction that can fail NORI1 after the orbit flip is at most (9/16)^t, giving an information saving of t log_2(16/9) bits.

PROOF. Each witness contributes two independent events, each requiring two distinct free orbit bits to be red. For that witness the chance of neither event is (3/4)^2=9/16. The 4t sets of orbit variables are disjoint by hypothesis, so these failure conditions are independent across witnesses. Any bad completed coloring must satisfy them all. This proves the stated bound.

BOUNDARY. The packing theorem applies only to square modifications whose new orbit bits are free under the conditioning and disjoint across witnesses. The existence of linearly many such independent square neighborhoods in every critical coloring has not been shown. The theorem supplies a rigorous entropy-compression mechanism from simultaneous witness compatibility, without claiming an unrestricted NORI1 proof.

For n=3 it enumerates all eight assignments to the three new bits and finds four successes. The universal proof above does not depend on computation.
EXPLICIT Q5 CERTIFICATE. Let e be the direction-0 edge {0,1}. Take a red full geodesic rooted at 2 with order (1,0,2,3,4), and another rooted at 8 with order (3,0,4,1,2). They have compatible prescribed red edge-orbit colors, with only e shared. Their four pivot-adjacent square exchanges introduce eight pairwise distinct new orbit variables, none contained in either original support. Thus the four repair paths include an all-red geodesic in 175/256 uniform completions of those eight bits, after flipping e. An independent verifier enumerates all 256 assignments. The fresh-square packing hypothesis is therefore attainable already on Q5.LOCAL PACKING UPPER BOUND. Fix an unoriented pivot edge e of direction i. A swap of that pivot with an adjacent step in any full geodesic replaces the old route through the unique (i,j)-square containing e, for some j distinct from i. Among its two fresh orbits is the direction-i edge parallel to e across that square. For n>=4 these parallel-i edge orbits are distinct for distinct j. Every four-orbit two-swap witness used in the independent packing theorem therefore consumes two different square directions j,k. If t such witnesses have mutually disjoint sets of fresh orbits, all 2t square directions must be distinct, giving t<=floor((n-1)/2). The displayed Q5 certificate attains this ceiling with t=2. Thus local pivot-adjacent square packing by itself saves at most O(n) independent orbit bits, whereas the full coloring has n*2^(n-2) free orbit assignments; entropy compression on that scale requires longer-range witness transport or a method using overlapping constraints.
