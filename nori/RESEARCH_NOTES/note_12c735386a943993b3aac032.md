## Research note

**Theorem.** For every m>=4 there is a connected canonical oriented-basis graph of the matroid M=U_{2,m} direct-sum U_{1,2}, equipped with its intrinsic entered-element (parallel-class) coloring lambda and an involutive matroid automorphism tau preserving lambda, such that the minimum ordinary color degree is m but a reversal-odd binary edge coloring chi has L_1(G,lambda,chi)<=12 ceil(log_2 m)-5. In particular the ratio L_1 / minimum color degree tends to zero.

**Graph and resource coloring.** Let B_m have vertices ordered pairs (a,b) of distinct elements of [m], with edges (a,b)--(b,c) for distinct a,b,c, directed from (a,b) to (b,c). Set G=B_m Cartesian-product K_2, with sheet s in {0,1}. In sheet zero give this edge ordinary color c; in sheet one give it ordinary color a. Give every intersheet edge the same fresh resource *. Set tau((a,b),s)=((b,a),1-s). Then tau is a fixed-point-free involutive graph automorphism, preserves lambda, and fixes no edge. Each vertex has m-2 distinct singleton resource colors, one repeated color, and the intersheet resource *, hence its color degree is m.

**Matroid realization.** The rank-three matroid U_{2,m} direct-sum U_{1,2} has bases {a,b,y_s} where y_0,y_1 are parallel. Its oriented bases admit the representatives (a,b,y_0) in sheet zero and (b,a,y_1) in sheet one. Two oriented bases are adjacent when their induced orientations on their common two-element face are opposite. The overlap edges and intersheet edges above are precisely these adjacencies: deleting a from (a,b,y_0) induces (b,y_0), while deleting c from (b,c,y_0) induces -(b,y_0); in the second sheet the signs reverse; intersheet exchange reverses the ordered a,b face. Orient each adjacency from positive to negative induced face, using the ground ordering with y_0,y_1 last, and color by the parallel class of the element inserted at the head. This yields color c in sheet zero, color a in sheet one, and * across sheets. The involution tau is induced by the matroid automorphism interchanging y_0 and y_1.

**Binary coloring.** Put t=ceil(log_2 m). For a!=b, let j(a,b) be a bit position in which their t-bit encodings differ, choosing the highest such position, and let h(a,b)=2j(a,b)+bit_j(a). Then h takes values in {0,...,2t-1} and is a proper vertex coloring of B_m. Indeed, for an arc (a,b)->(b,c), if j(a,b)=j(b,c), the selected bits of a,b differ and thus the second components of h(a,b),h(b,c) differ. Define chi_0 on an undirected B_m edge oriented u->v to be 1 if h(v)>h(u), and 0 otherwise. Define chi_1(e)=1-chi_0(tau_B(e)), where tau_B(a,b)=(b,a), which reverses the arc orientation. Thus chi_1 on u->v is 1 if h(tau_B v)>h(tau_B u), so it likewise measures increase in the proper height h composed with tau_B. Label the intersheet edges arbitrarily in complementary pairs under tau. The full labeling satisfies chi(tau e)=1-chi(e).

**Path bound.** In sheet zero all incoming edges at (a,b) share ordinary color b, while outgoing edges have distinct ordinary colors. A rainbow undirected path therefore has a traversal-orientation word consisting of backward edges followed by forward edges. In sheet one all outgoing edges at (a,b) share ordinary color a, while incoming edges have distinct colors; its rainbow paths have orientation word forward-then-backward. Thus in either sheet a rainbow path has at most one traversal-orientation change. Along a segment of uniform traversal orientation and uniform binary bit, its height h (sheet zero) or h composed with tau_B (sheet one) changes strictly monotonically, hence the segment has at most 2t-1 edges. A path with at most one binary switch is divided by the two possible boundaries into at most three such segments, giving length at most 3(2t-1) within either sheet. Any rainbow path uses the intersheet resource * at most once. Its two portions therefore have at most 3(2t-1) edges each, and the full path has at most 6(2t-1)+1 = 12t-5 edges. QED.

The theorem rules out oriented matroid basis-graph geometry and correctly prescribed triangle/square exchange holonomy, even with the canonical inserted-element coloring and lambda-preserving involution, as sufficient for a universal linear one-switch bound. The two original homogeneous families remain unaffected.
