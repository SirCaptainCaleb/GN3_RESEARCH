## Research note

On Q4 let phi(x)=1 for integer-encoded vertices x in {4,8,13,14}, and phi(x)=0 otherwise. Color every direction-i edge by c_i(x)=phi(x)+phi(x xor 2^i) in F2. The coloring is undirected and flat on every square, since it is a gradient. One checks phi(x)+phi(bar x)=x_0+x_1+x_2+x_3, so c_i(bar x)=1+c_i(x). Therefore it is legal antipodally odd and has just one unmarked autonomous flat block C={0,1,2,3}. Its full-profile square-exchange space V is the even-parity subspace, and the affine parity restriction on a full geodesic from root x is sum_i w_i=sum_i x_i. Root x=3 has even vertex parity, so the all-zero and all-one profiles both satisfy its complete affine restriction. Nevertheless neither monochromatic antipodal geodesic exists from x=3: phi(3)=phi(12)=0, but vertex 12 has zero incident red edges because all four neighbors 4,8,13,14 have phi=1. Similarly vertex 3 has zero incident blue edges because all four of its neighbors 1,2,7,11 have phi=0. Thus there is no mono antipodal path at this root. Other roots do have mono full geodesics, so this is not a NORI1 counterexample. It shows that satisfying all rooted affine parity constraints, even for a globally flat coloring, need not imply monochromatic reachability. Rootwise affine-coset saturation is a false shortcut to grand closure.
