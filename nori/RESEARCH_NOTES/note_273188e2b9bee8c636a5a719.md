## Research note

Let \(c:E(Q_n)\to\mathbb F_2\) be antipodally odd, \(n\ge2\). For \(a=0,1\), let \(P_a(c)\) be the poset of ordered distinct pairs \((u,v)\) joined by a monochromatic color-\(a\) cube geodesic, ordered by support-disjoint monochromatic endpoint extension. Let \(X_n\) be the order complex of all directed nonempty cube intervals, identified with the positive-dimensional cell poset of the torus \(D^n\), where each factor \(D\) has two vertices and two parallel edges. Regard \(K_a=\Delta P_a(c)\) as a simplicial subcomplex of \(X_n\), and put \(K=K_0\cup K_1\).

On the ordered-pair vertices define
\[
J(u,v)=(v,u),\qquad A(u,v)=(\bar u,\bar v),\qquad R=AJ.
\]
The maps are commuting involutions on \(X_n\). The first preserves each color complex; antipodal oddness gives \(A(K_a)=K_{1-a}\); hence \(R\) exchanges the two color complexes and preserves \(K\). In the torus realization, \(J(t)=-t\), \(A(t)=t+\mathbf1\), and \(R(t)=\mathbf1-t\).

**Theorem (fixed-center and Lefschetz characterization).** The fixed point set of \(R\) on \(K\) is a finite discrete set consisting of the barycenters of the rank-\(n\) cells \(C(u,\bar u)\) for which a monochromatic full geodesic from \(u\) to \(\bar u\) exists. Consequently
\[
\boxed{L(R|_K)
=\#\{u\in Q_n:\text{a monochromatic full geodesic starts at }u\}
=2\,g(c),}
\]
where \(g(c)\) is the number of antipodal unordered vertex pairs supporting such a geodesic. In particular,
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{NORI1}\text{ holds for }c\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L(R|_K)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Fix}(R|_K)\ne\varnothing.}
\]

*Proof.* The involution acts on endpoint pairs as \(R(u,v)=(\bar v,\bar u)\). Therefore a vertex of \(X_n\) is fixed under \(R\) precisely when \(v=\bar u\), namely when its Hamming rank is \(n\). Since each simplex of \(X_n\) is a chain of intervals of strictly increasing rank and \(R\) preserves rank, an \(R\)-invariant simplex must fix every vertex individually. Only isolated rank-\(n\) vertices can therefore contain fixed points.

A rank-\(n\) vertex belongs to \(K\) precisely when there is a monochromatic full geodesic joining its ordered antipodal endpoints. For such endpoints, existence of a red geodesic implies existence of a blue geodesic with the same orientation: antipodally translate the red path and reverse it. Thus every fixed vertex of \(K\) lies in \(K_0\cap K_1\), and conversely. No positive-dimensional simplex of \(K\) is fixed setwise. The simplicial Lefschetz number is the alternating sum of the traces on chain groups; only the fixed rank-\(n\) zero-simplices contribute, each with coefficient one. Endpoint reversal pairs the contributing vertices at \(u\) and \(\bar u\), so their number is twice \(g(c)\). \(\square\)

This reformulates NORI1 as a fixed-point theorem for two intrinsic monochromatic directed-interval complexes inside the punctured torus. The ambient involution \(R\) has \(2^n\) fixed centers; the conjecture asserts that at least one survives the color-sensitive geodesic filtration. The Lefschetz formula provides a possible homological target, but no lower bound on \(L(R|_K)\) has been established from the coloring constraints alone. In particular, the color-independent ambient Lefschetz number cannot be transferred to \(K\) without a chain-level argument.
