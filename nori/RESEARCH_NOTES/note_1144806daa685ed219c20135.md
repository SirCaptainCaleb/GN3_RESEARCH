## Research note

## Translation-rigid nonlinear legal colorings in every even dimension

Fix an even integer n >= 4 and index the dimensions cyclically 0,...,n-1. For each i, list the n-1 other indices in their cyclic order j_0,...,j_(n-2), with subscripts on j read modulo n-1. Define
\[
c_i(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{n-2}x_{j_t}x_{j_{t+1}}\pmod2. \tag{R1}
\]

**Theorem (maximal rulewise translation rigidity, despite monochromatic completion).** Formula (R1) gives a legal antipodally odd undirected-edge coloring of Q_n. Each rule has Boolean algebraic degree two, depends essentially on *every* bit other than its own, and its full translation stabilizer is precisely
\[
\operatorname{Stab}(c_i)=\{0,e_i\}. \tag{R2}
\]
Thus no nonzero translation preserves two distinct rules, no rule is affine under any invertible affine change of vertex variables, and no invertible affine change of vertex variables and reindexing of outputs puts the rules in the nonlinear triangular profile-bijection form c_i=y_i+h_i(y_1,...,y_(i-1)): that form requires its first rule to be affine. Nor can the family have a linear private-reader direction as required in the paired-gate three-phase construction.

Nevertheless there is an explicit color-zero full antipodal geodesic. Start at the alternating vertex x with x_(2t)=1 and x_(2t+1)=0; traverse all even-indexed dimensions first, in any order, followed by all odd-indexed dimensions, in any order. At an even-indexed step i, the 1-bits among the other dimensions form a subset of the even indices distinct from i. These vertices form an independent set in the (n-1)-cycle obtained by deleting i and adjoining its two cyclic neighbors, since the only newly introduced adjacency joins two odd indices. Hence c_i=0. At an odd-indexed step i, all even-indexed dimensions have already been flipped, and the 1-bits form a subset of the odd indices distinct from i; the newly joined cyclic neighbors are even, so c_i=0 again. Every dimension is used once, proving the claim. Antipodal translation gives a color-one path.

*Rigidity proof.* On m=n-1 odd vertices, put q(z)=sum_(t mod m) z_t z_(t+1). Its polar bilinear form has matrix the mod-two adjacency matrix A of the odd cycle: (Av)_t=v_(t-1)+v_(t+1). Thus Av=0 implies v_(t-1)=v_(t+1) for every t; since m is odd, v is constant. Therefore ker A = span{1_m} and rank A=m-1=n-2. A translation v preserves q at every z iff Av=0 and q(v)=0. But q(1_m)=m=1 mod 2, so only v=0 preserves q. Adding the ignored self bit gives (R2). Every derivative in a different bit is a nonconstant sum of its two neighbors. Also q(z+1_m)=q(z)+q(1_m)=q(z)+1, since A1_m=0, proving antipodal oddness. Each q contains nonzero quadratic terms and has degree 2, preserved by invertible affine changes of vertex variables.

This is an obstruction to methods requiring a nontrivial translation-invisible helper, unary readout, or affine triangularization **in the given cube**. It does not obstruct arbitrary nonlinear root/order control and is not a counterexample to NORI1.

**Independent finite checks.** For n=4,6,8,10, direct simulation verifies the alternating-root two-block path is monochromatic. For n=4 all 24 fixed direction orders have image size 8 (out of 16) under the rooted full-profile map; for n=6 all 720 orders have image size 28,32,34,36, or 40 (out of 64), with respective counts 12,324,72,108,204. These counts show that this family need not admit any fixed-order profile bijection, even while admitting many red geodesics. The structural statements hold for every even n by proof.

### Maximal-degree refinement: every cross-rule derivative can be highly nonlinear

For every even n>=6, the preceding example can be modified without changing its displayed color-zero path, so that every rule has algebraic degree n-2, every derivative in a different direction has degree n-3, and Stab(c_i) remains {0,e_i}.

For each i, let m=n-1, let q_i be the odd-cycle quadratic on its m other bits, and let z_i be the projection (delete bit i) of the vertex just before the i-step of the previously constructed monochromatic path. Choose a_i in F2^m outside {z_i,z_i+1_m}. Define
\[
h_i(z)={\bf1}_{\{a_i,a_i+1_m\}}(z),\qquad
c_i'(x)=q_i(x_{-i})+h_i(x_{-i}). \tag{R3}
\]
The set {a_i,a_i+1_m} is an affine line of dimension one, so its indicator has Boolean degree m-1=n-2. It is invariant under the all-ones translation, hence c_i' remains antipodally odd; it ignores bit i and h_i(z_i)=0, so the original monochromatic path remains valid. The highest-degree component of c_i' comes from h_i, since q_i is quadratic and n-2>=4.

For any nonzero v on the m other variables distinct from 1_m, the derivative D_v h_i is the indicator of the four-point affine plane a_i+span{1_m,v}; it has Boolean degree m-2=n-3. The derivative D_v q_i is nonconstant affine linear, as the odd-cycle polar matrix has kernel span{1_m}. Therefore D_v(c_i') has degree n-3>=3 and cannot vanish identically. For v=1_m, D_v h_i=0 and D_v q_i=1. Thus the only translation period of c_i' is its ignored direction e_i. In particular D_j c_i' has degree n-3 for every j!=i. No rule has an affine triangular presentation or an affine private-reader form after invertible affine vertex substitution.

The choice of a_i is always possible since m>=5. Direct truth-table enumeration in dimensions n=6,8,10 verified legality, the preserved red witness, degree n-2 for each rule, degree n-3 for every cross-rule derivative, and absence of nonzero translation periods outside e_i. This is a verified realizable obstruction to intrinsic methods which require any bounded-degree or translation-invisible gate readout, while leaving nonlinear order/root control open.

### Flat maximal-degree refinement by one antipodal vertex-potential perturbation

**Theorem.** For every even n>=6 there is a legal coloring with zero curvature on every square, algebraic degree n-2 in each direction rule, algebraic degree n-3 in every cross-direction Boolean derivative, translation stabilizer Stab(c_i)={0,e_i} for every i, and a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic.

**Construction.** In F2 arithmetic set
\[
F(x)=\sum_{0\le j<k<n}x_jx_k+
\prod_{j=0}^{n-2}(x_j+x_{n-1}),\qquad
c_i(x)=F(x)+F(x+e_i). \tag{RG1}
\]
Write F=Q+G. The function G is the indicator of the antipodal pair a=(1,...,1,0), bar a=(0,...,0,1), whose Hamming weights are n-1 and 1. It satisfies G(bar x)=G(x). The derivatives of Q are D_iQ=sum_(j!=i)x_j, which are antipodally odd for even n. Consequently every c_i is legal: its rule ignores x_i, complementing x complements its value, and all cubical square curvatures vanish because D_jc_i=D_ic_j=D_iD_jF.

**Degree and stabilizer proof.** On the m=n-1 variables other than i,
\[
c_i(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_j+h_i(z),\qquad
h_i(z)=\mathbf1_{\{a_{-i},\bar a_{-i}\}}(z). \tag{RG2}
\]
Here m is odd. The indicator of an antipodal pair has ANF degree m-1: when expanded as the XOR of the two point indicators, its degree-m terms cancel, while each of the m monomials of degree m-1 has coefficient one. Consequently deg(c_i)=m-1=n-2. For any nonzero v distinct from 1_m, the derivative D_v h_i is the indicator of the four-point affine plane a_{-i}+span{1_m,v}. Its ANF degree equals m-2=n-3, whereas the derivative of the affine part of (RG2) is constant. Thus D_v c_i is nonzero of degree n-3. For v=1_m, D_v h_i=0 and D_v(sum z_j)=m=1. Hence no nonzero translation of the m remaining bits preserves c_i; in the full cube its stabilizer is {0,e_i}. Each cross-direction derivative D_jc_i has degree n-3. No rule becomes affine under invertible affine vertex substitution, and no nonzero translation preserves two distinct rules.

**Monochromatic witness.** Choose any root x with n/2 ones. Traverse the n directions in an order that alternates the initial bit values (n/2 mod 2), (1+n/2 mod 2), and so forth; each type occurs n/2 times. At step t starting from zero, current vertex weight has parity n/2+t and the chosen unflipped bit has value n/2+t mod 2. Therefore D_iQ=sum_(j!=i)x_j=0 at every step. All visited vertices have Hamming weight n/2 or n/2+1 if n/2 is even, and n/2 or n/2-1 if n/2 is odd. For n>=6 these weights lie between 2 and n-2, excluding a and bar a, hence G vanishes throughout the path and D_iG=0 on every traversed edge. The geodesic is monochromatic zero; its antipodal image is monochromatic one.

**Independent checks.** Truth-table verification for n=6,8,10 confirmed legality, flat squares, the displayed geodesic, algebraic degrees n-2 and n-3, and the translation stabilizers. The preceding proof applies for all even n>=6.

This obstruction survives even complete square integrability. It defeats mechanisms requiring translation-invisible reset directions or affine-normalizable rules, without refuting NORI1. Unlike the previous rulewise perturbations, this one comes from a single antipodally even vertex potential supported on one antipodal pair.

### Universal maximal-degree law and path-preserving orbit flips

**Theorem (all legal colorings).** Let n>=4, and let c be any legal NORI1 coloring. Write m=n-1, and regard c_i as a Boolean function f_i of the m vertex bits distinct from its edge direction. Then:

(a) deg(f_i)<=m-1=n-2. If equality holds, the homogeneous degree-(m-1) component is
\[
e_{m-1}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^m\prod_{k\ne j}z_k. \tag{MD1}
\]
For every v in F2^m other than 0 and 1_m, the derivative D_v f_i has algebraic degree m-2=n-3. Moreover D_(1_m)f_i=1, so f_i has trivial translation stabilizer on its m inputs. In the full cube Stab(c_i)={0,e_i}.

(b) Flipping the colors of one antipodal pair of direction-i edges toggles whether deg(f_i)=n-2. Consequently any legal coloring can be modified on at most one antipodal edge orbit in each direction so that every direction rule has degree n-2, every cross-direction derivative has degree n-3, and every rule stabilizer is {0,e_i}.

(c) Given any rooted full antipodal geodesic P in the original coloring, the modifications in (b) may be selected so that the new coloring agrees with the original on every edge of P. Hence every color-profile word carried by any chosen geodesic can coexist with degree-maximal, translation-rigid rules.

**Proof.** Expand f=f_i in algebraic normal form:
\[
f(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}a_S\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\]
Antipodal oddness f(z+1_m)=f(z)+1 implies f is balanced. Since m>=3, the parity sum of all 2^m values of f is zero. This parity sum is the coefficient a_[m], so deg(f)<=m-1. Put b_j=a_([m]\setminus{j}). The coefficient of the degree-(m-2) monomial missing {j,k} in D_(1_m)f is b_j+b_k: terms of degree <=m-2 contribute only lower degrees. The derivative equals constant one, hence b_j=b_k for every pair j,k. Therefore all b_j coincide. If deg(f)=m-1, their common value is one, proving (MD1).

For any v, the degree-(m-2) coefficient of the same monomial in D_v f is v_j+v_k (using b_j=b_k=1). Unless v=0 or v=1_m, at least one such pair has distinct entries; therefore deg(D_v f)=m-2. For v=1_m the derivative is the constant one by legality. This proves (a), including the translation statement.

For a in F2^m, let h_a be the indicator of {a,a+1_m}. Expanding the two point indicators shows that the degree-m terms cancel and every degree-(m-1) monomial has coefficient one, regardless of a. Thus f+h_a remains antipodally odd and toggles its entire leading layer (MD1), proving (b). In the cube, h_a recolors only the pair of antipodal undirected edges in direction i represented by {a,a+1_m}; it does not affect any other direction. Finally a full geodesic uses just one orbit in each direction, while there are 2^(n-2)>=4 such orbits per direction. Choose a different orbit for every required flip. This preserves the path colors and proves (c).

**Interpretation.** Maximum Boolean degree and absence of nontrivial translation-invisible controls are highly sensitive to sparse legal edge recoloring. They can be imposed simultaneously around any prescribed geodesic without changing that geodesic's color profile. Thus these rulewise invariants alone cannot distinguish successful witnesses from general legal colorings. The flat refinement (RG1) shows that these extreme rulewise properties can also coexist with zero cubical curvature.
