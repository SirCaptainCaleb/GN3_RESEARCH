## Research note

**Theorem (root-fiber switch spectrum for a symmetric NORI1 coloring).** Let n=2m>=4 be even. Define a binary coloring of the undirected edges of Q_n by c_i(x)=sum_(j!=i) x_j (mod 2), where an edge is {x,x xor e_i}. The coloring is edge-well-defined because it ignores x_i and antipodally odd because n-1 is odd.

For a root x and a full antipodal geodesic with direction order pi=(pi_1,...,pi_n), write r=sum_j x_j (mod 2). Its kth edge color is
  b_k=r+x_(pi_k)+(k-1) (mod 2).
Thus consecutive edge colors differ iff the corresponding ROOT bits x_(pi_k), x_(pi_(k+1)) are equal. Minimizing the number of changes over all permutations is the binary alternating-arrangement problem. If x has Hamming weight w, the minimum equals
  mu_x(c)=max(0,|n-2w|-1).
Indeed at least |n-2w|-1 equal-neighbor pairs remain if one binary value has a surplus, and alternation followed by all surplus occurrences realizes the bound.

In particular, at root 0 every complete geodesic for every order has binary word 0101... and n-1 switches. A monochromatic complete geodesic exists rooted at x iff w=n/2, witnessed by alternating the directions whose root bits are zero and one. The successful roots form the middle Hamming layer of size binomial(n,n/2), but an arbitrarily prescribed root may be as unfavorable as possible. Translating the coloring by a single dimension sends all successful roots to the translated middle layer, which lies in Hamming-weight layers with parity opposite to n/2; thus a prescribed middle Hamming layer cannot serve as a universal root-selection set.

**Unification implication.** The fixed-root ordered-permutation complex has an antipodal direction-reversal symmetry but that alone cannot give a one-switch full path from an arbitrarily chosen root. Any full NORI1 result must couple distinct roots or use an equivalent root-changing operation. Boundary-tournament three-window labels are independent of cube root, so the shared mechanism must tolerate that distinction rather than imposing the root-dependence of NORI1 onto the boundary model. The cyclic one-switch-to-zero-switch root-rotation lemma is already proved in Article IX, not new here.

**Corollary (linear-radius obstruction to root selection).** Write n=2m. A rooted complete geodesic with at most one color switch exists in this example at root x if and only if |x| belongs to {m-1,m,m+1}; this follows immediately from mu_x=max(0,|n-2|x||-1). Hence every full geodesic rooted within Hamming distance m-2 of 0 has at least three binary switches, regardless of direction permutation. To find any one-switch geodesic starting from root 0 by a sequence of moves that change the root by at most one dimension at a time, one must perform at least m-1 root changes, even if arbitrarily many permutations are tried at each intervening root. A monochromatic rooted path requires at least m root changes. In particular a bounded-depth root-perturbation theorem cannot replace a global root-selection argument.
