## Research note

**Theorem (intrinsic diagram opposition).** Let \(W\) be a finite real reflection group, with fundamental chamber \(C\), simple reflections \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\), and longest element \(w_0\). The geometric antipodal involution \(J\) on its spherical Coxeter complex sends the chamber \(wC\) to \(ww_0C\). There is an involutive automorphism \(\iota\) of the Coxeter diagram such that
\[
w_0(\alpha_i)=-\alpha_{\iota(i)},\qquad
Jr_iJ=r_{\iota(i)}
\]
for the canonical chamber rank flips \(r_i\) (right multiplications by \(s_i\)).

*Proof.* The longest element sends all positive roots to negative roots, hence \(w_0C=-C\). Therefore \(-wC=w(-C)=ww_0C\). It permutes the negatives of simple roots, inducing an automorphism \(\iota\) of the Coxeter diagram; \(w_0^2=1\) makes \(\iota^2=1\). On chamber labels the calculation is
\[
Jr_iJ(w)=ww_0s_iw_0=ws_{\iota(i)},
\]
giving the assertion.

For the simplex Coxeter system of rank \(d=n-1\), the longest element reverses the ordering of \(n\) vertices and \(\iota(i)=d+1-i\), so \(JTJ=T^{-1}\) for \(T=r_1\cdots r_d\). For the cube Coxeter system of rank \(d=n\), the longest element acts as \(-I\) and \(\iota(i)=i\), so \(JTJ=T\); in this case \(J=T^d\). Thus the distinct positional reversal laws in the boundary and cube problems are intrinsic manifestations of the same longest-element opposition, rather than separate artificial axioms.

**Caution.** Every simple polytope has a spherical barycentric flag complex, but not every such complex supports a free simplicial opposition: the triangular prism obstruction is proved in the accompanying research note. Finite Coxeter complexes always have geometric opposition, while arbitrary simple polytopes need an explicit compatible involution. Even in an opposed Coxeter complex, odd coloring of arbitrary chamber vertices is insufficient for a one-switch theorem: the local projective-incidence descent is essential.
