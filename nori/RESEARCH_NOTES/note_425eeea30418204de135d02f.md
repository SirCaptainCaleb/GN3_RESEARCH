## Research note

CORRECTION: This is not a full unification. The full boundary conjecture asks for all n vertices on one tight path with at most one change in its consecutive triple colors; the rank n-1 graphic resource must be exhausted. The prior square-root bound is already proved and does not imply that Hamiltonian goal. Article IX contains the corrected target and the cubical one-switch rotation lemma.

**Intrinsic class.** Let \(G=H(k,q)=K_q^{\square k}\), \(k\ge1,q\ge2\), characterized intrinsically as a connected Cartesian product of complete graphs of the same order (equivalently, among partition-matroid basis graphs, by second-eigenspace independence of every vertex star). Write \(d=k(q-1)\). Let \(N=N_{\rm sp}(G)\) be its canonical adjacency-second-eigenspace edge-difference matroid. Its rank is \(d\), its vertex stars are bases, and every graph cycle is dependent.

On each nonbacktracking ordered turn \((u,v,w)\), choose \(b(u,v,w)\in\mathbb F_2\) satisfying
\[
b(u,v,w)+b(w,v,u)=\mathbf 1_{\{uw\in E(G)\}}.\tag{1}
\]
Require a global edge potential \(c:E(G)\to\mathbb F_2\) such that
\[
b(u,v,w)=c(uv)+c(vw)\quad\text{whenever }uw\notin E(G).\tag{2}
\]
For each nonidentity involution \(\tau\) in the center of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) that acts without fixed edges, require
\[
c(\tau e)=1-c(e).\tag{3}
\]
For \(H(k,2)\) with \(k\ge2\), the single such involution is simultaneous complementation of all coordinates. For \(q\ge3\), the automorphism-group center is trivial, so (3) is vacuous. These conditions are isomorphism-invariant and use the same syntax for every \(k,q\). At a turn within one \(K_q\) factor, the outside vertices are adjacent and (1) is an unrestricted tournament reversal rule. At a turn between factors, the outside vertices are nonadjacent and (2) is the edge-color-agreement rule.

A **witness** is a path \(P=(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell)\) whose graph edges are independent in \(N\) and for which \(b(v_{i-1},v_i,v_{i+1})=0\) at every internal turn.

**Auxiliary Hamming-root growth conjecture (weaker than the common full-strength target).** Every object above has a witness with
\[
\boxed{\ell\ge\left\lceil\max\left\{k,\frac12\sqrt{k(q-1)}\right\}\right\rceil.}\tag{U}
\]

**Verified reductions.** (A) In the cubical case \(q=2\), \(G=Q_k\), there are no triangle turns. A binary edge coloring \(c\) with \(c(\tau e)=1-c(e)\) determines (1)--(2) by \(b(u,v,w)=c(uv)+c(vw)\). A witness of \(\ell\ge k\) edges has every direction once because the spectral resource matroid is the coordinate-partition matroid of rank \(k\), so its endpoints are antipodes. The zero-turn equalities make every edge the same color. Thus (U) implies unrestricted NORI1.

(B) In the boundary case \(k=1,q=n\), \(G=K_n\), every turn is triangular and (1) permits every reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament coloring. No edge-potential constraint is imposed by (2) or (3); choose \(c\) constant. The spectral resource matroid is the graphic matroid \(M(K_n)\), so a witness is a vertex-simple tight path whose ordered triples all have color zero. The conjectured length is at least \(\frac12\sqrt{n-1}\) edges, hence \(\Omega(\sqrt n)\) vertices. Reversal oddness interchanges the two constant colors by reversing a path. Thus (U) recovers only the previously proved square-root boundary theorem, and does not imply the one-change Hamiltonian boundary conjecture.

**Proved range.** The two-color triangular-turn terminal theorem proves \(\ell\ge \frac12\sqrt{d}\) for every such object, with no central oddness assumption. Consequently (U) holds whenever \(q-1\ge4k\), when the square-root term dominates. It also holds for \(k=1\), by the complete-graph tournament theorem. For \(k=2\), an arbitrary \(K_q\) factor with \(q\ge3\) contains an accepted two-edge path: choose three symbols in a single factor and orient the resulting length-two word in the direction for which its one triangular turn has \(b=0\). When the right-hand side of (U) exceeds two, the established square-root bound applies. For \(k=q=2\), the deck-odd coloring of the four-cycle forces two adjacent equally colored edges and hence a two-edge witness. Thus (U) holds for all \(k\le2\), all \(q\), and for all \(q\ge4k+1\).

**Obstruction checks.** The mixed-factor \(Q_t\square K_3\) phase-frustration construction fails the homogeneous Hamming spectral-star condition: its valency is \(t+2\) while its second-eigenspace dimension is \(t\). Higher-face NORI constructions do not automatically satisfy the edge-potential condition (2). Arbitrary signed covers and noncanonical gallery systems need not have a Hamming root or a cycle-dominating star-independent resource matroid. These checks exclude the recorded failures by structural conditions independently motivated by the spectral rigidity theorem.

**Open gap and scope.** The difficult region of (U) is \(k\ge3\) and \(2\le q\le4k\), where the required factor-count bound \(\ell\ge k\) exceeds the proved universal square-root estimate. In particular \(q=2\) is the full NORI1 conjecture. No proof or counterexample is presently established for intermediate \(q\ge3\) in this region. The class is defined on canonically recovered *root graphs* and their resource matroids: the boundary input matroid \(U_{2,n}\) enters through its intrinsic line-graph root \(K_n\), whereas the cubical input enters through its own basis graph \(Q_n\). This is a graph-root unification rather than a claim that both original input matroids are direct members of the same homogeneous-rank matroid class.
