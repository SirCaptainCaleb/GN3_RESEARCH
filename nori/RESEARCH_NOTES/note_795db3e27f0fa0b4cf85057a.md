## Research note

Let c be any undirected red/blue edge coloring of Q_n, and assume two antipodal vertices lie in the same red component. Let P=(v_0,...,v_L) be a shortest red path between an antipodal pair, with direction word (i_1,...,i_L). Necessarily L=n+2q, q>=0, because every direction appears an odd positive number of times. For a red path edge e in direction j and a different direction i, call (e,i) a transverse blocking incidence if the i-parallel translate of e is blue. Put E(P)=number of blocking incidences (e,i) with e an edge of P and i distinct from its direction.

**Theorem (curvature charge for shortest red antipodal detours).** E(P)>=q=(L-n)/2. More precisely, for each direction i appearing 2r_i+1 times, pair its successive occurrences (first with second, third with fourth, ..., (2r_i-1)-st with (2r_i)-th), leaving the last occurrence unpaired. Every such pair encloses at least one path edge e in another direction j whose i-translate is blue. For a fixed i, these enclosed subpaths are edge-disjoint, so the selected blocking incidences (e,i) are distinct. Pairs belonging to distinct i also yield distinct ordered incidences because their transverse indices differ. Summing gives E(P)>=sum_i r_i=q.

*Proof of the enclosed-defect assertion.* Suppose consecutive occurrences of direction i enclose only red j-edges whose translations across bit i are also red. Their subpath has form x --i--> x^i, followed by a path S with no i-steps from x^i to y^i, followed by y^i --i--> y. The two i-edges are red. Translate every edge of S across i: by assumption the translated edges are red, and the translated path runs from x to y. Replacing the full segment i,S,i with this translated S removes two steps, leaves its endpoints and the rest of P unchanged, and preserves red coloring. This contradicts the shortest-path choice. The translated walk need not be simple for the contradiction, since removing loops only shortens it further.

**Unoriented square count.** Let F(P) be the number of distinct nonflat 2-faces witnessing at least one blocking incidence along P. A single nonflat square can support at most two blocking incidences with red P-edges, one in each of its two edge directions. A shortest path is simple and does not repeat edges, so E(P)<=2F(P). Therefore F(P)>=ceil(q/2). The ordered-incidence bound E(P)>=q is stronger.

**Explicit local certificate.** In the legal Q4 coloring c_0=1-x_2, c_1=majority(x_0,1-x_2,x_3), c_2=majority(1-x_0,1-x_1,x_3), c_3=x_2, a shortest red path from integer root 2 to its antipode 13 is (2,6,7,3,11,15,14,12,13), direction word (2,0,2,3,2,0,1,0), length 8=4+2*2. For the paired 2-steps at positions (1,3), the intermediate red 0-edge 6--7 has blue 2-translate 2--3. For the paired 0-steps at positions (2,6), the intermediate red 2-edge 11--15 has blue 0-translate 10--14. These give two distinct blocking incidences, as required.

This is a necessary path-local obstruction to removing repeated directions from a shortest monochromatic antipodal path. It makes the missing dimension-simple straightening step measurable in terms of signed square exchanges, without claiming it establishes NORI1.
