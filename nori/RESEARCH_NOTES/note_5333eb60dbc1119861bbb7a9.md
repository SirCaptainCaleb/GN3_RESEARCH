## Research note

Let \(V\) have \(n\ge6\) symbols and fix an oriented Hamilton cycle \(C=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\), cyclically indexed. Its \(n\) Hamilton cuts correspond to the maximal cones of the complete positive simple-root fan \(K_i=\operatorname{cone}\{e_{v_j}-e_{v_{j+1}}:j\ne i\}\). There is an ordinary reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament \(b\) whose triple-bit word along every one of these cuts has at least \(n-4\) switches; when \(n\) is even every cut has \(n-3\) switches. The same bounds hold for every cut of the reversed cycle, the antipodal fan.

Proof. Prescribe the cyclic triple bits \(s_i=b(v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2})\) by \(s_i=i\bmod2\) for \(1\le i\le n\), and extend to all other ordered triples arbitrarily subject to \(b(c,h,a)=1-b(a,h,c)\). Such an extension exists: the displayed \(n\) triples lie in pairwise distinct reversal orbits for \(n\ge4\). Every linear cut has a consecutive length-\((n-2)\) segment of the periodic word \((s_i)\), hence \(n-3\) successive bit comparisons. If \(n\) is even, all adjacent cyclic bits differ, so every comparison changes color. If \(n\) is odd, all comparisons change color except possibly the unique transition \(s_n=s_1\), so each cut has at least \(n-4\) switches, and some cut attains that bound. Reversing the Hamilton word complements and reverses its triple-bit word; the number of switches is unchanged. \(\square\)

Therefore even a complete spherical simplicial fan whose maximal cones all encode Hamilton root bases can be entirely devoid of one-switch galleries, together with its antipodal mate. A topological selector that looks only within one cyclic fan cannot prove the unrestricted boundary one-switch conjecture; inter-cycle incidence and the coloring must enter. The cyclic switch-count identity itself is already established in Article IX.
