## Research note

**Proposition (complete second-switch signed palette on one parabolic cell).** In \(Q_5\) there is a shared-edge-consistent coloring of the twelve complete rooted geodesics starting at \(0\) whose dimension orders are arbitrary permutations of the block \((1,2)\), followed by arbitrary permutations of the block \((3,4,5)\), such that every word has at least two switches and all six signed second-switch labels \(\{\pm e_2,\pm e_3,\pm e_4\}\) appear.

*Witness.* For a direction \(d\) edge, encode the endpoint \(y\) with \(y_d=0\) by the integer \(\sum_i y_i2^{i-1}\). Prescribe the following sixteen distinct undirected edge bits \((d,y,c)\):
\[
(1,0,0),(1,2,0),(2,0,1),(2,1,1),
\]
\[
(3,3,1),(3,11,1),(3,19,0),(3,27,1),
\]
\[
(4,3,0),(4,7,1),(4,19,0),(4,23,1),
\]
\[
(5,3,0),(5,7,0),(5,11,0),(5,15,0).
\]
The twelve color words, in the ordering
\[
12345,12354,12435,12453,12534,12543,
\]
\[
21345,21354,21435,21453,21534,21543,
\]
are respectively
\[
01110,\ 01101,\ 01010,\ 01001,\ 01001,\ 01001,
\]
\[
10110,\ 10101,\ 10010,\ 10001,\ 10001,\ 10001.
\]
Every word has at least two switches. The words \(01110,01101,01010\) have second switches at positions \(4,3,2\) with first bit \(0\); the words \(10001,10010,10110\) have second switches at positions \(4,3,2\) with first bit \(1\). No two assigned edges are antipodal: a direction in the first block is always traversed before the second block, and a direction in the second block is always traversed after the first block, so some dimension of the other block retains a common bit in every pair of parallel edges. Consequently these assignments extend to an antipodally odd coloring of the entire cube, by complementing edge colors on antipodal mates.

The conclusion only rules out a universal *cellwise missing-label hemisphere*. It does not assert that the boundary map of this cell has nonzero degree, nor does it contradict global monochromatic geodesic existence. It confirms that shared-edge descent and full rank do not force incomplete signed spectra in a single primary obstruction cell.
