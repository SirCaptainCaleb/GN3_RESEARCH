## Research note

DIMENSION-UNIFORM LOCAL RED-STAR TRAP. For any m>=4, make arbitrary edge coloring b of Q_m with bits x0,...,x_(m-1): b0=0, b1=x2 XOR x3, b2=x1 XOR x3, b3=x1 XOR x2, and bi=0 for i>=4. Every edge incident with vertex zero is red. Yet b has no monochromatic full m-geodesic. Every full path uses direction 0, which is always red, precluding an all-blue path. For an all-red path, its projection to directions 1,2,3 must be a red three-direction full geodesic. But the red graph of the corresponding Q3 is two disjoint 3-stars centered at 000 and 111; no such three-direction path exists.

Make a legal odd coloring C of Q_(m+1), with new direction t, by C_i(x,0)=b_i(x), C_i(x,1)=1-b_i(bar x), and C_t(x,t)=x0. Every rule ignores its own bit and reverses its color under global antipodal complementation. For both frozen t facets, their original-m-direction coloring has no full red geodesic. For t=0 this is the previous result; for t=1 red would correspond to a blue full path of b, impossible because direction 0 is red throughout b. Nevertheless every pair of the original m directions admits a red two-edge path, all at the same center (x,t)=(0,0), where ALL original m incident directions are red.

The standard paired balanced lift from C to Q_(2m+2) remains odd and balanced at all vertices and on complete-pair faces. It still has no red full geodesic within any frozen original-m face, though some such faces have vertices where all m original direction edges are red. This is a family in every m>=4 disproving the suggestion that a full common red direction star or a Hamilton ordering of locally red-compatible directions automatically extends to one full geodesic on that support. The full lifted cube may have geodesics involving the additional directions; this is not a NORI1 counterexample.

LINEAR-GAP STRENGTHENING FOR ANY h>=1. Let b denote the 4-cube seed with signed directions b0=0, b1=x2 XOR x3, b2=x1 XOR x3, b3=x1 XOR x2. In its red graph, direction 0 is always available, while on directions {1,2,3} the red graph is the disjoint union of two degree-three stars, so its longest rainbow red path has length 3. In its blue graph direction 0 is unavailable, so its longest rainbow blue path also has length at most 3; a blue three-edge path exists on {1,2,3}. Thus both monochromatic maximum direction-simple lengths equal 3 in dimension 4. Moreover every one of the four direction edges at root zero is red.

Form the h-fold independent Cartesian product b^h on Q_(4h). Every single-color direction-simple path projects to a single-color direction-simple path in each factor, so the length in either color is bounded above by 3h; concatenating factorwise witnesses shows the maximum is 3h. At its all-zero root every one of the 4h incident edges is red, although every monochromatic geodesic uses at most 3h of the 4h direction labels, leaving at least h=one quarter of all labels unused.

Complete b^h to a legal antipodally odd coloring C on Q_(4h+1) with two facets (b^h) and (1-b^h after antipodal reflection), and bridge rule given by the first original bit. In BOTH facets and BOTH monochromatic colors, the longest direction-simple path restricted to the original 4h directions has length 3h. The lower facet still has a red star on all 4h original directions at its zero vertex. Apply the paired complementary-direction lift to Q_(8h+2), which is balanced and legal odd. Every original-4h face with all other direction bits frozen retains this linear fractional obstruction, while the global balanced cube has the same full-geodesic existence status as C. This is not a NORI1 counterexample. It proves that local red-star saturation, red pairwise-turn availability, and a Hamilton order of locally compatible labels, even together with global balance and antipodal oddness, do not imply a monochromatic geodesic of length 4h-o(h) confined to those selected directions.
