## Research note

Fix m>=2 and an arbitrary binary coloring d of the undirected edges of Q_m, with no antipodal symmetry imposed. For each vertex y and color i let R(y;i) be the vertices accessible from y by an i-monochromatic geodesic. Define the one-switch interface
Z(y) = [R(y;0) intersect R(bar y;1)] union [R(y;1) intersect R(bar y;0)].
Because the direction supports of a geodesic y to z and a geodesic bar y to z are disjoint and together form [m], z belongs to Z(y) if and only if a full antipodal geodesic from y to bar y changes color at z once (allowing endpoint degeneracies only when a monochromatic full path exists).

CONJECTURE (complementary interface dichotomy). For every m and every d as above, either d has a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic, or there exist y,z with z and bar z both in Z(y). Equivalently, some root has two one-switch antipodal geodesics whose switch vertices are antipodal in Q_m.

FACET SPLICE THEOREM. This conjecture in dimension m implies the unrestricted NORI1 monochromatic antipodal-geodesic assertion in dimension m+1. Proof: Let c be antipodally odd on Q_{m+1}; restrict it to any m-facet F and denote its coloring d. A monochromatic m-edge full geodesic within F extends via the existing penultimate extension lemma to a monochromatic (m+1)-edge full geodesic. Otherwise choose full one-switch paths P,Q within F with common root y and complementary switch vertices z,bar_F z. Apply full-cube antipodal reflection followed by path reversal to P. This gives a path P* in the opposite facet F^a, with root y^a, end bar_F y^a, and switch at bar_F z^a, matching the projected switch of Q; P* has the same initial phase as P (because reverse and complement preserve the initial phase of a one-switch binary word). Let alpha/beta be initial/final colors of Q and gamma/delta those of P*, with beta=1-alpha and delta=1-gamma. Splice Q's initial monochromatic prefix, transverse a-edge at bar_F z, and P*'s terminal monochromatic suffix, obtaining a full geodesic if alpha=gamma. Its bridge word has at most one change irrespective of the transverse edge color. If alpha!=gamma, the initial prefix of Q and terminal suffix of P* both have color alpha, whereas the initial prefix of P* and terminal suffix of Q both have color gamma. Whichever color the transverse a-edge has, choose the corresponding orientation to obtain a monochromatic full geodesic. In the first case the existing odd-switch rotation lemma converts the full at-most-one-switch path to monochromatic. Thus the conjecture implies NORI1 in dimension m+1.

The root must be allowed to vary. For Q3, the arbitrary coloring c1(x)=1-x2, c2(x)=1-x1, c3(x)=0 has at root y=000 no monochromatic full antipodal path and switch set Z(000)={100,010}, which contains no complementary vertices. Yet this coloring has monochromatic antipodal geodesics at other roots.

FINITE EVIDENCE. Independent explicit path enumeration and two-color dynamic reachability calculations confirm the dichotomy for all arbitrary edge colorings of Q2 (16 cases) and Q3 (4096 cases). Of the 4096 Q3 colorings, 90 have no monochromatic full antipodal geodesic, and all 90 have complementary interface witnesses. For Q4, encode all counterexamples with 32 binary edge variables and 4032 deduplicated forbidden-pattern linear inequalities, excluding monochromatic full paths and excluding every pair of one-switch full paths from a common root with complementary switch supports. SciPy/HiGHS MILP reports this 32-variable system infeasible. This is computational finite evidence and has no independent checkable unsatisfiability certificate in the current note.

The conjecture remains open for general m. The facet splice applies to undirected edge coloring and uses antipodal oddness only when transporting a facet path to the opposite facet and removing a possible final switch. A positive result would settle unrestricted NORI1; failure of this stronger conjecture would leave NORI1 open.
