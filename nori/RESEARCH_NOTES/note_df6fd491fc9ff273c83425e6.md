## Research note

**Antipodal root-pair criterion (unrestricted, dimensionwise).** For an arbitrary binary undirected edge coloring f of Q_d let A_0(f) be the set of roots admitting a full antipodal geodesic with color word 0^*1^*. Then unrestricted NORI1 for all legal colorings of Q_(d+1) is equivalent to A_0(f) containing at least one antipodal pair for every f.

**Proof.** Fix a direction b as the bridge in Q_(d+1). Every legal odd coloring is determined by its lower-facet coloring f, upper horizontal colors c_i(u,1)=1-f_i(bar u), and bridge rule c_b(u,s)=h(u) where h(bar u)=1-h(u). The standard two-facet folding correspondence shows that the extended coloring has a zero full geodesic iff A_0(f) meets h^{-1}(0). Every monochromatic-one full geodesic antipodally translates to a monochromatic-zero full geodesic, so NORI1 requires this intersection for every f and every odd h. A subset A of Q_d meets the zero set of every odd h iff A contains an antipodal pair: the forward implication is the transversal property; when A is antipodal-free, take h=1 on A and h=0 on its antipodes, extending oddly over unused pairs. This proves both directions.

**Product law.** For independent edge colorings f,g, A_0(f box g)=A_0(f) x A_0(g): projection of a nondecreasing color word remains nondecreasing, while a product path is obtained by taking every zero run first and every one run afterwards. Therefore antipodal-root-pair sets also tensorize. By the one-exception theorem, the pair property holds for arbitrary f whose edge rules are each constant or unary readers (their odd bridge extension has just one nonunary rule). The unrestricted pair property is a precise grand-closure target.
