# Full reconstruction of antipodally odd cube-edge colorings from projective turn labels

Let n>=3 and F_n=Q_n/tau be the folded cube graph, tau(x)=bar x. Its vertices are pairs v=[x] and its edges are antipodal pairs of original undirected cube edges, with direction i. At each v and distinct incident directions i,j, prescribe a symmetric binary turn value t_v(i,j), with t_v(i,i)=0.

THEOREM (flat-cut reconstruction). The following conditions are necessary and sufficient for a legal edge coloring c_i(x)=c_i(x xor e_i), c_i(bar x)=1+c_i(x), inducing t_[x](i,j)=c_i(x)+c_j(x):
(A) At every v, t_v(i,j)+t_v(j,k)+t_v(k,i)=0 for all i,j,k. Each star is a binary cut metric.
(B) For every original square x,x^i,x^(i+j),x^j, sum of t at its four projected corners for the pair (i,j) is zero.
(C) On ONE projected full antipodal geodesic cycle with successive directions pi_1,...,pi_n and vertices v_0,...,v_(n-1), the turn sum t_(v_0)(pi_n,pi_1)+sum_(k=1)^(n-1)t_(v_k)(pi_k,pi_(k+1)) equals 1.

Any two legal colorings with the same turn values are global complements. In particular the turn data contain all original edge-color information needed for NORI1, modulo the immaterial global color reversal.

PROOF. Necessity of (A) follows by cancelling three incident edge-color bits. Necessity of (B) follows because each original square edge contributes twice to the four local turn comparisons; note n>=3 keeps a square distinct from its antipode. Necessity of (C) is antipodal holonomy: the projective cycle lifts to an x-to-bar x geodesic, whose twisted binary word has odd variation.

For sufficiency, first use (A) to choose at each projective vertex v incidence bits b_v(i) such that t_v(i,j)=b_v(i)+b_v(j). They are unique up to simultaneously toggling all b_v(i) at one v. For an edge e=uv in direction i of F_n, put gamma(e)=b_u(i)+b_v(i). For any closed projective edge walk W, the turn sum of W equals sum_(e in W)gamma(e), since its two incidence terms at every visited vertex combine into the local turn value.

Consequently (B) says gamma sums to zero around every projected cubical square. Let Y=boundary(Q_n)/tau, the projective cubical complex RP^(n-1). The first homology of Y over F2 is F2, generated by the projected path of any full antipodal geodesic. Thus (C) makes [gamma] equal to the nonzero generator u=w1 of Q_n->Y.

Choose one representative s(v) of each projective vertex. Let a(e) be the sheet voltage of the antipodal double cover along the edge e=uv: starting at s(u), its original direction-i lift terminates at s(v) if a(e)=0, and at bar(s(v)) if a(e)=1. Then a is another 1-cocycle representing u. Therefore gamma+a is a coboundary on the folded graph, so there are vertex bits g(v) with g(u)+g(v)=gamma(e)+a(e) for every e. Define the original color on the i-edge incident to s(v) by c_i(s(v))=b_v(i)+g(v), and on its antipodal edge by c_i(bar(s(v)))=1+c_i(s(v)). The voltage equation guarantees both endpoints assign the same bit to their common undirected edge. The resulting legal coloring induces the prescribed t.

If c,c' induce the same turn values, then at every original vertex x the difference c_i(x)+c'_i(x) is independent of i. Undirected edge consistency forces this difference to be constant along every original cube edge, hence globally constant by connectedness. Thus precisely two lifts remain: c and 1+c.

DIMENSION AND SECTOR INTERPRETATION. F_n has V=2^(n-1) vertices and E=n*2^(n-2) edges. Every star satisfying (A) has n-1 free bits, giving V(n-1) bits total. Conditions (B) and (C) cut out an affine F2 space of dimension E-1: there are 2^(E-1) realizable odd turn fields, since all 2^E legal colorings pair under global complementation. The independent compatibility codimension after the star triangle laws is (n-2)*2^(n-2)+1, the cycle-space rank E-V+1 of F_n. The projected square cycles span the nullhomologous subspace of rank E-V; condition (C) fixes the remaining projective bit. If (C) is replaced by even holonomy, the reconstructed coloring is antipodally even instead. This gives two projective holonomy sectors.

A projective full geodesic C has integer turn energy D_t(C)=sum_(v,i,j in C)t_v(i,j). By (C) and (B) every such energy is odd, and it equals the ordinary twisted defect D of the reconstructed legal coloring. NORI1 is therefore equivalent to a faithful projective flat-cut systolic theorem: every turn field obeying (A),(B),(C) has a direction-rainbow essential n-cycle of turn energy one.

The reconstruction resolves the shared-edge consistency issue for proposed local-turn labels. It does not establish a one-turn cycle: the still-open forcing step must use the 0/1 support of a globally flat, star-cut turn field rather than only its mod-two holonomy.
## Twisted incidence and edge-sign rigidity

Let G=Q_n/tau (n>=3) be the folded cube. Choose a representative s(v) of each projective vertex and orient the edge orbits. For e:u->v let a_e in F2 describe whether its original lift from s(u) ends at s(v) or tau(s(v)). Define twisted integral incidence B[e]=(-1)^(a_e)[v]-[u]. Let h_e be the sign (-1)^c of its oriented lifted edge, and D_h=diag(h_e).

For a direction-rainbow essential projected cycle C, let chi_C be its oriented edge chain. The vector b=BD_h chi_C takes values in {0,2,-2} at projective vertices. Its nonzero support consists of the color seams of the antipodal geodesic lifting C, and its l1 norm equals twice the twisted seam number D(C). Indeed, at an intermediate vertex the boundary term is a sheet sign times (s_k-s_(k+1)), where s_k denotes the signed color of the kth lifted edge. At the projective root the term is a sheet sign times -(s_n+s_1). The projected vertices are distinct. Thus unrestricted NORI1 is equivalent to min_C ||BD_h chi_C||_1=2 for rainbow essential cycles C. This represents the earlier projective turn systole through an integral boundary operator; it is not a new proof of the minimization.

The integral image of B is the lattice of projective vertex vectors with even entry sum, and B has full row rank over R. To prove this, use sign changes of vertex rows to make all spanning-tree voltages zero. The tree columns generate the integer vectors of entry sum zero. Nontriviality of the antipodal double covering supplies a non-tree edge of negative voltage, whose column has entry sum 2 or -2. Combining it with tree columns generates the full even-sum lattice; all columns already have even sums. The Smith invariants are 1,...,1,2. In particular 2[v] lies in B Z^E for every v; the one-seam boundary is feasible by unconstrained integer chains. The difficulty is realization by D_h chi_C with C a single rainbow cycle and h prescribed by the common edge coloring.

For two legal colorings with edge sign assignments h,h', let D_delta=diag(h'_e/h_e). Their signed edge-orbit chamber maps differ by D_delta, an invertible diagonal linear operator. This makes the raw edge-chain equivariant topology independent of coloring. But twisted incidence is rigid under vertex-sign changes: if BD_delta=GB, where G is diagonal with entries +/-1 on vertices, each edge forces delta_e=G_uu=G_vv; graph connectedness makes every delta_e identical. Thus the pair (B,D_h) retains the coloring, up to global complementation, even though the raw edge-chain map alone does not.

For a proper original face F with at least one free direction S, every chamber incident to its dual cell D_F uses |S| edges inside F. No two such internal edges are antipodal. A color-corrected linear functional on the signed edge-orbit space is therefore |S| on all these chamber vectors, so their convex hull avoids zero. This excludes every nonroot dual cell, but not a root dual cell (S empty). Reversing the order at fixed root negates the edge-orbit chain, producing a root-cell central zero in barycentric interpolation. Since the target dimension is larger than n-1, root-cell zeros can be filled away while preserving geometric antipodality between paired root cells. Root-order reversal is a separate symmetry and need not be preserved by this filling.

Neither topology of raw edge chains nor the linear integer boundary module supplies the structured rainbow-cycle forcing step.


## Canonical seam-majority vertex and first-Walsh rigidity

Fix a rooted full antipodal geodesic P=(x,pi). Let w_i(P) be the bit color of its direction-i edge. Define q_i(P)=x_i+w_i(P) mod2. Under the root slide across its first edge (with that direction moved to the end), q is unchanged: the root bit and displaced edge color both complement. Geometric antipodality also leaves q unchanged. Reversal of the full path complements q. Hence the projective class Q(C)=[q] depends only on the unoriented direction-rainbow essential projected cycle C. A one-seam full path exists on C iff Q(C) is one of the n projective vertices of C. Indeed q=x^S for a prefix direction set S iff the color word is 1 on S and 0 afterward; sliding the root to the junction gives an all-red path. The antipodal case gives the all-blue counterpart.

Extend P to its doubled 2n-edge antipodal cycle, with vertices u_0,...,u_(2n)=u_0 and signed edge colors s_k in {+1,-1}, where s_(k+n)=-s_k. Let Z(P) be the vertices where the incoming edge is RED and the outgoing edge is BLUE. There are d=D(P) such vertices, one per red-to-blue seam in the doubled cycle. For each direction i define the odd Walsh character chi_i(y)=(-1)^(y_i). Then

  sum_(z in Z(P)) chi_i(z) = -(-1)^(q_i(P)).        (S1)

Proof. On the doubled cycle put a_k=(s_k-s_(k+1))/2 at u_k, with cyclic indexing. Red-to-blue seams carry a=+1 and blue-to-red seams carry a=-1; the latter vertices are the antipodes of Z(P). Hence sum_k a_k chi_i(u_k)=2 sum_Z chi_i(z). Cyclic summation by parts gives
  sum_k a_k chi_i(u_k)
     =(1/2) sum_(k=1)^(2n) s_k (chi_i(u_k)-chi_i(u_(k-1))).
The character changes only on the two direction-i edges, an antipodal pair. Before the first such edge, the i-bit is x_i. Each of the two contributions inside the last sum is -2s_i chi_i(x), where s_i=(-1)^(w_i(P)); the half-sum is -2s_i chi_i(x)=-2(-1)^(q_i). Division by 2 proves (S1).

Let d=2r+1. By (S1), for every direction i, precisely r seam vertices of Z have bit q_i and r+1 have bit 1-q_i. Consequently m(P)=bar(q(P)) is their UNIQUE coordinatewise majority vertex. Its majority margin is one in every direction, and the Hamming distance potential has the global identity
  sum_(z in Z(P)) d_H(y,z) = r*n + d_H(y,m(P))
for EVERY cube vertex y. Thus m is the unique minimizer of the sum of distances to the red-to-blue seams. Its projective class is the previously defined Q(C).

For d=3, the three red-to-blue seam vertices are alternate seam boundaries along the doubled path. Their consecutive cyclic gaps are sums of two neighboring monochromatic run lengths and lie below n, so they give the admissible three-point median triangle of the existing three-route conservation theorem. The canonical majority m is that triangle's cubical median, and its three arms have disjoint direction supports covering all directions. This links the signed-boundary Fourier representation and the median descent programme, but does not assert that those arms are monochromatic.

There is also a discrete gradient interpretation. For every edge of the ORIGINAL rooted P, flipping direction i changes the Hamming distance to m by -1 when that edge is red and by +1 when it is blue. This follows from m_i=1+x_i+w_i and the fact that bit i has not been flipped before its own edge. Accordingly the red-to-blue seams in the doubled path are the local minima of the distance-to-m height profile. NORI1 asks for an essential rainbow cycle whose self-selected median lies on the cycle, equivalently a single minimum of this self-consistent height. The median moves under an adjacent exchange of directions i,j only in i,j, with flips given by the two original square derivatives, and remains fixed under root slides. A global no-avoidance theorem for these constrained median selectors remains open.


## Edge orientations of the folded cube

For n>=3, legal antipodally odd colorings of the original cube edges correspond bijectively to arbitrary orientations of the projective folded cube G=Q_n/tau. If a projected direction-i edge is represented by the lifted directed edge u->u^i, define ell=u_i+c_i(u) mod 2. Reversing the edge toggles ell while translating the edge antipodally leaves ell fixed. Orient each projected edge in the unique direction with ell=0. Conversely, any orientation determines c_i(u)=u_i on an oriented lifted edge, uses the same bit for its reverse and the complementary bit on its antipode. The constructions are inverse.

For an essential projected n-cycle C using each direction once, let q_i(C)=0 if the direction-i edge of C agrees with the assigned edge orientation and q_i(C)=1 otherwise. Its oriented bit vector equals x_i+c_i(e_i) on any rooted lift P. Reversing C complements q, so Q(C)=[q(C)] is intrinsic to the unoriented cycle. Hence unrestricted NORI1 is equivalent to a statement about ALL orientations of the folded cube: some direction-rainbow essential cycle contains the projective vertex Q(C) determined by its own backward-traversed directions. This is equivalent to the preceding canonical seam-median selector theorem, but expresses its input as an arbitrary graph orientation.

The folded cube is the Cayley graph of F2^n/<1> with direction generators [e_i], whose sole nontrivial binary dependence is the full sum of all n generators. A rainbow essential cycle is generated by a permutation of those generators. The self-incidence condition Q(C) in V(C) is the remaining fixed-incidence target; it is not established by this graph-orientation equivalence. The adjacent-square transport of Q changes only the exchanged two direction bits and is constrained by the original square derivatives.


## Affine-frame seam selector and square-pivot transport

Let \(H=\mathbb F_2^n/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\), \(n\ge3\), and let \(P=(x,\pi)\) be any rooted full antipodal geodesic. Put \(v_j=[x+\sum_{k=1}^j e_{\pi_k}]\in H\) for \(0\le j<n\), \(v_n=v_0\), and \(g_k=[e_{\pi_k}]=v_{k-1}+v_k\). The \(n\) projected cycle vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}\) form an affine basis of \(H\), since the \(n-1\) differences \(v_j+v_0=\sum_{k=1}^j g_k\) form a linear basis.

Let \(w_k\in\mathbb F_2\) be the color of the \(k\)-th edge, and define \(t_0=1+w_n+w_1\), \(t_j=w_j+w_{j+1}\) for \(1\le j<n\). These are the seam indicators at the projective cycle vertices, including the antiperiodic closing seam. Their sum in \(\mathbb F_2\) is one, and their integer sum is the odd twisted defect \(d(P)\).

THEOREM (affine seam identity). The canonical seam-majority selector \(Q(C)=[x+\sum_k w_k e_{\pi_k}]\) of the projected cycle \(C\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{Q(C)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}t_jv_j\quad\text{in }H.}\tag{AF1}
\]
Thus its unique odd affine support in the frame \(V(C)\) is precisely the seam set. The projective majority of the red-to-blue seam vertices equals their sum in \(H\). The selector belongs to \(V(C)\) iff \(d(P)=1\), equivalently a cyclic root slide gives a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic.

PROOF. In \(H\), \(Q(C)=v_0+\sum_{k=1}^nw_k(v_{k-1}+v_k)\). Collecting the coefficients at each \(v_j\) gives (AF1); affine independence gives uniqueness. The established first-Walsh seam identity identifies the seam-majority projective class with \(Q(C)\). A single nonzero coefficient characterizes a frame vertex and hence defect one. QED.

THEOREM (legal square-pivot law). Swap consecutive directions \(i=\pi_j\), \(h=\pi_{j+1}\), \(1\le j<n\), obtaining \(P'\). Let \(\delta_i\) be the XOR of the direction-\(i\) edge colors in the two square routes, and \(\delta_h\) the analogous direction-\(h\) XOR. The only changed frame vertex obeys
\[
v'_j=v_{j-1}+v_j+v_{j+1},
\]
with subscripts cyclic modulo \(n\). The selectors satisfy \(Q(C')=Q(C)+\delta_i g_j+\delta_h g_{j+1}\), and the seam indicators in their respective affine frames satisfy
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
t'_{j-1}&=t_{j-1}+t_j+\delta_h,\\
t'_j&=t_j+\delta_i+\delta_h,\\
t'_{j+1}&=t_{j+1}+t_j+\delta_i,
\end{aligned}}\tag{AF2}
\]
with every other indicator unchanged. These equations hold in \(\mathbb F_2\), for arbitrary nonlinear, shared-edge-consistent legal colorings.

PROOF. The two routes around the same original square replace \(v_j\) by its fourth vertex, giving the frame exchange. The direction-indexed color bits change only in directions \(i,h\), by \(\delta_i,\delta_h\), giving the selector exchange. Substitute \(g_j=v_{j-1}+v_j\), \(g_{j+1}=v_j+v_{j+1}\), then \(v_j=v'_j+v_{j-1}+v_{j+1}\) into the old affine expression for \(Q(C')\). Collecting the coefficients in the new affine basis gives (AF2). QED.

Cyclic root slides cyclically relabel the frame and its seam support; antipodal translation preserves their projective values. Consequently the formulas supply a globally compatible affine-basis exchange operation retaining all original directions once and every original edge color. In a hypothetical NORI1 counterexample every such frame has an odd support of size at least three. The missing forcing theorem must rule out an everywhere nonsingleton support assignment under these globally integrable square derivatives. Individual pivots can increase or preserve support size, so (AF2) alone does not prove descent.


## Obstruction to a global Euclidean square-affine interpolation

Let \(H=\mathbb F_2^n/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\), \(n\ge3\), with generators \(g_i=[e_i]\). Suppose a map \(\psi:H\to V\) into a real vector space obeys the parallelogram law
\[
\psi(u)+\psi(u+g_i+g_j)
=\psi(u+g_i)+\psi(u+g_j)
\tag{AF3}
\]
for every \(u\in H\) and every pair of distinct original directions \(i,j\).

THEOREM. Every such \(\psi\) is constant.

PROOF. Put \(D_i(u)=\psi(u+g_i)-\psi(u)\). Equation (AF3) gives \(D_i(u+g_j)=D_i(u)\) for every \(j\ne i\). The \(n-1\) generators \(\{g_j:j\ne i\}\) form a basis of \(H\), so \(D_i\) is constant on \(H\). Yet \(D_i(u+g_i)=-D_i(u)\), since \(2g_i=0\). Therefore \(D_i=0\) and \(\psi\) is constant. QED.

This prohibits a nonconstant **global real affine embedding** of the folded cube that sends every projective original-square exchange to a Euclidean parallelogram. It does not prohibit nonlinear PL maps, local affine charts with nontrivial transitions, or color-dependent carriers. In particular the finite-field pivot identities (AF1)-(AF2) cannot be transferred verbatim into a Euclidean convex-map construction: a proposed topological decoder must supply additional nonlinear or twisted compatibility.


## Integral seam charges in moving real sign frames

For a rooted full antipodal path \(P=(x,\pi)\), let \(u_j=x+\sum_{k=1}^je_{\pi_k}\in Q_n\) for \(0\le j\le n\), \(u_n=\bar x\). Put \(s(u)=((-1)^{u_i})_{i=1}^n\) and let \(U_P\) be the \(n\times n\) real matrix of columns \(s(u_0),\ldots,s(u_{n-1})\). The signed original-edge colors are \(a_k=(-1)^{w_k(P)}\). For the canonical selector \(q_i=x_i+w_{\pi^{-1}(i)}\pmod2\), put
\[
\lambda_0=(a_n+a_1)/2,\qquad
\lambda_j=(a_{j+1}-a_j)/2\quad(1\le j<n).
\tag{RF1}
\]

**THEOREM (integral frame decoder).** For every legal nonlinear edge coloring,
\[
\boxed{s(q)=U_P\lambda,\quad
\det U_P=2^{n-1}(-1)^{|x|}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi),\quad
\sum_j\lambda_j=a_n.}
\tag{RF2}
\]
Each \(\lambda_j\) is \(0,\pm1\), and \(|\lambda_j|=t_j\), the twisted-seam indicator at projective cycle vertex \(j\). Put \(\alpha_j=a_n\lambda_j\) and \(p_P=a_ns(q)\). Then \(p_P\) and the path simplex \(T_P=\operatorname{conv}\{s(u_0),\ldots,s(u_{n-1})\}\) lie in the same outer-cube facet, and
\[
\boxed{p_P=\sum_j\alpha_js(u_j),\quad
\sum_j\alpha_j=1,\quad
\sum_j(-\alpha_j)_+=(d(P)-1)/2.}
\tag{RF3}
\]
Therefore NORI1 is equivalent to existence of a path \(P\) whose selected facet vertex \(p_P\) lies in its path simplex \(T_P\).

*Proof.* Multiply the sign lifts by the diagonal root-sign matrix and arrange rows in the order \(\pi\). Then the \(k\)-th entry of \(s(q)\) equals \(a_k\); the \(k\)-th entry in column \(j\) of \(U_P\) is \(+1\) if \(j<k\), and \(-1\) otherwise. The last row of \(U_P\lambda=s(q)\) gives \(\sum_j\lambda_j=a_n\), consecutive row differences give \(2\lambda_j=a_{j+1}-a_j\), and the first row gives \(2\lambda_0=a_1+a_n\). Column elimination yields the stated determinant. Nonzero \(\lambda_j\) occur precisely at the antiperiodic seam positions. After scaling by \(a_n\), the integer affine coefficients sum to one; their negative mass is \((d-1)/2\). Every vertex \(s(u_j)\) has its \(\pi_n\)-entry equal to the root sign, as does \(p_P\), so the point lies in their supporting facet. The columns of \(U_P\) are linearly independent and span this facet affinely. The point is inside the simplex iff all its unique affine coefficients are nonnegative, which occurs iff \(d=1\). QED.

**Legal moving-frame exchanges.** Under the cyclic root slide \(P'=(x^{\pi_1},(\pi_2,\ldots,\pi_n,\pi_1))\),
\[
U_{P'}=[s(u_1),\ldots,s(u_{n-1}),-s(u_0)],\quad
\lambda(P')=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},-\lambda_0).
\tag{RF4}
\]
Hence \(U_{P'}\lambda(P')=U_P\lambda(P)=s(q)\). After all \(n\) root slides, \(U\) and \(\lambda\) both change sign. This is the antiperiodic monodromy of the projective cycle. Under a legal adjacent interchange of directions in positions \(j,j+1\), the single changing path vertex obeys
\[
\boxed{s(u'_j)=s(u_{j-1})-s(u_j)+s(u_{j+1}),}
\tag{RF5}
\]
with \(s(u_n)=-s(u_0)\). All other columns are unchanged. This linear relation holds in the moving root-dependent frames, while each signed seam vector is obtained from its own original colored path. It evades the preceding no-go theorem for one global Euclidean square-affine chart.

**Topological forcing obligation.** The negative affine mass equals half the excess cyclic switch defect over one. The signed moving frames provide a color-faithful geometric decoder with legitimate square and root-slide operations. No proof presently forces a selected outer-facet vertex into its own simplex. Such a global incidence or degree theorem would prove unrestricted NORI1.
