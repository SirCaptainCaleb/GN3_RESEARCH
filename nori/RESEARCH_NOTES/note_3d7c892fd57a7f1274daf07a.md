## Research note

In vertex-balanced Q_(2m), every color-b monochromatic m-face uses all m available b incident edges at each vertex, so every boundary edge has the opposite color. A monochromatic zero m-face F and monochromatic one m-face G, if disjoint, cannot have adjacent vertices: their joining boundary edge would be forced to have both colors. For a hypothetical bad coloring, F and the antipode of every other zero m-face Fprime are disjoint, and therefore separated by Hamming distance at least two. Let S,T be the free direction sets of F,Fprime and let a,a-prime be their fixed bit assignments. The distance equals the number of jointly fixed directions h outside S union T on which a_h equals a-prime_h. Thus there are at least two such directions. In particular abs(S intersect T) >= 2 for every pair of zero maximal faces. Their free direction sets form a pairwise two-intersecting family, and their fixed bit labels satisfy a stronger agreement condition. This global obstruction holds for any hypothetical balanced counterexample.
