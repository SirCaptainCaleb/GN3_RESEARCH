## Research note

### Triangle-generation obstruction with bounded one-switch rainbow paths

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge5\), there is a connected finite simple graph \(G_n\), an ordinary edge coloring \(\lambda\) using \(n+1\) colors, a binary edge labeling \(\chi\), and a fixed-point-free vertex involution \(\tau\) such that
\[
\widehat\delta(G_n,\lambda)=n,\quad
\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e),\quad L_1(G_n,\lambda,\chi)\le7,
\]
and the **entire binary cycle space of \(G_n\) is generated by triangles**. In particular, even the generation of all graph cycles by length-three cycles fails to imply \(L_1\ge c\widehat\delta\) for any fixed \(c>0\).

**Proof.** Let \(B_n\) denote the graph with vertices \((u,v,s)\), where \(u\ne v\in[n]\) and \(s\in\mathbb F_2\), and edges \(\{(u,v,s),(v,w,1-s)\}\) for pairwise distinct \(u,v,w\). As established in the companion parity-cover note, color this edge by \(w\), give it binary label \(s\), and define \(\tau(u,v,s)=(v,u,s)\). Then \(\widehat\delta(B_n)=n-1\), \(\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e)\), and \(L_1(B_n)=3\).

Let \(G_n=B_n^2\), the **square of the graph**: join pairs of vertices at graph distance one or two in \(B_n\). Retain the old ordinary and binary edge labels. Give every new edge one fresh ordinary color \(\star\). A vertex originally sees \(n-1\) ordinary colors and has at least one new neighbor, so \(\widehat\delta(G_n)=n\). Every new edge joins vertices in the same sheet because \(B_n\) is bipartite; the old edges join opposite sheets. Because \(\tau\) preserves graph distance, it acts on \(G_n\). No new edge is fixed setwise by \(\tau\), as follows.

**Common-neighbor lemma.** For a fixed sheet \(s\), any two distinct vertices \(x=(u,v,s)\) and \(y=(a,b,s)\) have a common neighbor in \(B_n\) unless \(y=(v,u,s)=\tau x\). Indeed,
\[
N_{B_n}(u,v,s)=\{(v,z,1-s),(z,u,1-s):z\notin\{u,v\}\}.
\]
The neighbor sets of \(x\) and \(\tau x\) are disjoint. For any other \(y\), if \(u=a\), choose a common neighbor of form \((z,u,1-s)\), with \(z\notin\{u,v,b\}\); if \(v=b\), choose \((v,z,1-s)\), with \(z\notin\{u,v,a\}\). Otherwise \(a\ne u\) and \(b\ne v\). If \(a\ne v\), the vertex \((v,a,1-s)\) is a common neighbor. If \(a=v\), then \(b\notin\{u,v\}\) because \(y\ne\tau x\), and \((b,u,1-s)\) is a common neighbor. This covers every case. Consequently, the graph induced by either sheet in \(G_n\) is the complete graph on \(n(n-1)\) vertices minus the perfect matching of reversal pairs \(x,\tau x\).

Thus \(\tau\) fixes no edge setwise: it preserves sheets, it fixes no vertex, and within a sheet the only pair it could interchange is an absent reversal pair. Label the new edges by \(0,1\) independently on their two-element \(\tau\)-orbits. This extends the binary labeling with \(\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e)\).

Any rainbow path uses at most one edge of color \(\star\). Deleting that edge splits the path into at most two old-edge subpaths; each inherits at most one binary switch and hence has length at most three. Therefore \(L_1(G_n)\le3+1+3=7\).

It remains to show triangle generation of the binary cycle space. Let \(A,B\) be the two sheets. Every new edge \(xy\) in \(G_n\) has a common old neighbor \(z\) in the opposite sheet. Hence its edge \(xy\) is congruent, modulo the triangle \(xyz\), to the two-edge old path \(xzy\). Replacing all new edges this way shows that any binary cycle of \(G_n\), modulo a sum of triangles, lies in the binary cycle space of \(B_n\). Every cycle of the bipartite graph \(B_n\) alternates between sheets. Replace every consecutive two-edge passage \(x-z-y\), with \(x,y\in A\), by the new edge \(xy\); this again changes the cycle by a triangle. The resulting cycle lies entirely in \(G_n[A]\).

Finally, \(G_n[A]\) is a complete graph minus a perfect matching on \(m=n(n-1)\ge20\) vertices. Its binary cycle space is generated by triangles: a chord splits any cycle into shorter cycles, every induced cycle of length at least five has a chord because each vertex has only one nonneighbor, and any induced four-cycle can be coned to an external vertex adjacent to all four cycle vertices (any vertex outside its two full matching pairs works). Thus every cycle in \(G_n[A]\), and therefore every cycle in \(G_n\), is a binary sum of triangles. \(\square\)

**Research consequence.** Neither complete continuation of ordered colors, a triangle through every edge, nor global triangle generation of the entire graph cycle space suffices for a linear one-switch rainbow-path bound. These are sharp negative tests for proposed additions to Article XIII. A useful positive hypothesis must control **the interaction of ordinary edge colors with short cycles and admissible paths**, rather than merely the existence or spanning properties of the short cycles. No candidate sufficient condition or minimality claim is established here.
