## Research note

For every integer r>=1 there is an antipodally odd vertex-balanced binary edge-coloring of Q_(4r) such that: (i) every monochromatic connected component has at most 12^r vertices; (ii) the set of roots of monochromatic antipodal geodesics has cardinality 8^r, a proportion 2^(-r); and (iii) the number of rooted oriented zero-colored antipodal geodesics is (4r)!/3^r.

Proof. In Q_4 (vertex bits x_0,...,x_3), put c_0(x)=x_1, c_1(x)=x_0, c_2(x)=1-x_1, c_3(x)=1-x_0. Each rule ignores its own direction, so this defines an undirected-edge coloring. Complementing all four bits reverses every color, and each vertex has two edges of each color. The zero-colored graph is 2-regular: the four-cycle has vertex set {3,7,11,15}, while the other component is the twelve-cycle
(0,1,9,8,10,14,12,13,5,4,6,2,0),
with vertex integers encoding bits in binary. Among vertices on the twelve-cycle, only {1,2,5,6,9,10,13,14} have their antipodes on that same cycle; the antipodal pairs are (1,14),(2,13),(5,10),(6,9). These pairs are separated by four edges along the twelve-cycle, so each of the eight roots admits precisely one zero-colored antipodal geodesic. No such geodesic starts in the four-cycle. The antipodal map sends zero-colored geodesics to one-colored geodesics, while path reversal makes each root set antipodally invariant. Therefore the one-colored geodesic roots coincide with those eight vertices.

Take r copies of this coloring on disjoint four-dimensional blocks, assigning each edge its factor's color. Balance and antipodal oddness persist, and the zero-colored graph is the Cartesian product of the factor graphs. Each component has cardinality 4^a 12^(r-a) (0<=a<=r), so the maximum is 12^r out of 16^r vertices. Antipodal inversion gives the identical bound for one-colored components. A monochromatic full geodesic projects onto a monochromatic antipodal geodesic in every factor. Conversely, any selection of such factor geodesics, and any shuffle of their orders preserving each internal factor order, forms a monochromatic full geodesic of the product. Consequently the monochromatic-root set is the r-fold Cartesian product of the base eight-element root set and has 8^r elements. Each such root has (4r)!/(4!)^r zero geodesics, hence the total number is 8^r (4r)!/(4!)^r=(4r)!/3^r.

This rules out any dimension-independent positive lower bound on the fraction of roots supporting a monochromatic antipodal geodesic in the balanced antipodally odd class, and any dimension-independent positive lower bound on the fraction occupied by its largest monochromatic component. Thus a Wu--Yang extension or Hall/topological argument requiring a positive density of either roots or components cannot settle NORI1 without further cube incidence structure.

Further properties. Let k be fixed and sample k distinct dimensions uniformly from the 4r available. The probability that some two belong to the same four-dimensional factor is at most 3*binom(k,2)/(4r-1), by a union bound. On the complementary event, the k direction-color functions evaluated at a uniform cube vertex are independent fair bits, since their respective blocks are disjoint. Thus a proportion 1-O(k^2/r) of direction k-tuples have uniform joint color distribution, even while the proportion of successful roots equals 2^(-r).

Likewise, in every square whose two free directions lie in distinct factors, opposite parallel edges have the same color: each direction rule depends only on its own factor. Hence every nonflat square has both directions in one factor. At most 3/(4r-1) of the direction pairs support such a square. The family therefore combines vertex balance, almost-everywhere fixed-order local color independence, asymptotically sparse square curvature, and exponentially sparse successful roots. In particular, these local distribution conditions cannot yield a dimension-independent positive density of monochromatic full-geodesic roots.

**Connected-interaction strengthening (all dimensions n=4r, r>=3).** One may additionally require the direction-dependency interaction graph to be connected while preserving asymptotically vanishing monochromatic-geodesic root density.

Define the interaction graph on directions 0,...,n-1 by joining i to j if there is a vertex x with c_i(x) != c_i(x XOR 2^j), or c_j(x) != c_j(x XOR 2^i). Begin with the r-factor coloring above. Flip all four edge colors of the square with variable directions i=0,j=6 and all other bits zero, and simultaneously flip all four edges of the antipodal square with all other bits one. Denote the resulting coloring by c*. In the source square, i-edge colors were zero and j-edge colors one, so switching all four colors leaves the incident zero degree at each square vertex unchanged; similarly in the antipodal square. The two squares are antipodal mates. Thus c* remains a legal antipodally odd vertex-balanced coloring.

The interaction graph of c* is connected: the changed color rule for direction 0 has support on the two square faces and depends on every direction k not in {0,6}; in particular each k not in {0,1,6} gives a new nonzero Boolean derivative, while k=1 was already a dependency and remains one away from those faces. The changed direction-6 rule depends on direction 2 (since its change is localized by fixing bit 2); hence direction 6 joins direction 2, which joins direction 0. All n directions are therefore in one connected interaction graph. The complete dependence check is immediate from the indicator of the two fixed faces; exhaustive checks in n=8 and n=12 independently confirm the graph is connected.

Let G_0 be the root set of zero-colored antipodal geodesics in the original coloring, with |G_0|=8^r. Let G* be the analogous set after the flips. There are four newly zero-colored edges (two per square). If x belongs to G* minus G_0, every zero geodesic from x to its antipode in c* traverses some newly zero edge. Consider the first such edge e, from v to w along that path. Its preceding path consists entirely of original zero edges, and x belongs to the original zero connected component C(v). Allowing both endpoints of the four newly zero edges, the set of such x is contained in a union of at most eight original zero components. Every such component has cardinality at most 12^r. Hence
|G*| <= 8^r + 8*12^r,
and thus
|G*|/2^(4r) <= 2^(-r) + 8*(3/4)^r -> 0.
Since antipodal inversion followed by path reversal maps zero-geodesic root x to one-geodesic root x, G* is also the full root set of monochromatic antipodal geodesics of either color.

For r>=3 this family is guaranteed to retain a zero full geodesic: in every Q4 factor choose the zero geodesic with vertices 1,9,8,10,14. Concatenate the r factor paths. Every intermediate block state belongs to {1,9,8,10,14}, omitting both 0 and 15. Both changed squares have all untouched blocks fixed entirely at 0 or entirely at 15. There is a third block untouched by the square's two variable directions, so the displayed product geodesic avoids all eight flipped edges, and remains a zero full geodesic in c*.

Furthermore, fixed-order local independence survives: for fixed k, a random k-tuple of directions avoids {0,6} and chooses distinct four-dimensional blocks with probability 1-O(k^2/r); on this event the k corresponding color functions are unchanged independent unbiased bits. The proportion of direction pairs that can support nonflat squares tends to zero: every cross-block square with neither direction in {0,6} is still flat, leaving only O(r) exceptional direction pairs out of binom(4r,2). Thus the coloring family is balanced, antipodally odd, connected in its full dependency interaction graph, mostly locally independent with sparse square curvature, and nevertheless has a vanishing proportion of successful roots.

**General sparse perturbation bound.** For an undirected red subgraph H of Q_n, write G(H) for the set of antipodal full-geodesic roots and C_H(v) for the connected component of v. If H' differs from H by adding a set F^+ of edges and arbitrarily deleting other edges, then
|G(H')| <= |G(H)| + sum_{uv in F^+} (|C_H(u)|+|C_H(v)|).
Indeed a geodesic from a newly successful root must cross its first added edge, whose near endpoint is reachable from its root using only H-edges. This is an unrestricted, all-dimensional stability estimate for root sets under sparse color perturbations, explaining the connected construction above.

**Near-complete direction interaction with vanishing successful-root density (strengthening).** For every integer r>=2, n=4r, there exists a balanced antipodally odd coloring c^dense of Q_n such that the direction interaction graph contains K_n minus a perfect matching (and in the construction equals that graph), a red antipodal geodesic exists, yet
|G(c^dense)|/2^n <= 2^(-r) + 4n*(3/4)^r -> 0.
Thus even minimum interaction degree n-2, diameter two, high edge connectivity, fixed-k almost-independent direction colors and vanishing fraction of nonflat square *faces* do not force a dimension-independent lower density of successful roots.

Construction and proof. Begin with the r-copy Q4 base coloring. For every block b=0,...,r-1 take the two disjoint pairs of direction indices (4b,4b+2) and (4b+1,4b+3), pairing one initially zero direction to one initially one direction. For each pair (i,j) flip all four edges of the face where dimensions i,j are free and every other bit is zero, and also the four edges of its antipodal face where every other bit is one. Different pairs have disjoint edge sets, though faces may share vertices. Each such face initially has two zero i-edges and two one j-edges, or the antipodal complement, and flipping it preserves incident color degree at each vertex. Hence all flips collectively preserve balance and antipodal oddness.

For any direction i, let p(i) be its paired mate. Its new color function is
c_i^dense(x) = c_i(x) XOR T_(i,p(i))(x),
where T_(i,j)(x)=1 precisely when all bits outside {i,j} are zero or all those bits are one. This is independent of bit i and bit j. For every k distinct from i and p(i), the Boolean difference of T_(i,p(i)) in bit k is nonzero at an appropriate vertex; if c_i originally depends on bit k, that dependence persists at vertices away from both flipped faces. Thus c_i^dense depends on every bit k distinct from i and p(i). In the chosen matching c_i originally ignores p(i), so the symmetric interaction graph is K_n minus the matching, with all degrees n-2. A finite exhaustive color/degree/interdependence audit in n=8 and n=12 confirms the formula and degrees (6 and 10).

The matched pair flips introduce four newly red edges per pair. There are n/2 pairs, hence 2n added red edges. The previous sparse perturbation lemma, together with |G_0|=8^r and maximum original red component size 12^r, gives
|G(c^dense)| <= 8^r + 2*(2n)*12^r = 8^r+4n*12^r.
The antipodal oddness makes the blue-geodesic and red-geodesic root sets identical. To confirm nonemptiness in every r>=2, take the original red full geodesic obtained by concatenating the Q4 red path 1,9,8,10,14 in each block. At every vertex of this path each block state lies in {1,9,8,10,14}, avoiding the all-zero and all-one block states. Every changed face has two free dimensions in one block, and forces each other block to be all-zero or all-one; since r>=2 the exhibited full geodesic misses every changed face and remains red.

For a fixed k, a uniformly random set of k directions lies in distinct four-blocks with probability 1-O(k^2/r). On this event the original k direction colors are independent fair bits at a uniform vertex. Each changed direction-color rule differs from its original rule on only 8 of 2^n vertex arguments, so a union bound gives total variation difference at most 8k/2^n. Thus the same almost-everywhere fixed-k independence persists for c^dense.

Finally, the original coloring has nonflat square faces only in O(r) direction pairs out of binom(n,2). Modifying 4n edges changes curvature only in at most 4n(n-1) additional square faces. Among binom(n,2)*2^(n-2) total square faces, this is an exponentially vanishing fraction. Therefore the overall fraction of nonflat square faces is O(1/r)+O(2^(-n)). Note the distinction: many direction *pairs* now admit some nonflat squares, but almost all individual square faces remain flat.

This establishes a robust obstruction to global closure approaches based solely on high interaction-graph connectivity or degree, balance, fixed-size local color independence, sparse face curvature, or a positive geodesic-root density conclusion.
