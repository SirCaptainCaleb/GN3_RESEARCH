## Research note

Let \(V=\{0,1,2,3\}\). For a cube geodesic specified by its root \(x\in\mathbb F_2^4\) and dimension permutation \(\pi=(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)\), denote the \(i\)-th undirected edge by \(e_i(x,\pi)\). For an antipodally even edge coloring \(a:E(Q_4)\to\mathbb F_2\) and a reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament \(b\), define the additively separable four-slot word
\[
L(x,\pi)=(a(e_1)\oplus u,\ a(e_2)\oplus u,\ a(e_3)\oplus v,\ a(e_4)\oplus v),\qquad
u=b(v_1,v_2,v_3),\quad v=b(v_2,v_3,v_4).
\tag{1}
\]
The triple word is padded at both ends. Since \(a(\bar e)=a(e)\) and \(b(q,h,p)=1-b(p,h,q)\), this construction obeys
\[
L(x,\pi^{\rm rev})=\mathbf1-\operatorname{rev}L(x,\pi).
\tag{2}
\]
This is the reversal-parity identity common to both source problems.

**Proposition (four-symbol counterexample).** There are such \(a,b\) for which the switch count of \(L(x,\pi)\) is always \(2\), for all \(2^4\cdot 4!\) root/order flags. Thus the candidate unified one-switch statement for additively separable, reversal-odd signed-chamber words is false.

*Proof.* Let \(a(e)=1\) on edges in dimension \(0\), and zero on the other cube edges. For distinct \(p,h,q\in V\), let \(d\) be the omitted symbol, let \(\varepsilon(p,h,q,d)\) denote the inversion parity of the indicated four-symbol permutation relative to \(0<1<2<3\), and put
\[
b(p,h,q)=\varepsilon(p,h,q,d)\oplus\mathbf1_{\{h=0\}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Exchanging \(p\) and \(q\) changes permutation parity, so \(b\) is reversal odd. Put \(t=\varepsilon(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)\), \(a_i=\mathbf1_{\{v_i=0\}}\). Cyclically moving the first of four symbols to the end changes parity, whence
\[
u=t\oplus a_2,\qquad v=t\oplus1\oplus a_3.
\tag{4}
\]
Substitution into (1) yields
\[
L=(u\oplus a_1,\ u\oplus a_2,\ v\oplus a_3,\ v\oplus a_4).
\]
Its switch-indicator vector is
\[
(s_1,s_2,s_3)=
\bigl(a_1\oplus a_2,\ 
u\oplus v\oplus a_2\oplus a_3,\ 
a_3\oplus a_4\bigr)
=
\bigl(a_1\oplus a_2,\ 1,\ a_3\oplus a_4\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
Precisely one \(a_i\) equals one, so precisely one of \(s_1,s_3\) is one. Therefore \(s_1+s_2+s_3=2\). The coloring is independent of \(x\); hence the calculation holds for every root. \(\square\)

The additive family includes either motivating source class by setting the other layer to zero and assigning odd reversal parity to the nonzero layer. The counterexample instead gives the even edge layer and odd triple layer nonzero values. Its aggregated word has the requisite reversal law, yet no one-switch complete path exists. Accordingly, parity alone and additive superposition of the two natural color-incidence layers cannot support the proposed common selection theorem. This does not refute either original conjecture.
