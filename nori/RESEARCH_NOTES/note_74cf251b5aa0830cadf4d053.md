## Research note

THEOREM (vanishing-density bridges in balanced odd direction compatibility). Fix even n=2m>=6 and pair directions {i,m+i} for i=1,...,m. Write z_j(x)=(-1)^x_j, S(x)=sum_(j=1)^n z_j(x). For each i choose an index r_i outside {i,m+i} and define a Boolean signed function F_i(x)=sign( sum_(j not in {i,m+i}) z_j(x) + (1/2) z_(r_i)(x) ). Since the integral sum inside is even and the tie breaker is +/-1/2, F_i always equals +/-1 and never vanishes. It ignores both bits in its own pair and reverses under the antipodal map, F_i(bar x)=-F_i(x). Define signed edge colors sigma_i=F_i and sigma_(m+i)=-F_i, with red sign +1. Every direction rule ignores its own bit; the coloring is antipodally odd and pointwise balanced.

Let A={1,...,m} and B={m+1,...,2m}. If |S(x)|>=4 then, for every i, sign F_i(x)=sign S(x), because the expression defining F_i differs from S(x) by at most 5/2. Hence for every pair i in A,j in B the event that BOTH incident direction edges are red is contained in {|S(x)|<=2}, including the opposing pair i,m+i where the event is empty. With X uniform on Q_n, define p_ij=Pr_X[sigma_i(X)=sigma_j(X)=+1]. Then every cross pair obeys p_ij <= Pr[|S(X)|<=2] = 2^(-n)*(binom(n,n/2)+2binom(n,n/2-1)) = O(n^(-1/2)). Conversely for i,j in A, p_ij >= Pr[S>=4] = 1/2-O(n^(-1/2)), while for i,j in B, p_ij >= Pr[S<=-4] = 1/2-O(n^(-1/2)).

In particular for every fixed threshold epsilon with 0<epsilon<1/2, all sufficiently large even n admit such balanced odd colorings whose graph of direction pairs witnessed as simultaneously red on at least epsilon fraction of roots consists of two disjoint cliques K_m union K_m. Every Hamilton cycle of the unthresholded pairwise compatibility graph must cross between A and B at least twice, and all its cross transitions have root witness probability O(n^(-1/2)). Thus one cannot strengthen the universal balanced Hamilton-cycle theorem to an ordering where all consecutive direction pairs are simultaneously red on a uniform positive fraction of cube roots.

The construction has arbitrarily many nonlinear direction rules and applies to the counterexample-preserving balanced class. It does not contradict NORI1: the global monochromatic geodesic may depend crucially on the exceptional root states with |S|<=2. It isolates a failure of naive random-root gluing based only on pairwise local transition density; the next goal must transport the root deliberately across near-disjoint clusters of favorable local states.
