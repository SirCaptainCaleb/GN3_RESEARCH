## Research note

This note retains the original short-port amplification proposal and its earlier scope tests. Both amplification versions are now refuted by the single balanced-shortcut construction in note_nori_xi_balanced_shortcut_refutes_port_amplification. That construction satisfies all four hypotheses below, including universal PI_2, while failing P_4. The canonical cube and resource-marked boundary conjectures remain open.

For a pointed binary matroid \((M,p)\), write \(g_p=\min\{|Y|:Y\in\mathcal C^*(M),p\in Y\}\), and \(\mathcal Y_p=\{Y\in\mathcal C^*(M):p\in Y,|Y|=g_p\}\). Say \(\mathrm{PI}_2\) holds if for every binary cycle \(X\) containing \(p\) some port-containing cocircuit \(Y\) (possibly long) meets \(X\) in two elements.

**Refuted conjecture (short-port amplification, original statement).** Suppose \(M\) is binary and:
(i) \(M\setminus p\) is cographic;
(ii) \(Z(M^*\setminus p)\) is generated by circuits of sizes three and four;
(iii) the graph on \(\mathcal Y_p\) joining cocircuits with symmetric difference of size at most six is connected; and
(iv) \(\mathrm{PI}_2\) holds.
Then every binary cycle \(X\) containing \(p\) meets a minimum-size port cocircuit \(Y\in\mathcal Y_p\) in at most four elements. A bolder candidate drops conditions (ii),(iii); it is refuted by the same counterexample. Article XI establishes all four hypotheses for its cube-derived pointed dual \(D_n\), using local star-triangle/square generation, adjacent geodesic exchanges, and Wu--Yang's 2026 nongeodesic antipodal-path theorem.

**Counterexample A (binary and cographic deletion alone fail).** Form the pointed extended lift of the signed cycle \(H=C_5\), whose sole graph cycle has odd monodromy. Its dual \(M\) is \(U_{1,6}\), with \(M\setminus p=U_{1,5}=M(C_5)^*\). The ground set \(X=E(M)\) is a binary cycle; the unique cocircuit is \(Y=E(M)\), and \(|X\cap Y|=6>4\).

**Counterexample B (arbitrarily high oriented-matroid rank and corank three).** The graph \(G_k=C_{10}\square Q_k\) is the tope graph of a direct sum of a simple rank-two oriented matroid on five elements and a Boolean oriented matroid on \(k\) elements. Its oriented-matroid rank is \(k+2\) and its corank is three. Label the ten-cycle vertices by \(j\in\mathbb Z/10\). Antipodality is \((j,z)\mapsto(j+5,\bar z)\). Color a cycle edge from \(j\) to \(j+1\) by \(j\bmod2\); color every cube-factor edge above \(j\) by \(j\bmod2\). Opposite edges receive complementary colors. Every antipodal geodesic uses five successive ten-cycle edges, whose colors alternate; deleting intermediate cube-factor edge colors cannot increase the number of changes, so at least four switches are compulsory. Traversing all cube-factor steps first and then the five cycle steps achieves four. Hence \(\mu=4\) for every \(k\ge0\). Therefore blanket extensions to all antipodal partial cubes or all oriented-matroid tope graphs are false, even for arbitrarily high rank and fixed corank three. One exploratory stronger geometric hypothesis is pairwise crossing of partial-cube coordinate classes in squares; the ten-cycle factors fail it.

**Counterexample C (local cycle generation plus adjacent shortest witnesses fail without \(\mathrm{PI}_2\)).** Let a theta graph have three internally disjoint terminal-to-terminal paths \(P_0,P_1,P_2\) of lengths \(4,2,2\). Give odd monodromy to \(P_0\cup P_1\) and \(P_0\cup P_2\), and even monodromy to the balanced square \(P_1\cup P_2\). Its pointed extended-lift dual \(M\) has cographic deletion, and \(Z(M^*\setminus p)\) is generated by that square. Its two shortest port cocircuits have cardinality seven and symmetric difference four. Choose a turn signature equal to one on all four edges of \(P_0\) and one edge of each \(P_1,P_2\). It represents the stated monodromy, and each shortest port cocircuit intersects the associated cycle \(X\) in six elements. The \(\mathrm{PI}_2\) hypothesis fails here as well.

Caution: Houshan Fu, arXiv:2610.06033 (2026), disproved Oxley's broader intersection-size decrement conjecture with a binary Golay-matroid example. Its quantifiers differ from this pointed minimum-cocircuit problem and do not resolve it. The balanced-shortcut refutation resolves the short-port amplification proposal. Support-controlled geodesic exchange and square-crossing partial-cube candidates remain separate directions.
