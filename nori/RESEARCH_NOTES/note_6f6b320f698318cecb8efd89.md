## Research note

## Profile-critical antipodal edge orbits

For a legal NORI1 coloring c and an attained profile w, call an antipodal orbit O of undirected edges **w-critical** if every full antipodal geodesic realizing w traverses an edge belonging to O.

**Proved obstruction in Q3.** On Q3, take c_0(x)=1+x_2, c_1(x)=x_2, c_2(x)=x_1 and target profile w=(0,0,0). The order (0,2,1) from root (x_0,x_1,x_2)=(0,0,1) is a monochromatic zero full geodesic. Yet every such geodesic uses the direction-0 edge orbit determined by (x_1,x_2)=(0,1) (the antipodal companion has (x_1,x_2)=(1,0)). Proof: c_0=0 forces x_2=1 at the direction-0 step, while c_1=0 forces x_2=0 at the direction-1 step. Thus direction 2 occurs strictly between directions 0 and 1, and the order is either (0,2,1) or (1,2,0). In the first case the condition c_2=0 says x_1=0 before direction 2; in the second it says x_1=0 after direction 1. In either case x_1=0 and x_2=1 when direction 0 is traversed. Therefore this orbit intersects every zero full geodesic.

**Computational finite results in Q4 and Q5.** Exhaustive enumeration over all 2^16=65536 legal Q4 colorings, all 16 roots, all 24 direction orders, all 16 target profiles, and all 16 antipodal edge orbits found zero w-critical orbit instances. A separate 0-1 integer-feasibility search gave infeasibility for every symmetry class of a w-critical orbit in Q5.

**Q5 model and reproducibility.** Fix a hypothetical witness of w and independently complement all direction color rules whose entries in w are one, reducing to a zero-colored path. A cube automorphism maps this witness to the canonical path starting at 0 and traversing dimensions (0,1,2,3,4). Its selected critical orbit has one of five positions along the canonical path; reversal reduces these to k=0,1,2. Use one Boolean variable for each antipodal orbit of undirected edges (40 variables in Q5), indicating the color of its canonical representative. Prescribe zero on the five canonical path edges. For every other rooted full geodesic P avoiding the selected orbit, require at least one direction edge to have color one:
\[
\sum_{e\in P}{\bf1}_{\{c(e)=1\}}\ge1.
\]
Each indicator is either the corresponding Boolean orbit variable or its complement. There are 32*5!=3840 rooted full paths, 480 paths meeting the selected orbit, and hence 3360 avoidance inequalities. Scipy/HiGHS binary integer feasibility returned infeasible in the three cases k=0,1,2 (approximately 0.43, 0.46, 0.47 seconds). The same implementation gives feasible at k=0 and infeasible at k=1 in Q3, agreeing with the obstruction, and infeasible at both k=0,1 in Q4, agreeing with independent full enumeration.

These computations are finite certificates at solver-verification status, rather than a dimension-uniform proof or a machine-independent resolution certificate. The Q6 integer formulation has 96 orbit variables and 43200 avoiding rooted-path constraints; an initial 30-second solve for k=0 reached its time limit without determining feasibility. No conclusion for Q6 follows.

**Conditional mechanism.** If for every n>=4 the w-criticality property is absent whenever w is attained, then a recoloring of one antipodal orbit does not change full-profile support: each attained profile has a witness avoiding that orbit, in either of the adjacent colorings. All legal colorings are joined by such single-orbit recolorings, and the cyclic-reader coloring has full profile support. Therefore universal absence of w-critical orbits would imply unrestricted NORI1. This is a potential proof mechanism and remains open; the finite checks do not establish it.

**A separate unrestricted rooted-projection theorem** has been added to version 68 of the existing full-geodesic profile Subsection: every two-direction projection of the rooted profile set is all four patterns except for an autonomous reciprocal-reader pair, when it consists of two complementary patterns. This theorem follows from adjacent-swap sensitivity and antipodal reversal, and is independently checked by complete enumeration in dimensions two through four.
