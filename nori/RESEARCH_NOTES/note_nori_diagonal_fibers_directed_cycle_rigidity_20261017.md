## Research note

Let n>=2. For a legal antipodally odd cube-edge coloring c, W_x is the family of direction-indexed edge-color profiles of full antipodal geodesics from root x. For z in F2^n define the diagonal root fiber A_z={x:x+z in W_x}.

THEOREM 1 (directed-cycle classification). For each z orient every direction-i edge e from the endpoint s whose i-th bit is c_i(e)+z_i. The orientation is preserved by cube antipodality. A full geodesic rooted at x has profile x+z iff all its edges follow this orientation. Any such directed geodesic x to bar x can be followed by its antipodal copy bar x to x; this is a directed isometric cycle of length 2n, since its direction word is pi,pi and each cyclic segment of length at most n uses different directions. Conversely every vertex of that cycle begins a directed antipodal geodesic of length n. Consequently A_z is a union of vertex sets of such directed antipodal isometric cycles. In particular either A_z is empty or |A_z|>=2n; also every x in A_z has bar x in A_z and at least two A_z vertices at each intermediate Hamming distance k=1,...,n-1 from x.

Every orientation of Q_n preserved by antipodality arises from a legal coloring for the chosen z, by setting c_i(e)=s_i+z_i for its directed source s. This correspondence is reversible and uses no linearity, degree restriction, or connectedness hypothesis.

THEOREM 2 (sharp singleton-cycle realization, all n>=2). Fix an antipodally symmetric isometric 2n-cycle C of Q_n with antipodal halfturn and a signature z. Give C a cyclic directed orientation. Put d(v)=distance_Qn(v,C), constant on antipodal vertex pairs. Rank antipodal vertex pairs by distinct integers r(v)=r(bar v). Orient all other edges from larger d to smaller d, and when d ties from lower r to higher r. No edge joins antipodes for n>=2, so ranks distinguish the endpoints of a tied edge. Since C is isometric it has no chords. Every edge from outside C into C points toward C; lexicographic potential (d,-r) strictly decreases along every other directed edge. Thus C is the only directed cycle. Convert this orientation to a legal coloring as in Theorem 1. Every directed antipodal geodesic doubles to a directed cycle, so A_z=V(C), and every vertex of C begins a directed antipodal n-arc. This attains |A_z|=2n for all dimensions. For n>=3 choose root e_0 and direction order (1,0,2,3,...,n-1). The resulting antipodal isometric cycle avoids 0 and 1. Translating its vertices by z gives A_z of size 2n disjoint from {z,bar z}. This controls one diagonal fiber; it is not a NORI1 counterexample.

THEOREM 3 (disjoint-cycle programming). For any vertex-disjoint family C_1,...,C_t of antipodally symmetric isometric 2n-cycles, direct each cycle, order the cycle components, orient edges between distinct components in increasing component index, and orient all edges outside their union using distance and antipodal-pair rank as above, pointing into the union. The only directed cycles are the prescribed C_j. The legal coloring obtained from this orientation satisfies A_z=union_j V(C_j). For n>=4 the cycle above and its translate by e_1+e_2 are disjoint, giving |A_z|=4n.

THEOREM 4 (empty and full supports). Orient each cube edge by increasing rank of its antipodal vertex-pair; the orientation is antipodally invariant and acyclic, giving A_z empty. For z=0 the legal coloring c_0(x)=x_1, c_1(x)=1+x_0, and c_i(x)=x_0 for i>=2 has A_0=Q_n: for a root x, place every direction j>=2 before direction 0 iff x_j=x_0, and place 1 before 0 iff x_1 differs from x_0. Every direction color then equals its root bit. Flipping the colors of all edges in any prescribed subset of directions moves the full-fiber result to any z.

THEOREM 5 (unique directed 2n-cycle forces strong directed influence). Let D(c) have an arc j->i iff the direction-i edge color depends on vertex bit j. Suppose the associated antipodally invariant orientation O_z has a directed full antipodal geodesic, but no directed cycle shorter than 2n. Then D(c) is strongly connected. Proof: If not, take a nonempty proper source strongly connected component S of D(c). All edge rules c_i for i in S ignore every bit outside S. Thus they restrict to a legal antipodally odd coloring and a common directed orientation on each S-face. Project a directed full geodesic of O_z onto S by deleting its steps outside S: the result is a directed full antipodal S-geodesic. Appending its S-antipodal image gives a directed cycle of length 2|S|<2n inside an S-face, contradiction. A source component of size one is itself impossible because a rule ignoring every other bit would be constant and violate antipodal oddness. Thus D(c) is strongly connected.

The singleton-cycle orientation of Theorem 2 contains just the one directed 2n-cycle C. It therefore has strongly connected directed influence for EVERY n>=2. In particular for all n>=3, legal colorings with strongly connected directed influence can have a diagonal fiber A_z of the minimum 2n vertices while that fiber avoids its label pair {z,bar z}. This disproves attempts to force a self-labeling fiber using strong influence connectivity plus single-fiber cycle structure.

NORI1 EQUIVALENCE. A red monochromatic full geodesic exists iff z belongs to A_z for some z, because at root x=z the required profile x+z equals 0. The theorems do not imply failure of this condition across all z; they specify one chosen signature fiber. Root-rotation profile cycles were previously established in the archive. The new statements characterize entire diagonal fibers as directed cycle unions, prove their sharp realizability, and connect unique-cycle orientations to strong directed influence.

QUANTITATIVE STRENGTHENING OF THEOREM 5. If S is a nonempty proper source strongly connected component of the directed influence graph and O_z has a directed full antipodal geodesic, then O_z contains at least 2^(n-|S|) pairwise vertex-disjoint directed cycles of length 2|S|. Indeed every S-rule ignores outside bits, the projected full geodesic on Q_S yields a directed 2|S|-cycle by antipodal doubling, and an identical cycle occurs in each of the 2^(n-|S|) distinct S-faces. Thus either absence of cycles shorter than 2n or uniqueness of a directed cycle implies that the directed influence graph is strongly connected. The singleton-cycle realization in Theorem 2 satisfies both conditions.

SHARP RADIAL SEPARATION AND EXPONENTIAL CROSS-FIBER MUTATION (n>=3). Fix a legal antipodally odd coloring c. For each label z let A_z={x : x+z is a direction-indexed profile of a full antipodal geodesic from x} and define R_z=min_{x in A_z} HammingDistance(x,z) for nonempty fibers. Theorem A: R_z<=floor((n-1)/2) for every nonempty fiber. Proof: every nonempty A_z contains an antipodally invariant isometric 2n-cycle. When n=2m+1, antipodality gives a vertex distance <=m from z. When n=2m, a cycle with every vertex distance >=m would, by antipodality, have every vertex at distance m, impossible for adjacent cube vertices; so R_z<=m-1. The bound is sharp for EVERY n>=2, with |A_z|=2n and strongly connected directed influence for n>=3.

Construction: for n=2m+1 start at the vertex whose first m bits are one and use direction order (m,0,m+1,1,...,2m-1,m-1,2m), alternating distances m and m+1 from zero. For n=2m start at the vertex with first m bits one and use order (0,m,1,m+1,...,m-1,2m-1), alternating m and m-1 in the first geodesic half; the antipodal half lies at distances m and m+1. Antipodally double the path to make an isometric 2n-cycle C; its minimum weight is r=floor((n-1)/2). Direct C cyclically, and all other edges by first decreasing graph distance to C and then increasing an antipodally invariant unique vertex-pair rank for ties. The only directed cycle is C, since all off-cycle edges strictly decrease the lexicographic potential (distance,-rank), and C has no chords. Give an edge in direction i the binary color equal to the i-bit of its directed source. Antipodal invariance makes c legal. The diagonal fiber A_0 consists of C alone, so size 2n and R_0=r. Translating every vertex by a prescribed z produces the analogous A_z=z+C. By the earlier source-SCC short-cycle theorem, the unique directed 2n-cycle with a directed antipodal half-path forces the directed influence graph to be strongly connected.

Theorem B (robust cross-fiber mutation). For any 0<=k<=floor(n/2), choose k disjoint adjacent-position pairs (i_t,j_t) along the initial half-path of C, and independently exchange directions within any selected subset T. For each original two-edge segment a->a+e_i->d, its alternate corner u=a+e_j lies outside C. Both edges u->a and u->d are forced inward toward C, so swapping the two steps changes precisely the profile bit j; the square shift is Delta=e_j. The 2^k mutated antipodal isometric cycles C_T therefore have 2^k distinct labels z_T=sum_(t in T)e_(j_t), share 2n-2k root vertices, and certify K_(2n-2k, 2^k) in the TRUE root-label incidence graph. Each displayed cycle C_T has Hamming distance from its own label at least r-|T|>=r-k at every vertex. Indeed unchanged vertices v in C satisfy wt(v+z_T)>=r-|T|. Each new corner u=a+e_(j_t) has u+z_T=a+z_(T minus {t}), where a belongs to C, so its distance is >=r-(|T|-1); same for antipodes. Taking k=floor(n/3) yields exponentially many labels 2^floor(n/3) with a common linearly large root set and all displayed cycles linearly far from their own labels (distance at least floor((n-1)/2)-floor(n/3)). NOTE: this distance lower bound concerns the SELECTED cycles C_T, not whole fibers A_(z_T), which can contain additional cycles. Thus the theorem is a method obstruction, not a counterexample to NORI1.
