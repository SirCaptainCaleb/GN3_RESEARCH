## Research note

Using the team's rank-at-most-four all-dimensional peeling theorem and its minimal-counterexample column compression, a rank-five minimal counterexample has at most sixteen labels and the column quotients belong to the fifteen nonzero vectors of F2^4, with at most one duplicated quotient class. The sixteen-label pattern is impossible by xor. The cases of fifteen and fourteen labels reduce to an eight-label affine coset whose full column xor vanishes, followed by rank-four peeling on the residual quotient hyperplane. In the thirteen-label case the missing quotient classes q,r,s are independent, the duplicated class equals q+r+s, and the unique hyperplane containing these four classes leaves a complete eight-point affine coset. When that coset sums to zero, delete it; otherwise delete the five remaining hyperplane labels (verified by complete enumeration of 8192 restricted row-factor assignments), leaving an odd-sum eight-point coset. Two finite Boolean formulations using system Z3 report UNSAT for counterexamples to the following local assertions: an eight-point affine coset with zero full-column sum can all be legally peeled, and one with odd full-column sum has a legal peel to a nonsingular principal core. Both are computational findings without independently checked UNSAT logs. Conditional on their certification, every rank-five minimal counterexample has at most twelve labels. At twelve labels, the four missing quotient classes form a basis and their xor is the duplicated class; every quotient hyperplane has a nonzero column xor outside, blocking this hyperplane reduction. Full derivation and test program: nori_rank5_projective_reduction.md and nori_rank5_projective_certificates.py. This is not a closure theorem for arbitrary rank or arbitrary nonlinear paired-flat colorings.
