## Research note

**Proposition 1 (two-step corner coloring defeats an incidence-free flag conjecture).** Let \(n\ge5\) be odd. Color each nonbacktracking directed two-edge cube walk \(u\to v\to w\) by
\[
a(u,v,w)=\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\pmod2.
\]
This coloring is odd under simultaneous cube antipodality: \(a(\bar u,\bar v,\bar w)=1-a(u,v,w)\). Along every full antipodal geodesic of \(Q_n\), its \(n-1\) consecutive two-edge windows have middle vertices at consecutive path positions \(1,\ldots,n-1\). Each cube edge reverses vertex parity, so the corresponding word alternates and has \(n-2\ge3\) switches.

Yet this coloring does **not** descend to the projective face incidence \(\Xi_2\). In any cube square spanned by distinct dimensions \(i,j\), the two walks
\[
x\to x\oplus e_i\to x\oplus e_i\oplus e_j,
\qquad
x\oplus e_j\to x\oplus e_i\oplus e_j\to x\oplus e_i
\]
have the same supporting square and the same ordered projective direction lines \(([e_i],[e_j])\); their middle vertices differ by \(e_j\), so their colors are opposite. Consequently no coloring of \(\mathscr I_2(Q_n)\) produces this example.

This refutes a proposed one-switch principle based only on full face flags, a complete set of adjacent flag exchanges, an odd involution on arbitrary directed local walks, and observation length at most two. The *projective face descent* is a substantive additional property, satisfied by the original cube-edge and simplex-turn colorings.

**Proposition 2 (not every simple polytope has a combinatorial flag antipode).** Let \(P=\Delta_2\times[0,1]\) be the triangular prism. The barycentric subdivision of its boundary has no fixed-point-free simplicial involution.

*Proof.* A vertex of this subdivision is a nonempty proper face of \(P\), with graph degree equal to the number of comparable proper faces. A prism vertex is incident with three edges and three facets, giving subdivision degree six. A prism edge has two endpoints and two incident facets, giving degree four. A triangular facet has three vertices and three edges, giving degree six. Each rectangular facet has four vertices and four edges, giving degree eight. Thus there are precisely three subdivision vertices of degree eight, one per rectangular facet. Every simplicial automorphism preserves vertex degree, so an involution on the subdivision must fix at least one of these three vertices. Such a fixed vertex is a fixed point of the induced geometric action. \(\square\)

Although the flag complex of every convex \(d\)-polytope is a sphere, a free simplicial opposition requires additional combinatorial structure. The cube has one from central inversion; the simplex has one from complementary proper faces. These distinct mechanisms are both admitted by the opposed projective-window formulation.
