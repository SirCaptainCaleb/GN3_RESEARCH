## Research note

Let \(xy\) be a red edge in dimension \(i\), and let \(\mathcal K_x,\mathcal K_y\) be the red Freudenthal prefix complexes of Article XIV. Let
\[
C_x=\mathcal K_x\cap H_{i,x_i},\qquad
C_y=\mathcal K_y\cap H_{i,y_i}
\]
be their restrictions to the opposite cube facets perpendicular to dimension \(i\). The rooted Freudenthal triangulations \(\mathcal T_x,\mathcal T_y\) agree on either facet, because their only difference is the orientation of dimension \(i\).

**Theorem (contractible common bridge).** Both \(C_x,C_y\) are nonempty contractible simplicial cones, with apexes \(x,y\), respectively. They satisfy
\[
C_x\subseteq\mathcal K_y,\qquad C_y\subseteq\mathcal K_x.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{B_{xy}=C_x\cup[x,y]\cup C_y\ \subseteq\mathcal K_x\cap\mathcal K_y}
\]
is a canonical contractible polyhedral subcomplex of their geometric intersection. All the path witnesses defining these inclusions use each dimension at most once.

*Proof.* Restricting \(\mathcal K_x\) to the facet containing its apex retains all red geodesic prefixes avoiding dimension \(i\), and is coned at \(x\); similarly for \(C_y\). Given any red geodesic prefix from \(x\) within its facet, prepend the red edge \(y\to x\). Its dimension set is \(\{i\}\) disjoint from the prefix's support, so every simplex of \(C_x\) also lies in \(\mathcal K_y\). The symmetric argument gives the other inclusion. The red edge \([x,y]\) is a simplex of both prefix complexes. Since \(C_x,C_y\) lie in opposite disjoint facets, their union joined by \([x,y]\) is a tree-of-spaces consisting of two contractible cones attached at their apexes to the ends of an interval. It deformation retracts to that interval and is contractible. \(\square\)

For a blue edge \(xy\), its antipodal edge \(\bar x\bar y\) is red, and the corresponding common bridge occurs between the second entries \(\mathcal K_{\bar x},\mathcal K_{\bar y}\) of the carrier-valued rook pairs. Thus Wu–Yang's equality of one component label across each colored edge is replaced by a **canonically contractible common overlap** equipped with rank-preserving facet transport.

**Boundary of the method.** This does not imply that \(\mathcal K_x\cap\mathcal K_y\) as a whole is contractible; nor does it establish coherent acyclic carriers on every cubical face. The next proof obligation is precisely to construct compatible higher-dimensional bridges for cubical squares and their higher analogues, then obtain a target dimension bound for an equivariant chain map.
