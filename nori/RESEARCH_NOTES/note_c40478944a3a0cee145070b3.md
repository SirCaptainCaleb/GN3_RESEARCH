# Rank-simple path certificates and the obstruction to component quotients

**Lemma 1 (rank tests for path simplicity).** Let \(x_0,\ldots,x_m\) be a walk in \(Q_n\), and let \(d_i\) be the dimension traversed at its \(i\)-th step. Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}\{e_{d_1},\ldots,e_{d_m}\}
=|\{d_1,\ldots,d_m\}|.
\]
Consequently the walk is dimension-simple if and only if its move vectors are independent. If \(m=n\), this means that all dimensions occur once and its endpoint is \(\bar x_0\).

Let \(v_0,\ldots,v_m\) be a walk in the complete graph on \(V\), with distinct consecutive vertices, and put \(r_i=e_{v_{i-1}}-e_{v_i}\in W_V\). Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}\{r_1,\ldots,r_m\}
=|\{v_0,\ldots,v_m\}|-1.
\]
The roots are independent if and only if the vertex word has no repetitions; if \(m=|V|-1\), this is equivalent to a Hamilton ordering. The alternating-rook encoding uses these incidence roots with changing signs, so it has the same rank criterion.

*Proof.* Distinct standard basis vectors are independent, and a repeated dimension duplicates its column. The unoriented support graph traced by the boundary walk is connected, and the incidence matrix of a connected graph on \(t\) used vertices has rank \(t-1\). Independence of \(m\) columns therefore holds if and only if \(t=m+1\), equivalent to absence of repeated vertices. Negating any root does not affect rank. \(\square\)

**Lemma 2 (support-sensitive concatenation).** Concatenating two dimension-simple cube paths at a shared endpoint yields another dimension-simple path precisely when their used dimension sets are disjoint. Concatenating two vertex-simple walks on \(V\) at a shared endpoint yields another vertex-simple walk precisely when their vertex supports meet only at that endpoint. In both cases this is equivalent to rank additivity of the two move families.

*Proof.* For the cube, the standard basis representation makes rank additive precisely for disjoint sets of dimensions. For the boundary walks, disjointness apart from the junction gives a connected path on \(m_1+m_2+1\) distinct vertices. An additional shared vertex introduces a cycle or a duplicated edge and makes the incidence-column rank smaller than \(m_1+m_2\). \(\square\)

**Proposition 3 (geodesic reachability is not transitive even under antipodal oddness).** In \(Q_3\), prescribe red edges
\[
000-100,\quad100-110,\quad110-010
\]
and the blue edge \(000-010\). These four edge constraints belong to distinct antipodal edge orbits and therefore extend to a full antipodally odd coloring by assigning complementary colors to antipodal edges and arbitrarily completing the remaining orbits. There is a red geodesic \(000\to100\to110\) and a red geodesic \(110\to010\), while the sole geodesic \(000\to010\) is blue. Hence red geodesic reachability does not define equivalence classes. The Wu–Yang construction using red connected-component pairs cannot be reused by simply substituting geodesic-component labels.

**Proposition 4 (full rank plus complement-reversal parity does not force a low-switch certificate).** Suppose a nonempty collection \(\mathcal F\) of complete full-rank path certificates carries a fixed-point-free involution \(R\). For any \(L\ge3\) there exists a word assignment \(w:\mathcal F\to\{0,1\}^L\) satisfying
\[
w(Rf)=\mathbf1-\operatorname{rev}w(f)
\]
for all \(f\), such that every \(w(f)\) has \(L-1\) switches. Assign an alternating word \(a\) to one member of every \(R\)-pair and the complementary reversed word to its partner. Both alternate. \(\square\)

**Implications for Article XIV.** Wu and Yang's Freudenthal simplices are already monotone and dimension-simple in the source cube. The step that loses dimension support is the reduction of arbitrary monochromatic connectivity to connected-component indices, preceding the chain map. In the two problems under study, an admissible certificate must retain the rank of its move family through every proposed path concatenation. Color words must also descend to shared local incidences: cube edges for NORI1, and overlapping directed length-two vertex walks for reversal-odd boundary 3-tournaments. Rank and parity conditions on complete words alone permit the obstruction in Proposition 4. A successful common chain obstruction must encode both local incidence and rank-sensitive support; no sufficiency assertion is made here.
