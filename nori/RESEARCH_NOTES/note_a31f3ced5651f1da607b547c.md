## Research note

**Theorem (cyclic-word criterion for boundary one-switch Hamilton paths).** Let \(b(a,b,c)\in\mathbb F_2\) be any coloring of ordered triples of distinct vertices in an n-set, with n>=4 (the reversal-odd condition is not needed for the algebraic identity). For a *cyclic order* \(\pi=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\), index positions modulo n and define the cyclic triple-color word
\[
t_i=b(v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}),\qquad d_i=t_i\oplus t_{i+1}\in\{0,1\},\qquad D(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^n d_i.
\]
The circular switch count \(D(\pi)\) is even. A Hamilton tight path obtained by cutting the cyclic order at one vertex has a binary word of n−2 consecutive values of t, and hence its switch count is the sum of the corresponding n−3 consecutive values of d. Equivalently, for the best choice among all n rotations of this cyclic order,
\[
\boxed{\min_{\text{rotation}}\mathrm{sw}=\ D(\pi)-\max_{j\in[n]}(d_j+d_{j+1}+d_{j+2}).}
\]
The omitted part is three consecutive *comparison positions*: two boundary comparisons and the one separating the two omitted triple labels. This formula is valid without any assumption on how ordinary edge colors transform under the graph vertex involution.

**Corollary (equivalent circular-variation reformulation).** A reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament admits a one-switch Hamilton tight path if and only if some cyclic ordering \(\pi\) has either (i) \(D(\pi)\le2\), or (ii) \(D(\pi)=4\) and three consecutive comparison bits d are all 1. Indeed D is even, and the displayed formula is at most 1 precisely in those cases. This is a full-strength equivalence, rather than a weaker long-path conclusion. The root-free boundary problem is thereby reduced to finding a circular ordering with small and, in the four-switch case, suitably clustered transition set.

**Comparison with NORI1 (known cyclic rerooting lemma in Article IX).** For a full antipodal cube geodesic whose n edge bits are \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), iterated root slide yields the *antiperiodic* extension \(w_{i+n}=1-w_i\). Its n periodic comparison bits \(d_i=w_i\oplus w_{i+1}\) satisfy an odd total \(D=\sum_{i=1}^nd_i\), because \(w_{n+1}=1-w_1\). Every full n-edge geodesic in this cyclic root-slide orbit omits just one of these n comparison positions, so the minimum switch count in the orbit is \(D-1\). In particular a monochromatic full geodesic exists within the orbit iff \(D=1\). This is Article IX's previously proved lemma, not a new claim. The common view is a *cyclic variation minimization* for overlapping complete paths. The cube's label circle has antiperiodic monodromy and n observed edges; the boundary circle is periodic and has n−2 observed triples. The root-changing mechanism in the cube and the root-independent triple rule in the boundary problem remain different, so this reformulation alone does not prove a unifying selection theorem.

**Local exchange observation.** Swapping adjacent vertices in a cyclic ordering alters at most four consecutive triple-color positions t_i. Therefore D can change by at most four and only by an even integer. This is a bounded-support variation functional on the permutation-exchange graph. It invites a discrete-topological minimax or parity argument using both the color-transition set and the ordinary Hamiltonian order, but no such argument is proved here.
