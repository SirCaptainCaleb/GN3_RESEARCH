## Research note

Let \(n\ge5\), and hypothetically assume no monochromatic full antipodal geodesic in an antipodally odd cube-edge coloring. The signed second-switch labeling gives an equivariant map on the dual Coxeter 1-skeleton into \(S^{n-3}\). Write \(X\cong S^{n-1}\) for the dual type-\(B_n\) complex, \(X_{\mathrm{good}}\) for the two-end-stable subcomplex of Article XIV, consisting of dual cells of parabolic type \(J\) with either \(1\notin J\) or \(0,n-1\notin J\).

**Theorem (localization of the primary obstruction).** There exists an equivariant extension of the prescribed 1-skeleton map to
\[
X_{\mathrm{good}}\cup X^{(n-3)}
\]
whose restriction to \(X_{\mathrm{good}}\) lies in the nested contractible carriers of the two-end localization theorem. For *every* such equivariant extension, the attaching map of at least one **bad** \((n-2)\)-cell \(D=gW_J\), with
\[
|J|=n-2,\qquad 1\in J,\quad(0\in J\text{ or }n-1\in J),
\]
has nonzero degree as a map
\[
\partial D\cong S^{n-3}\longrightarrow S^{n-3}.
\]

*Proof.* Fill the good cells via their nested contractible carriers, as in the previous subsection. For a bad cell of dimension \(j\le n-3\), its attaching sphere is \(S^{j-1}\); since \(\pi_{j-1}(S^{n-3})=0\) for \(j-1\le n-4\), its boundary map extends. Do this inductively over free antipodal cell pairs, preserving the prescribed good-cell fillings. This gives the stated partial extension.

Every good cell in dimension \(n-2\) was already filled, so its attaching-map degree vanishes. Suppose all bad \((n-2)\)-cell degrees also vanished. Then all boundary maps of the \((n-2)\)-cells would be nullhomotopic, allowing an equivariant extension over the entire \((n-2)\)-skeleton \(X^{(n-2)}\). This is impossible: after taking antipodal quotients, there would be a map
\[
X^{(n-2)}/(\pm1)\longrightarrow\mathbb RP^{n-3}
\]
pulling back the canonical degree-one \(\mathbb F_2\)-class \(u\) to the first Stiefel-Whitney class of the antipodal double covering. But \(u^{n-2}=0\) on \(\mathbb RP^{n-3}\), whereas its pullback has nonzero \((n-2)\)-nd power on \(X^{(n-2)}/(\pm1)\), the corresponding skeleton of \(\mathbb RP^{n-1}\). Contradiction. Thus some bad cell has nonzero attaching-map degree. \(\square\)

**Interpretation.** This is a topological localization theorem, not a proof of NORI1. It says that all the unavoidable primary equivariant obstruction can be concentrated among cells varying the first two dimensions together with the root or the final edge. A full Wu–Yang-style contradiction would follow from a separate **color-sensitive rank/cancellation theorem forcing zero degree on all these remaining bad cells**. Pure topology cannot supply that additional vanishing, since the preceding theorem forces at least one nonzero degree for every attempted low-skeleton extension.

One caveat: the theorem uses the known nontriviality of powers of the generator in the cohomology of the real projective space and of its \((n-2)\)-skeleton. The result therefore identifies the location and algebraic nature of the remaining obstruction rather than constructing its cancelling chain.
