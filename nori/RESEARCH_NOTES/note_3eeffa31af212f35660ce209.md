## Research note

**Theorem (one-sided maximal-clique incidence rigidity).** Let \(M=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}U_{r_j,m_j}\) have nontrivial uniform summands \(1\le r_j<m_j\). Let \(B=\Gamma(M)=\square_jJ(m_j,r_j)\), and let \(I(B)\) be the bipartite incidence graph between vertices of \(B\) and maximum-cardinality maximal cliques of \(B\). Suppose \(I(B)\) is connected, one entire bipartition side has degree two, and suppressing that side gives a simple graph \(R\) that is itself a matroid basis graph. Then either (i) \(M=\bigoplus^tU_{1,2}\), \(R=Q_t\), or (ii) \(M=U_{2,m}\) or \(U_{m-2,m}\), \(m\ge5\), \(R=K_m\), up to loops and coloops.

*Proof.* The maximal cliques of \(J(m,r)\) are top cliques of order \(m-r+1\) and bottom cliques of order \(r+1\). Product cliques lie within one Johnson factor. Put \(s=\max_j\{m_j-r_j+1,r_j+1\}\), so every clique vertex of \(I(B)\) has degree \(s\). If \(s=2\), each factor is \(U_{1,2}\), and \(I(B)\) subdivides the edges of the cube \(Q_t\). Suppose \(s\ge3\). The degree-two vertices must be on the basis side. Each basis belongs to
\[
h=\sum_j\bigl(r_j\mathbf1_{\{m_j-r_j+1=s\}}+(m_j-r_j)\mathbf1_{\{r_j+1=s\}}\bigr)
\]
maximum cliques, so \(h=2\). Connectedness forces each nontrivial factor to contribute: an omitted factor would split the incidence graph into fibers indexed by its basis choices. Either one factor contributes two, or two factors contribute one each. In the first case, attaining the maximum only by top cliques requires \(r_j=2\), and attaining it only by bottom cliques requires \(m_j-r_j=2\); attaining both would contribute \(m_j\) and is impossible for \(s\ge3\). The inequality separating the two clique sizes yields \(m_j\ge5\), and suppression recovers \(K_{m_j}\). In the second case, both factors are \(U_{1,s}\) or \(U_{s-1,s}\), so \(B=K_s\square K_s\) and \(R=K_{s,s}\). This cannot be a matroid basis graph: every bipartite matroid basis graph is a hypercube, while two same-side vertices of \(K_{s,s}\) have \(s\ge3\) common neighbors rather than the two common neighbors of any distance-two pair in a hypercube. \(\square\)

The exceptional rank-two graph \(J(4,2)=L(K_4)\) has tied top and bottom maximum cliques and four incidences per basis, so its line-graph root is accessible by Whitney inversion rather than this one-sided incidence construction.

**Consequence.** The specific maximum-clique incidence root method, when required to yield another matroid basis graph, captures essentially the two original partition-matroid model shapes and no intermediate partition-matroid family. The spectral-star requirement cannot enlarge this class. A useful broader conjecture should instead place root graphs and cycle-dominating resource matroids among its primary objects, or use a different intrinsic reconstruction.
