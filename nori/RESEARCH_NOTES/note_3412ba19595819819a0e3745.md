## Research note

Let \(n\ge5\). The cube basis graph \(B(\bigoplus_{i=1}^nU_{1,2})=Q_n\) admits an intrinsically defined, fixed-point-free involution on bases, whereas the Johnson basis graph \(B(U_{2,n})=J(n,2)\) admits **no nonidentity automorphism-equivariant self-map of its basis set**. Thus a common theory that insists on a nontrivial vertex-level polarity obtained canonically from the underlying ordinary matroid cannot include both systems.

**Proof.** In \(\bigoplus U_{1,2}\), the parallel classes are canonically the \(n\) two-element summands. The automorphism group is \(C_2^n\rtimes S_n\). Its center (for \(n\ge3\)) is generated by simultaneous exchange of both members in every parallel class. On bases this is the fixed-point-free complement map \(B\mapsto E\setminus B\), and on the basis graph it is the cubical antipode.

For \(U_{2,n}\), every permutation of the \(n\) ground elements is an automorphism. An equivariant self-map \(F:\binom{[n]}2\to\binom{[n]}2\) must map \(\{1,2\}\) to a pair fixed by its stabilizer \(S_{\{1,2\}}\times S_{\{3,\dots,n\}}\cong S_2\times S_{n-2}\). Because \(n-2\ge3\), the only such pair is \(\{1,2\}\). Transitivity forces \(F\) to be the identity everywhere. In particular, there is no canonical nontrivial involution on the bases, whether or not one additionally requires it to be a graph automorphism. (At \(n=4\), the complement of a two-set gives the exceptional equivariant involution.)

The resource geometry nevertheless *is* recoverable intrinsically: the \(n\) coordinate directions of \(Q_n\) are the equivalence classes of edges generated by opposite sides of induced squares; the \(n\) labels of \(J(n,2)\) are its maximal cliques of order \(n-1\), namely the stars \(\{\{a,b\}:b\ne a\}\). These are distinguished from its other maximal cliques (triangles) since \(n-1\ge4\). A Johnson basis \(\{a,b\}\) is the intersection of the stars labeled \(a\) and \(b\). Accordingly the alternating-port property and freshness of the decoded symbols are recoverable from the basis graph, but no vertex antipode is.

The boundary coloring has a *different canonical polarity*: reversal on **directed basis exchanges** \((B,C)\leftrightarrow(C,B)\), freely exchanging both arrows on each edge of \(J(n,2)\). It does not arise from an automorphism of the basis set. Conversely, NORI1 imposes opposite colors on cubically antipodal *undirected* exchange edges. These are distinct categorical levels, and any intrinsically defined unified object must admit a polarization of exchange flags/arrows rather than require a matroid-functorial involution on bases.

There is also a local algebraic obstruction to using a single unlifted star-turn coboundary. At a cube vertex, the differences \(d_{ij}=c(e_i)+c(e_j)\) among incident edge colors satisfy \(d_{ij}+d_{jk}+d_{ki}=0\) on every local triangle of the line graph. At a fixed symbol \(b\) of \(U_{2,n}\), all edges of the star clique \(K_{n-1}\subset J(n,2)\) can be oriented independently by the reversal-odd values \(b(a,b,c)\), including a cyclic orientation of any triangle. Thus no triangle-flatness axiom imposed directly on the Johnson basis graph can include all ordinary boundary tournaments. The pointed signed-cover line-graph lift restores a common cut-cochain description only at a higher level; the resource-simple path language must still be retained.

**Scope.** This excludes a canonical *basis-vertex* polarity and a common unlifted local-cut axiom. It does not preclude a theorem formulated for an ordinary matroid together with arbitrary anti-equivariant exchange-arrow data, nor a common colored flag/exchange geometry. It supplies a precise necessary design constraint, not a common path-existence theorem.
