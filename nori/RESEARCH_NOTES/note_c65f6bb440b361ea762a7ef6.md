## Research note

**Theorem (majority guard-pair construction).** Let c be any legal antipodally odd binary edge coloring of Q_n. Introduce bits u,v and define on Q_(n+2):
g_u(x,u,v)=1-v, g_v(x,u,v)=u, and g_i(x,u,v)=Maj(1-u,v,c_i(x)) for i in [n], where Maj is the three-input majority Boolean function. Then g is legal and antipodally odd.

Its red graph is the disjoint union of an isolated all-red Q_n on layer (u,v)=(1,0), and two copies of the red graph R(c) on layers 00,11 with each matching pair (x,00),(x,11) connected by a subdivided two-edge link through (x,01). At 01 all old directions are blue and both guard edges red; at 10 all old directions are red and both guard edges blue. At 00 and 11 the old-direction colors equal c, and just one guard edge is red.

Therefore, for s,t in {0,1}, the red graph metric obeys
d_g((x,s,s),(y,t,t))=d_c(x,y)+2[s!=t]
(infinite distance permitted). A vertex whose guard bits differ is red-disconnected from its antipode.

Every red full antipodal geodesic starts at equal guard bits, and the two guard directions must occur consecutively: order v,u when the initial bits are 00; order u,v when they are 11. Between the two guard moves the state is 01, where all old directions are blue. Deleting the guard pair gives a red full geodesic of c. Conversely, insert the ordered guard pair in any of n+1 gaps of a red full geodesic of c, and choose either of the two equal initial guard states. These operations are inverse. In particular, letting P(c) count unoriented red full antipodal geodesics and S(c) their possible initial vertices,
P(g)=2(n+1)P(c), S(g)=S(c) x {00,11}.
The coloring g has a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic iff c does. The shortest red antipodal-path surplus (min_x d_c(x,bar x)-n)/2 is unchanged by this operation.

The isolated all-red Q_n gives a red direction-simple path of length n=(n+2)-2 whose two unused guard directions cannot extend it at either endpoint. This construction applies to arbitrary nonlinear c and obstructs a universal augmentation claim based only on a monochromatic path missing two directions.
