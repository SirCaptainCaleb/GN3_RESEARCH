## Research note

Let \(X_n\) denote the order complex of all oriented nonempty cube intervals, with order given by support-disjoint interval containment. Let \(K=K_0\cup K_1\) be the union of the monochromatic geodesic endpoint-poset complexes inside \(X_n\). For an oriented interval \(q\), define the monochromatic subinterval carrier \(\mathcal C(q)\subseteq K\) to consist of all simplices whose endpoint-pair vertices are directed subintervals of \(q\), using the uncolored interval order. These carriers are nested under subinterval containment. A rank-one interval \(p\) has the singleton carrier \(\mathcal C(p)=\{p\}\).

**Theorem (universal obstruction to face-local transfer).** For every antipodally odd coloring of \(Q_n\), \(n\ge2\), there is no continuous map \(f:|X_n|\to|K|\) with
\[
f(|\sigma|)\subseteq|\mathcal C(q)|
\tag{1}
\]
for every simplex \(\sigma\) of \(X_n\) whose largest interval vertex is \(q\). The obstruction occurs already on an edge of \(X_n\) incident with a rank-two interval. No equivariance assumption is needed.

*Proof.* For a rank-two oriented interval \(q\), the lower interval consists of its four directed square-boundary edges and \(q\) itself. Each of the four rank-one intervals belongs to its carrier \(\mathcal C(q)\). The vertex \(q\) belongs to \(K\) if and only if one of the two 2-step paths through the square is monochromatic. If it belongs, its neighbors in the carrier are precisely those boundary edges participating in a monochromatic two-step path. There are no other carrier vertices or edges.

For any mixed-color square, one can choose a diagonal orientation \(q\) with disconnected carrier. The cyclic square-edge patterns, up to symmetry and simultaneous complementation, are \(0001,0011,0101\). For \(0001\), every diagonal orientation has at least one boundary edge separated from the rank-two vertex's component. For \(0011\), select the diagonal whose two routes both have one switch; its carrier comprises four isolated vertices. For \(0101\), both paths across either diagonal have one switch, giving the same four isolated vertices. Thus in each mixed square at least one rank-two directed interval has disconnected carrier.

If a map \(f\) satisfies (1), then \(f(p)=p\) for every rank-one interval, because its carrier is a singleton. Every rank-one face \(p\prec q\) gives an edge \([p,q]\) of \(X_n\), whose image must connect \(p\) to \(f(q)\) inside the carrier \(\mathcal C(q)\). Therefore all four rank-one faces of \(q\) must lie in one connected component of \(\mathcal C(q)\), contradicting the mixed-square classification.

Finally, every antipodally odd coloring has a mixed square: if each square were monochromatic, each pair of incident edges would share one color, and connectivity of the line graph of \(Q_n\) would force the entire cube edge coloring to be constant, violating oddness. Hence every legal coloring has the stated obstruction. \(\square\)

**Scope.** This is a universal negative theorem about the strict face-local carrier condition (1), including legal colorings that possess monochromatic full geodesics. It complements Article XIV's rank-two zero-switch hole classification. It does not preclude maps defined using larger overlapping carriers, nonlocal root transport, or a switch-filtered intermediate complex. In particular it leaves intact the equivariant sphere map of the new two-color endpoint-intersection subsection: that map goes *out of* \(K\), whereas the obstructed map would go *into* \(K\).
