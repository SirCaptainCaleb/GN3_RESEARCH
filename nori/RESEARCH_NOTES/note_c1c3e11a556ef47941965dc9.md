## Research note

**Proposition (sharp obstruction to locality across all type-B chamber walls).** Let \(n\ge3\), let \(x\in\mathbb F_2^n\), and let \(\pi=(i,d_2,\ldots,d_n)\). The two full-geodesic flags \((x,\pi)\) and \((x\oplus e_i,\pi)\) correspond to adjacent chambers of the type-\(B_n\) reflection arrangement, separated by the wall where the signed component \(z_i\) vanishes (the absolute values are ordered with \(i\) first). Their first geodesic edges coincide as undirected edges. For any prescribed common first-edge bit and any two independently prescribed binary suffixes of length \(n-1\), an antipodally odd cube-edge coloring realizes those two complete geodesic color words.

*Proof.* The first step of either geodesic traverses the same undirected \(i\)-edge. For each \(k\ge2\), the \(k\)-th edges of the two geodesics are parallel in dimension \(d_k\) and their initial vertices differ by \(e_i\). Their edge orbits under vertex complementation are distinct because, besides dimensions \(i,d_k\), at least one other dimension exists and has the same value in both edges, whereas antipodal parallel edges have opposite values in every dimension other than their own. Edges belonging to different positions \(k\) have different dimensions and hence different antipodal edge orbits. None of those suffix orbits equals the first edge's orbit, which is in direction \(i\). Therefore the common first edge and all \(2(n-1)\) suffix edges belong to different orbits of undirected edges under antipodal complementation. Color these representatives as prescribed, give their antipodal mates complementary bits, and extend the coloring arbitrarily on all remaining orbits. \(\square\)

In particular one of these adjacent type-B chambers can have a monochromatic full-geodesic word while the other has an alternating word. Thus an imposed bounded-window color-change rule under *every* Coxeter chamber adjacency would exclude unrestricted NORI1. By comparison, an adjacent transposition in the type-A Hamilton-order chamber graph alters at most four consecutive triple labels. For NORI1, the root-slide move \((x,\pi)\mapsto(x\oplus e_{\pi_1},\operatorname{rot}\pi)\), which also changes the permutation, gives the appropriate local overlap transport: it preserves the remaining \(n-1\) edges and appends the complement of the removed first edge.
