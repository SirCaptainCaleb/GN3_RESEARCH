## Research note

Fix any tree \(T\) on an even number \(m\ge2\) of coordinates, and choose a vertex \(v\). Define affine undirected-edge colorings
\[
d_i(x)=\bigoplus_{j:ij\in E(T)}x_j,\qquad
e_i(x)=d_i(x)\oplus[i=v].
\]
For a spanning geodesic from root \(x\), the sum mod two of its edge colors equals
\[
\bigoplus_i d_i(x)\oplus |E(T)|,
\]
independent of its direction order: each interaction edge contributes once. The tree indegree-parity lemma in Article X shows a monochromatic spanning geodesic exists from \(x\) if and only if this sum is zero (as \(m\) is even). Thus, writing \(\ell_T(x)=\bigoplus_j(\deg_T(j)\bmod2)x_j\),
\[
E_0(d)=\{x:\ell_T(x)=|E(T)|\bmod2\},\qquad
E_0(e)=\{x:\ell_T(x)=|E(T)|\oplus1\bmod2\}.
\]
These formulae apply to terminals as well as roots because \(\ell_T(\bar x)=\ell_T(x)\), since \(\sum_j\deg_T(j)=2|E(T)|\) is even. Therefore \(E_0(d)\cap E_0(e)=\varnothing\), and the sets partition \(Q_m\). Nevertheless Article X's rooted tree theorem gives \(E_1(d)=E_1(e)=Q_m\).

Already \(T=1-2-3-4\) provides disjoint monochromatic terminal sets, even though the two colorings differ by recoloring only one direction. Hence an endpoint intersection theorem at switch threshold zero is false for independently colored adjacent codimension-two faces. The one-switch threshold remains fully viable for this family. This observation does not obstruct the target two-switch endpoint coincidence.
