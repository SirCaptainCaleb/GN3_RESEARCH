# Infinite square-generated counterexamples with bounded one-switch rainbow paths

**Theorem.** For each \(n\ge7\), a connected finite simple graph \(G_n\) admits edge coloring \(\lambda\), binary edge labeling \(\chi\), and a fixed-point-free involutive vertex automorphism \(\tau\) such that
\[
|V(G_n)|=4n,\quad\deg(v)=2(n-2),\quad
\widehat\delta(G_n,\lambda)=n-1,\quad
L_1(G_n,\lambda,\chi)=3,
\]
\(\lambda(\tau e)=\lambda(e)\), \(\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e)\); each ordinary color class is the disjoint union of four \(K_{1,n-2}\) stars; and the binary cycle space of \(G_n\) is generated by four-cycles.

**Construction.** Set \(I=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), \(f(i)=i+1\bmod n\), and \(V=\{L_i^s,R_i^s:i\in I,\ s\in\mathbb F_2\}\). Define \(d(i,j)=0\) for \(i<j\) and \(-1\) for \(i>j\), with sheet indices modulo 2. For each \(i\ne j\) and \(s\in\mathbb F_2\), insert:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{condition}&\text{edge}&\lambda&\chi\\
i\ne f(j)&L_i^sR_j^{s+d(i,j)}&i&0\\
j\ne f(i)&L_i^sR_j^{s+d(i,j)+1}&j&1.
\end{array}
\]
The two kinds of edge have different right endpoints if both are admitted, so this is a simple graph. Define \(\tau(L_i^s)=R_i^s\) and \(\tau(R_i^s)=L_i^s\).

**Color-degree, symmetry, and connectivity.** At \(L_i^s\), the \(n-2\) binary-0 edges all have ordinary color \(i\), and the \(n-2\) binary-1 edges have distinct colors \(j\in I\setminus\{i,f(i)\}\). At \(R_i^s\), the binary-1 edges have repeated color \(i\), and the binary-0 edges have the remaining single-occurrence colors. Thus every vertex has color degree \(n-1\). For each color \(a\), its edges form four pairwise vertex-disjoint stars centered at \(L_a^0,L_a^1,R_a^0,R_a^1\). Since \(d(i,j)+d(j,i)=-1\), the involution \(\tau\) exchanges the two binary edge kinds, preserves the ordinary color, and complements the binary label.

For every \(i\), choose \(j\notin\{i,f(i),f^{-1}(i)\}\). Both edge kinds link left index \(i\) with right index \(j\), with sheet shifts differing by one. Thus \(L_i^0,L_i^1\) have a common neighbor; the same holds for \(R_i^0,R_i^1\). Contract these two-vertex connected classes. The quotient is \(K_{n,n}\) without the index-matching, since every \(i\ne j\) admits at least one edge kind. Therefore \(G_n\) is connected.

**One-switch bound.** Any two consecutive binary-0 edges of a rainbow path must meet on the right, because all binary-0 edges incident to a left vertex have the same ordinary color. Similarly, two consecutive binary-1 rainbow edges must meet on the left. Hence a constant-binary-label rainbow run has at most two edges. A four-edge path with at most one binary switch must have binary word \(0011\) or \(1100\). Its first two edges would meet in one bipartition class and its last two in the other, although these meeting vertices occupy positions one and three of the same bipartite path; contradiction. Therefore \(L_1\le3\). The three-edge route \(L_0^s,R_1^t,L_3^u,R_2^v\), using edge kinds \(0,0,1\), has distinct ordinary colors \(0,3,2\); the superscripts are determined by the edge rules. Thus \(L_1=3\).

**Dense-bipartite lemma.** Let \(H=(A,B,E)\) be bipartite, with \(|A|=|B|=m\ge13\) and minimum degree at least \(m-4\). Its binary cycle space is generated by four-cycles.

*Proof.* By splitting at chords, it suffices to express any induced cycle of length \(2r\) as a sum of four-cycles. For \(r\ge3\), each of its \(r\) left vertices has \(r-2\) nonneighbors among the cycle's \(r\) right vertices. Each left vertex has at most four nonneighbors altogether; hence \(r\le6\). If no right vertex outside this cycle were adjacent to all \(r\) left cycle vertices, counting missing incidences gives
\[
4r\ge r(r-2)+(m-r),\qquad m\le r(7-r)\le12,
\]
a contradiction. Take a common outside right neighbor \(z\) of all left cycle vertices. Every consecutive two-edge portion of the cycle, closed through \(z\), gives a four-cycle; their binary sum is the original cycle. \(\square\)

Each side of the bipartition of \(G_n\) has \(m=2n\ge14\) vertices, and every vertex has degree \(m-4\). The lemma proves the square-generation assertion.

**Implication.** The following conditions together fail to guarantee \(L_1\ge c\widehat\delta\) for any constant \(c>0\): connectedness, regularity, uniform color degree, prescribed star-shaped ordinary color classes, a color-preserving vertex involution reversing the binary label, and generation of the full binary cycle space by four-cycles. Any further hypothesis needs independent validation on NORI1 and boundary 3-tournaments. This note proposes no sufficient or minimal strengthening.

**Further theorem: vertex transitivity and an abelian regular automorphism group.** The graph \(G_n\) constructed above is a Cayley graph of the abelian group \(\mathbb Z_{2n}\times\mathbb Z_2\). In particular, requiring color-equivariant vertex transitivity, even under an abelian regular group, does not restore a positive linear one-switch rainbow-path bound.

**Proof.** Let \(f(i)=i+1\bmod n\) as above, and put \(t_i=-1\) when \(i=n-1\), and \(t_i=0\) otherwise. Define an automorphism \(\rho\) on vertices by
\[
\rho(L_i^s)=L_{f(i)}^{s+t_i},\qquad \rho(R_i^s)=R_{f(i)}^{s+t_i},
\]
with sheet arithmetic modulo 2. The previously defined edge shift \(d(i,j)\) obeys
\[
d(f(i),f(j))=d(i,j)+t_j-t_i
\]
as an identity modulo 2 (and in fact over the integers, for distinct \(i,j\)). Hence \(\rho\) carries every admitted binary-0 edge to an admitted binary-0 edge, and similarly every binary-1 edge to one of its own kind. Thus
\[
\chi(\rho e)=\chi(e),\qquad \lambda(\rho e)=f(\lambda(e)).
\]
The map \(\rho\) has order \(2n\): one full cycle of its index rotates through every index and shifts the sheet by \(-1\), so \(\rho^n\) swaps the two sheets. Consequently its orbit on all left vertices has size \(2n\), and similarly on all right vertices. The involution \(\tau\) exchanges left and right vertices, commutes with \(\rho\), and has order two. Thus \(\langle\rho,\tau\rangle\cong\mathbb Z_{2n}\times\mathbb Z_2\) acts freely and transitively on the \(4n\) vertices. It is a regular abelian automorphism group, and \(G_n\) is its Cayley graph. \(\square\)

**Strengthened obstruction.** The infinite family simultaneously has abelian Cayley symmetry, regular degree and color degree, four-star ordinary color classes, square-generated cycle space, a color-preserving involution reversing binary labels, and \(L_1=3\).

**Stronger theorem (nearly every edge must switch).** In the same family, every rainbow path \(P\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sw}_\chi(P)\ge |E(P)|-2.}
\]
Consequently, for every \(s\ge0\), \(L_s(G_n,\lambda,\chi)\le s+2\), while
\[
\boxed{L_\infty(G_n,\lambda)=n.}
\]
Thus this is an obstruction exclusively to low-switch rainbow paths: full-palette rainbow paths exist, but they require at least \(n-2\) binary switches.

**Proof of the switch inequality.** Enumerate the consecutive edges of a rainbow path by \(e_1,\ldots,e_\ell\), with binary labels \(b_1,\ldots,b_\ell\), and intermediate vertices \(v_i=e_i\cap e_{i+1}\). In this bipartite construction, two consecutive binary-0 rainbow edges can meet only in the right class, while two consecutive binary-1 rainbow edges can meet only in the left class. Suppose there are at least two indices \(i<j\) satisfying \(b_i=b_{i+1}\) and \(b_j=b_{j+1}\), with no such index strictly between \(i\) and \(j\). Then \(b_{i+1},\ldots,b_j\) alternates, so
\[
b_j-b_i\equiv j-i-1\pmod2.
\]
On the other hand, the forced bipartition classes of \(v_i,v_j\), together with the fact that walking from \(v_i\) to \(v_j\) uses \(j-i\) edges, give
\[
b_j-b_i\equiv j-i\pmod2,
\]
a contradiction. Hence at most one consecutive pair of binary labels is equal. Every other adjacency changes label, proving \(\operatorname{sw}_\chi(P)\ge \ell-2\) and \(L_s\le s+2\).

**Proof that all \(n\) colors are realized.** Start at \(L_0^0\) and visit the indices \(0,1,2,\ldots,n-1,0\), alternating the left and right vertex types at each edge, using binary labels \(0,1,0,1,\ldots\). For each transition from index \(i\) to \(i+1\), \(0\le i<n-1\), the edge rules admit the prescribed binary label, and the sheet is \(0\) throughout. The last transition from index \(n-1\) back to \(0\) is also admitted and ends in sheet \(1\): in the odd case it is the type-0 edge \(L_{n-1}^0R_0^1\), and in the even case it is the type-1 edge \(R_{n-1}^0L_0^1\). Thus the \(n+1\) vertices are distinct (the final index \(0\) is in a different sheet from the first), and the ordinary edge colors are \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\) in order. This is a rainbow path of length \(n\). As there are only \(n\) ordinary colors, \(L_\infty=n\). \(\square\)

**Interpretation.** No loss of ordinary rainbow-path length is responsible for the short \(L_1\) value. Every sufficiently long rainbow path in these highly symmetric, square-generated graphs necessarily alternates its binary labels at almost every edge.

**A common local color law in NORI1 and boundary-tournament graphs.** For the standard ordinary edge-colorings of the cube and the ordered-pair boundary graph, no four-cycle has precisely three distinct ordinary edge colors. This is a directly checkable graph-coloring condition, though it alone is insufficient for a linear one-switch bound.

On \(Q_n\), color every edge by its direction. Every four-cycle is a two-direction square, with cyclic ordinary-color word \(abab\), \(a\ne b\).

For the ordered-pair boundary graph \(B_n\), give edge \((u,v)(v,w)\) ordinary color \(w\). Traverse an edge from \((u,v)\) either forward to \((v,w)\), denoted \(F\), or backward to \((t,u)\), denoted \(B\). Every simple four-cycle has, up to rotation and reversal, orientation word \(FFFF\) or \(FBFB\). Indeed, words with a change of orientation and an adjacent pair of equal orientations force a repeated ordered-pair state within four steps (the representative case \(FFBB\) repeats the intermediate state); the other forbidden cases follow by reversal and rotation. For \(FFFF\), the states are \((a,b),(b,c),(c,d),(d,a)\) with four distinct indices, and the color word is \(c,d,a,b\), all distinct. For \(FBFB\), the states have form \((a,b),(b,c),(d,b),(b,e)\), with \(c\ne e\), and the color word is \(c,c,e,e\). Thus the four-cycle color multiplicities are either \(1+1+1+1\) or \(2+2\), never \(2+1+1\).

Our present Cayley graph \(G_n\) violates this local law. The cycle
\[
L_0^0,\ R_2^0,\ L_0^1,\ R_3^0,\ L_0^0
\]
exists for every \(n\ge7\) and has ordinary-color word \(0,2,3,0\), with three distinct colors.

By contrast, the previously constructed two-sheet parity cover of \(B_n\) inherits the no-three-color-four-cycle law, because any simple four-cycle in the bipartite two-sheet lift projects to a simple four-cycle in \(B_n\). Yet that cover has \(L_1=3\), and its four-cycles span a subspace of codimension one in its binary cycle space (verified for \(n=5,6,7\), and consistent with its nontrivial two-sheet monodromy). Thus the local color law alone is insufficient.

**Research direction only.** Together, short-cycle generation and restrictions on how ordinary colors occur on short cycles distinguish the principal known constructions, but neither their conjunction nor a minimal sufficient graph-theoretic condition is established. This comparison provides explicit constraints on \((G,\lambda,\chi,\tau)\) for subsequent tests, without positing any auxiliary state system or matroid.
