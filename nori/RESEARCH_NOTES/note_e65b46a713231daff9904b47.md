## Research note

Let n>=4, choose distinct directions a,b and D=[n] minus {a,b}. Fix y in the (n-2)-face F fixing a,b, set x=y^D, and take an interior vertex z=y^S for some nonempty proper S subset D. For epsilon in {0,a,b,ab}, say that face F^epsilon carries a phase-i switch witness at z^epsilon if it has a geodesic from y^epsilon to x^epsilon, with one color change at z^epsilon, whose prefix has color i and suffix has color 1-i. The two geodesic segments use directions S and D minus S, respectively.

TWO SPLICE RULES. (1) If two adjacent faces, differing in a single direction a or b, carry witnesses at the same projected z with opposite phases, then there is a monochromatic (n-1)-edge cube geodesic. Indeed, when P has prefix i and suffix 1-i, and Q has prefix 1-i and suffix i, crossing along their common transverse edge after P's prefix and before Q's suffix yields an all-i (n-1)-edge geodesic if that transverse edge has color i. Otherwise cross after Q's prefix and before P's suffix, producing an all-(1-i) (n-1)-edge geodesic. In either case the paths use each direction in D and the transverse direction once. By the antipodally odd penultimate extension lemma, this yields a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic.

(2) If two opposite faces F and F^{ab} carry witnesses at the same projected z with the SAME phase i, then there is a full antipodal cube geodesic with at most one color change. Take any two-edge exterior square route z to z^{ab}. Either its color word or its reverse is weakly ordered as i's followed by (1-i)'s (every binary word of length two has this property). In the forward case prepend the color-i prefix of the first face witness and append the color-(1-i) suffix of the opposite face witness. In the reverse case interchange the two faces. The resulting path traverses all directions once and has at most one switch. Under global edge antipodal oddness, rotation at its switch turns it into a monochromatic full geodesic.

FOUR-FACE PACKING. If no monochromatic full geodesic exists, each pair of adjacent occupied faces must have identical witness phases by (1), whereas each pair of diagonally opposite occupied faces must have opposite witness phases by (2). Any three vertices of the four-face square contain two adjacent pairs and one opposite pair. The two equalities and one inequality are inconsistent. Therefore for every y,S, at most two among F,F^a,F^b,F^{ab} carry any phase witness at the corresponding projected switch vertex.

ANTIPODAL SWITCH-LOCUS COROLLARY. For a fixed face F, root y and phase i, the switch-support collection Z_i(F,y) is complement-free: it cannot contain both S and D minus S. Indeed a witness at S in F antipodally reverses to a same-phase witness at D minus S in the opposite face F^{ab}; a further witness at D minus S in F would violate splice rule (2).

These statements retain shared roots, direction sets, and phase witnesses. They do not establish that three matching witnesses exist. A prospective topological application must prove such a simultaneous intersection, rather than using independent reachability of unmatched paths.
