## Research note

For every n≥3, define a legal antipodally odd coloring of Q_n by c_0(x)=1+x_1, c_1(x)=x_2, c_2(x)=x_1, and c_j(x)=x_2 for 3≤j<n, with arithmetic modulo 2. Fix any exterior state z and put a=(0,1,1,z), b=(1,1,1,z). These vertices span a red edge of direction 0; every other incident edge at both vertices is blue. Thus {a,b} is a two-vertex red connected component, U_0 interchanges a and b, and all U_j (j≠0) fix both. Every untransported square commutator K_ij=(U_jU_i)^2 fixes a and b, including if i=0 or j=0: its other generator fixes the pair, and U_0^2=1. Thus the subgroup generated by the untransported K_ij separates red-connected vertices a and b. This refutes in every dimension n≥3 the statement that untransported square holonomies recover red connected components. The normal closure of these commutators, allowing conjugate transports, remains capable of antipodal displacement by the even-generator holonomy theorem; no assertion about that normal closure is refuted here.
