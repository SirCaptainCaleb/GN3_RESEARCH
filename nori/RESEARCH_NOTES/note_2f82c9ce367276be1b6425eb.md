## Research note

Let \(n\ge3\) and let \(w=(w_1,\dots,w_n)\in\mathbb F_2^n\). Define its twisted cyclic comparison vector by
\[
\delta_j(w)=w_j+w_{j+1}\ (1\le j<n),\qquad
\delta_n(w)=1+w_n+w_1.
\]
Its Hamming weight \(\sigma(w)\) is odd. The root slide \(T(w)=(w_2,\dots,w_n,1+w_1)\) cyclically rotates \(\delta(w)\). Put \(\mathcal G_n=\{v\in\mathbb F_2^n:\sigma(v)=1\}\); these are the color words of full geodesics that become monochromatic under a root slide.

**Theorem 1 (three-switch gap formula).** Suppose \(\sigma(w)=3\), and let the three nonzero entries of \(\delta(w)\) divide the circular index set into arcs of lengths \(a,b,c>0\), with \(a+b+c=n\). Then
\[
\boxed{\min_{v\in\mathcal G_n}d_H(w,v)=\min\{a,b,c\}.}
\tag{1}
\]

*Proof.* Write \(m=\min\{a,b,c\}\). Toggling every word bit in the arc between two consecutive twisted comparison changes at circular distance \(m\) toggles those two comparison bits and no others, producing a word with \(\sigma=1\). Hence the distance in (1) is at most \(m\).

For the reverse inequality let \(v\in\mathcal G_n\), and put \(z_j=w_j+v_j\) in \(\mathbb F_2\). Because both twisted comparison vectors have twist one, their sum equals the ordinary cyclic comparison vector of the periodic word \(z\):
\[
z_j+z_{j+1}=\delta_j(w)+\delta_j(v),\quad j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\tag{2}
\]
The right-hand side has either two nonzero entries, when the unique change of \(v\) coincides with a change of \(w\), or four, when it does not. In the first case, the ones of \(z\) occupy an arc whose two endpoints are among the three change positions of \(w\), or its complementary arc. Both possible arcs have length at least \(m\). In the second case, the circular word \(z\) has two disjoint runs of ones. Since three of its four run boundaries are change positions of \(w\), at least one of its two runs has both endpoints among those positions. That run has length at least \(m\). Thus in either case \(\sum_jz_j=d_H(w,v)\ge m\), proving (1). \(\square\)

**Theorem 2 (linear separation in the complete-geodesic exchange graph).** Let \(c\) be any antipodally odd binary coloring of \(Q_n\). Let \(\Gamma_n\) be the graph whose vertices are complete geodesic flags modulo cyclic root slides, with an edge whenever two representatives differ by interchanging consecutive dimensions. Let \(Z(c)\subseteq V(\Gamma_n)\) consist of flags of twisted count \(h=0\). If a flag \(g\) has a word with three twisted changes and arc gaps \(a,b,c\), then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}_{\Gamma_n}([g],Z(c))
\ \ge\ \left\lceil\frac{\min(a,b,c)}2\right\rceil.}
\tag{3}
\]
The distance to an empty set is understood as infinity.

*Proof.* Define the word potential \(D(w)=d_H(w,\mathcal G_n)\). Root sliding is a Hamming isometry of \(\mathbb F_2^n\) preserving \(\mathcal G_n\), so \(D(Tw)=D(w)\). An adjacent dimension exchange in a rooted complete geodesic alters at most two consecutive edge colors; thus its new word \(w'\) obeys \(d_H(w,w')\le2\), and consequently \(|D(w)-D(w')|\le2\). The potential descends to the root-slide quotient and is two-Lipschitz on its exchange edges. It vanishes on \(Z(c)\). Theorem 1 gives \(D(w)=\min(a,b,c)\), so any walk from \([g]\) to \(Z(c)\) must have length at least the right-hand side of (3). \(\square\)

**Corollary 3 (height-one flags arbitrarily far from closure).** For every \(m\ge1\) there exists an antipodally odd coloring of \(Q_{3m}\) with a complete geodesic \(g\) whose word is
\[
\boxed{w(g)=0^m1^m0^m,\qquad h(g)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{dist}_{\Gamma_{3m}}([g],Z(c))\ge\lceil m/2\rceil.}
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, the \(3m\) edges of \(g=(0;1,\dots,3m)\) lie in distinct antipodal edge orbits, so the displayed word can be prescribed and then extended to any legal coloring. The three twisted changes are equally spaced by \(m\), so Theorem 2 applies. For \(m\ge3\), every adjacent-exchange neighbor of every root slide of \(g\) has positive \(h\), irrespective of the unspecified edge colors.

In particular, the scalar height \(h\) does not bound exchange distance to the monochromatic stratum: a flag with the minimum positive height \(h=1\) can be at linear exchange distance from every \(h=0\) flag. This is a universal metric obstruction to bounded-radius local closure, and is independent of the stronger color-word-flat constructions in the related note. It does not exclude global equivariant chain cancellation or longer exchange paths.

**Theorem 4 (all-switch gap formula).** The potential \(D(w)=d_H(w,\mathcal G_n)\) has a closed form for every antiperiodic comparison word. Suppose \(\sigma(w)=2h+1\), with its switch positions \(p_0,\dots,p_{2h}\) in circular order, and let \(a_j>0\) be the forward circular distance from \(p_j\) to \(p_{j+1}\), with indices modulo \(2h+1\). Put
\[
L_j=\sum_{s=1}^{h}a_{j+2s-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{D(w)=\min_{0\le j\le2h}\min\{L_j,n-L_j\}.}
\tag{5}
\]
For \(h=0\) this reads \(D=0\), and for \(h=1\) it recovers Theorem 1.

*Proof.* The vector \(\delta(w)\) has odd parity. Each good word \(v\) has comparison vector supported at one position \(k\), with two choices of \(v\) differing by complementation. The periodic discrepancy word \(z=w+v\) therefore satisfies
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{cyc}}(z)=\delta(w)+e_k.
\tag{6}
\]
For fixed \(k\), the two solutions are \(z\) and its complement, and the minimum Hamming discrepancy is \(\min(|z|,n-|z|)\), where \(|z|\) denotes the number of ones.

We first show that the minimum over \(k\) is attained at a switch position of \(w\). As \(k\) advances by one cyclic position, the right side of (6) changes by \(e_k+e_{k+1}\). We may therefore choose successive discrepancy solutions so that \(z^{(k+1)}=z^{(k)}+e_{k+1}\), toggling a single bit. Between two consecutive switch positions of \(w\), \(\delta(w)\) vanishes. Consequently the bit being toggled has the same value before each successive toggle in this interval: the intervening comparison equations say that the two neighboring untoggled bits agree. Thus \(|z^{(k)}|\) is affine in the location \(k\) on that circular interval. The function \(\min(|z^{(k)}|,n-|z^{(k)}|)\) is the minimum of two affine functions and hence concave; its minimum on the interval occurs at an endpoint. Repeating for every interval shows that some optimal \(k\) is one of \(p_0,\dots,p_{2h}\).

Take \(k=p_j\). The discrepancy comparison vector in (6) has its \(2h\) nonzero entries at the remaining positions \(p_{j+1},\dots,p_{j+2h}\). One discrepancy solution has runs of ones precisely on the alternating arcs
\[
(p_{j+1},p_{j+2}),\ (p_{j+3},p_{j+4}),\ \dots,\
(p_{j+2h-1},p_{j+2h}),
\]
whose total length is \(L_j\). Its complementary solution has \(n-L_j\) ones. Minimizing over \(j\) proves (5). \(\square\)

Formula (5) gives a root-slide-invariant, two-Lipschitz refinement of the unweighted switch number: it measures the least number of edge-color positions that would have to be changed along a chosen full geodesic before a root slide could make it monochromatic. This word metric supplies quantitative obstructions but does not by itself constrain the simultaneous color words across different rooted geodesics.
