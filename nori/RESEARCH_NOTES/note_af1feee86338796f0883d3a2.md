## Research note

**Theorem (boundary interior-exchange parity).** Let \(n\ge8\), let \(b(a,h,c)\in\mathbb F_2\) be any reversal-odd boundary 3-tournament, and let \(\pi'\) be obtained from a Hamilton ordering \(\pi\) by swapping entries in positions \(i,i+1\), where \(4\le i\le n-4\). Let \(S(\pi)\) count the color switches in the \((n-2)\)-word of consecutive triple colors. Then
\[
\boxed{S(\pi')-S(\pi)\in\{-4,-2,0,2,4\}.}
\]
No restriction beyond the ordinary local triple coloring is needed.

*Proof.* Exchanging positions \(i,i+1\) changes only ordered triple windows beginning at one of \(i-2,i-1,i,i+1\), a block of at most four consecutive word entries. Because \(i\ge4\) and \(i+1\le n-3\), neither the first nor the final triple window changes. The two words therefore have the same initial and terminal bits, and hence the same switch parity by the elementary telescoping identity \(S(u)\equiv u_1+u_{n-2}\pmod2\). Only five boundaries, those incident with the possibly changed four-letter block, can change their switch indicators, so the absolute difference is at most five; being even, it is at most four. \(\square\)

**Common principle.** For NORI1 an interior adjacent dimension exchange changes two neighboring color letters and preserves switch parity, with jump at most two (proved in Article XIV, Subsection 53). For boundary tournaments an interior Hamilton transposition changes up to four neighboring triple letters and preserves switch parity, with jump at most four. In both cases the complete order remains full rank. Hence a potential filtered Wu–Yang cancellation should distinguish parity-preserving interior exchanges from endpoint-changing exchanges and transformations of the root. This is shared structural information rather than a restricted family of colorings.
