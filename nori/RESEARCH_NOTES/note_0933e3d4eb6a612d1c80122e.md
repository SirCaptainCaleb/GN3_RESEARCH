## Research note

Let \(M=U_{1,3}\oplus U_{1,3}\), so its basis graph is the rook graph \(K_3\square K_3=L(K_{3,3})\). Its largest cliques are the three rows and three columns, and every basis lies in two; the bipartite incidence graph of bases and largest cliques is the subdivision of \(K_{3,3}\). Therefore the maximum-clique incidence condition shared by the cube and rank-two uniform models contains this further partition matroid.

Give the edges of \(K_{3,3}\), indexed by left/right symbols \(0,1,2\), distinct weights
\[
W=\begin{pmatrix}6&8&1\\9&2&4\\3&5&7\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Orient all adjacent basis pairs of \(L(K_{3,3})\) by increasing edge weight. This induces transitive local tournaments at every original vertex, hence reversal-odd tight turn labels \(b(a,v,c)=1\) when \(W(av)<W(vc)\).

A Hamilton path of \(K_{3,3}\) up to reversal has the form \((a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,a_3,b_3)\) where \(a,b\) independently permute \(012\). For the six permutation orders \(012,021,102,120,201,210\) in both directions, the number of switches among the four comparisons in the weight list
\[
W_{a_1b_1},W_{a_2b_1},W_{a_2b_2},W_{a_3b_2},W_{a_3b_3}
\]
is the following complete 36-case certificate:
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
2&3&3&2&2&3\\3&2&2&3&3&2\\3&2&2&3&3&2\\
2&3&3&2&2&3\\2&3&3&2&2&3\\3&2&2&3&3&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently every Hamilton path of this graph has at least two changes among its consecutive triple-color values. Path reversal complements and reverses the comparison word, preserving its switch count.

Thus a spanning one-switch conclusion is false for the natural class of matroids whose largest-clique incidence graph is a graph subdivision, even under one global strict edge ordering. This refutes that proposed class-wide strengthening while retaining the square-root terminal-edge theorem. Any spanning strengthening needs a further intrinsic hypothesis or a weaker quantitative conclusion.
