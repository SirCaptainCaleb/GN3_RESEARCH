## Research note

For every n>=4, construct a simple path P in Q_n from 0 to its antipode using all n dimensions, containing no pair of antipodal edges, yet with every contiguous direction-simple subpath of length at most three. Label dimensions 0,...,n-1. If n is even, take the direction word (0,1,0,2,0,3,...,0,n-1). If n is odd, take (1,0,2,0,3,...,0,n-1). Each direction i>=1 occurs once and direction 0 occurs an odd number of times (n-1 for n even, n-2 for n odd); hence the terminal vertex is the antipode of 0. The path is vertex-simple: its visited vertices have a nondecreasing number of bits equal to one among dimensions 1,...,n-1; on intervals with the same number, bit 0 changes at most once. Every four consecutive moves contain direction 0 at least twice, so every contiguous direction-simple subpath has length at most three; a three-direction segment also occurs. The unique step in direction n-1 is last. All preceding edges lie in the facet x_(n-1)=0, while their antipodal edges lie in x_(n-1)=1, where P has no edges; the last direction-(n-1) edge has no antipodal counterpart in P. Thus P is disjoint from its antipodal edge image. Assign all edges of P color zero, their antipodal edges color one, and choose opposite colors arbitrarily on every remaining antipodal edge orbit. This extends P to a legal antipodally odd two-coloring in which it is a monochromatic antipodal path. This construction shows that the existence of a monochromatic antipodal path, even with the antipodal-odd edge rule, does not ensure that the given path contains a dimension-simple subpath whose length grows with n. Therefore a proof transferring the Wu-Yang antipodal-path theorem to NORI1 cannot work solely by retaining a contiguous subpath of an arbitrary monochromatic witness. It must find a different route or use additional edges.
