## Research note

**Theorem (color-transitivity obstruction).** For every integer \(m\ge8\) there exists a connected finite simple graph \(G_m\), a coloring \(\lambda:E(G_m)\to[m]\), a binary edge labeling \(\chi\), and a fixed-point-free involutive graph automorphism \(\tau\), such that \(\hat\delta(G_m,\lambda)=m-1\), \(\lambda(\tau e)=\lambda(e)\), \(\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e)\), and the ordinary-color automorphism group of \((G_m,\lambda)\) acts transitively on the \(m\) colors. Nevertheless, every vertex-simple rainbow path with at most one binary switch has length at most \(12\lceil\log_2m\rceil-6\).

**Definition of the graph.** Let \(B_m\) be the undirected ordered-pair overlap graph on the states \((a,b)\), \(a,b\in[m]\), \(a\ne b\); its edges have a distinguished orientation \((a,b)\to(b,c)\), for pairwise distinct \(a,b,c\). Write \(R(a,b)=(b,a)\). Let \(H=B_m\square B_m\), with states \((x,y)\). On an edge changing \(x\) by the forward arc \((a,b)\to(b,c)\), assign ordinary color \(c\). On an edge changing \(y\) by that forward arc, assign ordinary color \(a\). Define \(T(x,y)=(Ry,Rx)\). This is an involutive automorphism of \(H\); the first-factor edge \((a,b)\to(b,c)\) is mapped to the second-factor edge \((c,b)\to(b,a)\), both of ordinary color \(c\). Thus \(T\) preserves ordinary colors and exchanges the two edge types. Delete the fixed vertex set \(F=\{(Ry,y):y\in V(B_m)\}\) and let \(G_m=H[V(H)\setminus F]\). Then \(\tau=T|_{G_m}\) fixes no vertex or edge.

**Ordinary color degree.** At \(x=(a,b)\), the colors on first-factor incident edges are \([m]\setminus\{a\}\): incoming edges have color \(b\), outgoing edges have the other \(m-2\) colors. At \(y=(c,d)\), the colors on second-factor incident edges are \([m]\setminus\{d\}\): outgoing edges all have color \(c\), incoming edges have the other \(m-2\) colors. Hence the full-product palette at \((x,y)\) has size \(m-1\) when \(a=d\), and \(m\) otherwise. If \(a=d\) and \((x,y)\notin F\), neither \(x\) nor \(y\) is adjacent in \(B_m\) to its corresponding forbidden state, since the potential states share their first, respectively last, symbol. No incident edge is removed. If \(a\ne d\), a deleted first-factor edge requires either \(b=d\), with incoming/outgoing ordinary color \(c\), or \(a=c\), with ordinary color \(b\). The corresponding deleted second-factor edge has the same ordinary color; in the first case color \(c\) remains on at least \(m-3\) other outgoing second-factor edges, and in the second case color \(b\) remains on at least \(m-3\) other incoming first-factor edges. The two cases cannot both produce an edge, because that would be an inadmissible backtracking triple. Therefore deletion loses no color, and \(\hat\delta(G_m,\lambda)=m-1\).

**Connectivity.** For \(m\ge8\), deleting any single vertex of \(B_m\) leaves a strongly connected oriented overlap graph. Indeed, given ordered pairs \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\) other than the forbidden state \((p,q)\), choose two distinct symbols \(e,f\notin\{a,b,c,d,p,q\}\). Then
\[
(a,b)\to(b,e)\to(e,f)\to(f,c)\to(c,d)
\]
is a directed walk avoiding \((p,q)\), with its intermediate states all valid; shorten any repeated endpoints if necessary. To connect arbitrary \((x,y),(x',y')\in G_m\), choose \(w\in V(B_m)\setminus\{Rx,Rx'\}\), and successively change the second factor from \(y\) to \(w\) while avoiding \(Rx\), the first factor from \(x\) to \(x'\) while avoiding \(Rw\), and the second factor from \(w\) to \(y'\) while avoiding \(Rx'\). Each one-factor route exists by the preceding observation and stays outside \(F\). Thus \(G_m\) is connected. Simultaneous permutation of the symbols in both factors commutes with \(\tau\), preserves \(F\), and permutes ordinary colors by the same permutation. This proves ordinary-color transitivity.

**Binary labeling and path bound.** Put \(t=\lceil\log_2m\rceil\), and encode the original symbols as distinct \(t\)-bit vectors. For \(a\ne b\) let \(j(a,b)\) be the highest differing bit position, and define \(h(a,b)=2j(a,b)+a_{j(a,b)}\in\{0,\ldots,2t-1\}\). For every arc \((a,b)\to(b,c)\), \(h(a,b)\ne h(b,c)\): if the selected positions coincide, the selected bits of \(a\) and \(b\) differ; otherwise the even parts differ. For each first-factor edge with forward arc \(u\to v\), set \(\chi_1(e)=1\) if \(h(v)>h(u)\), and \(0\) otherwise. For each second-factor edge corresponding to \(e\) in \(B_m\), set \(\chi_2(e)=1-\chi_1(R e)\), where \(R e\) is the undirected image under the reversal \(R\). The forward arc of \(R e\) is \(Rv\to Ru\), so \(\chi_2(e)=1\) precisely when \(h(Rv)>h(Ru)\). The definition gives \(\chi(\tau e)=1-\chi(e)\).

Project a rainbow path \(P\) in \(G_m\) onto either factor, retaining the successive edges that change that factor. The resulting sequence is a rainbow walk in \(B_m\), and its binary word is a subsequence of the binary word of \(P\). For first-factor edges, any forward traversal followed immediately (in the projected sequence) by a backward traversal would repeat the incoming-edge color at their shared state. Hence its orientation word is backward followed by forward. In the second factor, two outgoing edges at the same state have the same color, so the projected orientation word is forward followed by backward. On a block with fixed traversal direction and fixed bit, \(h\), respectively \(h\circ R\), changes strictly monotonically. Each such block has at most \(2t-1\) edges. With at most one binary switch there is at most one orientation boundary and one binary boundary, so at most three blocks per factor. Each projection therefore has at most \(3(2t-1)\) edges. Their lengths sum to \(|E(P)|\), yielding \(|E(P)|\le6(2t-1)=12t-6\). \(\square\)

This is a counterexample to replacing pair-homogeneity of ordinary colors by simple transitivity, even with a free color-preserving involution and large minimum color degree.
