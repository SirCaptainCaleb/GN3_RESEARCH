## Research note

**Proposition.** Let X be a free finite cellular complex with an involution, n>=5, and H a real vector space of dimension n-2 with the negation involution. Every odd nonzero vertex labeling X^(0)->H\{0} extends equivariantly over the (n-3)-skeleton. Indeed S(H)=S^{n-3} is (n-4)-connected; for each cell of dimension k<=n-3 the attaching map S^{k-1}->S(H) is nullhomotopic. Choose one cell from each opposite pair and extend; use equivariance on its partner.

For the type-A Coxeter sphere of full Hamilton orders on n symbols, the quotient is RP^{n-2}, with covering class alpha satisfying alpha^{n-2} !=0. There is no equivariant map on its (n-2)-skeleton into S^{n-3}. Its first possible obstruction appears on top-dimensional dual Coxeter cells, indexed by ordered two-block partitions of all n symbols. For the punctured affine reversal torus M_n, the quotient has projective boundary RP^{n-1}, so alpha^{n-2} !=0 there as well; its (n-2)-skeleton likewise cannot map equivariantly into S^{n-3}.

Combined with the explicit critical-dimensional contraction of rational switch bivectors, this localizes the proof target. Under the absence of favorable complete paths, odd nonzero flag vectors arise in both problems. All extensions through dimension n-3 exist for formal homotopy reasons, irrespective of color relations; an argument with this target must establish color-sensitive fillings in dimension n-2. In particular the rank-three convex obstruction is compatible with continuous nonconvex filling whenever n>=6. This is a sharp obstruction-localization theorem, not an existence proof.
