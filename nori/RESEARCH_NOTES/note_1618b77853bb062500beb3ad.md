## Research note

For a hypothetical antipodally odd NORI1 counterexample containing a bi-monochromatic directed interval of rank n−3, the opposed residual three-faces F_u,F_v have complementary alternating Hamming-layer colorings, by Article XIV's codimension-three rigidity theorem. Let U_u,U_v be their unions of zero-switch rooted Freudenthal prefixes. The sharp rank-three homology theorem gives
H_3(F_u,U_u;F2) ≅ F2² and H_3(F_v,U_v;F2) ≅ F2².
Each has two geometrically distinguished polar relative generators, detected by the lower and upper missing caps.

**Equivariant pairing observation.** Cube antipodality A:F_u→F_v sends the Hamming weight r relative to u to weight 3−r relative to v. Thus it exchanges the lower polar generator of the first face with the upper polar generator of the second, and exchanges the upper generator of the first with the lower generator of the second. Since A²=1, the direct sum of the two relative third-homology groups is a free F2[Z2]-module of rank two.

In particular the four local missing-cap classes come in two free antipodal orbits, with no fixed polar generator. Their individual nonvanishing does not yield a global Bockstein index obstruction or a failure of equivariant norm cancellation. One must prove a nontrivial global attachment, transport or filtered boundary property coupling these local classes across dimensions outside the residual three-faces. This warning applies to the chain-tower program, in which step obstructions [(1+R)z_j] are global homology classes depending on prior choices, not sums of unpaired local defect counts.
