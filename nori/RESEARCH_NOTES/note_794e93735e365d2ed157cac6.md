## Research note

**Proposition (directed-arc parity obstruction).** Let \(n\ge4\) be even. Color each undirected dimension-\(i\) cube edge by
\[
c(\{x,x\oplus e_i\})=\sum_{j\ne i}x_j\pmod2.
\]
This is independent of the endpoint chosen because it omits \(x_i\), and \(c(\bar e)=1-c(e)\) because \(n-1\) is odd. Under the orientation gauge \(\beta(x,x\oplus e_i)=c(\{x,x\oplus e_i\})+x_i\), one has
\[
\boxed{\beta(x,x\oplus e_i)=\sum_{j=1}^n x_j\pmod2.}
\]
For every directed cube path, successive tail vertices have opposite parities. Hence the \(\beta\)-word alternates on **every** full antipodal geodesic, giving \(n-1\) switches. Nevertheless \(c\) admits a monochromatic full geodesic: choose any direction order \(i_1,\ldots,i_n\) and root bits \(x_{i_k}=k-1\pmod2\). The \(k\)-th original edge color is
\[
c_k=\Big(\sum_jx_j\Big)+x_{i_k}+(k-1)
=\sum_jx_j\pmod2,
\]
independent of \(k\).

Thus even though every NORI1 coloring is gauge-equivalent to a directed-arc labeling invariant under the vertex antipode and odd under arc reversal, the one-switch conclusion is **not** invariant under forgetting the orientation gauge. The proposed common directed-arc selector must produce the low-switch word for \(\beta+\theta\) in the cube, not for \(\beta\). This counterexample holds for arbitrarily large even \(n\).
