## Research note

CONJECTURE (weighted detour endpoint inequality). For any finite simple graph F=(V,E) and real weights w:V→R, define D_F(u,v) as the greatest length of a vertex-simple u-to-v path when u,v are in the same connected component, D_F(v,v)=0, and D_F(u,v)=−∞ for different components. Then
Σ_{v∈V} max_{u∈V}[w(u)+D_F(u,v)] ≥ Σ_{v∈V} w(v) +2|E|.
The statement is open.

EQUIVALENT UNWEIGHTED ASSIGNMENT FORM (PROVED REDUCTION). The weighted inequality for all real w holds if and only if there is a permutation π of V such that Σ_v D_F(π(v),v)≥2|E| (with π respecting graph components). Proof: One direction follows by W(v)≥w(π(v))+D_F(π(v),v) and summing. Conversely the minimum over potentials w of Σ_v max_u(w(u)+D_F(u,v))−Σ_v w(v) is the dual of the finite maximum-weight transportation/assignment linear program, so strong LP duality and Birkhoff's theorem yield the maximum over permutations π of Σ_v D_F(π(v),v). For disconnected F, both optimization problems decompose over components.

CONDITIONAL ROOT-MATROID DIRAC THEOREM FOR HAMMING PRODUCTS. Let G=□_{j=1}^t K_{m_j}, with vertex set ∏_j [m_j], and give every step changing coordinate j from a to b the root label (j,{a,b}) in R=⊕_j M(K_{m_j}). If the weighted detour inequality holds for every finite F, then every edge-subgraph H⊆G with average degree d_H contains a path of at least d_H edges whose multiset of root labels is independent in R. Proof: Fix a total order of coordinate factors 1,…,t. For each v set L_0(v)=0 and let L_j(v) be the maximum length of a path ending v whose factor indices are weakly increasing, with each factor-j block visiting pairwise distinct symbols. All such paths are R-independent because each factor block forms a tree path in the corresponding graphic matroid; distinct factors have disjoint matroid supports. At stage j and for a fixed choice of all other coordinates, let F be the graph of H-edges changing j within that fiber. Any such path is a path of the earlier j−1 factors ending at some fiber vertex u followed by a simple F-path from u to v. Hence L_j(v)=max_u[L_{j−1}(u)+D_F(u,v)]. By the weighted detour inequality, summing L_j over the fiber exceeds the sum of L_{j−1} by at least twice the number of H-edges in that fiber. Summing fibers and then j proves Σ_v L_t(v)≥2|E(H)|. Thus max_v L_t(v)≥d_H. This would unify Leader–Long (all m_j=2) and Dirac's simple-path theorem (t=1), with the same direct-sum graphic resource-independence test.

FINITE FALSIFICATION SEARCH ONLY. Exhaustive enumeration of the unweighted assignment inequality was performed for all graphs on at most six labeled vertices (respectively 8,64,1024,32768 graphs on 3,4,5,6 vertices), with longest simple-path endpoint lengths by DFS and maximum assignment weight by subset dynamic programming. No failure occurred. A fixed-seed sample of 100000 labeled graphs on seven vertices also yielded no failure. Exhaustive enumeration of all 2^18 edge-subgraphs of K_3□K_3 yielded no failure of the conditional Hamming-product density conclusion. These calculations are evidence, not a proof. They do not establish a full monochromatic antipodal cube path or a quantitative extension for arbitrary reversal-odd boundary tournament comparisons.

MATHEMATICAL SIGNIFICANCE. The proposed scalar inequality would be a direct path-theoretic rank-growth principle interpolating the free root matroid U_{n,n} and M(K_n). Its weighted form is necessary for the fiberwise induction, and the assignment formulation isolates a finite, uncolored problem independent of matroid decorations. Even if proved, a second theorem must exploit the global color-polarity law to reach unrestricted NORI1, and local tournament comparisons remain essential for ordinary boundary tournaments.
