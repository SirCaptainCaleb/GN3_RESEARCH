## Research note

**Proved homogeneous line-graph-cover counterexample (2026-10-12).** Fix \(n\ge6\). Let \(F_n=Q_n/\tau\) and \(G=F_n\square C_5\). The graph \(G\) is regular of degree \(n+2\), vertex-transitive and 2-vertex-connected. Give it the binary covering-monodromy class \(\beta=\beta_{\rm cube}+\beta_5\), odd on the original folded-cube antipodal covering and on a loop around the \(C_5\) factor. The connected double cover may be realized as
\[
\widetilde G=(Q_n\square C_{10})/\langle(\tau_Q,s^5)\rangle\longrightarrow F_n\square C_5,
\]
where \(s\) rotates the ten-cycle by one edge. The residual deck involution is induced by \((\tau_Q,\mathrm{id})\). Restriction over a fixed \(C_5\) coordinate is the canonical antipodal cube cover, and over a fixed \(F_n\) coordinate it is \(C_{10}\to C_5\).

Set \(H=L(G)\), choose its induced line-graph monodromy cochain \(a\), and let \(D=M([B_H,0;a,1])^*\) have distinguished port \(p\). Then \(D\setminus p=M(H)^*\) is cographic.

**Theorem.** The matroid \((D,p)\) has unrestricted two-intersection \(\mathrm{PI}_2(D,p)\). Its minimum port cocircuits have size six and form a single orbit under cover-preserving automorphisms. But there is an admissible port cycle meeting *every* minimum port cocircuit in six elements, so \(P_4(D,p)\) fails.

*Proof of \(\mathrm{PI}_2\).* Any admissible port cycle \(X_d=\operatorname{supp}(a+\delta f)\cup\{p\}\) restricts on a cube-factor slice \(F_n\times\{t\}\) to an admissible antipodally odd cube-turn signature. Wu--Yang's nongeodesic monochromatic antipodal-path theorem and Article XI's turn-quotient reduction yield a simple monodromy-one cycle \(C\) inside its \(L(F_n)\) turn subgraph, with integer turn weight one. Then \(C\cup\{p\}\) is a cocircuit of the full matroid and intersects \(X_d\) in two elements.

*Shortest port cocircuits.* The line-graph cycle projection \(\varphi:Z_1(L(G))\to Z_1(G)\) preserves covering monodromy and does not increase support size. A monodromy-one turn cycle of length at most five therefore yields a monodromy-one base cycle of length at most five. In \(G=F_n\square C_5\), nontrivial cube monodromy requires at least \(n\) steps in the folded-cube factor, and nontrivial \(C_5\)-monodromy requires five \(C_5\)-factor steps. Thus the only shortest monodromy-one base cycles are the \(C_5\) fibers at fixed \(F_n\) coordinates. Each determines a length-five odd-monodromy turn cycle in \(H\) and a port cocircuit of size six. The cube automorphism group acts transitively on the fiber positions while preserving \(\beta\), so these shortest port cocircuits are one orbit.

*Failure of \(P_4\).* Over each vertex of \(F_n\), the lifted five-cycle fiber is a ten-cycle. Color those ten consecutive lifted edges alternately zero and one. Its deck shift through five positions reverses the colors, and all five turns on the corresponding quotient \(C_5\) disagree. The fiber edge sets are disjoint; the assignments can be made in all fibers simultaneously and extended to remaining deck-paired cube-direction edges in any antipodally odd way. For the resulting port cycle \(X_d\), every minimum port cocircuit \(Y\) comes from a fiber five-cycle, and
\[
|X_d\cap Y|=1+w_d(C_5)=1+5=6.
\]
Thus universal unrestricted two-intersection coexists with failure of shortest four-intersection. \(\square\)

**What this rules out.** Even a connected line-graph double-cover construction with a regular, vertex-transitive, two-vertex-connected base graph, cographic port deletion, and a single symmetry orbit of minimum port cocircuits need not admit the desired amplification. The unrestricted weight-one cocircuits are supplied by cube-factor slices, whereas all minimum port cocircuits come from the independent five-cycle factor. Thus homogeneity alone does not couple the low-cost certificates with the shortest monodromy cycles. A successful shared theorem needs additional resource-gallery geometry or a connectedness property of *different-length* witness families that prevents this factor separation.

**Scope.** The homogeneous product fails the short local balanced-relation generation hypothesis of the canonical cube and tournament galleries: a balanced cycle built as a sum of one cube-monodromy loop and one five-cycle loop cannot be generated solely by bounded local product squares and star triangles. Hence this counterexample does not refute the remaining marked independence-preserving gallery conjecture. It strengthens the earlier two-connector example by preserving vertex transitivity and homogeneity of the minimum port-cocircuit family.

**Proposition (short-balanced-relation deficiency detects factor separation).** Let \(\alpha_F,\alpha_5\in H^1(L(G);\mathbb F_2)\) be the two induced factor monodromy classes: apply the line-graph turn projection to the base cycle space, project to \(F_n\) or \(C_5\), and evaluate the respective cover monodromy. Then
\[
\alpha=\alpha_F+\alpha_5,\qquad \alpha_F,\alpha_5\text{ independent}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(R_{\le6}\) be the span in \(Z_1(L(G);\mathbb F_2)\) of all balanced simple cycles of length at most six. Then
\[
\boxed{R_{\le6}\subsetneq\ker\alpha.}\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, any balanced turn cycle \(C\) of length at most six must have \((\alpha_F(C),\alpha_5(C))=(0,0)\): the only other possibility compatible with \(\alpha(C)=0\) is \((1,1)\), which would require at least \(n\) steps in the folded-cube factor and five steps around \(C_5\), exceeding six. On the other hand, take one odd-monodromy turn cycle \(C_F\) from a length-\(n\) cube geodesic and one five-cycle turn \(C_5\) in a different factor fiber. Their binary sum \(C_F+C_5\) has total monodromy zero, but \(\alpha_F(C_F+C_5)=1\), so it lies outside \(R_{\le6}\). This proves (2).

The dimension
\[
\dim\bigl(\ker\alpha/R_{\le6}\bigr)\ge1
\]
is an intrinsic certificate of the *nonlocal balanced relation* coupling the two factors. In the canonical folded-cube and boundary-tournament turn graphs, Article XI proves that the corresponding balanced cycle spaces are generated by relations of size at most four and six, respectively; their analogous deficiency vanishes. This makes the local balanced-generator axiom meaningful: it excludes the homogeneous product obstruction, although the independent balanced-shortcut counterexample elsewhere in Article XI shows that local generation alone cannot force the desired shortest intersection inequality.
