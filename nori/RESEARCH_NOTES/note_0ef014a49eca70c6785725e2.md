## Research note

Let bit indices be \(0,1,2,3,4\). Define an undirected-edge coloring on \(Q_5\) by
\[
f_0(x)=x_1,\quad f_1(x)=x_0x_2,\quad
f_2(x)=(1-x_3)\lor x_1,\quad
f_3(x)=(1-x_2)(1-x_4),\quad f_4(x)=1-x_3.
\]
Every function is independent of its own bit. The interaction graph is the path \(0-1-2-3-4\); all four interaction links have influence in both directions.

The root \(0\), and hence its antipode \(31\), admits neither monotone orientation. To exclude \(1^*0^*\), if direction 1 precedes 0, its edge has color zero and the subsequent 0-edge has color one. If 0 precedes 1, its edge is zero; either 2 precedes 1 and the 1-edge is one, or 1 precedes 2 and the 2-edge is one. Every order therefore has a rise. To exclude \(0^*1^*\), if 4 precedes 3, its edge is one and the subsequent 3-edge is zero. If 3 precedes 4, the 4-edge is zero; either 3 precedes 2 and the 3-edge is one, or 2 precedes 3 and the 2-edge is one. Every order therefore has a descent.

Nevertheless the order \((0,1,2,3,4)\) from zero has word \(00100\). Its only one-colored edge is the internal edge from vertex 3 to vertex 7 in direction 2. Repairing that edge creates a zero full geodesic between the two empty roots.

The example extends to every \(d\ge5\) by adding directions \(i=5,\ldots,d-1\) with color \(1-x_{i-1}\), while keeping old colors independent of the new bits. Each added direction is removable by the sink-insertion identity, so the all-zero root still admits neither orientation. Traversing the dimensions in increasing order retains word \(00100\,0^{d-5}\). The interaction graph remains a path.

This is a local obstruction to a proposed face-transport rule that a one-edge exception on an interaction tree forces a monotone path at its endpoint root. It leaves the global paired-root conjecture intact. For the displayed five-dimensional coloring, direct enumeration gives 18 paired roots, all supporting monochromatic full geodesics. Thus closure must use transport from other roots or compatibility with the outside quadrilateral arms.
