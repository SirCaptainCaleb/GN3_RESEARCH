## Research note

**Theorem (joint short-cycle conditions fail).** For every \(n\ge5\), a connected graph with minimum ordinary color degree \(n\) and a vertex involution \(\tau\) reversing an auxiliary binary edge labeling exists with \(L_1\le7\), although its entire binary cycle space is generated by four-cycles and every four-cycle has ordinary color multiplicities \(2+2\) or \(1+1+1+1\).

**Construction.** Take vertices \((u,v,s)\), \(u,v\in[n]\), \(u\ne v\), \(s\in\mathbb F_2\). For distinct \(a,b,c\) and each \(s\), put an edge \((a,b,s)(b,c,1-s)\) of ordinary color \(c\) and binary label \(s\). Add one vertical edge \((u,v,0)(u,v,1)\) for each ordered pair, assigning every vertical edge the same fresh ordinary color \(\star\). Define \(\tau(u,v,s)=(v,u,s)\) and assign binary labels to vertical edges in complementary \(\tau\)-pairs, for example label zero when \(u<v\), one when \(u>v\). This is a vertex involution and reverses binary labels on every edge. Each vertex sees \(n-1\) old colors plus \(\star\), so its color degree is \(n\).

The subgraph of old edges has \(L_1=3\): at a sheet-\(s\) vertex the \(n-2\) edges of binary label \(s\) have distinct ordinary colors, while the other \(n-2\) all have color \(v\). Two consecutive rainbow edges labeled zero must meet in sheet zero, and two consecutive rainbow edges labeled one must meet in sheet one. An old-edge path alternates sheets; no binary-constant rainbow run exceeds two edges. A four-edge one-switch rainbow path would have binary word \(0011\) or \(1100\), requiring different sheets at positions one and three, impossible. Hence \(L_1\le3\) in the old graph. Since any rainbow path in the enlarged graph uses at most one edge of color \(\star\), deleting it leaves two old paths of at most three edges, giving \(L_1\le7\).

A four-cycle containing no vertical edge projects to a four-cycle in the ordered-pair graph, which has color multiplicities \(2+2\) (alternating forward/backward steps) or four distinct colors (four consecutive forward steps). With one vertical edge, the remaining three old edges project to a directed triangle and have three distinct colors, giving four distinct colors together with \(\star\). With two vertical edges, the other two edges are the two lifts of one old edge, giving color pattern \(\star,c,\star,c\). Three vertical matching edges cannot occur in a four-cycle. Therefore every four-cycle has the required ordinary-color pattern.

**Cycle-space lemma.** Let \(D\) be a graph whose binary cycle space is generated by triangles and four-cycles. Replace each vertex \(v\) with \((v,0),(v,1)\), replace each edge \(uv\) with the two cross-sheet edges, and add matching edges \((v,0)(v,1)\). The new graph has cycle space generated by four-cycles. Indeed, a cycle projects to a binary cycle of \(D\). Each base triangle lifts to a square (three old edges plus one matching edge), and each base square lifts to a square (four old edges). Subtract corresponding lifted squares until the projection is zero. Then every base edge occurs in either both its lifts or neither; subtract the square consisting of both lifts and their two matching edges. The remaining cycle lies in the matching alone and is empty.

The underlying ordered-pair graph \(D_n\) has triangle-and-square-generated cycle space for \(n\ge5\). A self-contained proof follows in the companion note continuation. Applying the cycle-space lemma to \(D_n\) proves that the enlarged graph has square-generated cycle space.

**Interpretation.** The shared condition is generation by triangles and squares. The example satisfies the stronger square-only generation condition, together with the four-cycle color law. The extra ordinary color \(\star\) is distinguished from the original colors, suggesting investigation of ordinary-color homogeneity, with sufficiency and minimality remaining open.

**Proof that ordered-pair graph cycles are generated by triangles and squares.** Let \(D_n\) have vertices \((a,b)\) for \(a\ne b\) and edges \(T(a,b,c)=\{(a,b),(b,c)\}\) for pairwise distinct \(a,b,c\). Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(w(a,b,c)\) be an edge weighting vanishing on all three- and four-cycles. Fix \(b\). The edges \(T(a,b,c)\) with middle index \(b\) form \(K_{n-1,n-1}\) minus a perfect matching between incoming states \((a,b)\) and outgoing states \((b,c)\). For \(n-1\ge4\), the binary cycle space of this bipartite graph is generated by its squares: chordless cycles have length at most six because each left vertex has only one forbidden right neighbor; a chordless six-cycle is the sum of three squares via a fourth right vertex adjacent to all three left vertices. Therefore \(w(a,b,c)=A_{ab}+B_{bc}\) for functions \(A,B\) indexed by ordered pairs.

Write \(z_{ab}=A_{ab}+B_{ab}\). The directed triangle through \((a,b),(b,c),(c,a)\) gives \(z_{ab}+z_{bc}+z_{ca}=0\). Let \(s_{ab}=z_{ab}+z_{ba}\). Both orientations of each triangle give \(s_{ab}+s_{bc}+s_{ca}=0\). Adding the directed triangle equations for \(abc,acd,abd,bcd\) gives \(s_{ac}=s_{bd}\) whenever \(a,b,c,d\) are distinct. For \(n\ge5\), any two unordered pairs can be connected through pairs disjoint from both, so all \(s_{ab}\) are equal; the triangle equation forces the common value to be zero. Hence \(z_{ab}=z_{ba}\), and the directed triangle equation implies \(z_{ab}=p_a+p_b\) for some vertex potentials \(p_a\).

For every middle index \(b\), adding \(p_b\) to all \(A_{ab}\) and all \(B_{bc}\) leaves \(w(a,b,c)\) unchanged and changes \(z_{ab}\) by \(p_a+p_b\). After this adjustment \(A_{ab}=B_{ab}=h_{ab}\), so \(w(a,b,c)=h_{ab}+h_{bc}\) is a graph coboundary on \(D_n\). Thus every edge weighting annihilating all triangles and squares annihilates every cycle. By binary linear algebra those short cycles span the cycle space, as claimed.

**Verification.** The augmented graphs were independently enumerated for \(n=5,6\). They have respectively 40 vertices, 140 edges, and 300 four-cycles of GF(2)-rank 101 (= cycle rank 101); and 60 vertices, 270 edges, and 900 four-cycles of rank 211 (= cycle rank 211). Neither instance has a four-cycle with three ordinary colors.

**Forced fresh-color lemma (sharp within matching completions).** Retain the original ordinary coloring \(\lambda(a,b,s)(b,c,1-s)=c\) on the two-sheet ordered-pair graph. Add all vertical matching edges \((u,v,0)(u,v,1)\), allowing arbitrary ordinary colors on these new edges. If every four-cycle is required to avoid precisely three distinct ordinary colors, then *each vertical matching edge must have a color outside the original palette \([n]\)*.

**Proof.** Fix an ordered pair \((u,v)\) and consider any third index \(w\notin\{u,v\}\). The three old edges tracing
\[
(u,v,0)\to(v,w,1)\to(w,u,0)\to(u,v,1)
\]
together with the vertical matching edge at \((u,v)\) form a four-cycle. Its three old edges carry the pairwise distinct colors \(w,u,v\). If the new edge's color belonged to \(\{w,u,v\}\), this square would use precisely three colors, violating the hypothesis. Varying \(w\) over \([n]\setminus\{u,v\}\), these three-element sets cover the entire original palette. Hence the vertical color must be fresh. Conversely, assigning every vertical edge a single fresh color preserves the no-three-color law for all four-cycles, as proved above.

**Significance.** This is a direct obstruction to simultaneously eliminating the two-sheet parity defect by vertical matching, retaining the four-cycle color law, and retaining the old palette. The matching completion necessarily introduces new color labels. It does not prove a lower bound for graphs with color homogeneity, nor characterize all possible parity-defect repairs.
