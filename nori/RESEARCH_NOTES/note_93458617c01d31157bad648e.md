# Parallel geodesic orbit decompositions and commuting gallery transfers in even dimensions

Let n>=4 be even. Write V=F_2^n, let 1 be its all-one vector, and let e_i (0<=i<n) denote the direction vectors. For any full direction order pi and starting root x, denote by P_(x,pi) the full antipodal geodesic and by W_pi(x,i) the color of its direction-i edge, indexed by direction rather than traversal position. The coloring is any legal antipodally odd coloring of the undirected edges of Q_n.

Let h:V -> F_2^2 be h(x)=(x_0, sum_(j=1)^(n-1) x_j), and set R=ker(h). Since n is even, h(1)=(1,1). Then |R|=2^(n-2), and the two vectors h(e_i),h(1) are linearly independent for every i.

**Theorem 1 (simultaneous antipodal edge-orbit decomposition).** For every direction permutation pi, the family {P_(x,pi): x in R} meets each antipodal orbit of undirected Q_n edges once. It therefore partitions the n 2^(n-2) edge orbits into 2^(n-2) full geodesics of length n. The same root set R works for all n! permutations. In the antipodal quotient graph Q_n/<x~x+1>, the images form a decomposition into cycles of length n. All cycles have pairwise disjoint edges, and their union is the quotient edge set.

**Proof.** For each direction i, let H_i=span{e_i,1}. This is a four-element subgroup of V. Two paths P_(x,pi) and P_(y,pi) use edges belonging to the same antipodal edge orbit in direction i if and only if x+y lies in H_i, because the prefix vertex added before the direction-i step is identical for every starting root. Since h maps H_i bijectively to F_2^2, R meets every H_i-coset once. Thus as x runs through R, the direction-i edges sample all 2^(n-2) antipodal edge orbits in that direction without repetition. Summing over i proves the partition. A full direction-simple geodesic has no antipodal pair of distinct internal vertices, since vertex separation along that path has Hamming distance strictly between 0 and n; only its endpoints are antipodal. Therefore its projection is a simple n-cycle in the quotient for n>=4.

**Theorem 2 (commuting swap law).** Let pi' arise from pi by exchanging two adjacent directions i,j. Define
r_(ij)= e_i+e_j if i,j>=1, and r_(ij)=1+e_i+e_j if {i,j} contains 0.
Define epsilon_(ij)=0 for i,j>=1 and epsilon_(ij)=1 otherwise.
Then r_(ij) is a nonzero member of R, and for every x in R,
W_(pi')(x,k)=W_pi(x,k) for all k not in {i,j},
W_(pi')(x,i)=W_pi(x+r_(ij),i)+epsilon_(ij),
W_(pi')(x,j)=W_pi(x+r_(ij),j)+epsilon_(ij),
with binary addition. Thus both exchanged columns undergo the same fixed-point-free translation of root labels, and both are complemented only when direction 0 participates. These operators on the full profile matrix commute pairwise, even when their direction pairs overlap.

**Proof.** At the two exchanged steps let u=x+prefix denote their common starting vertex in the original order. For direction i, the edge in pi' starts at u+e_j, whereas its edge in pi rooted at x+r_(ij) starts at u+r_(ij). The difference between these starting vertices is r_(ij)+e_j, which equals e_i if neither direction is zero and equals 1+e_i otherwise. Edges whose starting vertices differ by e_i are the same undirected edge; those differing by 1+e_i are antipodal undirected edges and have opposite colors. The direction-j comparison is identical, using r_(ij)+e_i in place of r_(ij)+e_j. Unexchanged steps have the same preceding vertex and retain their colors. Translation and complementation operations commute on binary-valued functions on the abelian group R, so all pair operators commute.

**Corollary 3 (closed inversion formula for the full permutation gallery).** Fix reference order pi_0=(0,1,...,n-1). For another permutation pi, let Inv_i(pi) consist of all j!=i whose order relative to i differs between pi_0 and pi. Set
s_i(pi)=sum_(j in Inv_i(pi)) r_(ij) in R, and
q_i(pi)=sum_(j in Inv_i(pi)) epsilon_(ij) in F_2.
Then for every legal coloring and every x in R,
W_pi(x,i)=W_(pi_0)(x+s_i(pi),i)+q_i(pi).
In particular, from one profile matrix indexed by R and n directions, the complete collection for all direction orders is obtained using explicit affine root permutations and bit complements, with no further color queries.

**Proof.** A reduced adjacent-swap sequence from pi_0 to pi exchanges each inverted pair once. Apply Theorem 2 along the sequence. The pair operators commute, so their total action on column i is the displayed sum of translations and complements, independent of the chosen adjacent-swap sequence.

**Theorem 4 (four-coset root recovery and universal matrix encoding).** For any direction order pi, the matrix M_pi=(W_pi(x,i))_(x in R,i in [n]) is an arbitrary element of F_2^(R x [n]) as the legal coloring varies. Indeed, assigning its entries assigns one independent color bit to each antipodal edge orbit via Theorem 1, so the correspondence from legal colorings to M_pi is bijective.

Moreover all starting roots can be recovered from M_pi using only four cosets of R. Fix t in {0,e_0,e_1,e_0+e_1}. For a direction i, write uniquely
h(t)=a_i(t)h(e_i)+b_i(t)h(1),
and put r_i(t)=t+a_i(t)e_i+b_i(t)1 in R.
For every x in R,
W_pi(x+t,i)=W_pi(x+r_i(t),i)+b_i(t).
Consequently M_(pi_0), the inversion formula in Corollary 3, and these four coset-transfer rules determine the direction-indexed full-geodesic profiles at every root and in every order for an arbitrary legal coloring.

**Proof.** The first assertion follows from orbit partition and independent choices of antipodally paired edge colors. For the transfer, the corresponding direction-i edges in the paths rooted at x+t and at x+r_i(t) have starting vertices differing by a_i(t)e_i+b_i(t)1; they are therefore the same undirected edge if b_i=0, and an antipodal pair if b_i=1. The transfer identity follows. The four representatives of the quotient V/R cover all roots.

**Theorem 5 (parity obstruction in odd dimensions).** Let n>=3 be odd. No family of full antipodal geodesics can partition the antipodal edge orbits of Q_n into single-orbit-selected paths. More quantitatively, any family of pairwise edge-orbit-disjoint full antipodal geodesics contains at most
2^(n-2)-ceil(2^(n-2)/n)
members.

**Proof.** The antipodal quotient graph has 2^(n-1) vertices, degree n, and n 2^(n-2) edges. Each full geodesic projects to a simple n-cycle. Any union of pairwise edge-disjoint cycles has even degree at every vertex. If the cycles cover only a subset of edges, the unused-edge subgraph has odd degree at every vertex, hence contains at least 2^(n-2) edges by the handshaking lemma. Its edge count is congruent to zero modulo n, since the total number of edges and the number removed are divisible by n. Therefore at least n ceil(2^(n-2)/n) edges remain uncovered, leaving at most 2^(n-2)-ceil(2^(n-2)/n) selected n-cycles. In particular full partition is impossible.

**Corollary 6 (column parity conservation).** For even n>=4, for each direction i the parity sum_(x in R) W_pi(x,i) is independent of pi. It is also independent of which coset R+t is used to list the starting roots. This follows since every permutation and coset transfer reindexes the column and may complement all of its entries, while |R| is even.

**Verification and scope.** A standalone verifier checked the orbit partitions and inversion/adjacent-swap/four-coset formulas on independently sampled legal Q4, Q6, and Q8 colorings. The results apply without any restrictions on nonlinear Boolean rules or the influence graph. They give a constructive resolvable edge-orbit design and a closed algebraic transport system for all full profiles, plus an odd-dimensional impossibility theorem. They do not themselves prove that any transported profile becomes monochromatic; that remains unrestricted NORI1.
