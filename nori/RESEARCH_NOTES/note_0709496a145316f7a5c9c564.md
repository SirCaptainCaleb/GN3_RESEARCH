## Research note

THEOREM (linear-root two-arm separation). Let G be a finite graph, F a field, and φ:V(G)→F^d an injective map. Label each edge e of G by an element λ(e) of a represented matroid R with nonzero representing vectors a_j∈F^d. Assume that for every oriented edge e=uv the difference φ(v)−φ(u) is a nonzero scalar multiple of a_{λ(e)}. Let P,Q be two edge paths ending at one common vertex t, and suppose the combined multiset of their edge labels is an independent set of R (in particular, no label is used twice). Then V(P)∩V(Q)={t}, and each path is vertex-simple. If the total number of edges is r(R), concatenating P with the reversal of Q gives a vertex-simple path of length r(R) whose labels form an R-basis.
Proof. A nonempty closed subwalk with pairwise independent labels yields a nontrivial linear relation by telescoping φ, impossible because every coefficient of an edge's representing vector is nonzero and labels are distinct. Thus each arm is simple. If P,Q had a second common vertex z, their terminal-to-z subpaths yield two different φ-displacement expressions from t to z; subtracting yields a nontrivial dependence on their combined independent labels. Hence the only common vertex is t. The union forms one simple path with all the independent labels; cardinality r(R) makes this set a basis.

THEOREM (opposition-parity two-arm gluing). In the same setting, suppose colors on oriented windows of path length s are defined, with s=1 or s=2. Assume reversal of a window changes its color by s−1 modulo two. Thus s=1 means undirected edge colors and s=2 means reversal-odd triple colors. If two arms P,Q ending at t are monochromatic in the same color ε on all their s-edge windows and have an independent union of full rank r(R), their concatenation P·reverse(Q) has at most s−1 color switches. Proof. For s=1, every edge of both arms retains color ε under reversal, so the concatenation is monochromatic. For s=2, windows wholly inside P have color ε; those wholly inside reverse(Q) have color 1−ε. There is one possible additional window crossing the junction at t, of arbitrary color. Its bit agrees with one of ε,1−ε, so the entire color word switches at most once. Short arms with no s-window are interpreted vacuously.

CANONICAL DECODING I (cube). Let G=Q_n, F=F_2, φ(x)=x, and label all coordinate-i edges by i with a_i=e_i. Then R=U_{n,n}, also the canonical exchange-root graphic matroid of ⊕ U_{1,2}. A pair of monochromatic same-color arms with independent combined labels of size n concatenates to a monochromatic full antipodal geodesic: every coordinate appears once and the two other terminals differ in all coordinates. Conversely every monochromatic full geodesic splits at an internal vertex into such two nonempty terminal arms. The NORI1 antipodal-odd edge-color law is preserved, but plays no role in the deterministic gluing theorem; its role is to guarantee existence of the arms.

CANONICAL DECODING II (boundary tournament). Let G=K_n, F=R, φ(v)=e_v∈R^n, label edge uv by its unordered pair, and take a_{uv}=e_v−e_u with a fixed edge orientation. R=M(K_n), the exchange-root graphic matroid of U_{2,n}. Give each oriented two-edge window (a,v,c) its reversal-odd tournament bit. Two same-color terminal arms with graphic-independent union of cardinality n−1 yield a spanning tree, and the theorem yields a vertex-simple Hamiltonian tight word with at most one color switch. Conversely every such Hamiltonian one-switch word splits at a color-change pivot into two same-colored arms whose undirected edge union is a spanning tree; the monochromatic case splits at any internal vertex. This is the two-arm theorem already in Article XI, now derived from the SAME linear-root separation and graded window-reversal lemma as the cube statement.

ALL-DIMENSIONAL OBSTRUCTION (necessity of gallery-exchange richness). For every r≥3, construct a cycle G_r=C_{2r} with vertices x_j∈F_2^r obtained by successively flipping coordinates 1,2,…,r,1,2,…,r around the cycle; x_0=0 and x_{j+r}=x_j+(1,…,1). These 2r vertices are distinct, and every arc of length at most r crosses different coordinates, so G_r is isometrically embedded in Q_r. Give its edges cyclic labels 1,2,…,r,1,2,…,r and use φ(x)=x. The vector matroid of coordinate labels is the free matroid U_{r,r}; the deck map τ(x)=x+1 is free on vertices and edges. Choose edge colors c(e_j)=j modulo two for j=0,…,r−1 and c(e_{j+r})=1−c(e_j). Then c(τ e)=1−c(e), but every monochromatic edge run has length at most two. Therefore no monochromatic path has an independent resource basis (length r), and no same-color terminal arms can cover a resource basis. The model has faithful linear voltage, the full-rank resource matroid, isometric cube geometry, a free antipodal involution and odd coloring, but lacks the square swaps realizing adjacent resource permutations. Thus these properties alone cannot support the proposed two-arm packing conjecture. The cube's full adjacent-coordinate interchange structure, or a comparably strong gallery-exchange axiom, is indispensable for any positive common theorem.

RESEARCH STATUS. The linear-root separation and graded gluing are proved general theorems; the common two-arm basis-packing EXISTENCE assertion remains open. The latter, specialized to the two canonical families, is equivalent to monochromatic NORI1 and to the spanning at-most-one-switch boundary conjecture respectively. A quantitative theorem for this same two-arm object would imply the required boundary square-root guarantee, but no such universally valid theorem follows solely from faithful linear voltage and opposition parity.
