## Research note

For permutation pi of [m], choose A containing the last pi label but not first, B=complement, and let W=(pi|A,pi,pi|B). The occurrence-interlacement graph J(W) has complete cliques on A and B. For a in A,b in B, their two occurrence chords cross if and only if b precedes a in pi. Proof: every A-letter occurs once in initial block and once in middle, with orders inherited from pi; every B-letter occurs once in middle and once in final block. Thus same-block pairs alternate, whereas mixed pairs alternate only in order B-before-A. This gives edge count |E(J)|=binom(|A|,2)+binom(|B|,2)+#(B-before-A inversions). Since pi begins in B and ends in A, cross inversions number at least |A|+|B|-1=m-1. The interlacement graph is connected and has quadratically many edges. J(W) is the complete graph K_m if and only if A is a terminal segment of pi, in which case W is rho rho for one permutation rho. Every nonsuffix A deletes at least one crossing from the two-identical-sweep pattern while retaining a simple base geodesic bigon. In the known odd Q6 counterexample to identical sweeps, root 2 and red order pi=(1,5,3,2,0,4) give A=(5,2,4), B=(1,3,0) and bigon W=(5,2,4,1,5,3,2,0,4,1,3,0). Its red return sends 2 to 61. The crossing graph has 12 of the 15 possible pairs; the missing pairs are {5,3},{5,0},{2,0}. So deleting just three crossing relations from a complete interlacement pattern allows a successful bigon despite the impossibility of all repeated identical sweeps for this coloring. This is independent of nonlinear-rule count and connects the canonical simple-bigon normal form to the manuscript's commutator factorization theorem. Crossings alone do not guarantee monodromy, as the refuted two-sweep conjecture has maximal crossing density.
