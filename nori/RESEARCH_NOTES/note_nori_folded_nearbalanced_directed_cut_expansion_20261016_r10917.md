## Research note

For odd d=2m+1>=3 let G_d be the folded d-cube, with N=2^(d-1) vertices, and orient its edges so every outdegree is m or m+1.

THEOREM (directed cut expansion). For each nonempty proper S, put s=min(|S|,N-|S|). Both the number of outgoing arcs from S and the number of incoming arcs to S are at least s*(3/2-2s/N), hence at least s/2. Thus every such orientation is strongly connected and contains a directed odd cycle.

PROOF. Realize G_d as the Cayley graph on the even-parity subgroup of F2^d, generated by a_i=1+e_i. Its undirected adjacency eigenvalues are d-2|w| for representatives w of even Hamming weight. The nonconstant eigenvalues are at most d-4, so the Laplacian spectral gap equals four. For any S, the undirected cut size B(S)>=4|S|(1-|S|/N). Each vertex's outdegree-minus-indegree belongs to {-1,1}, hence the net directed flux across S has absolute value at most |S|. When |S|<=N/2, each directed cut size is at least (B(S)-|S|)/2 >=|S|(3/2-2|S|/N)>=|S|/2. For larger S apply the result to its complement. Hence every nontrivial cut has arcs in both directions, proving strong connectivity. The graph is nonbipartite because its d generators sum to zero, yielding an odd d-cycle. If a strongly connected orientation of a nonbipartite graph had only even directed cycles, vertex parity of a directed path from a fixed root would be well-defined (compare via a return directed path) and would induce a proper bipartition of the underlying graph. Therefore there must be a directed odd cycle.

LIMITATION. Directed connectivity of the quotient is not connectivity of the alternating-incidence graph. In particular the companion note note_nori_arc_digraph_eulerian_high_chromatic_no_alt_cycles_20261016 constructs strongly connected 5-regular near-balanced orientations on 4q vertices, with odd girth five, codegree at most two, full-rank unimodular adjacency, and no almost-alternating odd closed walk. They lack the folded cube's square-completion structure. Thus directed expansion and nonsingularity do not suffice for NORI1's sharper requirement of one-transit shortest odd cycles.
