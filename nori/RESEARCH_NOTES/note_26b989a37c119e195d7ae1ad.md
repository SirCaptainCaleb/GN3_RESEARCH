## Research note

For any binary edge coloring d of Q_n, define s(d) as the smallest number of changes in the color word of a rooted full geodesic. Given colorings c,d, a product geodesic restricts to full geodesics in the two factors. Deleting letters cannot increase the number of switches, proving s(c square d)>=max(s(c),s(d)). Conversely, choose optimal factor paths with p>=q switches. Interleave their successive monochromatic runs: if their first colors coincide, align corresponding runs, giving p switches; if their first colors differ, put the shorter path's first run before the first run of the other, and align its subsequent runs with the corresponding later run, giving at most p+1. Thus max(s(c),s(d))<=s(c square d)<=max(s(c),s(d))+1. For h copies of the same d, optimal paths can always be chosen with identical color words and their runs perfectly aligned. Therefore s(d to the Cartesian power h)=s(d) for every h>=1. If both c and d are antipodally odd, the antipodal image of a path has all edge colors inverted, so the two optimal factor words can be chosen to have the same initial color. Consequently s(c square d)=max(s(c),s(d)) for every pair of legal odd colorings. Thus the switch defect is max-like, sharply contrasting with the additive Hamming holonomy defect. A coloring with switch count at least two retains that count under all Cartesian powers, precluding an amplification argument from a sublinear universal switch bound to the one-switch conjecture. These statements concern arbitrary nonlinear factor colorings. Strengthening: the equality s(c square d)=max(s(c),s(d)) holds whenever at least one factor is antipodally odd. The odd factor always has an optimal geodesic starting in either prescribed color: antipodally complement any optimal path to reverse every edge color without changing the number of switches. Therefore align its starting color with the arbitrary other factor and interleave their corresponding runs, regardless of which factor has more switches.
