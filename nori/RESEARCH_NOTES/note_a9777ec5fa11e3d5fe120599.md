## Research note

**Proposition (reversal-odd rooted obstruction on seven vertices).** There exists an ordinary reversal-odd binary coloring \(b\) of ordered triples of distinct vertices of \(V=\{0,1,\ldots,6\}\) such that **every** Hamilton ordering beginning at \(0\) has at least two switches in its five-bit consecutive triple word. Nevertheless the coloring has Hamilton orderings with only one switch, so this is a rooted obstruction rather than a counterexample to the unrooted boundary conjecture.

**Construction.** For each fixed middle vertex \(h\in V\), list the other six symbols in ascending order, and list their \(\binom62=15\) unordered pairs \((a,c)\), \(a<c\), lexicographically. Prescribe \(b(a,h,c)\) for those pairs as the corresponding digits of the following table; for \(a>c\), put \(b(a,h,c)=1-b(c,h,a)\).

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
h&\text{15 bits for }(a,c),\ a<c,\ a,c\ne h\\
0&000000000000000\\
1&001110111100111\\
2&001111111111111\\
3&001110111001111\\
4&001110100100000\\
5&001110000000001\\
6&001111100000000
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]

The row at \(h=0\) is irrelevant to paths starting at \(0\) and is chosen for simplicity. The other six rows are invariant under relabeling by \((1\,2\,3)(4\,5\,6)\), together with its action on ordered triples.

**Finite proof certificate.** The following standard-library Python checks every one of the \(6!=720\) orderings starting at \(0\). It computes the minimum switch count and its distribution, and displays an unrooted one-switch witness.

\`\`\`python
from itertools import permutations, combinations
from collections import Counter

rows = [
    "000000000000000",
    "001110111100111",
    "001111111111111",
    "001110111001111",
    "001110100100000",
    "001110000000001",
    "001111100000000",
]
b = {}
for h in range(7):
    others = [v for v in range(7) if v != h]
    for digit, (a, c) in zip(rows[h], combinations(others, 2)):
        b[a, h, c] = int(digit)
        b[c, h, a] = 1 - int(digit)

def bits(path):
    return [b[path[i], path[i+1], path[i+2]]
            for i in range(len(path)-2)]

def switches(path):
    w = bits(path)
    return sum(w[i] != w[i+1] for i in range(len(w)-1))

counts = Counter(switches((0,)+p)
                 for p in permutations(range(1,7)))
assert counts == {2:144, 3:360, 4:216}
assert switches((1,0,4,5,3,6,2)) == 1
\`\`\`

The enumeration verifies the claim, and the definition enforces reversal oddness by construction. The explicit unrooted witness \((1,0,4,5,3,6,2)\) has bit word \(00111\).

**Consequence for the mixed one-factor Hamming conjecture.** The rooted five-vertex tight-path theorem cannot extend to arbitrary \(m\): in dimension \(m=7\), a prescribed starting vertex may support no one-switch Hamilton tight path even when the coloring is reversal odd. This obstruction does not refute the mixed-product conjecture, because the complete-graph starting vertex and the position of its block can vary jointly with the cube geodesic. It rules out the particular gluing proof that demands a good boundary path from every prescribed start.

The coloring was found via binary integer feasibility with a three-cycle symmetry constraint and independently verified by full finite enumeration.
