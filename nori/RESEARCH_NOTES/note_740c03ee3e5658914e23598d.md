## Research note

Let n>=4. Define vertices (a,b,s), a!=b in [n], s in F2, and arcs (a,b,s)->(b,c,1-s), a,b,c distinct. Give each arc ordinary color c and bit s; tau(a,b,s)=(b,a,s). The graph is the connected parity double cover of the ordered-pair graph; tau reverses arcs and flips bits, fixes no vertex. It is 2(n-2)-regular with color degree n-1 and incoming-monochromatic/outgoing-proper incidence. Every permutation of [n] minus {a,b} is a directed rainbow path from (a,b,s), and adjacent resource swaps end at tau-paired states. A rainbow undirected path cannot traverse forward then backward because its two edges would share the same incoming color at the turning state, hence its orientation is minus^r plus^t. Bits alternate along each arm; two outgoing edges at the fork have equal bits. Switch count is r+t-2 for r,t>0, otherwise r+t-1. At most one switch therefore forces length at most 3. This rules out local incidence and full resource-permutation access as sufficient for a linear one-switch theorem, while leaving the undoubled boundary conjecture open.
