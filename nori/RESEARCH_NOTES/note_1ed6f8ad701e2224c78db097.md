## Research note

Let \(M\) be a finite matroid, \(\Gamma(M)\) its basis-exchange graph, and \(F_B\) the bipartite fundamental exchange graph on \(B\sqcup(E(M)\setminus B)\), with \(b\sim e\) if \(B-b+e\) is a basis.

**Proposition 1 (intrinsic local characterization of partition matroids).** \(M\) is a direct sum of uniform matroids if and only if, for every basis \(B\), every connected component of \(F_B\) containing an edge is complete bipartite.

*Proof.* The connected components of \(F_B\), including isolated loops and coloops, are the matroid connected components; this follows from the fundamental-circuit characterization of matroid connectivity. For a uniform summand \(U_{r,m}\), every fundamental exchange is permitted, giving \(K_{r,m-r}\). Conversely, fix a connected nonsingleton component \(N\), a basis \(B_N\), and any \(e\in E(N)\setminus B_N\), \(b\in B_N\). Connectivity of \(F_{B_N}\) and completeness of its bipartite component imply \(B_N-b+e\) is a basis. This argument applies at every basis of \(N\). Therefore any \(r(N)\)-subset \(A\) is reachable from \(B_N\) by exchanges successively replacing elements of \(B_N\setminus A\) with elements of \(A\setminus B_N\), each exchange maintaining a basis. Hence every \(r(N)\)-subset is a basis, so \(N\) is uniform. Singleton components are uniform as well. \(\square\)

**Proposition 2 (bipartite basis-graph rigidity).** The following are equivalent: (i) \(\Gamma(M)\) is bipartite; (ii) \(\Gamma(M)\) contains no triangle; (iii) every circuit of \(M\) has size at most two and every parallel class has size at most two; (iv) \(M\) is a direct sum of copies of \(U_{1,2}\), loops, and coloops. In particular, a connected nontrivial bipartite basis graph is a hypercube.

*Proof.* If \(C\) is a circuit of size at least three, choose \(e\in C\) and extend \(C\setminus\{e\}\) to a basis \(B\). For two distinct \(b,c\in C\setminus\{e\}\), the three bases \(B,B-b+e,B-c+e\) form a triangle. A parallel class of size at least three similarly gives a basis triangle by choosing its three possible representatives. Conversely, if all circuits have size at most two, the matroid decomposes into rank-one uniform parallel classes, loops and coloops; its basis graph is a Cartesian product of complete graphs indexed by nontrivial parallel classes. This product is bipartite precisely when those classes all have size two, in which case it is a cube. \(\square\)

**Proposition 3 (no functorial basis antipode on the rank-two model).** For \(n\ge5\), every \(S_n\)-equivariant map \(f:\mathcal B(U_{2,n})\to\mathcal B(U_{2,n})\) is the identity. For \(M_n=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n U_{1,2}\), the only \(\operatorname{Aut}(M_n)\)-equivariant maps of its bases are the identity and the coordinatewise complement.

*Proof.* Fix \(B=\{1,2\}\). Its stabilizer \(S_2\times S_{n-2}\) fixes only \(B\) among all two-element subsets for \(n\ge5\), so equivariance forces \(f(B)=B\), hence \(f=\mathrm{id}\) by transitivity. For \(M_n\), the stabilizer of a chosen basis permutes its \(n\) parallel classes and fixes only that basis and its coordinatewise complementary basis. Transitivity again determines \(f\). \(\square\)

**Corollary (orientation-gauge obstruction).** A function \(\phi:\mathcal B(M)\to\mathbb F_2\) satisfying \(\phi(B')+\phi(B)=1\) on every exchange edge exists precisely for the matroids in Proposition 2. On such a basis graph, each unoriented edge coloring \(c\) can be converted into a reversal-odd orientation coloring \(d(B,B')=c(\{B,B'\})+\phi(B)\). Thus this parity-gauge conversion works on the NORI1 cube, while its extension to \(U_{2,n}\) is obstructed by basis triangles. Independently, Proposition 3 shows that no isomorphism-natural fixed-point-free involution on bases can extend the cube antipode to \(U_{2,n}\) for \(n\ge5\). A common polarization therefore has to reside on exchange flags or a covering of the turn system, rather than on the bases alone.

**Scope.** These statements identify necessary distinctions in any intrinsic unification. They do not establish a common quantitative witness conjecture. In particular, the class of partition matroids characterized by Proposition 1 retains both target matroids, but local complete-bipartite exchange geometry alone supplies neither the cube antipodal path guarantee nor the tournament square-root guarantee.
