## Research note

**Proposition (centrally symmetric affine-basis obstruction).** For each odd integer \(m\ge3\), there is a centrally symmetric spanning configuration \(X\subset\mathbb R^m\), a connected vertex- and edge-transitive graph \(\Gamma\) on \(X\), and an antipodally odd undirected-edge coloring for which every complete affine-basis path has \(m-1\) switches.

*Construction.* Set \(X=\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_m\}\) and cyclically list
\[
v_0=e_1,\ldots,v_{m-1}=e_m,\quad
v_m=-e_1,\ldots,v_{2m-1}=-e_m.
\]
Take \(\Gamma\) to be the \(2m\)-cycle on these vertices (indices modulo \(2m\)). The antipode is \(\tau(v_i)=v_{i+m}\). The signed Coxeter rotation \(t:v_i\mapsto v_{i+1}\) acts transitively on its vertices and edges, and \(t^m=\tau\).

Color edge \(v_iv_{i+1}\) by \(c_i=i\bmod2\). Since \(m\) is odd, \(c_{i+m}=1-c_i\), establishing antipodal oddness. Every non-backtracking \(m\)-edge path runs along \(m\) consecutive cycle edges and so has alternating colors, with \(m-1\) switches.

Every such path is complete in the intrinsic affine-basis sense. Its \(m+1\) vertices contain one antipodal pair \(\{\pm e_j\}\) and one signed vector from each other dimension. They are affinely independent: in an affine dependence \(\sum a_r v_r=0\), \(\sum a_r=0\), the singleton-dimension coefficients vanish, and the two coefficients at the antipodal pair coincide; the zero-sum condition then makes them vanish as well. Therefore the \(m\) successive displacement vectors form a basis. Conversely a basis path cannot revisit a vertex, and hence traverses an arc of the cycle, so every complete path has \(m-1\) switches. \(\square\)

**Implication.** Basis-path completeness, antipodal symmetry and odd coloring, a signed Coxeter rotation with central half-turn, and vertex/edge transitivity together do not force a one-switch path. Unlike the full cube and simplex geometries, this cycle has no independent adjacent-exchange operations connecting alternative orders of basis steps. A successful unified class must capture rich full incidence geometry (in particular exchange completeness) rather than merely a centrally symmetric Coxeter orbit.
