### Bounded central structure and the zero-root theorem

Consider a positively balanced exact-root carrier \(F\) all of whose chambers have positive deficiency.

**Bounded central block theorem.** If \(F\) contains no zero root, then every varying exact-root coordinate is controlled by one face block \(B\) with
\[
|B|\le4.
\]
After the exterior block orders irrelevant to the root data are fixed, at most ten actual vertices determine every varying root label.

The strengthened classification of the nonzero configurations has the following consequence.

**Zero-root theorem.** If
\[
\kappa_2(H)\ge2,
\]
then every positively balanced exact-root carrier contains a chamber with
\[
p=c.
\]
Indeed, in the nonzero classification every case with central corridor length two has deficiency at most one. The only remaining four-vertex case has \(m=2s+4\); the distinguished-vertex structure permits a chamber with \(p,c\ge s+1\), and the global deficiency bound \(m-p-c\ge2\) forces \(p=c=s+1\), contrary to nonzero-rootness.

Thus for deletion distance at least two the exact-root route reaches a balanced canonical partial cover
\[
P\mid X\mid Q,\qquad |P|=|Q|,
\]
although \(X\) may be nonempty. The nonzero exact-root branch is therefore not a remaining large-scale obstruction.
