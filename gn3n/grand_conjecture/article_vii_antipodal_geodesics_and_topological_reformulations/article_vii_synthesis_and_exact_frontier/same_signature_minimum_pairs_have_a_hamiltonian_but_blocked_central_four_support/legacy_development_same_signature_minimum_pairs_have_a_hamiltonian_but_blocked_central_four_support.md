## Development

Let {x,y} be a minimum deletion pair for H, let H-{x,y}=P|Q with terminal vertices b=p_r and c=q_s, and suppose x,y have the same terminal signature tau(z)=h(b,z,c). Then K={b,c,x,y} is Hamiltonian. If tau(x)=tau(y)=1, both (b,x,c) and (b,y,c) are tight. Exactly one of (x,c,y),(y,c,x) is tight by boundary antisymmetry, so K has a Hamilton order b,u,c,v for a suitable ordering (u,v) of (x,y). If tau(x)=tau(y)=0, boundary antisymmetry gives both (c,x,b),(c,y,b) tight; choosing (u,v) so that (u,b,v) is tight gives a Hamilton order c,u,b,v. Thus the same-signature pair always supplies a Hamiltonian central four-support. However the canonical order is rooted on the blocked side: minimum-hole synchronization gives h(p_{r-1},b,u)=0 in the first case and h(q_{s-1},c,u)=0 in the second. Hence the canonical four-support cannot simply be appended to the inherited terminal edge of the corresponding complementary path. The genuine residue is therefore rerooting of an already Hamiltonian four-support, not existence of a Hamiltonian support.
