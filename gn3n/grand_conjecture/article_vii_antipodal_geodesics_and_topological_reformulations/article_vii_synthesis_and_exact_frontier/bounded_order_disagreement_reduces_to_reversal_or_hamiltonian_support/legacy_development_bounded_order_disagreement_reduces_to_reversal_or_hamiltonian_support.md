## Bounded Hamilton-order disagreement reduces to reversal or Hamiltonian support

Let P,Q be tight paths of order at least three whose common vertices occur in different relative orders.

By Section 4 of [[pathcalc01]], either there is a reversed common ordered edge, a tight triple reversing an ordered edge at an intersection, or a vertex-simple tight cycle.

A reversed common edge already yields a reversing triple: take any consecutive tight triple of P containing that edge. The same edge occurs oppositely in Q.

A tight cycle yields a Hamiltonian support by opening the cyclic order at any point; all consecutive triples remain tight.

Therefore any relative-order disagreement between two tight paths of order at least three yields either a positioned reversing tight triple or a Hamiltonian support contained in their union.

For the five-deletion paths used in the Article VII six-label packets, the Hamiltonian support has order at most six.

Hence [[the_four_endpoint_incidence_system_cannot_remain_featureless]] has only two essential bounded outputs: a Hamiltonian support of order at most six, or a positioned reversing triple. Order disagreement is not an independent frontier object.
