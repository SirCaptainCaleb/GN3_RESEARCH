## Four-of-six equality endpoints force local order disagreement

Retain branch (2) of [[four_endpoints_force_a_threefold_core_or_two_double_hole_equality_endpoints]]. Thus for at least two exposed endpoints \(e\), the six-set
\[
U_e=Y\cup\{e\}
\]
is non-Hamiltonian and has exactly four Hamiltonian five-deletions.

Write
\[
Y=\{x,y,r,s,t\},
\]
where \(x,y\) are the distinguished hole labels and
\[
A_e=G_e\cap\{r,s,t\}=\{r\}.
\]
Then the four good deletion labels are exactly
\[
e,\ x,\ y,\ r,
\]
while
\[
U_e-\{s\},\qquad U_e-\{t\}
\]
are non-Hamiltonian.

The four-good-deletion disagreement theorem used in [[line_rooted_small_support_descent_from_deletion_cover_lifts]] applies directly to \(U_e\): a non-Hamiltonian six-set with four Hamiltonian five-deletions cannot choose Hamilton orders on all four good deletions compatibly on their common supports. Hence two of the four good deletion states have relative-order disagreement.

Therefore every equality endpoint supplies a bounded **order-disturbance certificate** on six labels. In particular, branch (2) of the four-endpoint theorem is not a new static extremal configuration: it enters the existing disagreement/reversal machinery.

Combining with branch (1) gives the sharper dichotomy:

> Every hard rooted five-component either has a hole-preserving four-core accepting at least three exposed endpoints, or it has at least two six-label endpoint packets carrying forced Hamilton-order disagreement.

In the first branch, failed neutral transports give a single ordinary label reversing second-layer edges at three exposed ends, as in [[repeated_hole_preserving_cores_force_transport_or_synchronized_second_layer_reversals]]. In the second branch, the obstruction has already become a positioned local disturbance on two bounded packets.

Thus the five-component handoff no longer has an unconstrained four-of-six equality residue. Its two surviving mechanisms are synchronized reversal and bounded order disagreement, both already native to the Article III–V transport machinery.
