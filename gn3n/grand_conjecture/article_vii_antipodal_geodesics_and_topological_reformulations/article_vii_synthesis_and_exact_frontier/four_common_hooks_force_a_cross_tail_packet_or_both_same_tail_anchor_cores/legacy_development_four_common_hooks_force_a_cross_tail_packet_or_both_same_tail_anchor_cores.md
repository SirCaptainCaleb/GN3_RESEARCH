## Development

Let a,w be an ordered anchor pair and let P_0={p_1,p_2}, Q_0={q_1,q_2} be four distinct exterior labels satisfying h(a,w,z)=1 for every z in P_0 union Q_0. Then either there is a Hamiltonian support of order four or five containing {a,w} and at least one label from each of P_0,Q_0, or both four-sets {a,w,p_1,p_2} and {a,w,q_1,q_2} are Hamiltonian. Proof: apply the ordered-anchor three-hook theorem to {p_1,p_2,q_1}. If its Hamiltonian output is a five-set, or a four-set using q_1 together with one p_i, the cross-tail conclusion holds. Otherwise the only possible four-set output uses p_1,p_2, so {a,w,p_1,p_2} is Hamiltonian. Next apply the theorem to {p_1,q_1,q_2}. Again any five-set or mixed four-set gives the cross-tail conclusion; otherwise {a,w,q_1,q_2} is Hamiltonian. In a maximal-support normalization take (a,w)=(s_2,s_1), P_0={p_1,p_m}, Q_0={q_1,q_t}. Maximality supplies the four common-hook hypotheses. Hence the endpoint rectangle arises directly from the same ordered-anchor hook geometry as the rooted-corridor obstruction: either a cross-tail anchored Hamiltonian packet occurs, or both same-tail anchor four-cores occur.
