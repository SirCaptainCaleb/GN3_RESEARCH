# Recent minimum-pair seed theorem supersedes the opposite-hole-only SAT frontier — preserved pre-item development

Revision 1704 changes the interpretation of the twelve-label SAT residue. Let {x,y} be a minimum deletion pair with H-{x,y}=P|Q, P ending (...,a,b), and Q beginning (q_1,...). The new cross-boundary seed theorem guarantees that at least one of F={a,b,q_1,x,y}, K_1={a,b,x,y}, K_2={b,q_1,x,y} is Hamiltonian, and every such chosen seed contains both holes and has complement covered by inherited contiguous intervals of P,Q. The symmetric statement holds at the opposite Q-to-P seam. Hence the earlier SAT description as an opposite-hole-only frontier is too strong: although the particular packets forced by failure of P.Q and Q.P may choose opposite holes, each seam independently contains a bounded Hamiltonian seed containing both x and y. The correct finite frontier is therefore rooted conversion of these guaranteed both-hole seeds. The SAT model should add selector clauses Ham(F) or Ham(K_1) or Ham(K_2) at each seam and test whether every permitted Hamilton order of the selected seed can avoid an ambient-compatible splice. Seed-preserving maximalization may then be applied while retaining both holes and the inherited interval geometry. The old opposite-hole split remains relevant only as a description of the failure-generated rooted packets, not as the global terminal obstruction.
