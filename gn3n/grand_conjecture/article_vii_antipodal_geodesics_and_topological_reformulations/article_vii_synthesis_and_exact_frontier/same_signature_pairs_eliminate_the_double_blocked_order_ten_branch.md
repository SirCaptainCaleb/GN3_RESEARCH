## Development

Let {x,y} be a pair of minimum-hole vertices with the same initial and terminal signatures, and let G=H-(X-{x,y}) be the induced genuine two-deletion state. Put K_I={x,y,p_1,q_1} and K_T={x,y,p_s,q_t}. The bidirectional five-path root-advance theorem shows more than the endpoint-cap dichotomy requires. At the initial boundary, if the common initial signature is 1 then {p_2,p_1,x,y,q_1}=K_I union {p_2} is Hamiltonian; if it is 0 then {q_2,q_1,x,y,p_1}=K_I union {q_2} is Hamiltonian. Hence one of the two initial five-extensions is always Hamiltonian. The same argument at the terminal boundary shows that one of K_T union {p_{s-1}} and K_T union {q_{t-1}} is always Hamiltonian. Therefore the double-blocked hypothesis in the order-ten endpoint-cap theorem never occurs for a same-two-end-signature pair. Every such genuine two-deletion core enters the five-component endpoint-handoff interface from both opposite boundaries. The order-ten cap remains relevant only to more general bidirectionally compatible cores not supplied by same-signature minimum-hole pairs.
