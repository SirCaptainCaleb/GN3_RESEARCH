## Development

In [[five_component_endpoint_overlap_forces_two_layer_tail_synchronization]], the claimed first-edge reversals are not justified by the endpoint-replacement truncation theorem. Let T=(t_1,...,t_m), let r be the omitted five-component label, and suppose (X-{r}) union {t_1} is Hamiltonian while (T-{t_1}) union {r} is non-Hamiltonian. The inherited truncated path is (t_2,...,t_m). Noninsertability of r in this path forces h(r,t_2,t_3)=0 and hence h(t_3,t_2,r)=1. It does not involve t_1 and therefore does not imply h(t_2,t_1,r)=1. Symmetrically, failure of the exchange at t_m forces h(r,t_{m-1},t_{m-2})=1, but not h(r,t_m,t_{m-1})=1. Thus the valid conclusion is: every long tail has a common omitted label for its two endpoint cores; if both associated neutral exchanges fail, the same label reverses the two second-layer end edges h(t_3,t_2,r)=1 and h(r,t_{m-1},t_{m-2})=1. Any first-edge reversal requires an additional hypothesis, such as minimum-hole synchronization when r is itself a minimum-hole label. The stronger four-reversal statement in the cited addendum must not be used without such input.
