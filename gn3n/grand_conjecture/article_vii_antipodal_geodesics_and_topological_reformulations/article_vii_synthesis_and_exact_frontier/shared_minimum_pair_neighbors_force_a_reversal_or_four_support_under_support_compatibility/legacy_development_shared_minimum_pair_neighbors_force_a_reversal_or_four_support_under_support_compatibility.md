## Development

Let H satisfy kappa_2(H)=2 and let x have two distinct neighbors y,z in the minimum-pair graph. Put J=H-x. Then J has no two-cover, while J-y and J-z each have a two-cover. Choose two-covers F_y of J-y and F_z of J-z. Suppose F_y and F_z are support-compatible on their common domain. If their common-support orders disagree, the order-disagreement theorem gives a tight triple reversing an edge of one of the displayed support paths. Otherwise the covers are compatible. The insertion-slot lemma then puts y and z into the same common support, in equal or adjacent slots. Adjacent slots again give a reversing tight triple. Equal endpoint slots are impossible: exactly one of the two boundary orientations of y,z at the endpoint is tight, and adjoining both labels in that order would two-cover J. Hence an equal slot must be internal, say between consecutive common vertices u,v. Then (u,y,v) and (u,z,v) are tight, so the parallel-middle four-path lemma makes {u,v,y,z} a Hamiltonian four-support. Therefore any support-compatible pair of deletion covers associated with two neighbors of x forces either a displayed-edge reversal or a Hamiltonian four-support containing both neighboring hole labels. Consequently a difficult minimum-pair cycle must either expose such bounded local structure or make its deletion covers change support partitions incompatibly as one moves around the cycle.
