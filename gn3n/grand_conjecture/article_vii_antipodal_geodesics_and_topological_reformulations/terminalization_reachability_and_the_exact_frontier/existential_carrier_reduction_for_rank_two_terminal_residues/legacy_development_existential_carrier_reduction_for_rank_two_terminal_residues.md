# Existential carrier reduction for rank-two terminal residues — preserved pre-item development

## Existential carriers make every non-saturating rank-two residue automatic

The corrected relative-index target is the existential depth-filtered face space
\[
Y_{r+1}=\Delta(\mathcal P_{r+1}),
\]
where a proper permutahedron face belongs to \(\mathcal P_{r+1}\) as soon as it contains at least one chamber whose selected witness has depth \(>r\). It is not required that every chamber of that carrier face be outward. This makes rank-two coherence substantially easier than protected same-face escape.

Fix a square or braid-hexagon residue \(R\) in the separator at depth \(r\), and for every terminal sign-flip edge of \(R\) choose an outward replacement chamber supplied by terminal-support surgery. Let \(J_R\) be the union of the positional terminal intervals on which these replacements differ from their source chambers. Freeze every vertex position outside \(J_R\), and let \(G_R\) be the permutahedron face obtained by allowing the vertices occupying \(J_R\) to vary freely inside one block.

**Lemma (proper-span carrier).** If \(J_R\neq V(H)\), then every chosen terminal replacement chamber for \(R\) lies in the same proper face \(G_R\), and
\[
G_R\in\mathcal P_{r+1}.
\]
Consequently the terminal edge assignments on \(R\) extend across \(R\) inside the cone \(\operatorname{st}_{Y_{r+1}}(G_R)\).

**Proof.** Each terminal surgery changes only the order on its terminal positional interval and leaves all exterior positions fixed, so every replacement chamber lies in \(G_R\). Since \(J_R\neq V(H)\), at least one exterior positional relation is frozen, hence \(G_R\) is a proper permutahedron face. Every selected replacement chamber is outward by the terminal-support surgery lemma, so \(G_R\) contains a depth-\(>r\) chamber and therefore is a vertex of \(Y_{r+1}\). For each replacement chamber \(\sigma\subseteq G_R\), the edge \(\sigma<G_R\) is a simplex of \(Y_{r+1}\). Thus all selected images lie in the star of \(G_R\), which is contractible, and the rank-two boundary map extends. \(\square\)

This observation also clarifies why the compatible one-vertex-exchange lemma is sufficient when the union span would otherwise be all vertices. The two covers for supports \(T+x\) and \(T+y\) share one Hamiltonian side \(B\). Choosing the replacement orders with the same displayed order on \(B\) puts both replacements in a proper ordered-partition face that keeps \(B\) separated from the complementary block. No compatibility of the internal orders on \(A+x\) and \(A+y\) is required, because membership in \(\mathcal P_{r+1}\) is existential.

### Size of the only surviving residues

The terminal interval for one sign-flip edge has at most ten vertices.

For a braid residue generated by adjacent swaps \(s_i,s_{i+1}\), the three moving positions can meet at most one endpoint of a terminal interval. Passing around the hexagon therefore changes the terminal support only by exchanging vertices through one endpoint, so
\[
|J_R|\le 11.
\]
Hence every braid residue is automatically coherent unless the ambient tournament has order \(11\) and the residue saturates all vertices. In the one-vertex-exchange subcase, the compatible-exchange lemma supplies a proper common-side carrier even at saturation.

For a commuting square, if at most one generator meets an endpoint of the terminal interval then again \(|J_R|\le 11\) and the same argument closes it. The only square not covered by the proper-span argument is the genuinely two-ended case in which the two disjoint generators exchange one vertex through each endpoint. Then
\[
|J_R|\le 12.
\]
Thus a support-saturating obstruction can occur only at ambient order \(11\) or \(12\).

Therefore all non-saturating rank-two residues are formally closed by the existential carrier geometry. The only possible remaining cases are a support-saturating order-11 braid and a support-saturating order-11 or order-12 two-ended square. Reversal exchanges the two endpoint orientations, so only one orientation of each residue type needs analysis.
