## Development

## Exact fundamental-group kernel for moving the following label

Use the chamber-defined enlargement from [[moving_the_following_label_has_an_exact_first_homology_kernel]]. Namely B is finite, z is outside B, delta(z)=1, and D is the subcomplex of P(B union {z}) whose allowed chambers are exactly those ending in a label w with delta(w)=0 or having a tight final triple. Put D_0=D intersect (P(B)|{z}).

Let G be the mutual terminal-pair graph before z on its active terminal labels, K its clique complex, and S the active labels with zero exit status. Let K_S be the induced clique subcomplex. Assume G is connected and D_0 is nonempty. The terminal-pair equivalence identifies pi_1(D_0) with pi_1(K), with a fixed choice of basepoint.

Let N be the normal subgroup generated by the images of the fundamental groups of all connected components of K_S, transported to the basepoint along paths in K. This normal subgroup is independent of the transport paths. If K_S is empty or every component is simply connected, N is trivial.

**Theorem.** The kernel of
pi_1(D_0) -> pi_1(D)
is exactly N. Moreover the image of this map is a retract of pi_1 of the component of D containing D_0, and is isomorphic to pi_1(K)/N.

This strengthens the earlier first-homology kernel formula. It detects noncommutative loop obstructions as well as their abelian images.

**Proof.** Every component of the old subcomplex A consisting of faces whose terminal label before z lies in S maps null-homotopically into D. The same merging and endpoint-subset construction as in the first half of the earlier homology proof gives a null-homotopy of all of A into the contractible locus ending in S. The natural terminal-label cover identifies its components and their inclusion maps with those of K_S. Hence N lies in the kernel.

For the reverse inclusion and retraction, choose a spanning tree L in G containing a spanning tree of every connected component of the induced graph G[S]. Such L exists because those trees form a forest. Let t_u be its path from the basepoint to vertex u. Put R=pi_1(K)/N. To an oriented mutual edge u->v associate
gamma_uv = [t_u * (u,v) * t_v^{-1}] in R.
Then gamma_vu=gamma_uv^{-1}. For every clique triangle uvw,
gamma_uv gamma_vw gamma_wu=1.
If uv is an edge of G[S], then gamma_uv=1: the tree path between u and v lies in the chosen spanning tree of their S-component, so its based loop belongs to a conjugate of pi_1(K_S).

Assign an element of R to every oriented edge of D. Give value gamma_uv to an edge keeping final label z and changing the terminal label immediately before z from u to v by swapping those final two B labels. Give value 1 to every other edge.

The ordered product around every two-face is 1:
- In a commuting square, the two supported opposite edges contribute inverse values.
- In a hexagon on the three positions immediately before the fixed final z, all chambers can be allowed only when the three labels form a mutual clique. The supported transitions trace its triangle, so their product is 1.
- In a hexagon on the final three positions {u,v,z}, a supported mutual uv edge can occur in an entirely allowed hexagon only when delta(u)=delta(v)=0. Indeed the orders ending in u and v include the boundary reversals of the tight orders ending in z. Thus uv is in G[S], and its value gamma_uv is 1.
No other two-face touches a supported edge.

Consequently multiplication of the edge values defines a homomorphism rho:pi_1(D)->R. This uses only the ordinary edge-path presentation: backtracking cancels inverse values, and every cellular two-boundary has product 1. Higher cells impose no additional fundamental-group relations.

On D_0 this assignment is the quotient homomorphism pi_1(K)->R under the terminal-pair equivalence. Changes within a fixed terminal-label sector have value 1; a change of terminal label traverses the corresponding mutual edge. The cover's clique faces enforce exactly the triangle relations. Therefore
rho composed with [pi_1(D_0)->pi_1(D)]
is the quotient onto R.

It follows that a source loop in the kernel must already lie in N. The opposite inclusion was proved above, so the kernel is N. The induced injection R->pi_1(D) has left inverse rho, proving the retraction assertion. QED.

### Consequences

If the induced complex K_S has simply connected components, pi_1(D_0) injects into pi_1(D). In particular a forest on the labels with zero exit status cannot remove any old fundamental-group obstruction, regardless of unspecified triples elsewhere in the free block.

More generally, a proposed enlargement kills an old loop precisely when its class lies in the normal closure of loops carried by labels with zero exit status. This is a finite group-theoretic criterion for the pair-locus interface.

The theorem does not determine higher homotopy of D. A trivial fundamental-group image need not make the inclusion of a higher-dimensional source null-homotopic. For a source homotopy equivalent to a connected graph, however, triviality of its fundamental-group image is sufficient for null-homotopy, because the map can be reduced to a bouquet of loops and each loop filled individually.
