## Development

## The terminal edge-surgery coherence lemma

The remaining square/hexagon problem collapses once one uses the way the orientation on the fixed witness path was defined.

Let (u(pi)in E(T_m)) denote the selected **unsigned** witness edge of a bad chamber. The local-witness construction fixes an external antipodal gauge (g(pi)in{pm1}), for example the relative-order sign of two fixed vertices (a,b), and orients the selected witness edge by this gauge. Thus, on chambers with the same selected unsigned edge (e_r),
[
ell(pi)=g(pi)e_r.
]
This is exactly the mechanism used in [[a_local_pure_carrier_shows_same_face_escape_is_false]], where swapping the two gauge vertices changes the two chambers from (+e_r) to (-e_r).

### Gauge-flip edges in rank two

An adjacent transposition changes (g) if and only if it swaps the two gauge vertices (a,b). Therefore a Coxeter edge can be a terminal sign-flip edge
[
+e_rlongleftrightarrow -e_r
]
only if that edge swaps (a) and (b).

**Lemma (two gauge edges).** In every rank-two Coxeter residue there are at most two terminal sign-flip edges.

For a commuting square, if one generator swaps (a,b), the two edges of that generator are the only gauge-flip edges; they are opposite. The other generator cannot swap the same pair because its positions are disjoint.

For a braid hexagon on three moving labels, the unordered pair ({a,b}) is swapped exactly twice while one goes around the (S_3) hexagon; those two edges are opposite. If one or both gauge vertices are not among the three moving labels, there are no gauge-flip edges. A terminal sign-flip edge is a subset of these gauge-flip edges, since it must additionally retain the same unsigned selected witness (e_r). (square)

Hence a rank-two residue has zero, one, or two terminal surgery vertices to reconcile, never a three- or four-way compatibility problem.

### Ten-position normalization

Use [[ten_position_normalization_of_terminal_surgery]]. For the fixed unsigned witness edge (e_r), choose the same contiguous ten-position normalization window (J_r) for all terminal occurrences in the residue. If (nle10), the grand conjecture is already closed by the finite theorem, so assume (nge11).

For each terminal sign-flip edge (E), either endpoint chamber may be used as the source of the normalized terminal surgery: replacing the order on (J_r) by a Hamiltonian (5|5) two-cover gives an outward chamber of depth (>r).

If there is at most one terminal sign-flip edge in a square or hexagon, its chosen outward chamber gives a contractible target star and there is nothing to check. Assume there are two, (E,E').

### Commuting squares

Let the square generators be (s_i,s_j), (|i-j|>1). The two terminal sign-flip edges are the opposite edges corresponding to the generator that swaps the gauge pair. The other generator, say (s_j), carries (E) to (E').

Choose corresponding endpoint chambers of (E,E'), so that the gauge-generator state is the same on both. Compare their ten-position supports.

1. If (s_j) is entirely inside (J_r), the support vertex set is unchanged. Choose the same Hamiltonian (5|5) partition and the same displayed path orders. The two surgeries therefore have a common outward replacement chamber after forgetting the irrelevant source order.

2. If (s_j) is entirely outside (J_r), perform the same normalized surgery on both source chambers. The two outward replacements differ only by the commuting adjacent transposition (s_j), so they lie in one Coxeter edge, hence in a proper face belonging to (mathcal P_{r+1}).

3. If (s_j) crosses one endpoint of (J_r), the two support sets have the form
[
Tcup{x},qquad Tcup{y},qquad |T|=9.
]
By [[compatible_two_covers_under_a_one_vertex_terminal_support_exchange]], write
[
T=Asqcup B,qquad |A|=4,quad |B|=5,
]
so that (B,A+x,A+y) are Hamiltonian. Use the same displayed Hamilton order on (B) in both surgeries. At a right-end exchange choose
[
Bmid(A+x),qquad Bmid(A+y),
]
and at a left-end exchange use the reversed symmetric placement. The resulting full replacement chambers lie in a common proper ordered-partition face: outside (J_r) all unaffected positions are frozen, the common (B)-order is frozen, and the six vertices (Acup{x,y}) form the only varying block. This face contains outward chambers, so it is a vertex of (mathcal P_{r+1}), and its star in (Y_{r+1}) is contractible.

Thus every commuting square is coherent. Notice that even if the gauge generator crosses the opposite endpoint, choosing corresponding endpoints of (E,E') holds that exchange state fixed; only the non-gauge generator must be reconciled. There is therefore no four-support (2	imes2) compatibility problem.

### Braid hexagons

Now let the residue be generated by (s_i,s_{i+1}). If there are two terminal sign-flip edges, they are the two opposite edges on which the gauge pair is swapped.

If the three moving positions are all inside or all outside (J_r), the same-support or disjoint-commutation arguments above apply.

Otherwise the boundary of (J_r) cuts the three moving positions into one vertex on one side and two on the other. The possible ten-position support sets differ only by which moving label occupies the unique position across that endpoint. The two opposite gauge-flip edges have source endpoints that may be chosen so that their normalized supports are either equal or differ by exactly one vertex. Concretely, after naming the moving labels (a,b,c) with (a,b) the gauge pair, the two gauge-swap edges in the (S_3) hexagon are opposite; choosing on the crossing edge the endpoint that contains the same gauge label as the support on the opposite edge leaves only (c) exchanged across the boundary.

Hence the two chosen supports are again either identical or of the form
[
T+x,qquad T+y,qquad |T|=9.
]
The identical-support case is immediate. In the one-vertex-exchange case apply the compatible-exchange lemma exactly as in the square case. The two outward replacements lie in a common proper next-depth carrier face, whose star is contractible. Therefore every braid hexagon is coherent.

### Equivariance

Make the choices on one representative of each reversal orbit of terminal sign-flip edges and rank-two residues. On the reversed orbit use the reversed replacement chamber and reversed common carrier. Reversal preserves witness depth and sends (+e_r) to (-e_r), while the ten-position normalization and the compatible-exchange lemma are symmetric under reversal. Hence the entire carrier assignment is equivariant.

### Conclusion

Every terminal sign-flip edge has an outward replacement. On each rank-two Coxeter residue, the replacements can be chosen in one contractible next-depth carrier. The type-(A) Coxeter relations are generated by commuting squares and braid hexagons, so the rank-two carrier compatibility extends by the usual Coxeter-cell/acyclic-carrier induction to an equivariant map
[
S_rlongrightarrow Y_{r+1}.
]

Therefore the terminal edge-surgery coherence lemma holds.

Combining this with the separator inequality gives
[
gamma(Y_{r+1})ge gamma(Y_r)-1
]
at every witness depth. There are (m-2) witness depths and (gamma(Y_1)=m+1), so the final witness-free level is nonempty. A witness-free chamber avoids (001,011,0101), hence satisfies the exact two-cover criterion.

Thus the Article VII route closes the grand conjecture:
