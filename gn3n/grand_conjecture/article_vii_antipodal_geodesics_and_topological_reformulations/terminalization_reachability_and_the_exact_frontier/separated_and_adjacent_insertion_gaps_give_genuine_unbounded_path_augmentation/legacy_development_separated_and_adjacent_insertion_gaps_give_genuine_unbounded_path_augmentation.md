# Separated and adjacent insertion gaps give genuine unbounded path augmentation — preserved pre-item development

## Development

Let H be a boundary 3-tournament with maximum tight-path order r, and let D=(d_1,...,d_{r-1}) be a displayed tight path. Number its insertion gaps 0,...,r-1, including both endpoints. For y outside D let I(y) be the set of gaps at which inserting y into this displayed word gives a tight r-path. This is an ordered-path notion; Hamiltonicity of the support D+y does not imply I(y) is nonempty.

Separated-gap augmentation. If distinct x,y have i in I(x), j in I(y), and |i-j|>=2, insert both vertices into these gaps. Every consecutive triple is either inherited from D or from one of the two single insertions: no consecutive triple contains both new vertices. Thus the resulting word is a genuine tight (r+1)-path, contradicting maximality. This uses the sufficiency of the triple-local path definition and retains the entire old path.

Adjacent-gap augmentation. Suppose i in I(x) and i+1 in I(y). Put b=d_{i+1}. The simultaneous insertion has local word (...,x,b,y,...) and is tight if and only if h(x,b,y)=1; all other triples are inherited from the separate insertion witnesses. Therefore maximality forces h(x,b,y)=0 and h(y,b,x)=1. If x and y both belong to both adjacent insertion groups, the exchanged placement (...,y,b,x,...) is tight by boundary reversal, again contradicting maximality. Consequently adjacent insertion groups have intersection of size at most one. These statements include endpoint gaps.

Exact localization. Assume at least two distinct exterior vertices have nonempty I(y). Let p and q be the least and greatest occupied gap indices. If q-p>=2, the labels occupying p and q must be the same vertex x, by separated-gap augmentation. Any other insertable vertex y must occupy a gap within distance one of both p and q. Hence q-p=2, every other insertable vertex occupies only p+1, and x is the unique vertex occupying either extreme gap. Otherwise all occupied gaps lie in one gap or two adjacent gaps. This is a classification of all insertion records for one fixed displayed order, not a bounded-order search.

Endpoint exclusion in the odd uniform residue. Now assume n=2r+1, every r-set is Hamiltonian, and every (r+1)-set is non-Hamiltonian. At most r-1 exterior vertices can fit at either endpoint of any displayed D. For example, if x,y both prepend D, the word (x,y,D) is tight except possibly at (x,y,d_1). Maximality forces h(x,y,d_1)=h(y,x,d_1)=0. Reversal therefore gives h(d_1,x,y)=h(d_1,y,x)=1. If r distinct roots prepended D, take a Hamiltonian r-path on those roots. Prepending d_1 to that root path gives a tight (r+1)-path, contradiction. For the terminal endpoint, failed words (D,x,y) similarly give h(x,y,d_{r-1})=1 for all ordered distinct roots, so append d_{r-1} to a Hamiltonian r-root path.

Frontier. This proves actual augmentation whenever two insertion witnesses occupy separated gaps, or whenever two roots share two adjacent gaps. It leaves same-gap and directed adjacent-gap obstructions, the exceptional one-root three-gap case, and especially the passage from support Hamiltonicity to compatible displayed orders. No spanning two-cover or termination theorem is claimed. In the odd uniform residue, any tight (r+1)-path would already suffice: its r-vertex complement is Hamiltonian.
