## Development

Let A and M be vertex-disjoint tight paths, let uv be any displayed edge of A, and let w=m_j be a vertex of M. No endpoint hypothesis on uv is needed. First suppose h(w,u,v)=1. If w has two displayed successors m_{j+1},m_{j+2}, then exactly one of h(m_{j+1},w,u) and h(u,w,m_{j+1}) is tight. In the first case (m_{j+1},w,u,v) is a Hamiltonian four-path; in the second case (u,w,m_{j+1},m_{j+2}) is a Hamiltonian four-path. Hence, outside the Hamiltonian-four-support branch, every carrier w satisfying h(w,u,v)=1 must lie among the final two vertices of M. Dually suppose h(v,u,w)=1. If w has two displayed predecessors m_{j-2},m_{j-1}, then exactly one of h(u,w,m_{j-1}) and h(m_{j-1},w,u) is tight. In the first orientation (v,u,w,m_{j-1}) is a Hamiltonian four-path; in the second (m_{j-2},m_{j-1},w,u) is a Hamiltonian four-path. Therefore, outside the Hamiltonian-four-support branch, every carrier w satisfying h(v,u,w)=1 must lie among the first two vertices of M. Thus an arbitrary displayed-edge reversal has an oriented carrier-side constraint: carrier-first reversals can occur only in the final two positions of the carrier path, while carrier-third reversals can occur only in its first two positions, unless a Hamiltonian K4 is already forced.
