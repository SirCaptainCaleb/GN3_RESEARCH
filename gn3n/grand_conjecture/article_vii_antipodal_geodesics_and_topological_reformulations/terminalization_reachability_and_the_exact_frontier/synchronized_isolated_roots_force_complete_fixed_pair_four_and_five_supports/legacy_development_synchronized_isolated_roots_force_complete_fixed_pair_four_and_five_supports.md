# Synchronized isolated roots force complete fixed-pair four-supports — preserved pre-item development

## Development

Retain the quiet synchronized branch of an isolated-root extension star. Let D=(d_1,...,d_m) be the common Hamilton order, and suppose x and every y in Y insert in the same initial endpoint gap. Thus (x,d_1,...,d_m) and (y,d_1,...,d_m) are Hamiltonian. Since D+x+y is non-Hamiltonian, the two candidate orders (x,y,d_1,...,d_m) and (y,x,d_1,...,d_m) must both fail. Therefore h(x,y,d_1)=h(y,x,d_1)=0, and boundary antisymmetry gives h(d_1,y,x)=h(d_1,x,y)=1 for every y in Y. For distinct y,z in Y, exactly one of h(y,x,z),h(z,x,y) is tight. Hence exactly one of (d_1,y,x,z),(d_1,z,x,y) is a tight Hamiltonian four-path. Thus every pair y,z gives a Hamiltonian four-support {d_1,x,y,z}. The terminal-endpoint synchronized case is symmetric. No five-support conclusion is asserted: common-endpoint triples alone do not force a Hamiltonian five-window, as fenced by common_endpoint_constraints_fivewindow_counterexample01. Therefore the rigorous quiet residue is a complete fixed-pair family of Hamiltonian four-supports, not a complete five-support family.
