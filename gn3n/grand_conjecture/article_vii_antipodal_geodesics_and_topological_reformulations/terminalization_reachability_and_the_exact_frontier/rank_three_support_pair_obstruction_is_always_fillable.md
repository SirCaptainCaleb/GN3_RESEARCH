## Development

Let Khat(H) be the poset of ordered disjoint nonempty Hamiltonian supports, with singleton supports allowed, and let the canonical chamber map be the one already defined. Every rank-three permutahedron face is fillable. By the existing rank-three reduction, the only unresolved case is an extreme four-block S which is non-Hamiltonian, with a surviving opposite common support B. Choose two distinct exterior singleton labels e,r on the opposite side of the face, with r kept as a singleton support and e used for filling. Such labels exist in a counterexample because every boundary tournament on at most six vertices has a spanning two-cover by partition into sets of orders at most three. The boundary carrier retracts to the proper nonempty subsets of S paired with {r}, hence to the barycentric boundary of a tetrahedron. If S+e is Hamiltonian, (S+e,{r}) cones the sphere. If S+e is non-Hamiltonian, then every T_d=(S-{d})+e is Hamiltonian: otherwise S and T_d would be two non-Hamiltonian four-sets sharing three vertices, forcing their five-vertex union S+e to be Hamiltonian. The four Hamiltonian T_d and their subsets of order at most three form the simplicial cone e*boundary(S), a 3-ball whose boundary is the original tetrahedral sphere. Pairing each left face with {r} gives the required support-pair filling. Therefore the canonical support-pair map extends equivariantly through the full three-skeleton. The first possible obstruction is in dimension at least four.
