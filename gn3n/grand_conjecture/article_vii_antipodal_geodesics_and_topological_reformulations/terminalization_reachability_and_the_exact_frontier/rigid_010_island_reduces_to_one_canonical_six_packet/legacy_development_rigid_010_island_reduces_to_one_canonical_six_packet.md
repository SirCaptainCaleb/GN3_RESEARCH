# Rigid 010-island reduces to one canonical six-packet — preserved pre-item development

Let a genuine positive reflected span-two double have corridor C=(c_1,...,c_N) with status word 1^u010^v and kappa_2(H[J])=2, where J=C union {x,y}. The exact inversion criterion gives the unique corridor cut j=u+2, hence the canonical tight cover
P=(c_1,...,c_{u+2}), Q=(c_N,...,c_{u+3}).

Because the residue is genuinely two-deletion, both P and Q have order at least four. Therefore u>=2 and v>=1.

Define the central packet
S={x,y,c_{u+1},c_{u+2},c_{u+3},c_{u+4}},
and the two exterior tails
T=(c_1,...,c_u),
U=(c_N,c_{N-1},...,c_{u+5}).
Both T and U are contiguous subpaths of P and Q and have order at least two: |T|=u>=2 and |U|=v+1>=2. They partition J-S.

Thus the rigid 010-island branch is exactly a fixed six-packet / two-tail gluing problem. By the common six-packet bridge lemma, if bridge labels arising from any available tail decompositions of this same complement contain three distinct vertices of S, J has a two-cover. In a no-two-cover residue every such bridge label lies in the fixed bad-deletion set B(S)={z in S:S-z non-Hamiltonian}, of order at most two.

Unlike the monotone branch, there is no cut-mobility mechanism forcing several decompositions: the corridor cut is unique. The remaining local obligation is therefore to exploit source-face variation or endpoint-packet structure to force a third bridge label, a Hamiltonian packet with a compatible tail join, or an outward repair leaving the full determining span.

This gives the rigid branch a bounded-interface formulation parallel to the monotone branch. It is a reduction, not a closure theorem.
