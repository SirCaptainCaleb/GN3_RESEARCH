## Development

Let H be a minimum-order counterexample and fix x in the universal branch of the fixed-hole Ky Fan theorem. Put N=V(H)-{x} and r=floor(|N|/2). The universal conclusion says every balanced bipartition of N has Hamiltonian sides; in particular every r-subset of N is Hamiltonian. Choose one Hamilton order P_A for every r-subset A of N. Consider the Johnson graph J(N,r), whose adjacent vertices A,B have intersection of order r-1. If some adjacent P_A,P_B have relative-order disagreement on A cap B, the established order-disagreement theorem gives the reversal/disturbance interface. Assume no adjacent disagreement occurs. Then the local orders glue to a global total order on N. For any pair u,v in N, the family of r-subsets containing {u,v} is connected under Johnson adjacency, so the relative order of u,v is independent of the containing r-set. For any triple u,v,w, some r-set contains all three in the nontrivial r>=3 range, and its total order proves transitivity of the induced pair relation. Hence the pair relations define a total order of N. Now take three consecutive vertices in this global order and choose an r-set containing them. No other vertex of that r-set can lie between consecutive global vertices, so they are consecutive in P_A; therefore their ordered triple is tight. Every consecutive triple of the global order is tight, so H-x is Hamiltonian. But then H-x together with singleton {x} is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Therefore the universal fixed-hole branch necessarily contains an adjacent Johnson pair of balanced Hamiltonian supports with order disagreement, and hence produces the existing reversal interface. This is scale-independent and supersedes the need to analyze the universal branch via support-size caps or small-order cases.
