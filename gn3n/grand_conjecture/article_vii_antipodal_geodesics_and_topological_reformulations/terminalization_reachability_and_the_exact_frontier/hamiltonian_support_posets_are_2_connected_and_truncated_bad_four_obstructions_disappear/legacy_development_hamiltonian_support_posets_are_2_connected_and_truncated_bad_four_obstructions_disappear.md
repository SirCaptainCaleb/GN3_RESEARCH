# Hamiltonian-support posets are 2-connected and truncated bad-four obstructions disappear — preserved pre-item development

## Development

## The Hamiltonian-support poset is universally 2-connected

For a finite vertex set W, let L(W) be the inclusion poset of its nonempty Hamiltonian subsets, and write |L(W)| for its order complex. Hamiltonicity is not assumed hereditary.

Theorem. If |W|>=5, then |L(W)| is 2-connected: it is path-connected and pi_1=pi_2=0. This statement holds in every boundary tournament, without minimum-counterexample hypotheses.

Proof. Begin with supports of orders at most three. All are Hamiltonian, so their order complex is the barycentric subdivision of the full two-skeleton of the simplex on W. It is simply connected. Add Hamiltonian supports in increasing cardinality. Adding a support S attaches a cone on the order complex of its proper nonempty Hamiltonian subsets.

Every such lower interval for |S|>=4 is simply connected. To see this, it too starts with the full two-skeleton on S, and is built by attaching cones on lower intervals in increasing size. These intervals are connected because they contain all singleton and pair supports. Van Kampen therefore shows that each attachment preserves simple connectivity. This also proves simple connectivity of |L(W)|.

Moreover, these cone attachments cannot create H_2 over the integers: their relative H_2 is the reduced H_1 of the attaching lower interval, which is zero. Hence the H_2 of |L(W)| is generated by the images of the two-cycles in the initial full two-skeleton. Those cycles are generated by oriented boundaries of tetrahedra, since the full simplex has zero H_2.

Let S be any four-set. If S is Hamiltonian, its tetrahedral boundary is coned by the support-poset vertex S. Otherwise choose e in W-S and put U=S+e. If U is Hamiltonian, the vertex U cones the same boundary, even though S itself is absent. If U is non-Hamiltonian, the five-set theorem says it has at most one non-Hamiltonian four-subset. Since S is already such a subset, every (S-d)+e, d in S, is Hamiltonian. All nonempty faces of the simplicial three-ball e*boundary Delta(S) are consequently Hamiltonian: faces have size at most three or are one of these four Hamiltonian tetrahedra. Its barycentric subdivision embeds in |L(W)| and fills boundary Delta(S).

Every generator dies. Thus H_2(|L(W)|;Z)=0. Simple connectivity and the degree-two Hurewicz theorem give pi_2=0. QED.

## Fixed opposite supports

Let B be a nonempty Hamiltonian support. The subposet of support pairs (A,B), with B fixed exactly, is L(V(H)-B). Therefore it is 2-connected whenever |V(H)-B|>=5.

More generally consider pairs (A,C) with B contained in C. The order-preserving map (A,C) -> (A,B) is pointwise below the identity and retracts this subposet onto the fixed-B copy. It has the same homotopy type, so it is also 2-connected under the same cardinality hypothesis.

Consequently any already specified two-sphere lying in such a carrier fills there. This is stronger than the particular tetrahedral filling in subsections 247 and 250: arbitrary two-spheres in the fixed-support carrier fill.

## Audit of the truncated bad-four obstruction

Subsection 249 correctly presents H_2 of X_4(W), which contains all triangles and only Hamiltonian tetrahedra. Its saturated Johnson components are obstructions in THAT truncated simplicial complex.

They are not obstructions in the full Hamiltonian-support poset. If a five-set has two bad four-facets, the five-set must itself be Hamiltonian, by the at-most-one theorem for non-Hamiltonian five-sets. Its support-poset vertex cones both tetrahedral boundaries. If it has only the specified bad four-facet and is non-Hamiltonian, the four replacement tetrahedra fill that boundary as above. Thus the inclusion of the barycentric subdivision of X_4(W) into |L(W)| kills its entire H_2 when |W|>=5.

The support-poset cone needs only the selected boundary supports to be Hamiltonian. It does not require every subset of its Hamiltonian apex to be Hamiltonian. Requiring hereditary feasibility loses precisely this filling.

## Sharpness: a genuine three-sphere

Universal higher connectivity cannot be inferred. On labels 1,...,5, use the edge order
13 < 35 < 24 < 25 < 14 < 12 < 45 < 15 < 34 < 23,
with (u,v,w) tight iff uv precedes vw.

All four-subsets are Hamiltonian but the five-set is not. An exact finite certificate is the list of ALL increasing four-vertex words:
1352, 1354, 1432, 2415, 2451, 2543,
3145, 3521, 4123, 4215, 4251, 5432.

For these words respectively, the rank of their last edge and the rank of the edge to the unused label are
(4,3), (7,3), (10,4), (8,2), (8,1), (9,1),
(7,4), (6,5), (10,2), (8,2), (8,1), (10,6).
None extends. Every five-word would have one of these four-word prefixes, so there is no Hamiltonian five-path. The list contains a word on every four-subset. Exhaustive direct integer comparisons verified the list against all 120 four-words and all 120 five-words.

Therefore L(W) is exactly the proper nonempty Boolean subset poset, whose order complex is the barycentric boundary of a four-simplex, S^3. In particular it is 2-connected but not 3-connected. This is an intrinsic counterexample to a proposed connectivity strengthening, not a small-order cutoff or a counterexample to two-coverability.

## Scope and strategic reassessment

The local tetrahedral filling in 247/250 is valid. However, independently fillable face carriers do not by themselves prove that previously chosen boundary fillings lie in a common carrier. In particular, fixing b in B_F for a three-face requires its entire already chosen boundary image to stay in the b-containing subposet, or an established relative homotopy moving it there. Chamber vertices and edges having b on the right is insufficient to ensure this for previously chosen two-face disks. A globally coherent three-skeleton extension must specify a nested carrier system or prove the required relative extension. No such global conclusion is asserted by the theorem above.

This result removes the apparent bad-four Johnson obstruction from the full fixed-support target. It also explains why merely proceeding to the next face rank is not a closure strategy: universal connectivity already stops at a three-sphere. The useful remaining question is how minimum-counterexample blocked extension and complementary deletion covers control the coupling of the two supports or a relative obstruction. The grand theorem remains open; no actual spanning two-cover or terminating cover improvement is claimed here.
