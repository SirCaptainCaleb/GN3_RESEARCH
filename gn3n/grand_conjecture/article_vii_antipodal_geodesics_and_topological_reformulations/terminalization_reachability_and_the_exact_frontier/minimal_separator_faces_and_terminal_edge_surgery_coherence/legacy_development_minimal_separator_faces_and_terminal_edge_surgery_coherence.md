## Minimal separator faces are outward chambers or terminal sign-flip edges

The separator coherence problem can be reduced from arbitrary mixed faces to rank-one Coxeter transitions.

Work in a protected face complex at depth (r). A separator face (F) is one on which either no depth-(r) label occurs or both orientations (+e_r,-e_r) occur. Consider a separator face minimal under inclusion.

If (F) has a chamber with no depth-(r) witness, then that chamber itself is already a minimal separator face and is outward.

Otherwise every chamber of (F) is labeled (+e_r) or (-e_r), and both signs occur. The chamber graph of a permutahedron face is connected: it is generated by adjacent transpositions inside its ordered-partition blocks. Along a chamber-graph path from a (+) chamber to a (-) chamber there is an adjacent pair with opposite signs. The one-dimensional Coxeter face spanned by that pair is itself mixed. By minimality,
[
oxed{dim F=1.}
]
Thus every minimal non-outward separator face is a sign-flip Coxeter edge.

For such an edge there are only two chambers. The separable splicing branch cannot produce a third chamber inside the edge, so a non-outward sign-flip edge is necessarily one of the bounded terminal transitions. The dual-polarity classification then confines its determining support to the centered/overlapping terminal interval of order at most ten. The explicit (0101/1010) example in [[a_local_pure_carrier_shows_same_face_escape_is_false]] shows that these edges genuinely occur.

This changes the coherence problem substantially. The separator is generated upward from:
1. outward chamber vertices, which map tautologically to the next-depth space; and
2. bounded terminal sign-flip edges, for which terminal-support surgery supplies an outward chamber outside the edge.

Higher separator faces do not introduce new local terminal types; they only impose compatibility among the surgery choices attached to their minimal sign-flip edges.

Since the chamber complex of a permutahedron is the type-(A) Coxeter complex, compatibility of adjacent-transposition data is generated by the rank-two relations
[
s_i s_j=s_j s_iquad(|i-j|>1),
qquad
s_i s_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_i s_{i+1}.
]
Hence the remaining equivariant-map problem can be formulated as a **terminal edge-surgery coherence lemma**: choose the bounded outward surgery on each terminal sign-flip edge equivariantly so that the choices extend across every square and braid hexagon in the separator. Once this rank-two coherence holds, the assignments extend over the separator by the usual Coxeter-cell/acyclic-carrier induction, giving
[
S_rlongrightarrow Y_{r+1}
]
equivariantly and restoring the genus drop
[
gamma(Y_{r+1})gegamma(Y_r)-1.
]

Most rank-two residues are automatic. A generator supported disjointly from the terminal interval commutes with the local replacement; applying it before or after surgery changes only farther-out status windows. The only nontrivial residues are those meeting an endpoint or the interior of the bounded centered/overlapping terminal interval. Thus the global terminalization theorem has now been reduced to a finite Coxeter-coherence check localized at the terminal support, rather than an arbitrary mixed-face theorem.
