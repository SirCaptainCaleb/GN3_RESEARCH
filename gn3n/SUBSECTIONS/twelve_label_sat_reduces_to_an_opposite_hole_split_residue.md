## Development

A twelve-label finite model was built on the two initial endpoint pairs (p_1,p_2),(q_1,q_2), the two terminal three-label contexts (...,u,a,b),(...,v,d,c), and the two holes x,y. It imposed: inherited path-end tightness; minimum-hole four-end synchronization at all four ends; positive obstruction for all 24 permutations of the central four labels in both the P-to-Q-reversed and Q-to-P-reversed frames; exact failure of the displayed concatenations P.Q and Q.P; exclusion of every bounded two-tail routing and every one-deletion routing represented in the central band; and exclusion of the whole-block bridge templates that would splice P to Q or Q to P through both holes. The model remains feasible. However, failure of P.Q always forces a bounded Hamiltonian 4/5-packet crossing that boundary and containing at least one hole, and Q.P forces a second packet on the opposite side. SAT shows that a common hole need not serve both packets. The only way to avoid a common-hole synchronization is an opposite-hole split: up to exchanging x,y, the P-to-Q side can be forced to use y while the Q-to-P side uses x. In a canonical survivor the four associated five-sets are non-Hamiltonian; on the P-to-Q side the two four-sets retaining x are non-Hamiltonian while the corresponding four-sets retaining y are Hamiltonian, and the Q-to-P side has the mirror pattern. Thus the live finite residue is not a generic twelve-label state but an opposite-hole, unique-bad-four-deletion pattern on four overlapping non-Hamiltonian five-sets. This is the next target for edge-order overlap/amalgamation analysis.
