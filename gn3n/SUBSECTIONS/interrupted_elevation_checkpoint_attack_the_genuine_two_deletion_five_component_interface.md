## Interrupted elevation checkpoint: the five-component two-deletion interface

This note records the unfinished elevation direction reached immediately before stopping. It is a research checkpoint, not a closure claim.

Recent elevation reduced every minimum deletion hole of order at least three to a genuine two-deletion induced graph
\[
G
\]
with
\[
\kappa_2(G)=2
\]
and a spanning three-cover containing a distinguished Hamiltonian five-component. In the symmetric zero-root case the displayed profile is
\[
5\mid(s-1)\mid s.
\]

The next promising cross-article attack is therefore to combine this canonical five-component state with the established small-support and repartition toolkit, especially:

- the five-side versus long-path theorem: for a Hamiltonian five-set beside a path of order at least six, either an endpoint transfer is nonincreasing/strictly decreasing in quadratic potential, or a common four-core exists for the two endpoint extensions;
- the balanced \(4|4\) and \(5|5\) repartition theorems on eight- and ten-vertex unions;
- the four-of-six deletion theorem, which converts a six-label enlargement of a rooted five-path into multiple Hamiltonian five-deletions;
- prescribed-endpoint extension on a tight triple;
- the existing connector-exclusion and common-reverser/root-advance lemmas in the genuine \(\kappa_2=2\) layer.

The desired strengthening is not yet proved. A useful target statement is:

> A genuine \(\kappa_2=2\) boundary tournament with a spanning three-cover containing a Hamiltonian five-component either has a two-cover, or admits a bounded endpoint-core normal form stable under pairwise repartition.

In the symmetric root-advance profile \(5|(s-1)|s\), if \(s\ge7\), both neighboring tails are long enough for the five-side/long-path theorem to apply on either side. Thus any surviving large-\(s\) obstruction must simultaneously realize the exceptional common-core alternative against both tails. This suggests a two-sided synchronization attack: intersect the two good-label sets supplied by the left and right five-side lemmas, then use the resulting shared four-core with four-of-six or prescribed-endpoint extension.

This checkpoint deliberately does not assert that the two common-core alternatives synchronize automatically. Establishing that synchronization, or showing that failure forces a two-cover/root advance, is the next concrete elevation test.
