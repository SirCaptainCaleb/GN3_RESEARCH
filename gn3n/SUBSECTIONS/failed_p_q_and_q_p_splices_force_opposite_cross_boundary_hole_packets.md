## Development

Let {x,y} be a minimum deletion pair with H-{x,y}=P|Q. Write the terminal end of P as (...,a,b), the initial end of P as (p_1,p_2,...), the terminal end of Q as (...,d,c), and the initial end of Q as (q_1,q_2,...). Minimum-hole synchronization gives h(a,b,x)=h(a,b,y)=0, h(q_2,q_1,x)=h(q_2,q_1,y)=1, and symmetrically h(d,c,x)=h(d,c,y)=0, h(p_2,p_1,x)=h(p_2,p_1,y)=1. Since P followed by Q is not Hamiltonian, at least one junction fails. If h(a,b,q_1)=0, then q_1,x,y are three common zero-hooks through the ordered anchor (a,b). Passing to the complementary boundary tournament and applying the two-plus-one three-hook theorem gives a Hamiltonian support of order four or five containing {a,b}, q_1, and at least one of {x,y}; Hamiltonicity transfers back by reversing the Hamilton order. If instead h(b,q_1,q_2)=0, boundary flip gives h(q_2,q_1,b)=1. Then b,x,y are three common hooks through (q_2,q_1), and the same two-plus-one theorem gives a Hamiltonian 4/5-support containing {q_2,q_1}, b, and at least one hole. Thus failure of P.Q always forces a bounded Hamiltonian packet crossing the P/Q boundary and containing a hole. Applying the symmetric argument to the failure of Q.P gives a second cross-boundary hole packet rooted on the opposite side. Therefore the twelve-label frame supplies two opposite bounded packets, not merely the central eight-position constraints.
