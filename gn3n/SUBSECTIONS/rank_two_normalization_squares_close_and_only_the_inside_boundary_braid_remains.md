## Rank-two normalization: squares close and only the inside-boundary braid remains

Normalize every terminal repair to a contiguous ten-position window (J) containing the terminal determining span (I). Let (omega) be an outward chamber obtained by replacing the order on (J) by a Hamiltonian (5|5) two-cover order.

Call an adjacent generator safe relative to (J) if its transposed positions are both outside (J) or if it crosses one endpoint of (J). Applying a safe generator to (omega) changes only status coordinates meeting or lying outside an endpoint of (J). Since (Isubseteq J) and every witness of depth at most (r) has its determining window inside (I), these changed coordinates can create only witnesses strictly farther outward. Hence every safe translate of (omega) still lies in (Y_{r+1}). A generator whose two positions lie inside (J) will instead be called internal; because the support set (J) is unchanged across such an edge, terminal surgery may use the same repaired chamber on both endpoints and collapse that direction.

**Safe-transport lemma.** If (omega) is produced by normalized ten-position surgery at depth (r), then (omega s_i) is outward whenever (s_i) is safe relative to (J). If (s_i) is internal, the two chambers on the corresponding source edge may be assigned the same repaired chamber.

This already closes all commuting-square residues. For a square generated by (s_i,s_j) with (|i-j|>1), collapse every internal direction and transport along every safe direction. Safe generators remain safe after the disjoint other swap, and disjoint safe swaps commute. Thus the four assigned outward chambers form a point, an edge, or a commuting square entirely in (Y_{r+1}). In particular even the case in which the two generators cross the two opposite endpoints of (J) is automatic: starting from (omega), use (omega s_i,omega s_j,omega s_i s_j=omega s_j s_i). No simultaneous four-support Hamiltonian compatibility lemma is needed.

The same argument closes most braid hexagons. If both generators are internal, collapse the whole residue. If both are safe (the endpoint case is one boundary-crossing generator together with its outside neighbor), transport the repaired chamber through the six Coxeter words; every intermediate chamber remains outward and the braid relation (s_i s_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_i s_{i+1}) closes the hexagon.

The sole unresolved rank-two type is therefore a braid with one internal generator and the adjacent generator crossing an endpoint of (J). If the determining span (I) has slack inside (J) at the opposite endpoint, shift the ten-window one position toward the crossing; then the braid triple lies entirely inside the shifted window and both generators become internal, so the residue again collapses. Consequently a genuinely nontrivial braid can occur only when no such shift is possible, i.e. when the terminal determining span itself has the maximal length (|I|=10).

Thus terminal edge-surgery coherence reduces further to one finite maximal-support (A_2) problem: a reflected alternating terminal support occupying ten consecutive positions, with a braid acting on the last two positions of that support and the first position outside it (or its reversal at the left endpoint). All squares and every other hexagon are coherent by safe transport/collapse.

For the surviving braid, write the three permuted vertices as (a,b,c) and the other eight support vertices as (T). The three ten-vertex supports encountered around the hexagon are (Tcup{a,b}), (Tcup{a,c}), and (Tcup{b,c}). A strong sufficient closure statement is the following common-core lemma: there exists a Hamiltonian five-set (Bsubset T) such that, for (C=Tsetminus B), all three five-sets (Ccup{a,b},Ccup{a,c},Ccup{b,c}) are Hamiltonian. Then the three normalized surgeries share the fixed Hamiltonian side (B) and form a cone-like outward carrier, closing the braid.

Moreover, if one insists that neighboring supports be compatible by sharing an entire Hamiltonian side, cyclic pairwise compatibility around this support triangle forces exactly such a common (Bsubset T). Indeed, if the common sides for two consecutive support pairs are distinct, they must be the two complementary sides of the intervening (5|5) cover; tracing this once around the triangle forces the third pair of supposed common sides to overlap on four vertices and hence not be complementary. Therefore the common-core lemma is not merely a convenient symmetric strengthening of side-sharing compatibility: it is the exact finite statement for that compatibility model.

This removes the commuting-square obligation completely and reduces the braid obligation from arbitrary at-most-ten terminal supports to one order-eleven local configuration (Tcup{a,b,c}) with (|T|=8).
