## Protected commuting-square failures are supported on at most eight positions

Work in the positive witness language W_+={001,011,0101}. A positive occurrence is determined by at most six consecutive vertex positions: 001 and 011 use five vertices, while 0101 uses six.

Let a commuting Coxeter square be generated by adjacent transpositions s_i and s_j with i<j-1. Write its four chambers as
pi, pi_i=pi s_i, pi_j=pi s_j, pi_ij=pi s_i s_j.
Fix a witness depth r and suppose pi, pi_i, pi_j are protected at depth r, while pi_ij is not. Let W be a positive witness of depth <r appearing in pi_ij.

**Lemma.** The determining vertex interval of W meets both swap supports {i,i+1} and {j,j+1}. Consequently j-i<=6, and the entire interaction of the two generators is contained in at most eight consecutive vertex positions i,...,j+1.

**Proof.**
If the determining interval of W missed {i,i+1}, then applying or undoing s_i would not alter any ordered triple used by W. Hence the same occurrence would be present in pi_j, contradicting protection of pi_j. Similarly, if the determining interval missed {j,j+1}, W would already occur in pi_i. Therefore the interval meets both supports.

A determining interval has order at most six. To meet both {i,i+1} and {j,j+1}, an interval must contain some position at most i+1 and some position at least j, so its length is at least j-i. Hence j-i<=6. The union of the two swap supports lies in the consecutive interval [i,j+1], of order j-i+2<=8. square

The same conclusion holds if any one corner is the unique unprotected corner: relabel the square so that corner is pi_ij.

### Consequence for carrier compatibility

A failure of protected product-splicing across two commuting local freedoms cannot be genuinely long-range. If the two generators are separated by more than six positions, protection of three corners forces protection of the fourth. Thus every commuting-square obstruction to a protected carrier is localized to at most eight consecutive positions.

This is stronger than merely bounding face-block sizes. It bounds the actual positional support on which two otherwise independent choices can interact to create a new inward witness.

For the two-skeleton program, distant generator pairs are therefore automatic. Only:
- braid triangles, already supported on three adjacent positions; and
- commuting squares whose generators lie in one eight-position interval
can carry a new protection obstruction.

Hence the unresolved two-skeleton compatibility problem is a finite local interface problem. No unbounded corridor can enter through interaction of separated generators.
