## Protected parabolic carriers and the exact global gluing frontier

The existential carrier used in the current composition is unnecessarily weak on any region where one normalization window can be held fixed.

Fix a protected terminal sign-flip edge at depth (r), with terminal determining interval (I), and choose a contiguous normalization window (Jsupseteq I) of order at most ten together with one normalized outward repair chamber (omega).

Let (Q) be any Coxeter face containing the source edge. Among the simple adjacent generators of (Q), separate those that are internal to (J) from those that are safe relative to (J) (disjoint from (J) or crossing exactly one endpoint). On a sector of (Q) in which no rank-two residue contains one internal generator together with an adjacent boundary-crossing generator, define the repaired chamber assignment by

- collapsing every internal generator; and
- transporting (omega) by every safe generator.

The safe-transport lemma shows that every transported chamber has selected witness depth (>r). Internal directions do not change the assigned chamber. Commuting safe directions commute, and every braid involving only safe directions is transported through the ordinary Coxeter braid relation. Hence the assignment factors through the standard parabolic quotient obtained by deleting the internal generators.

The image is therefore not merely an existential family of outward chambers: it is a genuine Coxeter cell (C_J(Q)), a product of permutahedra for the retained connected components of the simple-generator diagram. Every chamber of (C_J(Q)) is outward, so
[
C_J(Q)subseteq X_{r+1}.
]
In particular (C_J(Q)) is contractible.

This removes the higher-face extension problem on every fixed-window sector. There is no need there to invoke a vague “usual Coxeter-cell/acyclic-carrier induction”: the carrier is an explicit protected parabolic cell.

### Where fixed-window sectors can fail

The only obstruction to using one fixed (J) on all of (Q) is exactly the rank-two pattern already isolated earlier: a braid generated by one internal simple reflection and the adjacent reflection crossing an endpoint of (J). Collapsing the internal generator while transporting the boundary generator is incompatible with the braid relation.

For a terminal determining interval of order at most eight, a ten-position window can be chosen with at least one slack position at each relevant endpoint, so both generators of every endpoint braid may be made internal simultaneously.

For order ten, [[maximal_ten_support_endpoint_braids_are_impossible]] excludes the endpoint sign-flip braid altogether.

Thus the only global gluing regime not absorbed by a single protected parabolic cell is the near-maximal slack case, especially an order-nine determining interval: the two possible ten-position normalizations extend (I) on opposite sides, and each normalization makes one endpoint braid internal while leaving the opposite choice to the neighboring normalization sector.

### Exact remaining gluing obligation

Let (J_-) and (J_+) be two adjacent ten-position normalization windows containing the same order-nine terminal interval (I). Their support sets differ by one endpoint vertex. Each window supplies a protected contractible parabolic carrier on its own sector.

To glue these sectors inside (X_{r+1}), it is sufficient to prove either of the following equivalent-strength local statements.

1. **Protected adjacent-window bridge.** The two normalized surgery chambers can be chosen so that they are joined by a path all of whose chambers have depth (>r).

2. **Protected overlap carrier.** The union of the two fixed-window protected parabolic carriers has a nonempty acyclic intersection in (X_{r+1}).

The existing compatible one-vertex (5|5) lemma proves only support-level compatibility: the two ten-sets have covers sharing a Hamiltonian side. Its current common ordered-partition carrier is existential and may contain non-outward chambers, so it does not yet prove either protected statement above.

The remaining obstruction is therefore an **ordered one-vertex exchange problem**, not a new global topological problem. One needs enough control of Hamiltonian path orders on the two exchanged five-sets to connect the two normalized repairs through outward chambers. A particularly strong sufficient form would put the exchanged vertices at compatible endpoints of the varying Hamiltonian side; that would make the two repaired orders differ by the boundary adjacent transposition itself. Such prescribed-endpoint Hamiltonicity is not automatic, so a more flexible short outward path may be the correct target.

### Consequence for the global audit gap

Once the adjacent-window bridge is proved, the normalization windows containing a fixed terminal interval form a linear chain under one-step shifts. Their protected parabolic carriers then glue successively along protected acyclic overlaps. The resulting carrier for every protected separator face is contractible and lies entirely in (X_{r+1}). This simultaneously supplies
[
S_rlongrightarrow X_{r+1}
]
and resolves the higher-dimensional carrier objection, giving the iterable inequality
[
gamma(X_{r+1})ge gamma(X_r)-1.
]

Thus the global audit gap has been reduced to a bounded ordered one-vertex exchange/adjacent-window bridge at terminal support order nine (and any analogous near-maximal slack normalization that cannot be covered by a single window).
