## Development

Assume pc(H)>2 and H-x=P|Q with the one-hole four-end reversal relations. Let a be an initial endpoint of one displayed path, with next inherited vertices a1,a2,a3 inward, and let b be a terminal endpoint of one displayed path, with preceding inherited vertices b1,b2,b3 inward. If h(a,x,b)=1, then (a1,a,x,b,b1) is a tight rooted five-path by four-end reversal. Let A be the residual inherited interval beginning (a2,a3,...) and let B be the residual inherited interval ending (...,b3,b2). If the five-path concatenated tightly into A, the untouched inherited residual interval on the other side would be the second path of a spanning two-cover. Likewise, if B concatenated tightly into the five-path, the untouched residual interval on the other side would give a spanning two-cover. Therefore both concatenations fail. Equivalently, h(b,b1,a2)=0 or h(b1,a2,a3)=0, and h(b3,b2,a1)=0 or h(b2,a1,a)=0. Boundary flip converts these into two explicit reverse-boundary shield disjunctions. Thus every positive entry of the opposite-boundary 2x2 cross matrix propagates one layer farther inward at both ends. No cyclic rotation, path reversal, or minimum-counterexample induction is used beyond the no-two-cover assumption.
