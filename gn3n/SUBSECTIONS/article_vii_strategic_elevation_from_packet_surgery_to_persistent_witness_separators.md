## Development

## Choice of attack

The next effort within Article VII should target the depth-r separator construction using persistent witness orientations. This is a research judgment, not a theorem that this route will close the conjecture.

The recent packet results establish many sufficient absorption tests, but genuine two-deletion instances have no small-packet tail connectors at all. More Hamiltonian five-supports do not by themselves produce a spanning two-cover: their long complements still need attachment. An internal outward repair of a full double span is equivalent to a two-cover of that span, even at deletion distance one. Thus continuing only with internal packet surgery risks confronting a large portion of the grand theorem inside a local repair obligation.

The positive-word framework itself remains useful: it matches the exact two-cover predicate and is antipodal. The weaker part is its choice of signed labels when both reflected occurrences are present. Such choices can introduce a separator although one actual witness orientation persists throughout the face. A universal external gauge does not cure this; it creates unrelated sign flips, as explained in [[universal_gauge_does_not_bypass_terminalization]].

The five-position obstruction has a stronger application earlier in the topology. By [[separated_protected_determining_windows_force_a_persistent_witness_or_same_face_escape]], a protected face at a reflected span-two depth whose full span has at least thirteen vertices has either an outward chamber in that same face or a persistent witness orientation. On a fixed face without an outward chamber, assigning that available orientation constantly removes its depth-r separator without finding a two-cover of the determining span.

This is the specific elevation: a lemma about free block orders now controls which long-span separator events are forced by witness geometry and which are induced by a tie choice. It neither bounds every determining span nor claims an unbounded carrier has become a small parabolic cell.

## Concrete next theorem

Construct an equivariant depth-r separator using locally persistent signs, patched through protected faces that already contain outward chambers. The desired construction must retain the genus loss of at most one and provide a valid next-stage object. A map into X_{r+1}, or a separately proved relabeling invariant on the separator itself, is still required.

The exact compatibility criterion is in [[compatibility_criterion_for_persistent_orientation_relabeling]]. Local signs can be made constant on a chosen family of faces precisely when each overlap component has a common available orientation and is paired with a distinct antipodal component. An opposite-sign overlap chain whose hull remains protected exposes an outward chamber by the product splice. The outstanding cases are chains which cannot be patched inside protected hulls and antipodally invariant overlap components.

Those are the immediate targets. If they cannot be resolved, that failure is evidence against this particular relabeling scheme and should be recorded rather than replaced by an assumed carrier theorem.

## Relation to earlier Article VII branches

Additional moments of the exact root do not distinguish its zero-root chambers, which can retain nonempty holes. The auxiliary exact-violation map avoids the extreme singleton facets and its side-set version closes singleton-auxiliary-vertex carriers, but its mixed median block remains unbounded. Neither route currently offers a proved shortcut around the actual-vertex compatibility problem.

The protected witness filtration has a direct index budget and existing bounded-support surgery. The proposed separator attack uses those advantages while trying to avoid demanding a two-cover of every long double span. Bounded nonpersistent faces, whose span is at most twelve by the new theorem, remain explicit local obligations; twelve vertices have not been proved universally two-coverable.

The grand theorem is not closed. The present choice is to work first on coherent persistent-orientation separators, using actual outward chambers where product splicing already supplies them, and return to full-span packet absorption when a demonstrated global obstruction makes it necessary.
