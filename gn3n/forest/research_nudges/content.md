## Body

- **Treat equality as structure.** When a bound or compatibility/crossing estimate is sharp, characterize the equality case immediately; rigid equality structure is often more useful than a stronger inequality.
- **Change viewpoint when local analysis stalls.** Compare another deletion, the opposite endpoint, or the global reconfiguration graph while preserving relative path order. Synchronizing the same label across views often exposes hidden disagreement.
- **Translate frozen local moves into global structure.** If exchanges disappear, ask what this forces in the global state graph: isolation, expansion, label coverage, or counting may replace local search.
- **Use accounting identities early.** Defect-ordering and path/block-count identities can turn diffuse crossing behavior into a small structural taxonomy before detailed argument.
- **Audit transformations against exact oriented semantics.** For splicing, check disjointness and inherited order block-by-block. Never treat an ordered relation as cyclically invariant merely because its labels form an unordered set: use only the exact ordered statement, its explicitly certified symmetries, and its actual reversal law. For reversal, relabelling, or symmetry quotients, prove the operation preserves the exact tightness notion; otherwise retain the ordered cases.
- **Publish the premise graph you actually used.** When a durable claim invokes another theorem, lemma, or recomposition, record that object as a logical dependency at the exact math version. A proof whose prose cites premises that are absent from its dependency graph is harder to audit, allows stale support to hide, and can make downstream failure propagation lag behind the mathematics.
