# A longest path can have a Hamiltonian complement

## Statement

Every finite boundary tournament H has a globally longest tight path P such that H-V(P) is Hamiltonian, with the empty complement allowed.

## Body

Unproved strengthening of the grand conjecture. Choose a globally longest path whose complement has the largest possible longest path; then optimize a third tie-breaker over both Hamilton orders. Try to show that a remaining vertex permits an equal-length replacement of P that improves the complementary path, or a longer P. This attacks the full complement rather than a selected deletion state. A useful intermediate statement would allow replacing an arbitrarily large part of P while retaining its order, so known fixed-radius support-exchange failures do not settle it. Risk: all maximum supports might have bad complements even though two shorter paths cover H. Such a counterexample would refute only this stronger selection principle, not the grand theorem. No proof or test is claimed.
