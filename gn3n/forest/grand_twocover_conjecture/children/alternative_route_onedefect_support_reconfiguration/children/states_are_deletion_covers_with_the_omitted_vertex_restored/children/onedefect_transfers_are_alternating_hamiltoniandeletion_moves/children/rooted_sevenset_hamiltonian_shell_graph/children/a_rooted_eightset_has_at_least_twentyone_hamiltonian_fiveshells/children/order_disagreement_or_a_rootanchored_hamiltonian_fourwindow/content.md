# Four roots around a seven-set force order disagreement or a root-anchored Hamiltonian four-window

## Statement

Let X be a seven-vertex set in a boundary tournament H and let E be four distinct vertices outside X. Then at least one of the following holds: (1) there is explicit relative-order disagreement between Hamiltonian paths on two overlapping induced supports contained in X union E; or (2) H contains a Hamiltonian four-vertex set containing at least two vertices of E. More precisely, there are a four-set D subset X and three distinct roots r,s,t in E such that D union {r}, D union {s}, and D union {t} are Hamiltonian; absent order disagreement, all three pair extensions D union {r,s}, D union {r,t}, D union {s,t} are Hamiltonian and yield the asserted root-anchored four-window.

## Body

Apply the rooted-eight shell lemma to X union {e} for each e in E. For each root e there are at least 21 triples T subset X of order three such that (X-T) union {e} is Hamiltonian. Across the four roots this gives at least 84 incidences on only binom(7,3)=35 triples. Since 84>2*35, some triple T is good for at least three roots r,s,t. Put D=X-T, so |D|=4 and D+r,D+s,D+t are Hamiltonian. Consider S_rs=D union {r,s}. If S_rs is non-Hamiltonian, its two deletions S_rs-r=D+s and S_rs-s=D+r are Hamiltonian, while four-of-six gives at least four Hamiltonian one-vertex deletions in total. Applying astra004fourgooddisagree to Hamilton paths on four good deletions yields order disagreement, outcome (1). Hence absent outcome (1), S_rs is Hamiltonian; similarly S_rt and S_st are Hamiltonian. Choose Hamilton paths on these three six-sets. If two chosen paths disagree on their common vertices, outcome (1) holds. Otherwise the three paths are pairwise compatible, so apply 92efe67cef7b with core D and special labels r,s,t. If D union {r,s,t} is Hamiltonian, take a Hamilton order of this seven-set. Among the three marked positions of r,s,t, some two lie within a block of four consecutive positions: otherwise consecutive marked positions would be separated by at least four places, impossible in a seven-term word. That four-term subpath is a Hamiltonian four-set containing at least two roots. If D union {r,s,t} is non-Hamiltonian, 92efe67cef7b puts r,s,t into one common insertion gap of the common D-order. Taking any pair, say r,s, the Hamilton path on D union {r,s} has a four-consecutive block containing r,s and two vertices of D (at an internal gap use the two adjacent core vertices; at an endpoint gap use the first or last two D-vertices). This again gives outcome (2).
