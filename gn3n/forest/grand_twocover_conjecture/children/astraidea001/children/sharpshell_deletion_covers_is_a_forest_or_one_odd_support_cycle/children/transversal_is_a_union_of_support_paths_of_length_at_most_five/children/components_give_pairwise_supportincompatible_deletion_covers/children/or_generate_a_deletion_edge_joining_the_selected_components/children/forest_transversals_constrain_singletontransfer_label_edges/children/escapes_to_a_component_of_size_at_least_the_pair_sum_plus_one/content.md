# A singleton-transfer replacement either stays in the larger component or escapes to a component of size at least the pair sum plus one

## Statement

Let H be in the sharp half-order shell. Among all deletion-cover transversals whose selected support graph is a forest, choose J so that the decreasing sequence of connected-component edge counts is lexicographically maximal. Let C,D be distinct components of J with alpha=|E(C)|>=beta=|E(D)|, and suppose selected deletion covers from C and D take the singleton-transfer branch of 68deffb43319, producing an alternative deletion edge f_x labelled x joining a support vertex of C to a support vertex of D. Let e_x be the selected edge of J with label x. Then e_x does not lie in D. If e_x lies in C and deleting it leaves the endpoint of f_x in a piece with r edges, then r<=alpha-beta-1. If instead e_x lies in a third component E, then |E|>=alpha+beta+1. In particular, when C is a largest component of J, e_x lies in C and the bound r<=alpha-beta-1 holds.

## Body

Replace e_x by f_x, obtaining another forest deletion-cover transversal J'. The replacement preserves the total number of selected edges.\n\nFirst suppose e_x lies in D. Deleting e_x splits D into two pieces. Let the endpoint of f_x lying in D belong to a piece with r>=0 edges. Adding f_x joins that piece to all of C, producing a component with alpha+r+1>alpha edges. Every component of J having more than alpha edges is unchanged, because only C and D are modified and both have at most alpha edges. Hence the decreasing component-size sequence of J' is lexicographically larger than that of J, contradicting maximality. Thus e_x does not lie in D.\n\nNext suppose e_x lies in C. Delete it, and let the endpoint of f_x lying in C belong to a resulting piece with r edges. Adding f_x joins that piece to D, producing a component with beta+r+1 edges; the other piece of C has alpha-r-1 edges. Again all components originally larger than alpha are unchanged. If beta+r+1>alpha, the new decreasing component-size sequence is lexicographically larger. Therefore beta+r+1<=alpha, so r<=alpha-beta-1.\n\nFinally suppose e_x lies in a third component E, of size gamma. Deleting e_x splits E, while C and D are merged by f_x into a component of size alpha+beta+1. If gamma<alpha+beta+1, then every component larger than max(gamma,alpha) is unchanged, while among the remaining entries the new merged component has size alpha+beta+1>max(gamma,alpha). Thus the decreasing component-size sequence strictly increases, again contradicting maximality. Hence gamma>=alpha+beta+1.\n\nIf C is a largest component, the third-component alternative is impossible because alpha+beta+1>alpha, so e_x lies in C and the preceding bound applies. ∎
