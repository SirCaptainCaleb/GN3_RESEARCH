# The current radius-two exchange evidence cannot serve as a proof-grade fractional transformation theorem

## Statement

The certified record exchange01 supplies finite/empirical evidence that a universal equitable replacement of one Hamiltonian support by another within old-support distance two can fail, even in the edge-orderable subclass. Therefore the current corpus does not provide a proof-grade universal radius-two replacement lemma from which fractional laminarization may be deduced. Combined with the proved sharp-half-order laminar fence, this directs any theorem-closing fractional transformation toward additional sharp-half-order/optimal-LP hypotheses, a stronger independently proved replacement theorem, or a more global transformation. This is an evidence-backed route assessment, not an independent proof of the finite counterexample recorded in exchange01.

## Body

This is a route assessment that keeps proof-grade and evidence-grade inputs separate.

First, fractionallaminarfence01 proves that a laminar optimal fractional cover would contradict the sharp half-order shell. Thus a theorem that genuinely transforms an optimal sharp-half-order fractional cover to laminar positive-weight path supports without increasing mass would close that shell.

Second, exchange01 is recorded by the project as certified finite/empirical evidence, not as a proof-grade theorem. It reports an edge-orderable example in which equitable replacement of one Hamiltonian support by another within old-support distance two fails.

Accordingly, the current corpus cannot justify fractional laminarization by citing a universal one- or two-vertex Hamiltonian-support replacement theorem: no such proof-grade theorem is available, and the retained evidence points against it in the unrestricted boundary-tournament class.

This does not prove that every bounded-radius replacement principle is false, nor does it refute a sharp-half-order replacement theorem. The sharp half-order shell carries additional structure absent from an arbitrary boundary tournament: all path supports carrying positive weight in an optimal fractional cover are globally longest; every vertex has fractional coverage exactly one; the dual optimum is uniform; deletion covers give a distinguished family of disjoint longest-support pairs; and ordered endpoint and support-transition data are available.

Hence a proof-grade fractional transformation should explicitly consume one or more of these sharp-half-order/optimal-LP hypotheses, independently establish whatever stronger replacement property it needs, or use a nonlocal transformation. The finite record exchange01 is evidence guiding that strategy, not a theorem premise that is silently upgraded to proof.
