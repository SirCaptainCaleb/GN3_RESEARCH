# Near-balanced quadratic-maximal three-block states have two barriers from one common block

## Statement

In the setting of astra006phimax01, suppose the three displayed block orders are all at least two and differ pairwise by at most one. For an ordered pair X|Y call the interface a barrier when both join triples are non-tight, equivalently when the interface trichotomy supplies the reversed tight four-path (y_2,y_1,x_p,x_{p-1}). Among the six ordered interfaces there are at least four barriers. Consequently some block X is the first block in barriers X|Y and X|Z to both other blocks; therefore two reversed tight four-paths share the terminal pair x_p,x_{p-1} of X.

## Body

Whole-block permutation freedom lets every ordered block pair be inspected within the same trapped Phi-maximal relocation component. For an equal-size unordered pair {X,Y}, astra006phimax01 gives a barrier in both orientations, contributing two directed barriers. For a size-gap-one pair, suppose neither orientation were a barrier. Put |X|=q+1, |Y|=q. The allowed single-tight pattern in X|Y gives (x_p,y_1,y_2) tight, while the allowed pattern in Y|X gives (y_{q-1},y_q,x_1) tight. Hence (x_p,y_1,...,y_q,x_1) is a tight path. Relocating the singleton x_1 from the start of X to immediately after Y yields a reachable three-block display X^o | (x_p,Y,x_1) | Z with pair orders (q-1,q+2) instead of (q+1,q). The quadratic change is (q-1)^2+(q+2)^2-(q+1)^2-q^2=4>0, contradicting maximality. Thus every gap-one unordered pair contributes at least one directed barrier. If all three block orders are equal, all six ordered interfaces are barriers. Otherwise the multiset is {a,a,a+1} or {a,a+1,a+1}: the equal pair contributes two barriers and the two gap-one pairs contribute at least one each, for at least four total. In a directed graph on three block labels with at least four arcs, some vertex has outdegree two. If X has arcs to Y and Z, the interface trichotomy gives reversed paths (y_2,y_1,x_p,x_{p-1}) and (z_2,z_1,x_p,x_{p-1}), sharing the same terminal pair of X.
