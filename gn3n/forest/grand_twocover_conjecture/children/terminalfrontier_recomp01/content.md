# Terminal quadratic minima reduce to canonical closure witnesses or an aligned same-end neutral transfer

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let C=P_1|P_2|P_3 minimize Phi in a trapped component of the pairwise-repartition graph. Then either H contains a proper Hamiltonian four- or five-set whose complement has path-cover number two; or a displayed pair exposes a cross triple, interval connector, inherited-order disagreement, inherited-edge crossing, or one of the bounded local defect-compression obstructions; or two component orders differ by one and a Phi-neutral endpoint transfer exists. In the last case, if two larger components transfer into the same smaller component and none of the preceding closure witnesses occurs, the two transferred labels are insertable only at one common endpoint gap. Any transferred label coming from the facing end of its donor path forces a reversed join; hence if no reversed join occurs, common left extenders must be initial donor endpoints and common right extenders must be terminal donor endpoints.

## Body

The size-gap compression handles every pair whose orders differ by at least two, yielding a proper Hamiltonian support or a cross/interval connector. If all three sizes are equal, the all-equal endpoint-probe compression yields inherited-order disagreement, inherited-edge crossing, or a bounded local defect-compression obstruction. Thus the only uncaptured profile is a gap-one pair. The gap-one dichotomy gives either paired noninsertion, which again collapses through the universal noninsertion theorem to the preceding closure witnesses, or a Phi-neutral endpoint transfer. If two larger sides A,C both transfer endpoints x,y into the same smaller displayed path B, then outside relative-order disagreement both x,y are uniquely insertable. The two-insertion local theorem shows that separated gaps give a Hamiltonian B+x+y, adjacent gaps give either the same or a reverse cross triple, and a common internal gap gives a Hamiltonian four-set; therefore only a common endpoint gap survives. Finally, if a transferred terminal endpoint a_m left-extends B, then the forward join triple (a_{m-1},a_m,b_1) cannot be tight, else A followed by B together with the third component is a two-cover. Boundary antisymmetry therefore gives the reversed join (b_1,a_m,a_{m-1}) tight. The right-extension statement is symmetric. Hence absence of a reversed join forces the stated aligned donor-end orientations.
