# A single-cut rigid residue through x localizes to a singleton endpoint block and forces a reversed endpoint hook

## Statement

Continue the no-disagreement, no-direct-crossing, single-cut branch of astra003lockedonecut. Let R=(r_1,...,r_m) be the split displayed long-side path and S the unsplit opposite long side. Then the R-block belonging to the mixed T-component and adjacent to x consists of exactly one endpoint of R. More precisely, up to the left-right alternatives, either the mixed component has terminal segment S,x,r_1 and the other component is R[2,m], in which case (r_2,r_1,x) is tight, or the mixed component has initial segment r_m,x,S and the other component is R[1,m-1], in which case (x,r_m,r_{m-1}) is tight. All other contiguous-cut placements permit an edge exchange or concatenation that makes either R union {x} with S as the other component, or all of K_e, Hamiltonian, contradicting the locked-label hypotheses. Thus the rigid two-edge residue through x is forced all the way to an endpoint singleton together with the standard reversed endpoint hook triple.

## Body

Let B be the R-block in the mixed component and C the residual R-block. By astra003lockedonecut, B,C are the two contiguous intervals of R and their internal T-orders agree with the inherited R-order. The unsplit S-block also has inherited order. There are four placement/orientation possibilities. First suppose B is a prefix and the mixed component runs B,x,S. Delete the T-edge joining the last vertex of B to x and insert the unique old R cut edge joining B to C. This produces two tight paths R and x,S, contradicting non-Hamiltonicity of S union {x}. Thus this placement is impossible. If B is a prefix and the mixed component runs S,x,B, then appending C across the old cut reconstructs all of R. When |B|>=2, both new consecutive triples at the cut are inherited from R, so S,x,B,C is a Hamilton path on all of K_e, impossible. Therefore B={r_1}. The candidate order S,x,r_1,r_2,...,r_m has every consecutive triple tight except possibly (x,r_1,r_2). Since K_e is non-Hamiltonian, that triple is non-tight, and boundary antisymmetry gives (r_2,r_1,x) tight. The suffix cases are symmetric. If B is a suffix and the mixed component runs S,x,B, deleting the x-B edge and restoring the old R cut gives S,x and R as two Hamilton paths, contradicting non-Hamiltonicity of S union {x}. If the mixed component runs B,x,S, then |B|>=2 would allow C,B,x,S to be a Hamilton path on K_e, so B={r_m}; the only possible failed triple in R followed by x,S is (r_{m-1},r_m,x), whose reverse (x,r_m,r_{m-1}) is therefore tight. This proves the endpoint-singleton normal form.
