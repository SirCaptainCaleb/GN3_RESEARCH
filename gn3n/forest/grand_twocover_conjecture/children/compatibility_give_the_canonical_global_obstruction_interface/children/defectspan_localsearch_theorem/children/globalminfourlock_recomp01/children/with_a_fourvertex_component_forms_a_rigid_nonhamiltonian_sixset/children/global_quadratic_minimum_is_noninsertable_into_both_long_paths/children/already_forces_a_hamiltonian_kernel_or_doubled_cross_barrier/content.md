# One four-side label already forces a Hamiltonian kernel or doubled cross barrier

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let X|P|Q be a globally Phi-minimal spanning three-cover with |X|=4 and |P|,|Q|>=6. Fix any x in V(X). Then at least one of the following holds: (1) H contains a proper Hamiltonian induced set W of order four or five such that H-W is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two; or (2) there exist p in V(P), q in V(Q) such that both (q,p,x) and (p,q,x) are tight. Thus a single four-side label already produces either a standard Hamiltonian-kernel input or a doubled cross barrier; no synchronization among several labels of X is required.

## Body

By aab7e8e9fe4a, x is noninsertable into every position of the displayed orders of both P and Q. Apply the failed-insertion normal form insert01 separately on P and Q. Suppose one obstruction is first-type. By 0425e03e2aa3 its four-vertex window W_4 is either Hamiltonian or the universal cyclic non-Hamiltonian K4. In the Hamiltonian case, W_4 is proper because the two other displayed paths have order at least six, and minimum-counterexample calculus gives pc(H-W_4)=2 with H-W_4 non-Hamiltonian. In the cyclic case choose any x prime in V(X)-{x}. The kernel W_4 consists of x and three vertices of one long path, so x prime lies outside it; 0425e03e2aa3 says W_4 union {x prime} is Hamiltonian. This five-set is proper, and minimum-counterexample calculus again gives a non-Hamiltonian complement of path-cover number two. Hence outcome (1) holds whenever either obstruction is first-type. It remains that both P and Q give second-type obstructions. Let their pivot gaps be p_i|p_{i+1} and q_j|q_{j+1}; boundary gaps are allowed. Apply 4efbe05b945a. Either one of the mixed four-vertex orders (p_{i+1},x,p_i,q_j) and (q_{j+1},x,q_j,p_i) is a tight path, in which case its four-vertex support is proper Hamiltonian and minimum-counterexample calculus gives outcome (1), or both (q_j,p_i,x) and (p_i,q_j,x) are tight, which is outcome (2). These cases exhaust the two failed-insertion normal forms.
