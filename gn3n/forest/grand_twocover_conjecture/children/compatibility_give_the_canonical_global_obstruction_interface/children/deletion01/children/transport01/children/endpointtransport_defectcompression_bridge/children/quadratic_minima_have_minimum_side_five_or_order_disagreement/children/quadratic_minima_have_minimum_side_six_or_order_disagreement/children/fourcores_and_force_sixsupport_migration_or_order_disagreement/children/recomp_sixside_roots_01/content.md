# Four endpoint roots over a six-set force a Hamiltonian four-set or a classified perfect-matching extension grid

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament and let C=X|P|Q be a spanning three-cover minimizing Phi in its pairwise-repartition component, with |X|=6 and |P|,|Q|>=8. Let E be the four displayed endpoints of P and Q. For each e in E define a graph G_e on V(X) by {a,b} in E(G_e) exactly when (V(X)-{a,b}) union {e} is Hamiltonian, and let mu(h)=|{e in E:h in E(G_e)}|. Then every G_e has minimum degree at least three and at least nine edges, and at least three edges h of K_6 satisfy mu(h)>=3. Moreover either: (A) two such high-multiplicity edges are adjacent, in which case they have at least two common roots and—unless order disagreement already occurs—the corresponding common-root six-sets are Hamiltonian; or (B) the three high-multiplicity edges form a perfect matching M, every edge of M has multiplicity four, every edge outside M has multiplicity two, every G_e is cubic with exactly nine edges, and G_e-M is a spanning 2-factor of K_6-M. In branch (B), put D_i=V(X)-M_i. Unless order disagreement occurs, D_i union {e,f} is Hamiltonian for every matching edge M_i and every pair {e,f} subset E; for each i this complete pair-extension family forces either a Hamiltonian seven-set D_i union T for some three-set T subset E or the endpoint four-set E itself to be Hamiltonian. In all cases H contains order disagreement or a Hamiltonian four-set. If H is a minimum counterexample, every proper Hamiltonian four- or seven-set produced here has non-Hamiltonian complement of path-cover number two.
