# Bad five-extensions admit overlapping four-paths with a common directed consecutive pair

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament, X a Hamiltonian four-vertex set, and a,b distinct exterior vertices. Suppose X union {a} and X union {b} are non-Hamiltonian. Then there exist distinct x,y,z in X and Hamilton tight paths R,R' on {a,b,x,y} and {a,b,x,z}, respectively, such that some two distinct vertices u,v in {a,b,x} occur consecutively in the order u,v in both R and R'.

## Body

Conjecture; no proof is asserted. The proved overlap theorem supplies the two Hamiltonian supports, but supplies no agreement between their Hamilton orders. This conjecture asks for a common directed consecutive pair on their three-vertex intersection. It does not assert cyclic invariance of a tight triple or that either Hamilton order can be reversed.

First approach: for the endpoint-rooted Hamiltonian-four graph, retain the full families of Hamilton orders at adjacent edges. Suppose every adjacent pair fails the displayed agreement and derive the resulting forced incident-edge comparisons. Exploit the acyclic comparison representations of X+a and X+b. A shared ordered pair could allow subsequent splice constructions to preserve actual path orders. The conjectured alignment, rather than support overlap alone, is the missing step.
