## Mathematical reasons

Put lambda=L(H) and sigma=2lambda-(n-1) in a minimum counterexample. Every deletion-cover size pair is lambda-a,lambda-b with a+b=sigma and a,b>=0. The prepared half-order model concerns sigma=0 only. Sigma=1 is the even-order near-equal case and larger sigma remains possible. No result read in this handoff reduces the entire grand conjecture to sigma=0.

In sigma=0, every possible D=1 support state has sizes lambda and lambda+1. Its single-vertex D=1 transitions are exactly the certified odd-graph transfers. A D=0 bipartition would contain a Hamiltonian support of order at least lambda+1, contradicting the assumed value of lambda. Consequently proving connectivity, recurrence, expansion, or label coverage inside the support graph does not itself provide an absorption. Such information is valuable only when coupled to a boundary-orientation argument producing a longer path or an actual two-cover.

The accompanying proved proposition makes this limitation precise with a support-only model: declare every nonempty set of size at most lambda allowable on 2lambda+1 vertices. It has cover number three, all proper subsets two-coverable, universal equal deletion covers, and the full odd graph with every transfer available. It is not a boundary tournament. It demonstrates that the listed support axioms alone cannot close the argument; an additional consequence of ordered tightness must exclude it. This is not a prohibition on support methods.

The same proposition gives a useful check on cycle proposals: even closed walks use every edge label evenly, while odd closed walks use every vertex label oddly and have length at least 2lambda+1. These identities follow from the ambient odd graph and are not contradictions. Nor may the good-deletion transfer invariant be generalized to every D=1 move outside the equality case.
