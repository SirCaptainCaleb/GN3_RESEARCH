# The unique-large equitable profile always exposes a standard transport disturbance

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let A|B|C be a Phi-minimal trapped three-cover with |A|=r+1 and |B|=|C|=r, r>=3. Then at least one canonical terminal-frontier witness occurs, or the neutral-transfer residue exposes one of the standard endpoint-transport / defect-compression inputs: order disagreement, at least three ordinary comparison-cover edges crossing a displayed three-part core partition, an inherited displayed-core edge whose endpoints lie in different comparison-cover components, a leave-and-return excursion through another displayed core, or an explicit bounded reverse cross triple. Thus the nonuniform equitable profile {r+1,r,r} has no remaining structureless neutral-transfer branch.

## Body

Write the endpoints of A as x,y. Apply the certified size-gap-one dichotomy to A|B and A|C. If either pair has no Hamiltonian endpoint enlargement, the paired-noninsertion branch and the universal two-noninsertable-vertex theorem already yield a canonical terminal-frontier witness. Hence assume each smaller path has at least one Hamiltonian enlargement by an endpoint of A. If the same endpoint, say x, Hamiltonizes both B+x and C+x, apply b5150f71d277: every two-cover of H-x has at least two edges crossing the equal-core partition (A-x)|B|C, and the transition-count equality case yields an inherited-core crossing or leave-and-return excursion, while three or more crossings give crossing multiplicity. Now suppose no endpoint Hamiltonizes both smaller paths. Since there are only the two endpoints x,y, after relabeling we have B+x and C+y Hamiltonian while B+y and C+x are non-Hamiltonian. If a chosen Hamilton order on B+x or C+y disagrees with the displayed order of its core, we have order disagreement. Otherwise x and y are successful insertions into B and C. Take a two-cover T of H-{x,y}. If T has order disagreement, we are done. If at least two T-edges cross the residual core partition M|B|C, where A=(x,M,y), then t>=3 gives crossing multiplicity and t=2 gives, by the transition-count argument, an inherited-core crossing or leave-and-return excursion. If t=1, d4e6e9fba9ed localizes the successful insertions to the two gaps adjacent to the unique core-core join, and f5a0b71835ae shows that attempting the corresponding enlarged concatenation forces an explicit reverse cross triple. These cases exhaust the two endpoint-enlargement sets for B and C.
