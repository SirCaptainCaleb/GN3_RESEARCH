# Aligned neutral-transfer cycles force a repeated vertex or order disagreement

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament and let C_0,C_1,...,C_k=C_0 be a cycle in the pairwise-repartition graph whose vertices are three-covers with path orders {r+1,r+1,r}, where r>=3. Suppose every edge is a neutral endpoint transfer of the following aligned form: one initial vertex of a path of order r+1 is deleted and inserted as the initial vertex of a path of order r, or one terminal vertex is deleted and inserted as the terminal vertex of a path of order r; all unaffected vertices retain their displayed relative order. Then either some vertex is transferred on at least two cycle edges, or two displayed path orders occurring on one common support along the cycle have order disagreement. In particular, an order-neutral aligned cycle must repeat a transferred vertex.

## Body

Track the three path positions from C_0 through the successive transfers, allowing their supports to change. Assume no vertex is transferred twice and no order disagreement occurs. Because the final three-cover equals C_0 up to permutation of its three displayed paths, each final tracked path support is one of the three original supports. Consider an original support A. If some vertex of A is never transferred, it remains in its original tracked path throughout. Since the three original supports are disjoint and the final support partition is the original one, that tracked path must finish with support A. Hence no vertex of A could have been transferred away. Therefore every original support is of one of two types: either none of its vertices is transferred, or every one of its vertices is transferred exactly once and all of them go directly to the same final tracked path. Since the cycle is nontrivial, at least one original support A=(a_1,...,a_m), m>=r>=3, is of the second type. Ignore vertices from the other original supports while tracking the relative order of the A-vertices. Each time an A-vertex leaves its original tracked path it must be the leftmost or rightmost remaining A-vertex, because the transfer deletes a displayed path endpoint. If it leaves from the left, aligned transfer inserts it before every A-vertex already present in its final tracked path; if it leaves from the right, it is inserted after every such A-vertex. Consequently the A-vertices removed from the left are exactly a_1,...,a_j in that order and occur finally as a_j,...,a_1, while those removed from the right are a_m,...,a_{j+1} in that order and occur finally as a_m,...,a_{j+1}. Thus their final relative order is (a_j,...,a_1,a_m,...,a_{j+1}) for some 0<=j<=m. For m>=3 this is never (a_1,...,a_m): if j=0 or j=m the whole order is reversed; if j=1 the second final vertex is a_m rather than a_2; and if 2<=j<=m-1 the first final vertex is a_j rather than a_1. Hence the initial and final displayed orders on support A disagree, contradiction. Therefore an aligned cycle with no order disagreement must transfer some vertex at least twice.
