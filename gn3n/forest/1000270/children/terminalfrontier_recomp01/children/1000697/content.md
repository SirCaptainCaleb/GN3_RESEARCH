# Opposite donor endpoints localize every one-crossing residue to join-adjacent insertion gaps

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let A=(x,M,y), B=(b_1,...,b_r), C=(c_1,...,c_r) be a Phi-minimal trapped three-cover with |A|=r+1, |B|=|C|=r and r>=3, so |M|=r-1. Suppose H[B+x] and H[C+y] are Hamiltonian while H[B+y] and H[C+x] are non-Hamiltonian. Assume Hamilton orders on B+x and C+y preserve the displayed relative orders of B and C, so x and y have successful insertion gaps in B and C respectively. Let T be a two-cover of H-{x,y}. Then either T has order disagreement with M,B, or C; or T has at least two ordinary edges joining distinct members of V(M)|V(B)|V(C); or T has exactly one such edge and, after possibly interchanging (x,B) with (y,C), one of the following holds: (i) B is an entire T-component, the other component is M followed by C, and every successful insertion gap of y in C is one of the first two gaps; (ii) M is an entire T-component, the other component is B followed by C, every successful insertion gap of x in B is one of the last two gaps, and every successful insertion gap of y in C is one of the first two gaps. The reversed concatenation C followed by B gives the mirror last-two/first-two conclusion. Thus the opposite-donor branch is either already a standard transport disturbance or is confined to endpoint-adjacent geometry at the unique core-core join.

## Body

Count ordinary T-edges crossing the partition V(M)|V(B)|V(C). Component drop gives at least one. If there are at least two, the second outcome holds. Suppose there is exactly one and no order disagreement. Cutting that edge leaves exactly three monochromatic blocks, so each of M,B,C is one whole T-block in its displayed order; one block is an entire T-component and the other two are concatenated in one of the two possible orders. First suppose B is isolated. Replace B by a Hamilton path on B+x. If the other T-component were C followed by M, then appending y to M would give the tight path C,M,y because (M,y) is inherited from A, and together with B+x this would two-cover H. Hence the other component must be M followed by C. If y had any successful insertion gap in C other than the first two gaps, inserting y there would leave the first two vertices of C unchanged, so both join triples from M into C would remain tight; M followed by that enlarged C-path, together with B+x, would again two-cover H. Hence every successful gap of y in C is among the first two. The case C isolated is symmetric: the other component must be B followed by M, and every successful gap of x in B is among the last two. Finally suppose M is isolated. If the other component is B followed by C, then using the tight path (M,y) on the isolated support shows that any insertion of x into B outside the last two gaps would preserve the B-to-C join and give a two-cover; hence x is confined to the last two B-gaps. Using (x,M) on the isolated support similarly shows that y is confined to the first two C-gaps. If the concatenation is C followed by B the conclusions reverse. These are all possibilities.
