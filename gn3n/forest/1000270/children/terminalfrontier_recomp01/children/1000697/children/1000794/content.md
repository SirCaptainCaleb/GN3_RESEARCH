# Every join-adjacent opposite-donor residue forces a reverse cross triple

## Statement

Under the one-crossing join-adjacent alternatives of d4e6e9fba9ed, H necessarily contains an explicit tight triple obtained by reversing a failed new join triple. More precisely, if B is isolated and the other path is M followed by C, with m the terminal vertex of M, then insertion of y before c_1 forces (c_1,y,m) tight, while insertion of y between c_1,c_2 forces (y,c_1,m) tight. The case C isolated is symmetric. If M is isolated and the other path is B followed by C, then after inserting x in one of the last two B-gaps and y in one of the first two C-gaps, at least one of the two consecutive triples straddling the enlarged B-C join is non-tight, so its reversal is tight. Thus every one-crossing opposite-donor residue supplies a bounded reverse cross triple.

## Body

Suppose first that B is an entire component of T and the other T-component is M followed by C. Replace B by any Hamilton path on B+x. By d4e6e9fba9ed, y inserts into C only in gap 0 or gap 1. If y inserts in gap 0, consider the sequence M,y,c_1,...,c_r. All consecutive triples are tight except possibly (m,y,c_1): the triple ending M with y is inherited from A=(x,M,y), and the triples from y onward belong to the successful insertion order. If (m,y,c_1) were tight, this path together with B+x would two-cover H. Hence it is non-tight and (c_1,y,m) is tight. If y inserts in gap 1, use M,c_1,y,c_2,...,c_r. The only possibly new triple is (m,c_1,y), so its failure is forced and (y,c_1,m) is tight. The case C isolated is symmetric: if T has B followed by M and x inserts after b_r, the only new triple is (b_r,x,m_1), forcing (m_1,x,b_r); if x inserts between b_{r-1},b_r, the only new triple is (x,b_r,m_1), forcing (m_1,b_r,x). Finally suppose M is isolated and T has B followed by C. Use a Hamilton path on M+x or M+y for the isolated component only to establish the localization from d4e6e9fba9ed; now simultaneously insert x into its permitted last-two gap of B and y into its permitted first-two gap of C in the concatenated B-C path. Every triple wholly inside the enlarged B-order or enlarged C-order is tight. Exactly two consecutive triples can be new at their join. If both were tight, the enlarged B-C sequence together with M would be a spanning two-cover of H. Therefore at least one is non-tight, and boundary antisymmetry makes its reversal tight. The C-followed-by-B case is identical with left and right interchanged.
