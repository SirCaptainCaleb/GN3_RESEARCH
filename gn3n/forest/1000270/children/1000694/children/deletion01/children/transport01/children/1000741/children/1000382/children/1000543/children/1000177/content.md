# Every sparse order-neutral endpoint comparison forces a bounded reverse boundary triple

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let A=(a_1,...,a_r), r>=4, be one path of a spanning three-cover A|B|C. Let d=a_1 and let T be a two-cover of H-d in the order-neutral sparse branch t=2,s=1 of a782f55502a4 relative to the inherited three-cover (A-d)|B|C. Then T necessarily exposes an explicit tight reverse triple in a constant-size window containing d and a boundary of one T-block. In particular, after the one-edge-for-two exchange of 580b7d9218c9, endpoint restoration either gives an immediate reverse-cross triple or insertion of d immediately before the surviving endpoint-adjacent inherited block fails at one of at most three new triples; one inherited triple is known tight, so reversing a failed predecessor-side or successor-side triple gives the required bounded witness. Thus the doubly sparse reciprocal-crossing residue produces a bounded reverse-cross witness without needing an opposite endpoint probe.

## Body

By 580b7d9218c9, the sparse t=2,s=1 branch is obtained from the inherited three-path forest J=(A-d)|B|C by deleting exactly one inherited ordinary edge e and adding exactly two cross-class edges.

First suppose e lies in A-d. Let L be the endpoint-adjacent block of A-d after deleting e. By 6431dd7d1ef2, either L is an initial singleton and an explicit reverse cross triple already occurs, or L is not initial in its T-path. In the latter case insert d immediately before L. Let u be the T-vertex immediately preceding L and, when it exists, let v be the T-vertex immediately preceding u. If |L|>=2, writing its first two vertices a_2,a_3, the only new triples are (v,u,d) when v exists, (u,d,a_2), and the inherited tight triple (d,a_2,a_3). Since restoring d cannot produce a spanning two-cover, one of the first two triples is non-tight; boundary antisymmetry yields respectively (d,u,v) or (a_2,d,u) tight. If L={a_2}, there may additionally be a successor w after a_2, and failure of restoration similarly yields one of (d,u,v), (a_2,d,u), or (w,a_2,d) tight, using only the triples that exist. Thus a bounded reverse triple is forced.

Now suppose e lies in B or C. Then no inherited edge of R=A-d is deleted, so by 580b7d9218c9 all ordinary edges of R remain in T and R occurs as one contiguous inherited-order block in one T-path. Because r>=4, R has at least three vertices. R cannot be initial: prepending d would create only the inherited tight triple (d,a_2,a_3), producing a spanning two-cover. Hence R has an immediate predecessor u in its T-path; let v be the predecessor of u if one exists. Insert d between u and a_2. The only new triples are (v,u,d) when v exists, (u,d,a_2), and the inherited tight triple (d,a_2,a_3). Restoration must fail, so one of the first two is non-tight, and boundary antisymmetry gives (d,u,v) or (a_2,d,u) tight. In all cases the witness lies in a constant-size boundary window.
