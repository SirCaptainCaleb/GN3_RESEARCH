# Internal restoration has a Hamiltonian, cyclic, or forced matching-block four-kernel

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample, let H-y=R|Q be a deletion cover whose smaller component R has globally minimum order mu, and let z be a terminal endpoint of R. Suppose a deletion cover of H-z is in the support-compatible internal-restoration branch of 89d96023752d and preserves the inherited order on R-z. Then, writing R=(P,b,a,z), the restored path necessarily ends (P,b,y,a), and on X={b,a,y,z} the five triples (b,a,z), (b,y,a), (z,a,y), (y,z,a), and (z,y,b) are tight. Consequently exactly one of the following holds: (1) X is Hamiltonian; then mu=3 gives a spanning two-cover, while mu>=5 gives a strict quadratic-potential descent by repartitioning R|{y} as P|X; (2) X is the exceptional cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-kernel; or (3) X is edge-orderable and its three opposite-edge matching blocks are forced in the order {ab,yz} < {az,by} < {ay,bz}. The initial-end version is symmetric.

## Body

Write R=(a_0,...,a_{mu-2},z). In the support-compatible internal-restoration branch, the varying path of the cover of H-z is obtained by inserting y internally into the inherited order A=(a_0,...,a_{mu-2}). If two or more A-vertices followed y, the last two A-vertices would remain consecutive at the end; appending z would then use only the inherited final triple of R and would produce a spanning two-cover together with Q. Hence exactly one A-vertex follows y. Write that last vertex as a and its predecessor as b, so R=(P,b,a,z) and the restored path ends (P,b,y,a). Thus (b,a,z) and (b,y,a) are tight. If (y,a,z) were tight, appending z to the restored path would again two-cover H with Q. Therefore (y,a,z) is non-tight and boundary antisymmetry gives (z,a,y) tight. The certified endpoint-hook theorem c38e8b5c48ee applied to the terminal endpoint z of the original cover H-y=R|Q gives the additional tight triples (y,z,a) and (z,y,b). No cyclic rotation is used.

Let X={b,a,y,z}. If X is Hamiltonian, repartition the pair R|{y}. For mu=3, P is empty and X itself together with Q is a spanning two-cover. For mu>=4, P is a tight path of order mu-3 and X is a tight path of order four. The change in quadratic potential from component orders mu,1 to mu-3,4 is (mu-3)^2+16-(mu^2+1)=24-6mu, which is strictly negative for mu>=5 and zero for mu=4. This proves the stated Hamiltonian consequences.

Assume X is non-Hamiltonian. By the four-vertex classification in smallset01, either its comparison digraph is cyclic, in which case X is the exceptional cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-kernel, or X is edge-orderable and the three opposite-edge perfect matchings form strict consecutive blocks. Translate the five known tight triples into edge-order inequalities: (b,a,z) gives ab<az; (b,y,a) gives by<ay; (z,a,y) gives az<ay; (y,z,a) gives yz<az; and (z,y,b) gives yz<by. Put M0={ab,yz}, M1={az,by}, M2={ay,bz}. The inequalities yz<az, yz<by, and ab<az force M0 before M1 in the matching-block order, while az<ay and by<ay force M1 before M2. Therefore the only possible matching-block order is M0<M1<M2, as claimed. The initial-end case is obtained by reversing the displayed role of the endpoint, with the same local argument.
