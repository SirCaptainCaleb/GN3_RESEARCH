# Deficit-one order-preserving comparisons either descend, reverse, or localize to one corridor gap

## Statement

Let A be a globally longest tight path and C an A-order-preserving tight path of order |A|-1 with no A-edge reversal witness. Choose a noncrossing charging mu:C-A -> A-C. Then either (1) some charge is strict, and the canonical charged replacement yields strict decrease of total binary-balance mass, an A-edge reversal witness, or one of the bounded reversed-triple obstructions; or (2) every charge stays within its common-vertex gap. In case (2), the A- and C-subpaths through every gap are parallel anchored tight corridors, all gaps are length-balanced except one surplus gap g* where the A-corridor has exactly one additional internal vertex, and by at most two prefix-suffix splices at the anchors of g* one obtains either an explicit reversed mixed-junction tight triple or a tight A-order-preserving path C* of order |A|-1 agreeing with A outside g*. Thus the non-descent, non-reversal residue is supported entirely in one unit-surplus corridor gap.

## Body

Let O=A-C and E=C-A. The noncrossing charging theorem gives an injection mu:E->O with every charged omission in the same gap as its exterior vertex or in an earlier gap, and |O|=|E|+1. If some charge is strict, the certified charged-replacement theorem gives strict binary-balance descent, an A-edge reversal witness, or one of its bounded reversed-triple obstructions, proving (1). Otherwise every charge lies in the same gap. Hence in each common-vertex gap the number of exterior vertices is at most the number of omitted A-vertices, while the total excess of omissions is one. Therefore exactly one gap g* has one extra omitted A-vertex and every other gap is balanced. Because A and C preserve the same common-vertex order, the contiguous A-subpath and C-subpath through each internal gap are both tight and have disjoint interiors; endpoint gaps give the analogous one-ended corridors. Thus all corridors are length-balanced except g*, whose A-side is longer by one internal vertex. Now splice at the anchors bordering g*. At a left anchor q, replace the C-prefix through q by the A-prefix through q. The only new consecutive triple is the mixed junction triple across q; if it is non-tight, boundary antisymmetry gives the reversed tight triple. If it is tight, the splice is a tight path and balance of all gaps left of g* preserves total order |A|-1. At a right anchor q prime, splice the resulting prefix through q prime to the A-suffix. Again there is only one new mixed junction triple; failure yields its reversed tight triple, while success is tight and preserves order because all gaps right of g* are balanced. Endpoint surplus gaps require only the opposite-side splice, and if there are no common anchors the unique gap already contains every discrepancy. Therefore, unless a reversed mixed-junction triple appears, the resulting path C* is tight, A-order-preserving, has order |A|-1, agrees with A outside g*, and differs inside g* by exactly one internal vertex in A favor.
