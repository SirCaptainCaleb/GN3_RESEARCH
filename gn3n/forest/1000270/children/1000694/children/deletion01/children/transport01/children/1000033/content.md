# Two alternative-2 pivots for one exterior vertex force a mixed Hamiltonian four-set or a doubled cross barrier

## Statement

Let Q=(q_1,...,q_m) and R=(r_1,...,r_s) be vertex-disjoint tight paths in a boundary tournament, and let x lie outside both. Suppose alternative 2 of the failed-insertion normal form from insert01 holds for x on Q at the internal gap q_i|q_{i+1} and on R at the internal gap r_j|r_{j+1}. Then either at least one of the four-vertex orders (q_{i+1},x,q_i,r_j) and (r_{j+1},x,r_j,q_i) is a tight path, or both cross triples (r_j,q_i,x) and (q_i,r_j,x) are tight. Thus two second-type pivots for one exterior vertex yield a mixed Hamiltonian four-set or an explicit doubled cross barrier on q_i,r_j,x.

## Body

Alternative 2 on Q gives the reverse-through-gap tight triple (q_{i+1},x,q_i), while alternative 2 on R gives (r_{j+1},x,r_j). If (x,q_i,r_j) is tight, then (q_{i+1},x,q_i,r_j) is a tight four-path. If (x,r_j,q_i) is tight, then (r_{j+1},x,r_j,q_i) is a tight four-path. Suppose neither mixed four-path occurs. Then (x,q_i,r_j) is non-tight, so boundary antisymmetry gives its actual reverse (r_j,q_i,x) tight. Likewise (x,r_j,q_i) is non-tight, so its actual reverse (q_i,r_j,x) is tight. This is the doubled cross barrier. The four displayed vertices in either Hamiltonian alternative are distinct because Q and R are disjoint and x lies outside both.
