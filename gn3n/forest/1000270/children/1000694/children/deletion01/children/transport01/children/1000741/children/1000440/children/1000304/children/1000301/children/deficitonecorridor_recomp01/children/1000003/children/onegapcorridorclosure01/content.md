# The one-gap unit-surplus corridor has no unbounded neutral residue

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample, let A be a globally longest tight path of order lambda, and let C be a tight A-order-preserving path of order lambda-1 that agrees with A outside one common-vertex gap. Then at least one of the following occurs: (1) a bounded discrepancy kernel of order at most five is obtained; (2) H contains a proper Hamiltonian support of order four or five whose complement is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two; (3) explicit order disagreement occurs; or (4) an explicit tight triple reverses an A-edge or a splice junction. Consequently the balanced longest-path exchange from onegapbalancedexchange01 is not a terminal residue, even for an internal discrepancy gap.

## Body

Apply onegapbalancedexchange01. Its bounded, Hamiltonian-support, order-disagreement, and reversed-junction alternatives give (1)-(4), so suppose its balanced alternative occurs. Thus for some z in V(A)-V(C), inserting z between consecutive exterior vertices of C gives a globally longest tight path B=C_z of order lambda. The paths A and B are A-order-preserving, agree outside the original discrepancy gap, and have equal-size support differences. If the positional-lag theorem exposes an A-edge reversing witness when comparing B with A, outcome (4) holds. Otherwise ba4a66971eb6 with deficit d=0 says the omission-minus-insertion prefix balance is zero at every surviving A-vertex, in particular at the new common vertex z. Equivalently, the A-prefix through z and the B-prefix through z have the same order; since |V(A)|=|V(B)|, the corresponding suffixes also have the same order. Form the two mixed sequences obtained by taking the A-prefix through z followed by the B-suffix after z, and the B-prefix through z followed by the A-suffix after z. They are vertex-simple because A and B induce the same relative order on their common vertices, and each has order lambda by the zero prefix balance. Every consecutive triple is inherited from A or B except for at most one mixed junction triple at z. If either existing mixed junction is non-tight, boundary antisymmetry gives outcome (4). Otherwise both mixed sequences are globally longest tight paths. Each agrees with A outside only one of the two subgaps into which z splits the former discrepancy gap, and its support difference from A is balanced in that subgap. Apply equalorderonegapclosure01 to either nontrivial mixed path. It yields a bounded gap support of order at most four, a proper Hamiltonian four- or five-support with path-cover-two complement, order disagreement, or a reversed splice-junction triple. These are respectively outcomes (1)-(4). Hence the balanced alternative is fully consumed.
