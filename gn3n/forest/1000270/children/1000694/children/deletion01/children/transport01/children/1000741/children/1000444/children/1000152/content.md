# Internal restoration at a minimum-side endpoint forces a reversed endpoint edge

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample, let mu be the globally minimum smaller-component order among deletion covers, and let H-y=R|Q be a deletion cover with |R|=mu. Let z be an endpoint of R, put A=V(R)-{z}, and let T be a deletion cover of H-z in the support-compatible restoration branch of 89d96023752d. Assume the Hamilton path on A union {y} preserves the inherited A-order and y is internal. Then y lies one vertex from the z-end of A, and a tight triple reverses the displayed endpoint edge of R incident with z. More precisely, if R=(a_0,...,a_{mu-2},z), then the varying path of T ends ...,y,a_{mu-2} and (z,a_{mu-2},y) is tight. The initial-end case is symmetric.

## Body

Assume z is the terminal endpoint of R and write R=(a_0,...,a_{mu-2},z). In the support-compatible internal-restoration branch, the varying path of T is obtained by inserting y into the inherited order A=(a_0,...,a_{mu-2}) with nonempty A-blocks on both sides. Write it as S=(a_0,...,a_i,y,a_{i+1},...,a_{mu-2}). If at least two A-vertices occur after y, then the final two vertices of S are a_{mu-3},a_{mu-2}. Appending z creates no new consecutive triple except (a_{mu-3},a_{mu-2},z), which is inherited from R and is tight. Thus (S,z) is a Hamilton path on A union {y,z}; together with the unchanged path Q it gives a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Hence exactly one A-vertex follows y, so i=mu-3 and S ends ...,y,a_{mu-2}. If (y,a_{mu-2},z) were tight, appending z would again give a Hamilton path on A union {y,z}, and together with Q a spanning two-cover. Therefore (y,a_{mu-2},z) is non-tight. Boundary antisymmetry forces (z,a_{mu-2},y) tight, which reverses the displayed terminal edge (a_{mu-2},z) of R. The initial-end case follows by reversing the argument: internal restoration must place y immediately after the first A-vertex, and failure to prepend z forces the tight reversal through that first displayed endpoint edge.
