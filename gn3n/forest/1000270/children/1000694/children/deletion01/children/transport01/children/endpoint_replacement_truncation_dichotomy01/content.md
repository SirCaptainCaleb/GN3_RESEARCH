# Every one-vertex extension either Hamiltonizes or locks insertion into the inherited path

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament, let R be any displayed tight path, and let w lie outside R. Then either H[V(R) union {w}] is Hamiltonian, or w is noninsertable into every position of the inherited displayed order R.

Consequently, in the endpoint-replacement setting with a disjoint vertex set W, a path P with displayed endpoint e, w in W, R=V(P)-{e}, and (W-{w}) union {e} Hamiltonian, the first outcome gives an equal-cardinality two-support exchange whenever H[W] is Hamiltonian. No assumption about W, e, replacement Hamiltonicity, ambient covers, extremality, or minimum-counterexample structure is needed for the dichotomy itself.

## Body

If R union {w} is Hamiltonian, the first outcome holds. Otherwise, inserting w at any position of the inherited displayed path R cannot produce a tight Hamilton path on R union {w}; hence w is noninsertable at every position.

The endpoint-replacement formulation is only a corollary used by transport arguments. If (W-{w}) union {e} is Hamiltonian and R union {w} is Hamiltonian, these two disjoint supports partition W union V(P); if H[W] is also Hamiltonian, this is the corresponding equal-cardinality exchange. Thus the actual enabling mechanism is simply non-Hamiltonicity of the one-vertex extension, not the replacement hypothesis.
