# Endpoint restoration orients the endpoint-adjacent block of a one-edge-for-two exchange

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament with pc(H)>2 and let d be a vertex such that H-d has both an inherited three-cover J=R_1|R_2|R_3 and an order-neutral two-cover T satisfying the clean one-edge-for-two exchange of 580b7d9218c9. Suppose R_1=(r_1,...,r_m) is inherited from a tight path (d,r_1,...,r_m) of H. Delete the unique edge e of J-T and let L be the component of R_1-e containing r_1. Then either L is not the initial block of its T-component, or L={r_1} and the next vertex z of that T-component gives the explicit reverse cross triple (z,r_1,d). Consequently, outside an explicit reverse-cross witness, the endpoint-adjacent block L is never initial: in the quotient normal form of ebefcaf161da it is either internal in the three-block path or is an endpoint block oriented at the terminal end of its T-component. The symmetric statement holds for a terminal omitted endpoint.

## Body

First suppose |L|>=2, say L=(r_1,...,r_j). If L were the initial block of its T-component Q, then Q begins (r_1,...,r_j,...), preserving the inherited order. Prepending d creates only the new triple (d,r_1,r_2), which is tight because (d,r_1,...,r_m) is the inherited displayed path. Hence (d,Q), together with the other T-component, would two-cover H, contradiction. Now suppose L={r_1}. If L were itself an entire T-component, adjoining d gives the two-vertex tight path (d,r_1), again producing a spanning two-cover with the other T-component. Therefore an initial singleton block has a next vertex z in the same T-component. If (d,r_1,z) were tight, prepending d to that T-component would again give a spanning two-cover: all later triples are inherited from T. Hence (d,r_1,z) is non-tight, and boundary antisymmetry gives the explicit reverse cross triple (z,r_1,d). Thus, unless this reverse-cross witness already occurs, L cannot be initial for any size. The quotient orientation conclusion follows exactly as before. The terminal-end statement is symmetric.
