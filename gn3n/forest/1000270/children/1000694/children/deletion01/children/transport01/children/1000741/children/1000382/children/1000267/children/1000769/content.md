# A one-edge-for-two exchange has only matching or three-block-path quotient type

## Statement

Let J=R_1|R_2|R_3 and T satisfy 580b7d9218c9. Let e be the unique edge of J-T, suppose e lies on R_1, and write R_1-e=L|R with L,R nonempty inherited subpaths. Contract L,R,R_2,R_3 to four vertices and retain the two edges T-J. Then the resulting quotient is a forest with exactly two edges and exactly two connected components. Consequently exactly one of two topological types occurs: (I) the two new edges are disjoint, pairing the four inherited pieces into two 2-vertex quotient components; or (II) the two new edges share one quotient vertex, forming a 3-vertex path while the fourth inherited piece is an untouched T-component. In particular every sparse order-neutral comparison residue is supported on at most three inherited blocks in one T-component, with the other T-component containing one or two whole inherited pieces.

## Body

By 580b7d9218c9 the ordinary forest of T is obtained from the ordinary forest of J by deleting the single inherited edge e and adding exactly two cross-class edges f,g. Deleting e from J changes its three path components into four nonempty path components L,R,R_2,R_3. Before adding f,g there are therefore four connected components. Adding f and g yields T, which has exactly two connected components, so each added edge decreases the component count by one. In particular neither added edge can have both endpoints already connected at the moment it is added, and the two additions create no cycle. Contract each of L,R,R_2,R_3. The images of f,g are therefore two ordinary edges of an acyclic graph on four vertices with exactly two components. A two-edge forest on four vertices is either a matching, with component sizes 2+2, or a path of order three together with an isolated vertex, with component sizes 3+1. These are exactly types (I) and (II). Since f,g are cross-class T-edges, neither quotient edge joins L directly to R, but no further topology is possible.
