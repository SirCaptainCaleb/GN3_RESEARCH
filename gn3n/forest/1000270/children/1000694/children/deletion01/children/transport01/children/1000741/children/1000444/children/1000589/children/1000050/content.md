# A same-end minimum-side replacement gives order disagreement, quadratic descent, or the 5|5|1 case

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and suppose H-y=(A,z)|Q and H-z=(A,y)|Q are deletion covers, where y and z occupy the same displayed endpoint position after the replacement, |A|+1=mu is the globally minimum smaller deletion-side order, and q=|Q|>=mu. Then at least one of the following holds: (1) a six-vertex induced subtournament has four Hamiltonian deletions whose Hamilton paths exhibit order disagreement; (2) the connected component of (A,z)|Q|{y} in the pairwise-repartition graph contains a spanning three-cover with strictly smaller quadratic potential; or (3) q=mu=5 and |V(H)|=11.

## Body

Write Q=(q_0,...,q_{q-1}). Minimum-counterexample size and deletion-cover side bounds give mu>=3. If q<=4, then |V(H)|=mu+q+1<=9, impossible, so q>=5. If q=5 and mu<=q, the minimum-counterexample lower bound |V(H)|>10 forces mu=5 and |V(H)|=11, giving outcome (3). Hence assume q>=6. Put M=(q_2,...,q_{q-3}) and U={q_0,q_1,y,z,q_{q-2},q_{q-1}}. The path M is nonempty. If H[U] is Hamiltonian, then a Hamilton path on U together with A and M gives a spanning three-cover of H with component orders 6,mu-1,q-4. Relative to the singleton lift (A,z)|Q|{y}, whose orders are mu,q,1, the change in Phi is 6^2+(mu-1)^2+(q-4)^2-(mu^2+q^2+1)=52-2mu-8q<0 for mu>=3,q>=6. Thus outcome (2) holds. Suppose H[U] is non-Hamiltonian. By the four-of-six theorem, at least four vertex deletions of U are Hamiltonian. If U-{y} is Hamiltonian, then its Hamilton path together with (A,y) and M gives a spanning three-cover with orders 5,mu,q-4; the Phi change from the same singleton lift is 5^2+mu^2+(q-4)^2-(mu^2+q^2+1)=40-8q<0. The same argument applies if U-{z} is Hamiltonian, using (A,z). Hence assume neither deletion is Hamiltonian. The four remaining deletions, obtained by deleting q_0,q_1,q_{q-2},q_{q-1}, must all be Hamiltonian. Applying astra004fourgooddisagree to the non-Hamiltonian six-set U and Hamilton paths on these four deletions yields explicit order disagreement. This is outcome (1).
