# A permanently internal absorbed endpoint forces a crossing of the original support cut

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let X|C|D be a spanning three-cover, where H[X] is Hamiltonian, C=(c_0,c_1,...,c_m) is a nontrivial tight path with m>=1, and D is a nonempty tight path. Suppose H[X union {c_0}] is Hamiltonian and c_0 is internal in every Hamilton tight path on X union {c_0}. Then every exact two-cover T of H-c_0 contains an ordinary path edge with one endpoint in X and the other endpoint in (V(C)-{c_0}) union V(D). Equivalently, no deletion two-cover of H-c_0 respects the coarse bipartition X | ((C-c_0) union D). Combined with 5ff05be1a5bc, every such T has at least two crossings of the finer four-class split induced by deleting c_0 from a Hamilton path on X+c_0, and at least one of those crossings necessarily crosses this coarse original-support cut.

## Body

Fix any Hamilton path R on X union {c_0}; by hypothesis c_0 is internal in R, though only Hamiltonicity of X+c_0 is needed for the coarse-cut conclusion. Put B=(V(C)-{c_0}) union V(D), so V(H-c_0)=X disjoint-union B and both sides are nonempty. Let T=T_1|T_2 be any exact two-cover of H-c_0. Suppose for contradiction that no ordinary edge of either T-component crosses the cut X|B. Then every T-component is contained entirely in one side of the cut: along a path, moving from a vertex of X to a vertex of B would itself use a crossing ordinary edge. Since T covers both nonempty sides and has exactly two components, after relabelling T_1 is a Hamilton path on X and T_2 is a Hamilton path on B. But H[X union {c_0}] is Hamiltonian by hypothesis, so choose any Hamilton path R_0 on X union {c_0}. Then R_0 | T_2 is a spanning two-cover of H, contradicting that H is a minimum counterexample and hence has path-cover number greater than two. Therefore every exact two-cover of H-c_0 has at least one X-B crossing edge. The final sentence follows by combining this conclusion with the certified finer four-class crossing theorem 5ff05be1a5bc.
