# The all-equal support triangle always forces relative-order disagreement

## Statement

In the all-equal {1,1,1} support-triangle setting, relative-order disagreement between tight paths is unavoidable. More precisely, the displayed support paths P_i=(t_i,M_i,t_{i+1}) together with endpoint insertion/barrier alternatives force two tight paths that order a common vertex pair differently. Hence the path-intersection calculus yields a reversed common ordered edge, a reversing tight triple at an intersection, or a vertex-simple tight cycle.

## Body

For each i modulo three, consider the special complementary endpoint x_i=t_{i+2}, which lies in E_i because it is the initial endpoint of A_{i+2}. If x_i is noninsertable into the displayed order P_i and H[S_i union {x_i}] is Hamiltonian, noninsertableorderdisagree01 gives relative-order disagreement immediately. If x_i is noninsertable and H[S_i union {x_i}] is non-Hamiltonian, apply the terminal-end barrier proposition from coversurg01 to S_i with D={x_i}. This gives the tight triple (t_{i+2},t_{i+1},a_i). But P_{i+1}=(t_{i+1},M_{i+1},t_{i+2}) orders the common vertices t_{i+1},t_{i+2} in the opposite order, so Section 4 of pathcalc01 gives relative-order disagreement. Therefore, if no relative-order disagreement occurs anywhere, every x_i=t_{i+2} must be insertable into P_i. Let R_i be the resulting tight Hamilton path on S_i union {t_{i+2}}, obtained by inserting t_{i+2} into the displayed order P_i. All three R_i contain the transferred-label triple T={t_1,t_2,t_3}. Deleting the inserted label from R_i leaves P_i, so R_i preserves the relation t_i before t_{i+1}. Thus R_1 forces t_1<t_2 on T, R_2 forces t_2<t_3, and R_3 forces t_3<t_1. If the three R_i induced one common relative order on T these inequalities would form a cyclic strict order, impossible. Hence some pair R_i,R_j orders two common transferred labels differently. Applying pathcalc01 again gives standard relative-order disagreement. Thus every branch forces order disturbance.
