# The three-plus-two cyclic defect type yields a persistent outer barrier and then a kernel, repeated descent, or generic transport obstruction

## Statement

Assume the certified {3,2} minimum-span cyclic defect geometry. There is a legal strict-Phi move to a three-cover A|T|B with |T|=3, |A|,|B|>=3 and a double-non-tight outer interface. If n>=14, choosing an outer path P of order at least six yields either a further strict descent preserving that outer barrier or a Hamiltonian four- or five-vertex kernel containing the far barrier endpoint. If n>=16, then either such a far-end kernel exists, or two consecutive legal moves strictly decrease Phi, or after the first barrier-preserving descent the two endpoints of the new four-side are noninsertable into one inherited middle subpath of the remaining long path and satisfy the generic paired-noninsertion structural alternative there.

## Body

The {3,2} geometry has a central three-run and a wrap two-run. Lifting the associated deletion cover and moving from the longer tight gap into the singleton gives a three-side T and decreases Phi; the two wrap defects remain the same outer double barrier. For n>=14 one outer path P has order at least six. If the endpoint of P facing T Hamiltonizes T, moving it into T gives another strict descent and does not alter the far outer interface. If not, either the far endpoint Hamiltonizes T or both endpoint four-extensions are bad, in which case repeated bad extension gives a Hamiltonian five-set containing both endpoints. Thus failure of barrier-preserving descent gives the far-end kernel. For n>=16, P may be chosen with order at least seven. After one strict move, the residual long path still has order at least six and the middle side has order four. Apply the general four-side descent/obstruction theorem: either it supplies a second strict descent, or both endpoints of the four-side are noninsertable into one inherited middle path, and the paired-noninsertion theorem gives the stated generic transport obstruction.
