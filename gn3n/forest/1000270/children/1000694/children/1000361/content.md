# The fixed-order dual certificate is a two-edge matching in the defect line

## Statement

Proposal: formulate the global dual problem as a transversal problem over defect-line matchings. For a fixed spanning ordering pi, the minimum number of contiguous tight paths equals one plus the maximum matching number of its defect line. Hence pi fails to give a two-cover exactly when its defect line contains a matching of size at least two. In a minimum counterexample every spanning ordering has such a two-edge defect matching, while some ordering has matching number exactly two. Seek a finite boundary-tournament certificate forcing one of these size-two matchings to survive every vertex permutation.

## Body

The certified defect-line identity gives the exact fixed-order min-max theorem that the terminal-pair cut model lacked: cuts are vertex covers of the defect line, and by Konig on a path their minimum size equals the matching number. The remaining difficulty is wholly global because the ordering itself is variable. Thus a genuine dual theorem must explain why all permutations carry two disjoint defect requirements. One possible certificate language is a bounded family of oriented triples such that every spanning ordering realizes two vertex-disjoint members as consecutive non-tight triples; the boundary-tournament reversal relations would then be used to cancel or reorient the certificate. This is a target, not an asserted certificate.
