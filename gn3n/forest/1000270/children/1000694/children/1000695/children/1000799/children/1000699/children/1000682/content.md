# The two-core transposition forces a Hamiltonian five-kernel or one of two core-to-core transport triples

## Statement

Work in the all-three-split support-switch branch and normalize the surviving two-core transposition as in compatswapgeom28: on the common R-order, x uses the left occupied gap and y the right gap around the shared core vertex r, while on the common S-order y uses the left occupied gap and x the right gap around s. Let z be the third label. Then either the five-set W={x,y,z,r,s} is Hamiltonian, in which case H-W has path-cover number exactly two, or at least one of the two cross-core triples (r,y,s) and (s,x,r) is tight. Thus all four z-alignment types collapse to a Hamiltonian five-kernel or one of two explicit transport orientations between the two core witnesses.

## Body

The transposition theorem gives the base reverse-cross triples (y,r,x) and (x,s,y). In the all-split support normal form compatsupportham22, every one-label enlargement of either core is Hamiltonian. Consequently no two-label enlargement of either core can be Hamiltonian: if, for example, R union {u,v} were Hamiltonian and w were the third special label, then the disjoint Hamiltonian support S union {w} would complete a spanning two-cover of H. Now distinguish only which occupied gap z uses. If z follows x on R, then z and y occupy the two adjacent R-gaps. Simultaneous insertion would Hamiltonize R union {z,y} except possibly for the cross triple (z,r,y); since that enlargement is non-Hamiltonian, (z,r,y) is non-tight and hence (y,r,z) is tight. If z follows y on R, the same argument applied to x,z gives (z,r,x) tight. Similarly, if z follows y on S then (x,s,z) is tight; if z follows x on S then (z,s,y) is tight. There are four alignment types. If z follows x on both cores, the sequence (x,s,y,r,z) has its first and last consecutive triples tight; if its middle triple (s,y,r) is tight then it is a Hamilton path on W, while otherwise boundary antisymmetry gives (r,y,s) tight. In each of the other three alignment types, one of the sequences (z,r,x,s,y) or (y,r,x,s,z) has its first and last consecutive triples tight and middle triple (r,x,s); if that middle triple is tight W is Hamiltonian, otherwise (s,x,r) is tight. Therefore the stated dichotomy holds. When W is Hamiltonian it is a proper five-set in a minimum counterexample, so H-W is non-Hamiltonian and has path-cover number exactly two by minimality.
