# Forward three-five exchanges force cycles of length at least six

## Statement

Let W be an eight-vertex set in a boundary tournament with no Hamiltonian 4|4 partition. Let S be the nonempty family of triples T subset W whose five-vertex complements F=W-T are Hamiltonian. Put D(F)={v in F:F-{v} is Hamiltonian}. Define a directed exchange T->Tprime by choosing a pair E subset D(F) and setting Tprime=F-E. Then W-Tprime=T union E is Hamiltonian, with a Hamilton path whose endpoints both lie in T. Every exchange is a legal neutral pairwise 3|5 repartition, and every vertex has outdegree binom(|D(F)|,2)>=1. Along any directed walk T_0->T_1->T_2->T_3, |T_0 intersect T_2|=1 and |T_0 intersect T_3|=2. Consequently the directed exchange graph has no directed cycle of length at most five. Every forward walk can be continued, and the finite graph therefore contains a directed cycle of length at least six. In a quadratic-minimal three-cover with a 3|5 pair, these exchanges all remain in the same pairwise-repartition component and preserve its minimum potential.

## Body

If e belongs to D(F), then F-e is Hamiltonian. Its complementary four-set in W is T+e; the no-4|4 hypothesis forces T+e non-Hamiltonian. Thus for E={x,y} subset D(F), both T+x and T+y are non-Hamiltonian. The controlled two-bad-four-extension lemma in localextend01 makes T+x+y Hamiltonian with both path endpoints in T. The remaining triple Tprime=F-E has a Hamilton ordering by the boundary axiom, so this is a legal 3|5 to 5|3 pairwise repartition. Distinct E give distinct Tprime; every such pair works. A Hamilton five-set has at least two Hamiltonian deletions, hence the outdegree is binom(|D(F)|,2)>=1. Write Fprime=T+E for the new five-side. Deleting x leaves T+y non-Hamiltonian, and deleting y leaves T+x non-Hamiltonian. Therefore D(Fprime) is a subset of T: the transferred pair is excluded from the next forward choice. This proves more than a mere prohibition on immediately undoing the move. For the walk, write A=T_0, B=T_1, C=W-(A union B), with sizes 3,3,2. The first transfer pair is C. The second transfer pair E_1 is a two-subset of A, by the exclusion just proved. Write A-E_1={a}. Then T_2={a} union C, so |T_0 intersect T_2|=1. The third transfer pair E_2 is a two-subset of B; write B-E_2={b}. Then T_3=E_1 union {b}, so |T_0 intersect T_3|=2. Loops are impossible because adjacent triples are disjoint. A directed two-cycle contradicts exclusion of the first transfer pair from the next deletion set. A directed three-cycle would require three pairwise disjoint triples inside eight vertices. In a directed four-cycle, T_0 and T_3 are adjacent and disjoint, contrary to their three-step intersection of order two. In a directed five-cycle, T_0 and T_2 have intersection of order one by their forward two-step walk, but the remaining three-step walk from T_2 to T_0 forces that same intersection to have order two. These are contradictions. The graph is finite (at most binom(8,3) vertices), and every vertex has an outgoing exchange, so repeatedly choosing one eventually yields a directed cycle. Its length is at least six. Finally, a quadratic-minimal cover with a 3|5 pair cannot admit a 4|4 repartition on its eight-vertex union, since that would lower potential by two. Every exchange above preserves the size contribution 3^2+5^2, hence remains at the same minimum within the original pairwise-repartition component. This strengthens the endpoint-based neutral rotation in astra003transfer35_recomp01 to all pairs of Hamiltonian deletion labels and supplies a precise restriction on forward transport. It does not exclude cycles of length six or greater, prove a balanced eight-cover, or close the grand theorem. The next consumer must couple a forced longer cycle and its controlled Hamilton path endpoints to the third cover path or another descent mechanism.
