# Every neutral three-five six-cycle has a Hamiltonian hub-pair four-core

## Statement

Assume the setting of five_three_sixcycle_normalform01 on an eight-vertex set W with no Hamiltonian 4|4 partition. Let a,d be the two hubs and A_0,A_1,A_2 the three pair-blocks. Then at least one of the three four-sets K_i={a,d} union A_i is Hamiltonian. Consequently, for such an index i the complementary four-set W-K_i=A_{i-1} union A_{i+1} is non-Hamiltonian. Thus the length-six equality shell always contains a Hamiltonian 4-side on the two hubs and one whole pair-block, facing a non-Hamiltonian four-vertex residue on the other two pair-blocks.

## Body

Suppose for contradiction that every K_i={a,d} union A_i is non-Hamiltonian. By five_three_sixcycle_crosshub_fiveshell01, for every i and every x in A_{i-1}, the five-set G_{i,x}=K_i union {x} has a Hamilton path whose two endpoints lie in A_i union {x}. Since K_i=G_{i,x}-{x} is non-Hamiltonian, x cannot be an endpoint of such a Hamilton path: deleting an endpoint from a tight five-path leaves a tight four-path on the remaining four vertices. Therefore the two endpoints are exactly the two vertices of A_i. Deleting either one shows that {a,d,u,x} is Hamiltonian for every u in A_i and x in A_{i-1}. As i runs modulo three, this proves that {a,d,y,z} is Hamiltonian for every pair y,z drawn from different pair-blocks.\n\nPut Y=A_0 union A_1 union A_2, so |Y|=6. There are 12 cross-block pairs {y,z} in Y. For each such pair, {a,d,y,z} is Hamiltonian. Because W has no Hamiltonian 4|4 partition, its complementary four-set Y-{y,z} must be non-Hamiltonian. Complementation is a bijection between two-subsets and four-subsets of Y, so at least 12 of the 15 four-subsets of Y are non-Hamiltonian. Hence Y has at most three Hamiltonian four-subsets.\n\nOn the other hand, the four-of-six theorem in smallset01 gives at least four Hamiltonian five-subsets of Y. Every Hamiltonian five-set contains at least two Hamiltonian four-subsets: delete either endpoint from any Hamilton tight path on that five-set. Count incidences (F,Z) where F is a Hamiltonian five-subset of Y and Z is a Hamiltonian four-subset of F. The four Hamiltonian five-subsets contribute at least eight incidences. But each four-subset Z of Y lies in exactly two five-subsets of Y, so if Y had at most three Hamiltonian four-subsets there could be at most six such incidences. Contradiction. Therefore some K_i is Hamiltonian. Its complementary four-set inside W cannot also be Hamiltonian, by the standing no-4|4 hypothesis.
