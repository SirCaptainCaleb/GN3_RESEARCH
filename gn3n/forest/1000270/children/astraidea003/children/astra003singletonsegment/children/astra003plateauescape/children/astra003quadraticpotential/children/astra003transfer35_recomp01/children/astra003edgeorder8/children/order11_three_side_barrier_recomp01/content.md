# Balanced-eight structure collapses trapped three-sides to the universal order-eleven barrier

## Statement

The following statements hold. (A) Every boundary tournament on eight vertices has a Hamiltonian 4|4 partition. (B) Let H be a minimum counterexample and let A|B|C be a spanning three-cover minimizing quadratic potential in a trapped pairwise-repartition component, with |A|>=|B|>=|C|=3. Then |V(H)|=11 and the component-order multiset is {4,4,3}; in particular no trapped quadratic-minimal three-cover of order at least twelve has a three-vertex side. (C) If H is a hypothetical order-eleven minimum counterexample, then for every three-set X subseteq V(H), H-X has a Hamiltonian 4|4 partition P|Q, the spanning three-cover X|P|Q lies in the unique connected component containing all spanning three-covers, and for every Hamiltonian 4|4 partition P|Q of H-X both seven-sets X union P and X union Q are non-Hamiltonian. Thus the balanced-eight theorem, the global three-side reduction, and the universal order-eleven two-sided no-merge barrier form one argument.

## Body

Replacing the displayed 5|3 pair by a Hamiltonian 4|4 partition changes the pair contribution to the quadratic potential from 25+9=34 to 16+16=32, again a strict contradiction. Hence a<=4. Since a>=b>=3, also b<=4, so |V(H)|=a+b+3<=11. Minimum-counterexample calculus gives |V(H)|>10. Therefore |V(H)|=11 and necessarily a=b=4. This proves (B).\n\nFor (C), let H be a hypothetical order-eleven minimum counterexample and fix any three-set X. Every three-vertex boundary tournament is Hamiltonian. The complement H-X has order eight, so by (A) it has a Hamiltonian 4|4 partition P|Q. Hence X|P|Q is a spanning three-cover.
