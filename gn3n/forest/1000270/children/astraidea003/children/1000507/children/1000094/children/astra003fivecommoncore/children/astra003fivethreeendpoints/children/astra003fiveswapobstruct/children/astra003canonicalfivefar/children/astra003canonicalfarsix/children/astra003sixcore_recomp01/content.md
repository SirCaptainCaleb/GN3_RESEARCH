# Synchronized far-end six-sets force order disturbance and a good common four-core

## Statement

In the synchronized non-Hamiltonian six-set U=D union {a,b} arising after a successful far-end exchange, let G={g in U:H[U-{g}] is Hamiltonian} and G_D=G intersect D. Then |G|>=4 with a,b in G; Hamilton deletion paths on the good deletions exhibit relative-order disagreement on some common pair; for each c in {a,b} there exists e in G-{c} with U-{c,e} Hamiltonian; |G_D|>=2; and if |G_D|>=3 then there exist distinct d,e in G_D with U-{d,e} Hamiltonian.

## Body

Because U is non-Hamiltonian while U-a and U-b are Hamiltonian, four-of-six gives |G|>=4. The certified bad-six-set order-disagreement theorem forces two Hamilton deletion paths to disagree in relative order, and the prescribed-overlap theorem gives, for each far endpoint c, a good partner e with U-{c,e} Hamiltonian. Restricting to D gives |G_D|>=2. If all four core labels are good, bipartiteness of the fixed-pair bad-extension graph yields a nonedge and hence a Hamiltonian common four-core. If exactly three core labels are good, failure of such a pair would force the remaining core vertex to form a full bad-extension star to the three good labels, placing all three good labels in one orientation class relative to a,b. The three-parallel-middle theorem would then make U minus the remaining core vertex Hamiltonian, contradicting that this vertex is not a good deletion. Thus whenever |G_D|>=3, two good core labels have a Hamiltonian simultaneous-deletion four-core.
