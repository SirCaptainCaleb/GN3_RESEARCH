# One long component already forces a full-path insertion obstruction

## Statement

Let X|P|Q be a quadratic-potential-minimal spanning three-path cover in a connected pairwise-repartition component containing no two-cover, with |X|=5 and P=(p_1,...,p_m), m>=7. Then there is x in V(X) such that, writing D=V(X)-{x}, both D union {p_1} and D union {p_m} are Hamiltonian. Either one endpoint e in {p_1,p_m} gives an equal-potential support exchange, meaning H[(V(P)-{e}) union {x}] is Hamiltonian, or x is noninsertable at every position of the displayed path order P. In the latter case insert01 gives a bounded local obstruction on the entire path P. Moreover, if H[V(P) union {x}] is Hamiltonian, every Hamilton order on that support has relative-order disagreement with P, so pathcalc01 Section 4 yields a reversed common edge, a reversing tight triple, or a vertex-simple tight cycle.

## Body

By astra003fivecommoncore, choose x in V(X) so that D=V(X)-{x} has the property that D union {p_1} and D union {p_m} are Hamiltonian. If for some endpoint e in {p_1,p_m} the set (V(P)-{e}) union {x} is Hamiltonian, then a Hamilton path on D union {e} together with a Hamilton path on (V(P)-{e}) union {x} is an exact two-path repartition of X union P with component orders 5 and m, hence an equal-Phi support exchange. Assume neither endpoint gives such an exchange. Then both endpoint-truncated enlargements (V(P)-{p_1}) union {x} and (V(P)-{p_m}) union {x} are non-Hamiltonian. Suppose x could be inserted into the displayed order P. If inserted before p_1, deleting p_m from the resulting tight path yields a Hamilton path on (V(P)-{p_m}) union {x}; if inserted after p_m, deleting p_1 yields a Hamilton path on (V(P)-{p_1}) union {x}; if inserted internally, deleting either displayed endpoint yields one of those two Hamilton paths. All cases contradict the corresponding non-Hamiltonicity. Thus every insertion position fails, so insert01 Section 3 supplies a bounded local obstruction involving x and at most four consecutive vertices of P. Finally, if H[V(P) union {x}] is Hamiltonian and R is any Hamilton path on that support, then the P-vertices cannot occur in R in the same relative order as in P, because otherwise R would be obtained by inserting x into the displayed order P. Hence R and P have relative-order disagreement on their common vertices, and pathcalc01 Section 4 gives the stated reversed-edge, reversing-triple, or tight-cycle conclusion.
