# A successful far-end exchange forces a non-Hamiltonian synchronized six-set

## Statement

In the setting of astra003canonicalfivefar case (iii), write D=V(C)-{z}, a=p_0, and b=q_s, so D union {a} and D union {b} are Hamiltonian. Suppose the equal-Phi exchange of z with a is legal, and let the resulting five-side be D union {a}. Then U=D union {a,b} is non-Hamiltonian. Moreover at least two vertices d in D satisfy U-{d} Hamiltonian. For each such d, either (V(B)-{b}) union {d} is Hamiltonian, giving a second equal-Phi exchange between the new five-side and B, or d is noninsertable into the inherited path B-b and hence has a bounded failed-insertion obstruction there. The symmetric statement holds when the first successful exchange is at b.

## Body

Assume z can be exchanged with a. Thus (V(A)-{a}) union {z} is Hamiltonian, while D union {a} is Hamiltonian, so one legal pairwise repartition replaces A|C by A'|C', where C' has support D union {a}, |C'|=5, and A' has order |A|. This first move preserves Phi. Suppose U=D union {a,b} were Hamiltonian. Since b is an endpoint of the displayed outer path B, the inherited truncation B-b is a tight path of order |B|-1. Repartitioning C'|B as U | (B-b) is therefore legal. Its Phi contribution is 6^2+(|B|-1)^2 instead of 5^2+|B|^2, a strict decrease of 2|B|-12>0 because |B|>=7. The two moves would reach a cover of smaller Phi in the same connected component, contradicting Phi-minimality. Hence U is non-Hamiltonian. Now apply the four-of-six theorem to U. Deleting a leaves D union {b}, Hamiltonian, and deleting b leaves D union {a}, Hamiltonian. Since at least four vertex deletions of a six-set are Hamiltonian, at least two vertices d in D also satisfy U-{d} Hamiltonian. Fix such d. In the post-first-exchange state, the pair C'|B has union (D union {a}) union V(B). The five-set U-{d}=(D-{d}) union {a,b} is Hamiltonian. If (V(B)-{b}) union {d} is Hamiltonian, these two Hamilton paths partition the pair union and have component orders 5 and |B|, so they give a second equal-Phi support exchange. Otherwise d cannot be inserted anywhere into the inherited tight-path order B-b, since any successful insertion would Hamiltonize that support. The failed-insertion theorem supplies a bounded local obstruction involving d and at most four consecutive vertices of B-b. The argument with a and b interchanged is identical.
