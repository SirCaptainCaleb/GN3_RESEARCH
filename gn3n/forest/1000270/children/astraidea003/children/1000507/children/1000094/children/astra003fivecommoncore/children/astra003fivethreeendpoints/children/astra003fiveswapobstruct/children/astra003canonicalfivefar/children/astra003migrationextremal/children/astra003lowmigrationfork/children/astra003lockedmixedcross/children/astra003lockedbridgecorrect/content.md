# Correction: absence of a direct long-side crossing forces a rigid two-edge x-bridge

## Statement

Correcting astra003lockedmixedcross: the earlier claim that every exact two-cover must contain a direct P-Q edge was too strong, because x can bridge a P-block to a Q-block. The valid statement is as follows. In branch (B) of astra003lowmigrationfork, assume no relative-order disagreement from a Hamiltonian P union {x} or Q union {x}, so both enlarged supports are non-Hamiltonian. Fix an endpoint e of P and put P'=P-e and K_e=P' union Q union {x}=H-(A union {e}). Then every exact two-cover T of K_e either contains a direct ordinary edge joining P' to Q, or has exactly two cross-class edges relative to the partition P'|Q|{x}; both are incident with x, one joins x to a P'-block and one joins x to a Q-block, and exactly one of P',Q is split into two path blocks. Equivalently, after cutting the two x-edges, T consists of four monochromatic blocks, with a mixed component B_P-x-B_Q and one residual block from whichever long side is split. The symmetric statement holds for endpoints of Q.

## Body

The setup before the erroneous last step of astra003lockedmixedcross remains valid: A union {e} is Hamiltonian, so K_e is non-Hamiltonian with pc(K_e)=2, and P' union {x} and Q union {x} are both non-Hamiltonian in the no-order-disagreement branch. Let T be an exact two-cover of K_e and partition its vertices into the three nonempty classes P', Q, {x}. Suppose T has no direct P'-Q ordinary edge. Then every cross-class edge is incident with x, so the crossing count t is at most two because x has ordinary path-forest degree at most two. The transition-count identity gives t>=3-2=1 because each of P',Q,{x} is Hamiltonian and T has two components. If t=1, cutting the unique crossing gives exactly three monochromatic blocks. Since each of the three classes is nonempty, there is exactly one block in each class. Thus x is joined to exactly one of the two long-side blocks and the other long side is the second component, so one component has support P' union {x} or Q union {x}. Either support would have to be Hamiltonian, contradicting the standing non-Hamiltonicity. Hence t=2. Cutting both crossings gives four monochromatic blocks. The singleton class {x} contributes one block, so the P'- and Q-block counts sum to three; one long side is a single block and the other is split into exactly two blocks. Both crossing edges are incident with x. They cannot both join x to blocks of the same long side: if both joined P'-blocks, those two blocks together with x would form one path component spanning P' union {x}, contradicting its non-Hamiltonicity, and similarly for Q. Therefore one x-edge goes to a P'-block and the other to a Q-block. They lie in the same path component through x, giving the mixed block pattern B_P-x-B_Q; the fourth monochromatic block is the residual block of the split long side. This proves the corrected dichotomy.
