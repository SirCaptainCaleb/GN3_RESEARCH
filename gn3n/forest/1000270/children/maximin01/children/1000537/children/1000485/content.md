# An extremal two-end barrier endpoint immediately yields a Hamiltonian four/five-window or a cross triple

## Statement

Let H-y=R|Q be the endpoint-barrier normal form of a6d3c9f247b1, where Q=(q_0,...,q_s), |Q|>=3, and z is an endpoint of R satisfying (q_1,q_0,z) and (z,q_s,q_{s-1}) tight. Then either: (1) (q_0,z,q_s) is tight, in which case if |Q|>=4 the five distinct vertices q_1,q_0,z,q_s,q_{s-1} form the tight Hamilton path (q_1,q_0,z,q_s,q_{s-1}); if |Q|=3, the four distinct vertices q_1,q_0,z,q_s form the tight Hamilton path (q_1,q_0,z,q_s). In either subcase this proper Hamiltonian support has non-Hamiltonian complement of path-cover number two. Or: (2) (q_0,z,q_s) is non-tight, so boundary antisymmetry forces the tight cross triple (q_s,z,q_0). Thus the endpoint-barrier branch of the global minimum-side normalization always enters the standard bounded Hamiltonian-window / cross-triple frontier.

## Body

There is only one join triple between the two known barriers. If (q_0,z,q_s) is tight and |Q|>=4, the three consecutive triples of (q_1,q_0,z,q_s,q_{s-1}) are respectively (q_1,q_0,z), (q_0,z,q_s), and (z,q_s,q_{s-1}), all tight. When |Q|=3, q_1=q_{s-1}, so use the four-path (q_1,q_0,z,q_s), whose two triples are the first barrier and the tested middle triple. The support is proper because R has order at least three and only z is used from R; minimum-counterexample complement calculus therefore gives a non-Hamiltonian complement of path-cover number two.

If (q_0,z,q_s) is non-tight, its reversal (q_s,z,q_0) is tight by boundary antisymmetry, giving the explicit cross triple. These alternatives exhaust the central triple.
