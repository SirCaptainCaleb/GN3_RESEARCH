# Deletion covers can be chosen with bounded inherited fragmentation

## Statement

There is an absolute integer B such that the following holds. If H is a smallest-order counterexample to the grand theorem, F is a spanning three-cover of H, and v is any vertex, then H-v has a two-cover obtained by partitioning the displayed paths of F-v into at most B nonempty contiguous intervals and concatenating those intervals in inherited orientation.

## Body

Conjecture, unproved. Arbitrary deletion covers currently produce uncontrolled crossings and order changes. This asks for the existence of a better chosen cover, not a restriction on every cover. A positive result would replace an unbounded ordering problem by a bounded number of attachment sites. First attack: minimize the number of inherited intervals among deletion covers and show that a long alternating sequence of intervals admits a splice reducing that number. No path reversal is permitted, and every new join must be checked.
