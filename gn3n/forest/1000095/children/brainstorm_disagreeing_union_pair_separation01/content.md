# A reversed common pair can be separated on the union of two long paths

## Statement

Let P,Q be tight paths in a finite boundary tournament H, each of order at least five and with at least five common vertices. Let u,v be distinct common vertices, with u preceding v on P and v preceding u on Q. Then the induced boundary tournament on V(P) union V(Q) admits a two-cover consisting of two paths that place u and v in different paths.

## Body

Conjecture; no proof is asserted. This is a prescribed-separation assertion for the particular union of two supplied paths, retaining their displayed orders and the particular reversed pair. It does not assume that arbitrary order disagreement in a larger ambient tournament already yields a spanning two-cover of that ambient tournament.

First approach: cut each supplied path between u and v and compare the four inherited path pieces. Seek a partition of their union into two tight paths separating u,v, accounting explicitly for all repeated vertices and checking every splice triple in its stated order. Begin with the common vertices immediately surrounding the first order disagreement; their relative positions may impose more than an abstract reversal witness. A local separation theorem would be useful only when the ambient route can also control the complement, so that complement obligation remains distinct.
