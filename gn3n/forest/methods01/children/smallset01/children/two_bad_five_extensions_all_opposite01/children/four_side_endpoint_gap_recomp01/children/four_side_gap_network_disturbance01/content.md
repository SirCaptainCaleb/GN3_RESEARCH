# The four-label gap network forces a positioned junction reversal or a proper tight cycle

## Statement

Assume outcome (3) of four_side_endpoint_lock_gap_network01 beside the Hamiltonian four-path X=(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3) and interior path M. Then the order of the four distinct obstruction gaps cannot agree with the displayed order x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3. Consequently there are i<j with g(x_j)<g(x_i). Let C be the gap-network tight connector from x_j to x_i through M, and let A=(x_i,...,x_j) be the displayed X-subpath from x_i to x_j. The certified path-intersection calculus applied to A and C forces either: (1) a tight triple on V(A) union V(C) that reverses a displayed ordered edge of A or C at their interaction; or (2) a proper vertex-simple tight cycle on V(A) union V(C). Thus the hard gap-network residue always yields a positioned reversal/cycle disturbance supported across the four-side and the path interior.

## Body

First exclude monotone gap order. Suppose g_0<g_1<g_2<g_3 in the X-order. Since g_0<g_1, we have g_1>=2. The second-type prefix for x_1 contains the tight triple (b_{g_1-1},b_{g_1},x_1). If (b_{g_1},x_1,x_2) were tight, then (b_{g_1-1},b_{g_1},x_1,x_2) would be a mixed Hamiltonian four-path, contradicting that we are in the no-mixed-support gap-network outcome. Hence boundary antisymmetry gives (x_2,x_1,b_{g_1}) tight. Similarly g_2<g_3 implies g_2<=|M|-2, so the second-type suffix for x_2 contains (x_2,b_{g_2+1},b_{g_2+2}). If (x_1,x_2,b_{g_2+1}) were tight, then (x_1,x_2,b_{g_2+1},b_{g_2+2}) would be a mixed Hamiltonian four-path; therefore (b_{g_2+1},x_2,x_1) is tight. These two tight triples concatenate to the mixed Hamiltonian four-path (b_{g_2+1},x_2,x_1,b_{g_1}), again a contradiction. Thus the gap order is not the X-order. Choose an inversion i<j with g(x_j)<g(x_i). By four_side_endpoint_lock_gap_network01, the pair x_j,x_i is joined in gap order by a tight connector C through M: an adjacent-gap cross triple when the gaps are consecutive, or the displayed interval connector when separated. Its internal vertices lie in M. The displayed subpath A=(x_i,...,x_j) lies in X, so A and C are internally vertex-disjoint and share exactly x_i,x_j, in opposite relative order. Apply Section 4 of pathcalc01. Its reversed-common-edge alternative cannot occur, because C has at least one internal M-vertex, so x_i,x_j are not an ordered edge of C. Hence pathcalc01 yields either a reversing tight triple at the intersection or a vertex-simple tight cycle. The cycle is proper because its support lies in X union M and omits both displayed endpoints of P (and the third cover component).
