# Two central-gap leaves force four specified Hamiltonian deletions at every order

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4,a,b be distinct vertices such that (k_1,k_2,a,k_3,k_4) and (k_1,k_2,b,k_3,k_4) are tight Hamilton paths. Put U={k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4,a,b}. Then the four specifically identified deletions U-a, U-b, U-k_1, and U-k_4 are Hamiltonian. Therefore, without any ambient-order assumption, either U is non-Hamiltonian and two Hamiltonian five-deletions exhibit order disagreement, or U is Hamiltonian and H-U is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two. If |V(H)|>=18, the Hamiltonian branch further gives order disagreement or strict quadratic descent. Thus the central-gap geometry supplies a positioned four-good-six package at every order, not merely the generic large-order disturbance.

## Body

The deletions U-a and U-b are Hamiltonian by the two given paths. Boundary antisymmetry gives exactly one of (a,k_2,b),(b,k_2,a), and concatenating the tight choice with k_3,k_4 Hamiltonizes U-k_1. Likewise exactly one of (a,k_3,b),(b,k_3,a) is tight, which Hamiltonizes U-k_4 by appending/prepending the inherited k_1,k_2 segment. Hence four named deletions are Hamiltonian.

The all-order structural conclusion follows from four_good_six_interface01. If U is Hamiltonian, H-U is pc2 non-Hamiltonian; if U is non-Hamiltonian, localized order disagreement occurs. Only after this geometry has been retained do we invoke ham6_large_escape01 for n>=18 to obtain the former large-order descent-or-disagreement corollary.
