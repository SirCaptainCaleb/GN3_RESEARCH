# Two disjoint four-sets force twenty-four mixed Hamiltonian five-sets

## Statement

Let H be any boundary tournament and let A,B be disjoint four-vertex sets. For every two-set T subset A, at least two vertices b in B make (A-T) union (B-{b}) Hamiltonian. Symmetrically, for every two-set U subset B, at least two vertices a in A make (B-U) union (A-{a}) Hamiltonian. Consequently A union B contains at least twelve Hamiltonian five-sets meeting A in two vertices and B in three, and at least twelve meeting A in three and B in two. Moreover some fixed b in B is omitted in Hamiltonian sets corresponding to at least three of the six two-sets T, and symmetrically some fixed a in A has the analogous property.

## Body

Fix a two-set T subset A and put S=A-T. Then |S|=2 and S is disjoint from B. Apply twofourhamdeletions01 to S and B. For at least two vertices b in B, the five-set S union (B-{b})=(A-T) union (B-{b}) is Hamiltonian. This proves the first assertion, and interchanging A and B proves the second.

There are six choices of T and at least two successful b for each, so there are at least twelve successful incidences (T,b). Distinct incidences give distinct five-sets because the intersections with A and B recover T and b. Thus there are at least twelve Hamiltonian five-sets of type 2A+3B. The symmetric argument gives at least twelve of type 3A+2B; the two types are disjoint, giving at least twenty-four mixed Hamiltonian five-sets in total.

Finally, averaging at least twelve successful incidences over the four labels b in B shows that some fixed b is successful for at least three of the six two-sets T. The symmetric statement follows identically.
