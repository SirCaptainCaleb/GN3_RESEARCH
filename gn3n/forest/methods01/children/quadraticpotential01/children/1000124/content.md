# Every two-step component-size gap at a quadratic minimum exposes the universal transport menu

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let C=X|P|Q be a spanning three-cover minimizing the quadratic potential Phi within its connected pairwise-repartition component. Let X=(x_1,...,x_k) and P=(p_0,...,p_{m-1}) be displayed tight paths with k>=2 and m>=k+2. Then the two endpoints p_0,p_{m-1} are both noninsertable into the displayed order of X. Consequently at least one of the following occurs: (1) H contains a proper Hamiltonian induced set of order four or five whose complement is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two; (2) for some x_i in X, one of (p_0,x_i,p_{m-1}) or (p_{m-1},x_i,p_0) is tight; (3) after possibly interchanging the two endpoints, there is a nonempty displayed interval I of X such that (p_0,I,p_{m-1}) is a tight path. Thus every displayed pair with order gap at least two enters the standard bounded-window/cross-triple/interval-connector interface without any component-size-specific analysis.

## Body

Suppose H[V(X) union {p_0}] were Hamiltonian. Repartition the pair X|P by replacing X with a Hamilton path on V(X) union {p_0} and P with its inherited endpoint truncation (p_1,...,p_{m-1}). The changed component orders are k,m -> k+1,m-1, so the change in Phi is (k+1)^2+(m-1)^2-k^2-m^2 = 2(k-m+1), which is at most -2 because m>=k+2. This contradicts local Phi-minimality. Hence H[V(X) union {p_0}] is non-Hamiltonian, and in particular p_0 is noninsertable into the displayed order of X. The same argument applies to p_{m-1}. Apply the certified paired-noninsertion theorem a8c9883902b1 to the displayed tight path X and the two exterior vertices p_0,p_{m-1}. Its Hamiltonian four-set and Hamiltonian five-set outcomes give (1). In its exceptional cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-set outcome, the other endpoint of P is exterior to that four-set, and the same theorem states that adjoining any exterior vertex Hamiltonizes it; hence this outcome also gives a Hamiltonian five-set and therefore (1). Its cross-triple outcome is exactly (2), and its direct interval-connector outcome is exactly (3). Every Hamiltonian set in (1) has order at most five and is proper because a minimum counterexample has order greater than ten. Its complement cannot be Hamiltonian, since otherwise the two complementary Hamilton paths would two-cover H; minimum-counterexample calculus gives path-cover number two.
