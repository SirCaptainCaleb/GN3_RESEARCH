# Equal-cover endpoint probes reduce to inherited-path destruction, order disagreement, or radius-two support migration

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample with an all-equal spanning three-cover A|B|C, A=(a_1,...,a_r). For arbitrary exact deletion covers at a_1 and a_r, at least one of the following occurs: (1) one deletion cover splits the surviving A-vertices into more than one monochromatic A-block or reorders them; (2) the two endpoint deletion-cover orders have relative-order disagreement on common vertices; (3) there exist distinct exterior vertices u,v such that (u,a_2,...,a_{r-1},v) is a tight path, giving a same-order Hamiltonian support A'=(A-{a_1,a_r}) union {u,v}. Thus crossing multiplicity need not be classified unless one wants finer information about outcome (1).

## Body

If either endpoint deletion cover fails to retain the surviving A-vertices as one contiguous inherited-order block, outcome (1) holds. Otherwise both endpoint covers satisfy the hypotheses of the intact opposite-end transport lemma. That lemma gives outcome (2) or (3). The argument uses no bound on crossing multiplicity and therefore subsumes the need to normalize two-crossing versus three-or-more-crossing endpoint probes merely to obtain a scale-free disturbance.
