# A four-side either descends in two moves or all four labels lock both complementary cores

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament and let X|P|Q be a spanning three-cover, where |X|=4, P=(p_1,...,p_m) has m>=6, and |Q|=q. Put M=(p_2,...,p_{m-1}). Assume 2m-q>7. Then either the pairwise-repartition component containing X|P|Q contains a spanning three-cover of strictly smaller quadratic potential, reachable in at most two legal pairwise repartitions, or all of the following hold simultaneously for every t in X: (i) F_t=(X-{t}) union {p_1,p_m} is Hamiltonian; (ii) M union {t} is non-Hamiltonian; (iii) Q union {t} is non-Hamiltonian. In the second alternative every t is noninsertable into the displayed paths M and Q, necessarily q>=3, and every t supplies a tight triple reversing a displayed edge of each path. Moreover, for every three-element subset Z of X, applying the three-noninsertables theorem to Z against M and against Q yields, on each path separately, at least one of: a Hamiltonian or cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-vertex failed-insertion window, a same-gap Hamiltonian four-set using two labels of Z, or a tight interval connector joining two labels of Z across separated obstruction gaps. Thus the no-descent branch carries a complete four-label double lock together with a finite bounded/interval obstruction menu on every label triple on both complementary cores. For a canonical double-wrap three-cover from a deletion state of order n>=19, choose P to be the larger complementary path. Then 2m-q>7 automatically, so the same dichotomy applies. At n=18 the only case in which the two-move inequality can fail to be strict is m=q=7, where the exchange is neutral.

## Body

First suppose X union {p_1} or X union {p_m} is Hamiltonian. Moving that endpoint from P into X and retaining the inherited endpoint truncation of P is one legal pairwise repartition with pair sizes (4,m)->(5,m-1), and new quadratic potential minus old equals 10-2m<0. Hence assume both endpoint five-extensions are non-Hamiltonian. By two_bad_five_extensions_all_opposite01, for every t in X the five-set F_t=(X-{t}) union {p_1,p_m} is Hamiltonian.

Fix t in X. If M union {t} is Hamiltonian, then F_t | (M union {t}) is a two-path repartition of X union P with sizes (5,m-1), again giving the strict one-move change 10-2m<0. Thus absence of a lower-Phi state forces M+t non-Hamiltonian for every t.

Now suppose Q union {t} is Hamiltonian for some t. Every three-vertex boundary tournament is Hamiltonian, so X-{t} is Hamiltonian. Repartition X|Q first as (X-{t}) | (Q union {t}), leaving P unchanged. Then repartition (X-{t})|P as F_t | M, leaving Q+t unchanged. Both moves are legal and preserve their selected pair-unions. The original component-order contribution is 16+m^2+q^2 and the final contribution is 25+(m-2)^2+(q+1)^2, so final minus original equals 14-4m+2q. The hypothesis 2m-q>7 is exactly 14-4m+2q<0. Hence absence of a lower-Phi state forces Q+t non-Hamiltonian for every t.

Since M and Q are displayed Hamiltonian paths, non-Hamiltonicity of M+t and Q+t implies t is noninsertable into every position of those displayed orders. The path M has order m-2>=4. Also Q+t non-Hamiltonian is impossible when q<=2 because every induced subtournament of order at most three is Hamiltonian, so q>=3. Apply noninsertable_family_reversal01 to the four labels X against M and against Q to obtain the complete reversal family.

Now fix any three-element subset Z of X. All three labels are noninsertable into M, so three_noninsertables_finite_menu01 applies and yields on M one of its three certified outcomes: a first-type four-vertex window that is Hamiltonian or the cyclic non-Hamiltonian K4 with universal one-vertex extension; a same-gap Hamiltonian K4 using two labels of Z; or a tight connector through the interval between two separated obstruction gaps. The same theorem applies independently to the same Z against Q. Since Z was arbitrary, all four three-label subfamilies of X carry this finite menu on both cores.

For the double-wrap corollary, the two complementary paths have orders m>=q and m+q=n-4. If n>=19, then m+q>=15. If m+q is odd, m>=q gives 2m-q>=9; if it is even, n>=20 and 2m-q>=8. Thus 2m-q>7. For n=18, m+q=14; equality 2m-q=7 occurs exactly at m=q=7, while every imbalanced profile still gives strict inequality.
