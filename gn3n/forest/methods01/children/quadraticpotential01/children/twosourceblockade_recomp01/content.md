# Global quadratic minima obey an exact two-source endpoint blockade and a near-balance-or-square dichotomy

## Statement

Let X|P|Q be a globally Phi-minimal spanning three-cover with c=|X|, p=|P|, q=|Q|. If endpoint blocks S of P and T of Q, of total orders s,t with s+t>0 and nonempty inherited remainders, are absorbed into X and the enlarged support is Hamiltonian, then s(p-c-s)+t(q-c-t)-st<=0. In particular, if p,q>=2 and p+q>2c+3, every cross-endpoint enlargement X+{e,f} is non-Hamiltonian and, in a minimum counterexample, has path-cover number exactly two. Therefore for p>=q>=c either (p-c)+(q-c)<=3, or the four displayed cross-endpoint pairs form an exact-pc2 square over the common core X.

## Body

If W=V(X) union S union T were Hamiltonian, then W together with the inherited paths P-S and Q-T would be a spanning three-cover with component orders c+s+t,p-s,q-t. Phi-minimality gives the inequality s(p-c-s)+t(q-c-t)-st<=0 after expansion. Taking s=t=1 shows that whenever p+q>2c+3, every endpoint pair e of P and f of Q gives a non-Hamiltonian support X+{e,f}. In a minimum counterexample this support is proper and therefore has path-cover number at most two; non-Hamiltonicity makes it exactly two. If the threshold fails, p+q<=2c+3, so (p-c)+(q-c)<=3. Thus every global minimum is either uniformly near-balanced in total excess or carries the four-cell exact-pc2 endpoint square.
