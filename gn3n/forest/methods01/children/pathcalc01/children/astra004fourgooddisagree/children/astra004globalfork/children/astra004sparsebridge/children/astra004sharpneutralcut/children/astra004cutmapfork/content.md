# The neutral sparse cut map repeats or produces a two-ended longest-support exchange

## Statement

Keep the sharp-shell setup A=(a_0,...,a_{lambda-1}), U=V(H)-V(A), and D={u in U:U-u is Hamiltonian}, and assume |D|<=2. Suppose that for every bad label u in U-D one can choose an exact cover of H-u having exactly two A|(U-u) crossings and completely neutral A-order, so astra004sharpneutralcut assigns it a cut k(u) in {1,...,lambda-1}. Then either two distinct bad labels have the same cut, or |D|=2 and k is a bijection U-D -> {1,...,lambda-1}. In the bijective case let u_L,u_R be the labels with cuts 1 and lambda-1. There exist w_L in U-{u_L} and w_R in U-{u_R} such that U-{u_L,w_L} and U-{u_R,w_R} are Hamiltonian and the displayed longest paths (w_L,a_1,...,a_{lambda-1}) and (a_0,...,a_{lambda-2},w_R) are tight. If w_L=w_R, insert01 forces explicit relative-order disagreement among these endpoint replacements and A. Otherwise w_L!=w_R and (w_L,a_1,...,a_{lambda-2},w_R) is a tight path of order lambda. Consequently a fully order-neutral deletion-sparse equality shell has only two global outcomes: a repeated cut shared by two bad deletion labels, or a genuine two-ended exchange to a new globally longest support.

## Body

# Proof\n\nThere are |U-D|=lambda+1-|D| bad labels and only lambda-1 possible cut values. If |D|<=1, pigeonhole already forces a repeated cut. If |D|=2, either a cut repeats or the lambda-1 bad labels use all lambda-1 cuts exactly once. Assume the latter.\n\nWe first record the block orientation implicit in astra004sharpneutralcut. In its neutral branch every component has order lambda and every common A-vertex a_i occurs at absolute position i. Hence the component containing the prefix A[0,k-1] must have that prefix as its first block; if the prefix came second then a_0 would occur at a positive position. Likewise the component containing the suffix A[k,lambda-1] must have that suffix as its second block; if it came first then a_k would occur before position k. Thus the normalized cover has forms\nA[0,k-1] R  |  S A[k,lambda-1],\nwhere R,S are the two C=U-u blocks of orders lambda-k and k.\n\nFor u_L with k=1, S has order one; write S=(w_L). Then the second component is exactly\n(w_L,a_1,...,a_{lambda-1}),\nwhich is tight, while R is a Hamilton path on U-{u_L,w_L}.\n\nFor u_R with k=lambda-1, R has order one; write R=(w_R). Then the first component is exactly\n(a_0,...,a_{lambda-2},w_R),\nwhile S is a Hamilton path on U-{u_R,w_R}. This proves the asserted two Hamiltonian double deletions and the two endpoint replacements.\n\nIf w_L=w_R=w, apply Section 1 of insert01 to X=V(A), Q=A, and exterior vertex y=w. The set A union {w} has order lambda+1 and is non-Hamiltonian by maximality of lambda, while the two displayed replacement paths are Hamiltonian on (A-{a_0}) union {w} and (A-{a_{lambda-1}}) union {w}. Its first consecutive triple is inherited from the left replacement path, its last consecutive triple is inherited from the right replacement path, and all intervening triples are inherited from A. Hence B is a tight path of order lambda, giving the genuine two-ended longest-support exchange. ∎
