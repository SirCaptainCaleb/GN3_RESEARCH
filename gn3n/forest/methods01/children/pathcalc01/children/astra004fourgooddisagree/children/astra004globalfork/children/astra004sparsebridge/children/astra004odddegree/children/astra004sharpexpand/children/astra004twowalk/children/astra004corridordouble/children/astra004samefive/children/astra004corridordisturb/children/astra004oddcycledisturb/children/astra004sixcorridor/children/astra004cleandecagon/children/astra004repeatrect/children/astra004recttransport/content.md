# A missing sixth corner transports the singleton rectangle to the far core endpoint

## Statement

Work in the clean repeated-label configuration of astra004repeatrect, and write V(H)=M disjoint-union N disjoint-union {a,b,c}, where the five displayed Hamiltonian lambda-supports are M+a, M+b, M+c, N+b, N+c. Assume the support component is 2-regular, every incident length-two walk is clean, and N+a is non-Hamiltonian. Let d be the endpoint of the chosen Hamilton order on N opposite the common attachment end of b and c. Then the second neighbor of M+b is (N-{d}) union {a,c}, the second neighbor of M+c is (N-{d}) union {a,b}, and both corresponding odd edges are labelled d. Hence, with N_prime=(N-{d}) union {a}, the same four supports M+b,M+c,N_prime+b,N_prime+c form a reciprocal singleton rectangle for the deletion H-d. Moreover N_prime inherits a Hamilton order, b and c attach to it at the same end as before, and the missing corner N_prime+d is exactly the original non-Hamiltonian support N+a. Thus failure of the sixth corner deterministically transports the omitted rectangle label from a to the far endpoint d while preserving the missing lambda-support.

## Body

# Proof\n\nFrom astra004repeatrect, the clean swaps give Hamilton orders on M+a,M+b,M+c in which a,b,c all replace one another at one fixed end of the same ordered core M. Likewise N+b and N+c use one fixed ordered core N, with b,c attached at one common end. Let d be the opposite endpoint of N.\n\nBecause the odd component is 2-regular, M+b has a second neighbor R distinct from N+c. Let x be the label of the edge R--(M+b). Since an odd neighbor of M+b is obtained by deleting one vertex from its (lambda+1)-vertex complement N union {a,c}, we have R=(N union {a,c})-{x}. The label x is not a, because deletion of a gives the already existing neighbor N+c. It is not c, because deletion of c gives N+a, which is non-Hamiltonian by hypothesis. Hence x lies in N.\n\nThe length-two walk R--(M+b)--(N+c) has consecutive labels x,a and is clean. Its clean swap says that x is an endpoint of the chosen Hamilton order on N+c and that replacing x by a at the same end gives the chosen order on R. The endpoints of N+c are c at the common attachment end and d at the opposite end. Since x lies in N, x cannot be c; therefore x=d. Consequently R=(N-{d}) union {a,c}.\n\nApply the same argument at M+c. Its known neighbor is N+b via label a. Its other neighbor R_prime has edge label y in N, because label a gives the known neighbor and label b would give the forbidden support N+a. The clean walk N+b--(M+c)--R_prime forces y to be the endpoint of N+b opposite the attached b, namely the same d. Thus R_prime=(N-{d}) union {a,b}.\n\nPut N_prime=(N-{d}) union {a}. In the clean walk R--(M+b)--(N+c), the chosen order on R is obtained from the chosen order on N+c by replacing the far endpoint d with a. Since c is the other endpoint, deleting c from R leaves a tight Hamilton order on N_prime. The symmetric walk through M+c yields the same inherited order on N_prime, with b replacing c only at the common attachment end. Therefore R=N_prime+c and R_prime=N_prime+b with b,c attached at the same end of N_prime.\n\nThe two edges labelled d give exact covers H-d=(N_prime+c)|(M+b) and H-d=(M+c)|(N_prime+b). Their four support intersections are {c},N_prime,M,{b}; the large blocks have the same inherited orders and each cover crosses the other partition exactly twice, at the singleton attachments. Thus they form a reciprocal singleton rectangle for H-d. Finally N_prime union {d}=N union {a} as vertex sets, so its absent sixth corner is exactly the original non-Hamiltonian support N+a. ∎
