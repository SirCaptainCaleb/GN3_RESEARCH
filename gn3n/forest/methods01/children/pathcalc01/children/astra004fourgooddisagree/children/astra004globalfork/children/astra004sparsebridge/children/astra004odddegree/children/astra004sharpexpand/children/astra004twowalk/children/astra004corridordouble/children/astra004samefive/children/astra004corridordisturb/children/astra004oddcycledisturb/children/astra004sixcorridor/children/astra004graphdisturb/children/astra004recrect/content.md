# Reciprocal two-crossing covers normalize to an alternating four-block rectangle

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample in the sharp half-order shell |V(H)|=2lambda+1. Fix x and let A|B and C|D be two distinct exact lambda|lambda covers of H-x. Assume each cover has exactly two ordinary edges crossing the support partition of the other, and assume that on every nonempty intersection of one support from {A,B} with one support from {C,D}, the two inherited block orders have no relative-order disagreement. Then the four intersections X=A∩C, Y=A∩D, Z=B∩C, W=B∩D are all nonempty and occur as single contiguous blocks in each of the four displayed paths. Moreover, at every intersection block of order at least two, the two incident maximum paths cannot be oriented through the block: both incident joins point out of the block or both point into it. Consequently, if all four blocks have order at least two, after swapping names and reversing the abstract source/sink labels (not path orders) the four maximum paths have the alternating rectangle form X Y, X Z, W Y, W Z, where each juxtaposition denotes the actual tight concatenation with the same inherited block order. The block sizes satisfy |X|=|W|=r and |Y|=|Z|=lambda-r for some 2<=r<=lambda-2.

By the no-order-disagreement hypothesis, each block carries one common inherited Hamilton order wherever it occurs.

Consider one block, say X, with |X|>=2. It lies in exactly two of the four maximum paths, namely A and C. Since |X|>=2, concatenate the incoming path YX and outgoing path XZ along their common X-block.
