# A linear 101 defect window slides or exposes an inherited-complement four-path

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament with path-cover number greater than two, and let pi=(v_1,...,v_n) be a spanning ordering whose only defect centers are i and i+2. Thus (v_{i-1},v_i,v_{i+1}) and (v_{i+1},v_{i+2},v_{i+3}) are non-tight while (v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}) is tight. Then i>=3 and i+4<=n. Moreover: (L) after swapping v_i and v_{i+1}, either the new ordering has exactly the two defect centers i-1 and i+1, hence again has linear pattern 101 shifted one position left, or W_L=(v_{i+3},v_{i+2},v_i,v_{i+1}) is a tight four-path; in the latter case H-W_L has the explicit two-path cover (v_1,...,v_{i-1}) | (v_{i+4},...,v_n). Symmetrically, (R) after swapping v_{i+1} and v_{i+2}, either the new ordering has exactly the two defect centers i+1 and i+3, hence the 101 window shifts one position right, or W_R=(v_{i+1},v_{i+2},v_i,v_{i-1}) is a tight four-path with explicit complementary two-cover (v_1,...,v_{i-2}) | (v_{i+3},...,v_n).

## Body

Write a=v_{i-1}, b=v_i, c=v_{i+1}, d=v_{i+2}, e=v_{i+3}. Because the defect pattern is 101, (a,b,c) and (c,d,e) are non-tight while (b,c,d) is tight. Boundary antisymmetry therefore gives (a,c,b) tight and (e,d,c) tight; cyclic symmetry of the tight middle triple gives (d,b,c) and (c,d,b) tight.\n\nFor the left swap, replace the local word a,b,c,d,e by a,c,b,d,e. The affected centers are i-1,i,i+1,i+2. Center i is tight because (a,c,b) is tight, while center i+1 is defective because (c,b,d) is the reverse orientation of the tight cyclic triple (d,b,c). All centers outside this affected interval remain exactly as before and hence are tight. Since pc(H)>2, the swapped ordering cannot have all of its defects contained in the adjacent pair {i+1,i+2}; otherwise it would have defect span at most two and therefore yield a two-path cover. Thus center i-1 must exist and be defective, so i>=3 and, writing g=v_{i-2}, (g,a,c) is non-tight. Boundary antisymmetry gives (c,a,g) tight. If center i+2 is tight, i.e. (b,d,e) is tight, the swapped ordering has exactly defects i-1 and i+1, so the 101 window has shifted left. If instead (b,d,e) is non-tight, antisymmetry gives (e,d,b) tight, and together with the already tight (d,b,c) this makes (e,d,b,c)=W_L a tight four-path. Deleting these four vertices leaves exactly the two inherited tight paths (v_1,...,v_{i-1}) and (v_{i+4},...,v_n); the second must be nonempty, for if i+3=n then the swapped ordering would have no possible defect disjoint from center i+1 on the right once center i-1 is fixed? More directly, apply the symmetric argument below to force i+4<=n.\n\nFor the right swap, replace a,b,c,d,e by a,b,d,c,e. Center i+1 is defective because (b,d,c) reverses the tight cyclic triple (c,d,b), while center i+2 is tight because (d,c,e) is the reverse orientation forced by the original defect (c,d,e). All other remote centers are unchanged. Again pc(H)>2 forces a second defect disjoint from center i+1; the only possible affected position is i+3. Hence i+4<=n and, writing f=v_{i+4}, (c,e,f) is non-tight, so (f,e,c) is tight. If center i is tight, i.e. (a,b,d) is tight, the right-swapped ordering has exactly defects i+1 and i+3, a right-shifted 101 pattern. Otherwise antisymmetry gives (d,b,a) tight; together with the tight cyclic rotation (c,d,b), this makes W_R=(c,d,b,a) a tight four-path. Its complement is exactly the inherited prefix (v_1,...,v_{i-2}) and suffix (v_{i+3},...,v_n).
