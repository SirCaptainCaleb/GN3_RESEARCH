# The half-order longest-path shell has an exact odd-graph exchange model

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample of order n and let lambda be the maximum order of a tight path. Then lambda >= ceil((n-1)/2). Every globally longest path has a non-Hamiltonian exact-two-coverable complement, and every exterior vertex simultaneously gives the two reversed endpoint barriers. If equality 2 lambda=n-1 holds, then n=2 lambda+1, every exact two-cover of every one-vertex deletion has orders lambda,lambda, and the D=1 reconfiguration graph is exactly the disjointness graph on Hamiltonian lambda-subsets: two states are adjacent precisely when their Hamiltonian supports are disjoint, with the unique omitted vertex as edge label. Every vertex of H labels at least one edge.

## Body

Let lambda be the maximum tight-path order. For any vertex x, minimum-counterexample calculus gives an exact two-cover H-x=P|Q. Since P and Q are tight paths, |P|,|Q|<=lambda, so n-1=|P|+|Q|<=2lambda. Hence lambda>=ceil((n-1)/2).

Now let A=(a_0,...,a_{lambda-1}) be any globally longest tight path and put U=V(H)-V(A). The complement principle gives pc(H[U])=2 and U is non-Hamiltonian. For each y in U, neither (y,a_0,...,a_{lambda-1}) nor (a_0,...,a_{lambda-1},y) can be tight, by maximality of lambda. Boundary antisymmetry therefore gives (a_1,a_0,y) and (y,a_{lambda-1},a_{lambda-2}) tight. Thus the whole complementary pc-two residue is synchronized against both ends of A.

Assume now 2lambda=n-1. Then n=2lambda+1. In every exact two-cover H-x=P|Q, the two component orders sum to 2lambda and each is at most lambda, so |P|=|Q|=lambda. Thus every deletion-cover component is globally longest. Also no (lambda+1)-set is Hamiltonian, by definition of lambda.

Define G to have as vertices the Hamiltonian lambda-subsets of V(H), joining two vertices A,B when A and B are disjoint. Since |V(H)|=2lambda+1, every edge AB has a unique label ell(AB), the vertex outside A union B. Conversely every edge AB gives an exact deletion two-cover of H-ell(AB): A|B covers that deletion, and H-ell(AB) cannot be Hamiltonian because a Hamilton path there together with the singleton ell(AB) would two-cover H. For every x, some exact cover of H-x exists and has orders lambda,lambda, so every x occurs as the label of at least one edge. Hence G is the Hamiltonian-support induced subgraph of the odd graph KG(2lambda+1,lambda), with a surjective edge-label map onto V(H).

Finally identify D=1 states. If S is a Hamiltonian lambda-set having at least one neighbor in G, then R=V(H)-S has order lambda+1 and contains a Hamiltonian lambda-subset (any neighbor of S), so d(R)=1; R itself is non-Hamiltonian because its order exceeds lambda. Thus R|S is a D=1 state. Conversely every D=1 state R|S has, by the one-defect/deletion-cover equivalence, a vertex x in R such that (R-{x})|S is an exact deletion two-cover. The equality shell makes both supports lambda-sets, so S and R-{x} are adjacent vertices of G. Therefore D=1 states are exactly the nonisolated vertices S of G, interpreted as Hamiltonian side S with deficient complement V(H)-S.

Let R|S be such a state and let v in R. Transferring v is legal exactly when R-{v} is Hamiltonian. But R-{v} is a lambda-set disjoint from S, so this is equivalent to the edge S--(R-{v}) of G, whose unique label is v. After the transfer, R-{v} is the new Hamiltonian side and S union {v} is the deficient complement. Hence legal D=1 transfers are exactly traversals of edges of G, in both directions, and the transferred vertex is exactly the edge label. This gives the claimed exact exchange model.
