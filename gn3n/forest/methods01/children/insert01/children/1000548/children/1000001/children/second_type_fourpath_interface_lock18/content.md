# Two second-type endpoint obstructions force reversed interfaces back into the four-path

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample of order at least eighteen. Let S=(u,s_1,s_2,v) be a tight four-path and let H-S=R|T, where R=(r_0,...,r_m) has order at least six and M=(r_1,...,r_{m-1}). Suppose u and v are noninsertable into M, and their failed-insertion normal forms are both second-type: u at the gap r_i|r_{i+1} and v at the gap r_j|r_{j+1}, with 1<=i,j<=m-2. If neither explicit order disagreement nor strict quadratic-potential descent at a generated Hamiltonian-support state occurs, then (s_1,u,r_i) and (r_{j+1},v,s_2) are tight. In addition, the second-type obstruction data give the reverse-through-gap triples (r_{i+1},u,r_i) and (r_{j+1},v,r_j), together with their corresponding tight prefix and suffix hooks on M.

## Body

The second-type normal form gives the usual prefix/suffix hooks for u at r_i|r_{i+1} and v at r_j|r_{j+1}, in particular the tight triples (r_{i+1},u,r_i) and (r_{j+1},v,r_j). Consider the interface between u and the initial part of S. If (r_i,u,s_1) were tight, then (r_i,u,s_1,s_2,v) would be a Hamiltonian five-path, using the two displayed tight triples of S. This five-set is proper; minimum-counterexample calculus gives its complement path-cover number two, and ham5_large_escape01 yields explicit order disagreement or strict quadratic-potential descent at the associated five-side state. In the stated unresolved branch this is excluded. Hence (r_i,u,s_1) is non-tight, and boundary antisymmetry gives (s_1,u,r_i) tight. Symmetrically, if (s_2,v,r_{j+1}) were tight, then (u,s_1,s_2,v,r_{j+1}) would be a Hamiltonian five-path and would be consumed in the same way. Therefore (s_2,v,r_{j+1}) is non-tight, so (r_{j+1},v,s_2) is tight. The conclusion is independent of whether the two second-type gaps coincide, are adjacent, or are separated; it therefore precedes the spacing trichotomy and remains available in both the interval-connector and opposite-cross residues.
