# An unresolved opposite endpoint cross carries two four-side interface reversals and both adjacent pivot hooks

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample of order at least eighteen. Let S=(u,s_1,s_2,v) be a tight four-path, let H-S=R|T with R=(r_0,...,r_m) of order at least six, and put M=(r_1,...,r_{m-1}). Suppose u and v are noninsertable into M and the paired-noninsertion reduction reaches its opposite-cross residue, while neither explicit order disagreement nor strict quadratic-potential descent at any generated Hamiltonian-support state occurs. Then for some 2<=k<=m-2 the following five tight triples hold: (v,r_k,u), (r_k,v,s_2), (s_1,u,r_k), (r_k,v,r_{k-1}), and (r_{k+1},u,r_k). Moreover the second-type obstruction hooks give tight paths (r_1,...,r_{k-1},v), (v,r_k,...,r_{m-1}), (r_1,...,r_k,u), and (u,r_{k+1},...,r_{m-1}).

## Body

The alternative-2 normal form gives the tight prefix/suffix paths (r_1,...,r_{k-1},v), (v,r_k,...,r_{m-1}), (r_1,...,r_k,u), and (u,r_{k+1},...,r_{m-1}), and hence the reverse-through-gap triples (r_k,v,r_{k-1}) and (r_{k+1},u,r_k). It remains to determine the two interfaces with the displayed four-path S. If (s_2,v,r_k) were tight, then (u,s_1,s_2,v,r_k) would be a Hamiltonian five-path. If (r_k,u,s_1) were tight, then (r_k,u,s_1,s_2,v) would be a Hamiltonian five-path.
