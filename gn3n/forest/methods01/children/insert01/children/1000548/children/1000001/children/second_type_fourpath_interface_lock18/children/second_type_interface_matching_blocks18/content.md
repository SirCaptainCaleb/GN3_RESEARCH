# Unresolved second-type endpoint interfaces form opposite-extremal matching-block four-sets

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample of order at least eighteen in the unresolved branch of second_type_fourpath_interface_lock18. Thus S=(u,s_1,s_2,v) is a tight four-path, H-S=R|T with R=(r_0,...,r_m), and u,v have second-type insertion obstructions on M=(r_1,...,r_{m-1}) at gaps r_i|r_{i+1} and r_j|r_{j+1}, while neither explicit order disagreement nor strict quadratic-potential descent at a generated Hamiltonian-support state occurs. Put A={s_1,u,r_i,r_{i+1}} and B={s_2,v,r_j,r_{j+1}}. Then A and B are non-Hamiltonian edge-orderable matching-block four-sets. In A, the opposite-edge matching {u r_i, s_1 r_{i+1}} is the highest matching block. In B, the opposite-edge matching {v r_{j+1}, s_2 r_j} is the lowest matching block. Consequently the following additional tight triples hold: (u,s_1,r_{i+1}), (u,r_{i+1},s_1), (r_i,s_1,r_{i+1}), (r_i,r_{i+1},s_1), (s_2,r_j,v), (r_j,s_2,v), (s_2,r_j,r_{j+1}), and (r_j,s_2,r_{j+1}).

## Body

By second_type_fourpath_interface_lock18, the u-side supplies the tight triples (s_1,u,r_i) and (r_{i+1},u,r_i), while the v-side supplies (r_{j+1},v,s_2) and (r_{j+1},v,r_j). If A were Hamiltonian, then A is a proper Hamiltonian four-set in a minimum counterexample, so H-A is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two. Applying the certified four-side escape theorem 7bab8dd31d87 gives either explicit order disagreement, strict quadratic-potential descent at the associated four-side state, or a Hamiltonian six-set with path-cover-two complement; ham6_large_escape01 consumes the six-set for order at least eighteen into order disagreement or strict descent. All are excluded by the unresolved-branch hypothesis. Thus A is non-Hamiltonian. The same argument applies to B. By smallset01, each non-Hamiltonian four-set arising here is edge-orderable with its three opposite-edge perfect matchings arranged as strict blocks.

For A, let M_0={u r_i, s_1 r_{i+1}}, M_1={u s_1, r_i r_{i+1}}, M_2={u r_{i+1}, r_i s_1}. Tightness of (s_1,u,r_i) gives u s_1 < u r_i, so M_1<M_0. Tightness of (r_{i+1},u,r_i) gives u r_{i+1}<u r_i, so M_2<M_0. Hence M_0 is the highest block. Therefore u s_1<s_1 r_{i+1}, u r_{i+1}<r_{i+1}s_1, r_i s_1<s_1 r_{i+1}, and r_i r_{i+1}<r_{i+1}s_1, yielding the four displayed u-side tight triples.

For B, let N_0={v r_{j+1}, s_2 r_j}, N_1={v s_2, r_j r_{j+1}}, N_2={v r_j, s_2 r_{j+1}}. Tightness of (r_{j+1},v,s_2) gives v r_{j+1}<v s_2, so N_0<N_1. Tightness of (r_{j+1},v,r_j) gives v r_{j+1}<v r_j, so N_0<N_2. Hence N_0 is the lowest block. Therefore s_2 r_j<r_j v, r_j s_2<s_2 v, s_2 r_j<r_j r_{j+1}, and r_j s_2<s_2 r_{j+1}, yielding the four displayed v-side tight triples. Thus the genuinely unresolved second-type residue is not an arbitrary connector/cross configuration but a pair of oppositely extremal matching-block interfaces.
