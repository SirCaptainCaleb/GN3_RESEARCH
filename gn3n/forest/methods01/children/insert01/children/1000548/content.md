# Two noninsertable exterior vertices on one path have a universal structural alternative

## Statement

Let H be a boundary tournament, let B=(b_1,...,b_m) be a tight path with m>=2, and let x,y be distinct vertices outside V(B). Suppose x and y are both noninsertable into the displayed order of B. Then at least one of the following holds: (1) a four-vertex set consisting of one of x,y and three consecutive vertices of B is Hamiltonian; (2) such a four-vertex set is the exceptional cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-vertex configuration, and adjoining any exterior vertex makes it Hamiltonian; (3) a five-set consisting of x,y and three consecutive vertices of B is Hamiltonian; (4) for some b in V(B), one of (x,b,y),(y,b,x) is tight; (5) after possibly interchanging x,y, there are indices i<j with j>=i+2 such that (x,b_{i+1},...,b_j,y) is a tight path. Thus paired noninsertability on an arbitrarily long path always reduces to a fixed finite menu of local Hamiltonian/cyclic/cross-triple outcomes or one direct interval connector.

## Body

Apply the failed-insertion normal form in insert01 separately to x and y. If at least one obstruction is alternative 1, say for z in {x,y} on three consecutive vertices a,b,c of B, then (z,b,a), (a,b,c), and (c,b,z) are tight. If H[{a,b,c,z}] is Hamiltonian, outcome (1) holds. Otherwise the same four-vertex classification used in the first-type insertion analysis applies: the three displayed tight triples together with boundary antisymmetry and non-Hamiltonicity force the exceptional cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-vertex configuration. By smallset01, adjoining any exterior vertex to that configuration is Hamiltonian, giving outcome (2). It remains that both x and y have alternative-2 obstructions. Apply 36fccff06d48 to their obstruction gaps. Equal gaps give a Hamiltonian four-set, separated gaps give the tight path (x,b_{i+1},...,b_j,y) after possibly interchanging x,y, and adjacent gaps give either a Hamiltonian five-set or a tight cross triple. These are outcomes (1), (5), (3), and (4), respectively. No bound on m is used beyond m>=2.
