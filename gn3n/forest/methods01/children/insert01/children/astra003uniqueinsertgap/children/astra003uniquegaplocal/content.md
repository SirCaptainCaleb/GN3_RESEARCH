# Unique equal-or-adjacent insertion gaps have an explicit local normal form

## Statement

Let B=(b_1,...,b_m) be a tight path and x,y exterior vertices, each with a unique successful insertion position, with the two positions equal or adjacent. If the positions are adjacent, say x inserts immediately before b_j and y immediately after b_j (including the endpoint-adjacent cases), then either x and y insert simultaneously in the order ...x,b_j,y..., or the reverse cross triple (y,b_j,x) is tight. If the two unique positions are the same internal gap between consecutive vertices u,v, then both (u,x,v) and (u,y,v) are tight, so the four-set {u,v,x,y} is Hamiltonian by the parallel-middle lemma. Thus the fully neutral two-label residue is reduced locally to either an explicit reverse cross triple at one old path vertex or a Hamiltonian four-set supported on one old path edge and the two continuation labels. Same-gap endpoint positions are the only degenerate case not covered by the four-set conclusion.

## Body

For adjacent positions, write the local displayed order as ... , with x inserted in the gap immediately before b_j and y inserted in the gap immediately after b_j. By individual insertability, every consecutive triple of the simultaneous candidate order obtained by making both insertions is already known tight except possibly the cross triple (x,b_j,y). Hence if that triple is tight, simultaneous insertion succeeds. If simultaneous insertion does not succeed, (x,b_j,y) is non-tight, so boundary antisymmetry gives the reverse triple (y,b_j,x) tight. This argument also covers adjacency involving the first or last insertion position, because the only new triple coupling the two insertions is still the displayed cross triple. Now suppose the common unique position is an internal gap u|v. Individual insertability gives (u,x,v) and (u,y,v) tight. The two-parallel-middle lemma in localextend01 therefore yields a Hamilton tight path on {u,v,x,y}. No claim is made for a common endpoint insertion position, where there is only a common left-extension or right-extension pattern and the parallel-middle hypothesis is unavailable.
