# A vertex noninsertable into two disjoint paths gives a Hamiltonian four/five-set or a doubled cross barrier

## Statement

Let H be a minimum counterexample and let X|P|Q be a spanning three-cover, where P,Q are vertex-disjoint tight paths and x is a vertex of X that is noninsertable into every position of both displayed orders P and Q. Then at least one of the following holds: (1) H contains a proper Hamiltonian induced set W of order four or five such that H-W is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two; (2) there are vertices p in V(P), q in V(Q) such that both (p,q,x) and (q,p,x) are tight. More precisely, outcome (2) occurs only when chosen failed-insertion normal forms for x on both P and Q are of second type; otherwise outcome (1) occurs.

## Body

Choose a failed-insertion normal form for x on each of P and Q. If at least one chosen obstruction is first-type, apply 0425e03e2aa3. Its four-vertex obstruction set W is either Hamiltonian or the exceptional cyclic non-Hamiltonian four-set. In the Hamiltonian case W is proper because a minimum counterexample has order greater than ten, and mincex01 gives that H-W is non-Hamiltonian with path-cover number two. In the cyclic case, choose any vertex d outside W; such a vertex exists since |V(H)|>10. The fifth-vertex extension theorem in smallset01 makes W union {d} Hamiltonian. This five-set is proper, and mincex01 again gives a non-Hamiltonian path-cover-two complement. Thus outcome (1) holds whenever either obstruction is first-type. It remains that both chosen obstructions are second-type. Apply 4efbe05b945a to the two disjoint paths P,Q and the common exterior label x. Either it gives a tight Hamiltonian four-path meeting both paths and x, whose support is a proper Hamiltonian four-set and hence yields outcome (1) by mincex01, or it gives the doubled cross barrier: for one pivot-side vertex p of P and one pivot-side vertex q of Q, both (p,q,x) and (q,p,x) are tight. This is outcome (2). No path reversal or cyclic permutation is used.
