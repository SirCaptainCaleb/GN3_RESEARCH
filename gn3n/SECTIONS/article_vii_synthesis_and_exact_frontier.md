# Synthesis and the exact topological frontier

**Summary:** Nonzero exact-root carriers reduce to four central vertices and directed two- or three-cycles. Projected omission balance forces zero chambers except on two exceptional facets; a matching-block example shows those exceptions are real. The grand conjecture remains open.

## Statement

Article VII retains its exact formulations. Nonzero exact-root carriers have the bounded central configurations of Section 7. For the rooted omission map, strictly positive constant-vector balance forces every chamber to have zero omissions unless all original vertices occupy a single block and the auxiliary vertex is a singleton extreme block. The matching-block tournament on four vertices gives positive balance without a zero chamber on either exceptional facet, refuting the unrestricted facewise omission-balance implication. Converting root configurations or forcing useful omission balance remains open.

## Composition

## Synthesis: minimum holes and the remaining frontier

The two routes now meet on the same structure.

First, every genuine reflected double is a two-element minimum deletion set. The Boolean-cube theorem therefore supplies its distance-one faces, four-end synchronization, and absolute nonaugmentability automatically. These are not separate terminal lemmas.

Second, every positively balanced exact-root carrier with
\[
\kappa_2(H)\ge2
\]
contains a zero-root chamber. Hence the high-deletion-distance problem is already on a symmetric canonical partial cover
\[
P\mid X\mid Q,\qquad |P|=|Q|.
\]

Minimum holes then descend sharply. Every three restored hole labels give one-step root-advance certificates from both boundaries. If \(|X|\ge3\), a suitable pair \(x,y\in X\) yields
\[
G=H-(X\setminus\{x,y\}),\qquad \kappa_2(G)=2,
\]
together with a spanning three-cover containing a Hamiltonian five-component. In the symmetric case its profile is
\[
5\mid(s-1)\mid s
\]
up to left-right symmetry. Independently, grouping hole labels by their two endpoint-orientation bits gives many pairs for which \(G\) has Hamiltonian four-support three-covers rooted at both opposite boundaries.

No recursive root-advance covering theorem is used: one-step root advance is retained only as a bounded local certificate.

The grand theorem is therefore not proved here. The remaining combinatorial interface is a genuine two-deletion state with synchronized blocked endpoints, often carrying a distinguished five-component or bidirectional four-support cores. The remaining topological interface is a protected four-label carrier loop. Closing either interface must produce a two-cover or a protected outward carrier; neither conclusion is asserted without the missing handoff.

This is the current Article VII frontier.
