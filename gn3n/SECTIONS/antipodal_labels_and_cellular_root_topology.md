# From antipodal labels to cellular root topology

**Summary:** The topology must use cells and extreme-switch roots, not merely antipodal labels on the permutation graph.

## Statement

Naive Tucker and rook-label arguments fail on the permutahedron graph, but their failure identifies the correct cellular objects: commuting squares, braid hexagons, and an odd extreme-switch root map.

## Cold composition

## Cellular antipodal roots

The chamber space of spanning orders is the Coxeter sphere obtained from the boundary of the permutahedron. Any chamber label that changes sign under reversal extends, by face averages on the barycentric subdivision, to a continuous odd map. A zero has a unique minimal carrier face \(F\), and on that face the origin is a strictly positive convex combination of the chamber labels.

This construction is used twice below: first for root-balance statements, and then for the exact inversion root. Its output is deliberately facewise. We use neither a graph-only complementary-edge argument nor a bounded-rank quotient of the chamber graph.
