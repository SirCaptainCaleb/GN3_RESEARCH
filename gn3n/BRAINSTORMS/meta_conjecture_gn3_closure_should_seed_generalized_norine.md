# Meta-conjecture: GN3 closure should seed generalized Norine

The boundary-tournament problem and Norine-type antipodal cube-geodesic problems share the same global Freudenthal/Coxeter geometry but differ in their local data.

For a boundary tournament, the status along a monotone cube geodesic is determined by a base-independent two-memory rule
[
h(u,v,w)in{0,1},qquad h(w,v,u)=1-h(u,v,w).
]
A more general bounded-memory cube coloring may depend on the current base set:
[
g(S;u,v,w)in{0,1},
]
with the corresponding antipodal reversal-complement condition. Ordinary Norine edge colorings are lower-memory members of this broader cube-geodesic world.

## Meta-conjecture

Whatever collection of arguments ultimately closes the boundary-tournament two-cover conjecture should contain a substantial subset that is not essentially tied to base-independence. That portable subset should be strong enough to seed, after suitable reformulation, a proof of a generalized Norine geodesic conjecture for antipodal bounded-memory colorings.

The conjecture is intentionally about proof structure rather than a presently fixed generalized theorem statement. The expected transferable ingredients include some combination of:

- Freudenthal/Coxeter chamber geometry;
- antipodal reversal-complement symmetry;
- Tucker/Borsuk-Ulam/Bourgin-Yang style topology;
- local forbidden-pattern or witness-tree compression;
- carrier-face recurrence and neutral-corridor arguments;
- bounded-memory locality under adjacent chamber moves;
- recursive terminalization of balanced carriers.

The ingredients most likely to require replacement are the GN3-specific steps that use translation invariance of the triple rule, especially arbitrary reordering inside a face block, endpoint-swap antisymmetry at a fixed direction triple, and blockwise splicing that preserves local statuses because the base set is irrelevant.

## Research program

Once the boundary-tournament conjecture is closed, decompose the proof into:

1. **pure chamber-topological arguments**, depending only on antipodality and bounded local memory;
2. **memory-local combinatorial arguments**, likely portable to base-dependent rules;
3. **GN3-specific translation-invariant arguments**, which must be generalized or replaced.

Then formulate the weakest abstract memory axioms under which the portable core survives. The target should include ordinary Norine edge colorings as a lower-memory special case.

A successful outcome would reverse the original heuristic direction: rather than merely borrowing the Norine analogy to understand GN3, the stronger two-memory GN3 proof would reveal the mechanism needed to prove a broader Norine-type geodesic theorem.
