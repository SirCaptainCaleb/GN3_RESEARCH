# Terminalization of a balanced local-witness carrier

The local-witness topology gives a balanced face with paired orientations of its innermost witness edge, but splicing one pair only produces an outer-labeled chamber; it does not remove the inner edge from the carrier. Prove a recursive terminalization theorem before treating the bounded terminal cases as a full grand-theorem reduction.

Let F be a balanced carrier for the fixed-path local-witness map and let e be the innermost edge occurring among its chamber labels. Positive balance gives both +e and -e. If their determining windows can be separated by a face boundary, blockwise splicing can produce a chamber with no e-witness, but this alone does not imply that F can be replaced by a balanced carrier avoiding e. The missing theorem is: either force a two-cover/bounded interface directly, or construct a new balanced carrier using only chambers whose labels lie strictly outward of e. A promising formulation treats the chambers with no e-label as a neutral corridor separating the +e and -e regions in the chamber complex of F. Restrict the remaining outer-edge coordinates to that separator and seek an antipodal/cohomological-index argument showing that it still carries a zero. Iterating would terminate because the witness tree is finite. This is the precise global gap between the current balanced-face theorem and the 12-vertex terminal interface theorem.
