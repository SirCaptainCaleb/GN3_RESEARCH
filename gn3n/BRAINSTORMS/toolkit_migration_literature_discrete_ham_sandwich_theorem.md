## Simplified statement

Any d finite point sets in d-dimensional Euclidean space have one hyperplane that simultaneously halves all d sets.

## Statement

Let P_1,...,P_d be finite point sets in R^d. There exists an affine hyperplane H that simultaneously bisects every P_i: each of the two open halfspaces bounded by H contains at most floor(|P_i|/2) points of P_i. In general position, the usual sharpenings control how many points lie on H.

## Body

Literature theorem. Standard discrete form of the ham sandwich theorem. For finite P_1,...,P_d subset R^d, there is a hyperplane H such that each open side contains at most floor(|P_i|/2) points of P_i for every i. This is commonly derived from the continuous ham sandwich/Borsuk-Ulam theorem by perturbation or thickening. Potential GN3 use: simultaneous balancing of several finite witness/root families, especially when a balanced carrier must satisfy several independent counting constraints at once. Keep distinct from Bourgin-Yang: ham sandwich gives simultaneous bisection/existence, not a dimension lower bound for the zero set. Literature references checked: standard combinatorial-geometry lecture formulations; see also the classical discrete ham-sandwich literature.
