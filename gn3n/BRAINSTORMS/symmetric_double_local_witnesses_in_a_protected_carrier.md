# Symmetric double local witnesses in a protected carrier

The local-witness path topology reduces the non-double branch to constant central width. The remaining unbounded branch is a chamber in a balanced carrier whose innermost selected forbidden witness occurs simultaneously at both reflected locations around a common protected good band.

Work globally, without a minimum-counterexample hypothesis. Let F be a zero carrier from [[local_witness_path_topology]], with innermost selected edge e as in [[local_witness_carriers_have_a_protected_central_band]]. The single-sided branch is handled by [[paired_local_witnesses_splice_or_compress_centrally]]: either the protected band expands or the witness is within constant distance of the center. The remaining branch is that a chamber contains both reflected occurrences of e. This can happen for arbitrarily large separation at the level of abstract binary words, so pure word combinatorics is insufficient. Use the fact that all chambers lie in one permutahedron face and all status bits come from one boundary-tournament triple function. Promising directions: exploit the dimension-two zero locus with additional mirrored-status coordinates; use the face block structure and [[five_protected_positions_are_impossible]] to force controlled variation of at least one reflected witness; or replace the antipodal tie-break gauge by a second-stage equivariant labeling on the double-witness locus. A successful theorem should show that a far-separated symmetric double witness either can be broken to a single-sided witness (returning to splice-or-compress) or already forces a two-cover.
