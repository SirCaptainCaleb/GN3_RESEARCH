# Coxeter curvature of prescribed tournament transversals

For a sequence of tournaments T_1,...,T_{m-1} on a common m-vertex set, orient the type-A permutahedron edge at adjacent gap i toward the pair order demanded by T_i. A sink is exactly a Hamilton path whose i-th arc belongs to T_i, i.e. a transversal Hamilton path with prescribed color order. Study the discrete 1-form and its curvature on Coxeter braid hexagons.

Let U be an m-element set and let T_1,...,T_{m-1} be tournaments on U. On the permutahedron Perm(U), an edge at generator s_i swaps the adjacent entries x,y in positions i,i+1. Orient that edge toward the endpoint in which x precedes y exactly when x -> y in T_i. Equivalently define an antisymmetric edge 1-form omega that is +1 when an edge is traversed in its preferred direction and -1 in the reverse direction. A sink of this orientation is precisely an order (u_1,...,u_m) satisfying u_i -> u_{i+1} in T_i for every i, i.e. a Hamilton path with the tournament colors used in the prescribed order.\n\nCOMMUTING SQUARES. If |i-j|>1, the swaps s_i,s_j involve disjoint adjacent positions. Performing one does not change the unordered pair seen by the other generator. Opposite edges of the Coxeter square therefore carry the same preferred direction, and the signed circulation of omega around every commuting square is zero.\n\nBRAID HEXAGONS. Consider the rank-two braid face generated by s_i,s_{i+1} on three symbols x,y,z. Write T(a,b)=+1 when a->b and -1 otherwise, and define the triangle score\n\n  C_T(x,y,z)=T(x,y)+T(y,z)-T(x,z),\n\nwhich lies in {+3,+1,-1,-3}; the values +/-3 are the two cyclic orientations and +/-1 are the transitive orientations relative to the displayed order. Traversing the hexagon\n\n  xyz -> yxz -> yzx -> zyx -> zxy -> xzy -> xyz\n\nclockwise, direct calculation gives\n\n  integral omega = C_{T_{i+1}}(x,y,z)-C_{T_i}(x,y,z).\n\nIn particular the hexagon is a fully directed cycle exactly when the restrictions of T_i and T_{i+1} to {x,y,z} are opposite directed triangles; this recovers exactly the n=3 exceptional pair in the transversal-Hamilton-path theory. More generally, any nonzero difference of triangle score is local Coxeter curvature.\n\nZERO-CURVATURE THEOREM. Suppose C_{T_i}(x,y,z)=C_{T_{i+1}}(x,y,z) for every adjacent i and every ordered triple of distinct x,y,z. Then omega has zero circulation on every 2-face of the permutahedron. Since the permutahedron is contractible (equivalently its cellular 1-cocycles are exact), there is a potential Phi on permutation vertices whose increment along every edge is omega. A vertex maximizing Phi is a sink, hence gives a prescribed-color Hamilton path. Therefore a sequence of tournaments with zero Coxeter curvature always has a transversal Hamilton path in the prescribed color order.\n\nCUT-REVERSAL CHARACTERIZATION. For two tournaments A,B, equality C_A=C_B on every ordered triple is equivalent to B being obtained from A by reversing a directed cut. Indeed let d(x,y)=B(x,y)-A(x,y) in {-2,0,2}. Equality of triangle scores gives d(x,y)+d(y,z)-d(x,z)=0. Fix r and put g(x)=d(r,x); then d(x,y)=g(y)-g(x). Because every d(x,y) lies in {-2,0,2}, the values of g occupy at most two levels separated by 2. Across the corresponding bipartition S|U-S all edges of A point uniformly from one side to the other and B reverses exactly those cut edges; within each side A and B agree. Conversely such a directed-cut reversal has zero triangle curl.\n\nHence: if each consecutive pair T_i,T_{i+1} differs by reversal of a directed cut, then there is a Hamilton path using T_i at the i-th edge. This is a genuine structural sufficient theorem, independent of minimum-counterexample analysis.\n\nGLOBAL-TO-LOCAL CONSEQUENCE. If no prescribed-color Hamilton path exists, the permutahedron orientation has no sink. A finite sinkless orientation contains a directed cycle. Pairing its positive omega-integral with a cellular 2-chain filling that cycle shows that some braid hexagon has nonzero curvature (commuting squares contribute zero). Thus failure is certified by an adjacent pair of tournament colors and a three-set on which their triangle scores differ. This local certificate is weaker than an opposite-cycle certificate but is exact at the level of the discrete 1-form.\n\nRELEVANCE TO GN3. In the odd-even interleaving model, fixing the mediator/color order on one parity produces exactly such a colored permutahedron. The second parity is what moves that color order. Thus the mutual-transversal problem can be reinterpreted as finding an ordering of mediator tournaments whose Coxeter curvature can be integrated (zero-curvature branch), or whose nonzero braid curvatures are compensated by the reciprocal parity transitions. The grand problem becomes a curvature-cancellation/aligned-breakpoint problem rather than a deletion-disturbance problem.
