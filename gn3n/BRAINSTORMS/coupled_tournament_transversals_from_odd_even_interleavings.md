# Coupled tournament transversals from odd-even interleavings

Reformulate an arbitrary boundary 3-tournament H using its local tournaments T_b on V-{b}, where a->c in T_b iff (a,b,c) is tight. Split a spanning order into odd-position and even-position subsequences. The status word is then exactly two interleaved transversal-path conditions in these local tournaments. Pursue Hamilton-transversal, temporal-tournament, and global exchange arguments directly on this coupled system, with no minimum-counterexample hypothesis.

For every b in V(H), define T_b on V-{b} by a ->_b c iff (a,b,c) is tight. Boundary 3-tournaments are exactly arbitrary self-indexed families of ordinary tournaments. Tight paths are vertex-simple paths in K_V whose consecutive edges follow the induced orientation of L(K_V).\n\nPATH/INTERLEAVING FORM. For pi=(a1,b1,a2,b2,...), epsilon_{2i-1}=1 iff a_i ->_{b_i} a_{i+1}, and epsilon_{2i}=1 iff b_i ->_{a_{i+1}} b_{i+1}. Thus the status word is two coupled tournament-transversal paths. For sufficiently large balanced parity classes, Chakraborti-Kim-Lee-Seo Theorem 1.6 implies full parity surjectivity: for any prescribed orientation word on one parity and any prescribed omitted mediator, some interleaving realizes exactly that parity word. The unresolved content is simultaneous coupling.\n\nAUXILIARY BREAKPOINT FORM. In H+ put the auxiliary vertex rho at an even mediator position b_t. Then the status immediately before rho is forced tight and the status immediately after rho is forced non-tight. A directed one-change order is exactly an interleaving for which the odd transversal stream has signs 1 for i<t, arbitrary at i=t (T_rho is free), and 0 for i>t, while the even stream has signs 1 for i<t and 0 for i>=t. Hence the grand conjecture is an aligned-breakpoint problem for two coupled oriented Hamilton transversals, with rho occupying the breakpoint mediator.\n\nCYCLIC FORM. For even n=2r, every Hamilton cycle may be written after parity-coloring its positions as an alternating cycle\n\n  a1,b1,a2,b2,...,ar,br,a1\n\nfor a balanced partition A disjoint union B. The transition signs at the B-vertices are exactly the edge orientations of a transversal Hamilton cycle on A whose colors are B: the edge a_i a_{i+1} receives color b_i and is forward precisely when (a_i,b_i,a_{i+1}) is tight. Symmetrically the A-transition signs are a transversal Hamilton cycle on B colored by A. Thus one alternating Hamilton cycle is exactly a pair of MUTUALLY COLORED transversal Hamilton cycles.\n\nThe cyclic two-cover criterion says that H has path-cover number at most two iff there is a Hamilton cycle whose non-tight transition graph on the cyclic cut positions has vertex-cover number at most two. In the alternating model, choosing two cut edges allows bad local transitions only at the four incident cycle vertices, at most two in A and two in B, with the A- and B-defects aligned through the same two cut edges. Therefore for even order the grand conjecture is equivalently a COUPLED TWO-DEFECT TRANSVERSAL-CYCLE theorem: find a balanced partition and mutually colored transversal Hamilton cycles on A and B whose wrong-way arcs are supported at endpoints of two common alternating cut edges.\n\nFor sufficiently large r, Chakraborti-Kim-Lee-Seo Theorem 1.7 says that each parity projection separately can realize every non-directed Hamilton-cycle orientation. Hence, individually, either parity can be forced to have only two (indeed arbitrarily prescribed nonconstant) defects. The difficulty is purely the mutual coupling of the two transversal cycles. This isolates a new structural target with no minimum-counterexample or deletion-cover input.\n\nSTANDARD TRANSITION-SYSTEM COMPARISON. Compatible-Hamilton-cycle theorems for bounded incompatibility systems (Krivelevich-Lee-Sudakov and successors) are not directly applicable. Here every unordered pair of incident edges has exactly one allowed traversal direction and one forbidden reverse direction; the number of forbidden successors of a fixed incoming edge can be linear and even n-2. The usable extra structure is that these directed local preferences form tournaments. Thus the relevant new theorem should exploit orientation/median/transversal structure, not sparsity of forbidden transitions.\n\nPromising research targets: (1) a mutual-transversal theorem for two self-indexed tournament systems; (2) a fixed-point/degree theorem for the correspondence sending one cyclic order/coloring to compatible transversal realizations on the opposite side; (3) a rotation-extension theorem for maximum directed compatible linear forests exploiting tournament-valued local transitions; (4) an absorption theorem modeled on transversal-tournament H-partitions but coupling vertices and colors simultaneously.
