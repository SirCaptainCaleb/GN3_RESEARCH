# Temporal Redei: one-peaked Hamilton paths through a tournament sequence

Given arbitrary tournaments S_1,...,S_{m-1} on the same m-vertex set, conjecture that there is a permutation x_1,...,x_m whose positionwise signs 1[x_i -> x_{i+1} in S_i] have form 1^*0^*. Equivalently all position-dependent tournament arrows along the Hamilton path point toward a single peak.

TEMPORAL REDEI CONJECTURE. Let U be an m-element set and S_1,...,S_{m-1} arbitrary tournaments on U. Then there exist a permutation (x_1,...,x_m) and t in {0,...,m-1} such that\n\n  x_i -> x_{i+1} in S_i for i<=t,\n  x_{i+1} -> x_i in S_i for i>t.\n\nEquivalently the binary word epsilon_i=1[x_i -> x_{i+1} in S_i] is 1^*0^*. Equivalently, if each edge x_i x_{i+1} is oriented according to the tournament assigned to its position, all arrows point toward the single peak x_{t+1}.\n\nThis is strictly weaker than prescribed-color directed traceability: a fixed t can fail, and the all-forward case can fail. The conjectured content is that allowing the breakpoint to move restores a universal Redei-type theorem. Exhaustive verification of the full tournament-sequence state space for m<=4 found no counterexample; exploratory search at m=5 also found none. These checks are only falsification attempts, not evidence used in place of a proof.\n\nPERMUTAHEDRAL FORM. For each t, orient the generator-s_i edges of Perm(U) toward the S_i-preferred adjacent pair for i<=t and in the reverse direction for i>t. A sink is exactly a temporal-Redei order with peak t. Thus the conjecture says that among the m adjacent reorientations O_0,...,O_{m-1}, at least one has a sink. Consecutive O_t differ by reversing the entire generator class s_t.\n\nFRONT DYNAMICS. In the unreoriented sign map lambda(pi), let p be the first 0 and q the last 1. If p<q, traversing the preferred generator edge s_p flips lambda_p from 0 to1. All coordinates farther than one from p are unchanged; only lambda_{p-1},lambda_{p+1} can also change. Hence the first-defect front either moves right (progress) or moves exactly one step left. Dually, flipping the last 1 either moves the right front left or exactly one step right. The obstruction is therefore a nearest-neighbor braid phenomenon, not a long-range disturbance. A backfiring front move is witnessed on one Coxeter braid hexagon. This gives a possible proof route: analyze the two defect fronts as a finite one-dimensional dynamics and show recurrent braid behavior itself contains a threshold vertex.\n\nCURVATURE CONNECTION. Fixing t gives a colored permutahedron orientation whose commuting-square curvature is zero and whose braid curvature at adjacent positions i,i+1 is C_{S_{i+1}}-C_{S_i}, with the sign reversed when a position lies on the opposite side of t. Moving t across i reverses one generator class and changes the local braid curvatures on only the neighboring rank-two residues. A discrete Poincare-Hopf or degree argument across the family O_t may therefore force a sink for some t even when no individual O_t is globally integrable.\n\nGN3 RELEVANCE. In an odd-even interleaving, once the mediator/color order on one parity is fixed, the opposite parity is exactly a temporal tournament sequence. Temporal Redei would therefore guarantee that the unfixed parity can always be ordered with a one-change word. The remaining GN3 coupling would reduce to choosing the mediator order so that the two resulting breakpoints agree (and in H+ agree with the auxiliary vertex rho). This would replace arbitrary orientation-word coupling by a one-dimensional breakpoint-matching problem.
