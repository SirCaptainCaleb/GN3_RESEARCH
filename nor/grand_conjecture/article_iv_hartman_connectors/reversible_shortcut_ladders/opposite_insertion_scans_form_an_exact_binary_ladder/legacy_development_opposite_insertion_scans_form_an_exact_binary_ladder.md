# Opposite insertion scans form an exact binary ladder — preserved pre-item development

## Development

Fix distinct exterior coordinates x,z and a good middle order O=(w_1,…,w_m). Define
X_i=α(x,w_i,w_{i+1}),
Z_i=α(z,w_i,w_{i+1}),
R_i=α(x,z,w_i).

Tetrahedral parity on {x,z,w_i,w_{i+1}} gives the exact rung law
R_{i+1}⊕R_i=X_i⊕Z_i.

Thus the two insertion scans are the rails of a binary ladder and the pair-signature R_i is the rung state. The rung flips exactly at rail disagreements. Pair insertion at a gap is controlled by the same four-face parity square, so the ladder is a genuine reversible local object rather than a purely formal encoding.

The crossed shortcut residue has opposite rail values at the two ends and fixed rung boundary data. This was the first concrete setting where Hartman/Hex language became literal.
