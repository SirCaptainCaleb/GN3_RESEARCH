# Monochromatic connector blocks and the parity-drop frontier

Article IV connector closure: In the flat split z→A→x, a spanning ternary-zero order on A∪{x,z} with forward first/last pairs closes NOR by insertion into a good B-order. The special vertices can move independently. Two fully ported shore zero paths glue through P,x,z,Q; one ported path glues as z,P,x. All shores of size at most six close. Collective block insertion has six boundary incidence conditions and one-vertex insertion has the 0011/1100 pattern; minimal obstructions have all such insertion blockers. Ordered tournament joins preserve ported two-path covers, so shores all of whose strongly connected components have size at most six also close. A source or sink module with one fully ported zero path absorbs into any compatible connector on the complement.

Deletion gluing: Four coherent fully ported two-path deletion covers produce a spanning cover. Their support graph is a forest or a single spanning odd cycle; a spanning support path closes. For arbitrary r-coordinate one-change words, 2r+2 coherent good deletion orders suffice; the witness is two adjacent opposite-color windows plus a later return-color window, supported on at most 2r+1 labels. Thus eight coherent ternary deletion orders suffice.

Three coherent ported deletion covers reconstruct at most two ordered shore classes except for one consecutive comparison-directed cyclic triple on the three deleted labels; that packet has color 1 and all other reconstructed windows are zero with forward extreme ports. In the single-class case, shore-path splits and the explicit L,u,z,v,x,w,R double-gap weave repair all exceptional collars except uniform e=f=000, i.e. q→{a,b,c}→p. The double-backward case admits no ternary-zero ordering of the seven-label packet p,{a,b,c,x,z},q with p and q fixed as endpoint boundaries, regardless of their mutual edge. Thus a closure proof must change an exterior boundary, recruit further support, or resolve the second reconstructed class. The no-go result is local and consistent with universal smaller-shore connector existence.

Hartman extraction still requires reversible full connector states, compatible endpoint targets, support-union or boundary-exclusion, and coherent selection of deletion witnesses. This section proves the stated complete closure criteria and localized obstructions; the general full-shore connector conjecture remains open.

Deletion-witness elevation (§50). A single fully ported zero Hamiltonian path on A\{a} immediately closes the entire shore by P,x,z,a; any two-path deletion cover with a singleton path closes by adjoining the omitted vertex to that singleton as a forward two-vertex path. Therefore in a minimal whole-shore obstruction, every available fully ported two-path deletion cover consists of two paths each of size at least two. This renders the single-path specializations of the cyclic three-deletion packet (§§44,46,48,49) globally redundant even though their fixed-collar repair and impossibility lemmas remain locally valid. The frontier is selection of two genuine nontrivial ported paths and their coherent reconciliation.

Canonical shore-block extraction (§51). Every compatible zero connector on R∪{x,z}, including separated independent x,z, decomposes R into at most three fully ported zero paths: each maximal shore block inherits zero internal windows, and its extreme edges are forced forward by the adjacent special-vertex windows α(s,u,v)=α(u,v,s)=1−t(u,v) or by global endpoint compatibility. A singleton shore block between x and z is allowed only in z,u,x. Thus minimal-obstruction deletion criticality already furnishes an actual fully ported shore cover of A\{a} by at most three paths for every a. The one-path case closes by §50; the two-path case with a singleton closes by pairing the omitted vertex with the singleton. Hence an unclosed deletion state has two paths each of size ≥2, or three paths. This is the first port-certificate extraction from arbitrary deletion connectors; its missing step is global elimination or coherent gluing of the third block.

Sharpened §51 bridge certificate. For separated special coordinates, the ported three-block order P,s,Q,t,R has actual reverse bridge edges first(Q)→last(P) and first(R)→last(Q), since for either special s, α(u,s,v)=t(u,v) on shore u,v. This directed bridge pair records extra structural information necessary for a local or topological third-block elimination; arbitrary 3-path covers alone do not supply it.

Full-state deletion gluing (§52) now applies directly to the compatible connectors supplied by minimality, including their x,z positions and up to three shore blocks. Four pairwise common-order-compatible deletion connectors reconstruct a spanning compatible connector by ternary locality. Two common-order-compatible deletion connectors already close when their omitted labels occupy the same gap (the alternating cocycle gives a zero two-label insertion) or gaps separated by at least two common coordinates. Adjacent gaps leave precisely one new triple; its zero color closes. Therefore every compatible pair surviving in an obstruction occupies adjacent gaps and exposes a single color-one triple. This provides a direct coherence target on actual full connector states alongside the two-path shore-cover route. A topological extraction may seek four distinct omission labels in a simplex of genuinely order-compatible connector states; boundary realization and forced existence of that simplex remain mathematical obligations.
