# Monochromatic connector blocks and the parity-drop frontier

Work in the normalized flat tournament split B→z→A→x. An ordered tuple C on shore coordinates together with x,z is called a *compatible zero connector* if every consecutive triple of C has ternary color zero and its first and last ordered pairs point forward in this same fixed switched tournament. Its reversal has the complementary monochromatic color. A compatible connector spanning A∪{x,z} is sufficient to finish the NOR construction: inserting C or its reversal at the single transition of a NOR-good complementary-shore order changes only the two adjacent boundary windows; the corresponding two-bit parity calculation does not depend on the length of C. In the minimal outside word 01 at least one orientation closes. This is the insertion theorem, not a theorem asserting existence of C.

The directed shore triangle u→v→w→u furnished by minimum-shore structure gives the seed C=(u,x,z,v,w). Its three consecutive ternary windows vanish by the special-pair identities and v→w, and its exposed endpoint pairs are forward. In particular the local seed has no unresolved endpoint hypothesis. Extending this seed to all of A is the essential issue: separate vertex-by-vertex absorption may use mutually incompatible connector orders and therefore does not by itself imply a single spanning connector.

A useful sufficient certificate consists of two disjoint *fully ported zero shore paths* P,Q covering A. Such a path has zero ternary color on all consecutive triples and forward first and final directed pairs whenever defined. Concatenating P,x,z,Q gives a spanning compatible connector; conversely, any compatible connector with consecutive special coordinates x,z decomposes in this manner. This equivalence is restricted to the adjacent-special form. Connector orders allowing the distinguished vertices to move independently can be more general, so a two-path formulation must not silently exclude them.

A particularly strong one-deletion principle eliminates part of the earlier cyclic-packet analysis. If A\{a} has one fully ported zero Hamiltonian path P, then (P,x,z,a) is already a spanning compatible zero connector: the terminal port of P makes the crossing triple into x vanish, the special-coordinate identities make the last two triples vanish, and the first and last ports are forward. Thus no minimal whole-shore obstruction can have such a one-path deletion witness. Any obstruction surviving two-path deletion coverings must genuinely use two nontrivial paths in the relevant cases. A seven-coordinate double-backward collar can indeed defeat every fixed-boundary monochromatic surgery while p and q remain frozen, but that local impossibility does not contradict unrestricted spanning connector existence or the one-path lemma, which releases the frozen boundary.

Coherence of deletion witnesses supplies another sufficient construction. For four distinct shore labels d with compatible deletion connectors C_d on (A∪{x,z})\{d}, if all relative pair comparisons agree on intersections, those comparisons define one total order: every triple can be checked in a deletion domain avoiding its coordinates, and the endpoint pairs are inherited. With two coherent deletions, simultaneously reinserting their omitted labels is likewise valid when the gaps are separated by at least two positions, and in a common gap the tournament cocycle chooses an orientation with both new triple colors zero. Distinct adjacent gaps alone can expose one new triple (d,w,e) of color one, giving a precise obstruction to this *particular gluing*. Three-deletion coherence may instead leave one cyclic omitted triple; the full coherent-family problem has not been resolved unconditionally.

The strongest justified reduction of the conjecture along this route is therefore conditional and explicit: construct one spanning compatible zero connector on the whole minimum shore, or establish a sufficient deletion certificate (one fully ported deletion Hamiltonian path, two coherent fully ported shore paths, or four coherent full deletion connectors). Then the insertion theorem yields the global NOR order. Existing local blockers delimit particular exchanges; none, as stated, excludes a global construction with changed collars or independently relocated special coordinates. The remaining unproved step is to force one of these sufficient certificates from the minimum-shore hypotheses, possibly by an admissible well-founded collective absorption process.
