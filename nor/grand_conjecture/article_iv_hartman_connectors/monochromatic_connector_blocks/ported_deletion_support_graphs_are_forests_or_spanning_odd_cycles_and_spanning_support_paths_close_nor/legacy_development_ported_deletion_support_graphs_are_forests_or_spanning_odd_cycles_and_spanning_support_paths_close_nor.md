# Ported deletion support graphs are forests or spanning odd cycles, and spanning support paths close NOR — preserved pre-item development

Fix the flat split and port convention of the preceding four-cover theorem. Suppose for every d∈A, A\{d} has a chosen cover by exactly two nonempty fully ported zero paths. Form a graph whose vertices are their support subsets and whose edge e_d joins the two supports in the d-cover. Supports, rather than path orders, identify vertices. The graph is simple: the union of the two endpoints determines the omitted label, so distinct labels give distinct edges.

THEOREM. This graph is a forest or one odd cycle containing every edge and every deletion label. In the latter case |A| is odd and every support has size (|A|−1)/2. If the graph is a path containing all deletion labels, a spanning compatible connector exists and the homogeneous-cut NOR instance closes.

PROOF. For any coordinate u∈A, membership of u alternates across each edge other than e_u, while both endpoints of e_u omit u. On a cycle containing e_u, the remaining edges connect two nonmembership endpoints by alternation, so their number is even and the cycle is odd. An odd cycle must contain e_v for every v∈A, since otherwise membership of v would alternate round an odd cycle. Thus a cycle consumes all edges and labels, establishing the dichotomy. Along an odd cycle the membership pattern for each u has its single double-zero at e_u and ones at (|A|−1)/2 vertices. Each support has that size as well: alternatively, the edge equations |S_i|+|S_{i+1}|=|A|−1 around an odd cycle force every size equal.

For a path containing all n=|A| labels, membership of each u flips on n−1 edges, with its sole nonflip e_u having two zero endpoints. Hence for even n the two terminal supports are complementary subsets of A; for odd n the two terminal supports are identical, contradicting their being distinct vertices of a simple path. Each terminal support carries an actual fully ported zero-path witness by construction. Their complementary paths glue as P,x,z,Q and yield full closure across B. QED.

These incidence statements transfer from GN3N without its local Hamiltonian extension lemmas. A general branching forest still needs an ordered, port-preserving comparison or exchange argument. The odd-cycle support geometry supplies no automatic order coherence or connector. This theorem assumes fully ported two-path deletion covers, a stronger interface than unrestricted shore connectors.
