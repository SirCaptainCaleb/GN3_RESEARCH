# Three coherent ported deletion covers glue except for a cyclic three-label packet

Three pairwise-coherent fully ported two-zero-path deletion covers on A\{a}, A\{b}, A\{c} reconstruct a compatible spanning connector unless their three omitted labels form a cyclic comparison packet in one path. For |A|>=4 the common-support agreements already force a global equivalence relation with at most two classes. The ordered comparison relation is transitive on every triple outside {a,b,c}. If the omitted triple is ordered acyclically, all needed ternary windows are inherited except possibly that triple; the switching-tournament four-face XOR identity, or forward two-vertex port data when its class has exactly three vertices, forces its zero color. Thus two fully ported zero paths cover A and close the homogeneous cut. In the cyclic case all other same-class labels lie outside its common gap. The three deletion collars are zero but the three cyclic orders of the triple have color one, giving a localized, explicit three-label reconstruction obstruction. The theorem assumes actual coherent ported covers, so it does not yet force closure from arbitrary deletion-critical connector states.
