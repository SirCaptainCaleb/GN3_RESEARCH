# Homogeneous-module expansion of compatible zero connectors

Statement:
Let T be a shore tournament, M⊆V(T) a nonempty homogeneous module, and Q=T/M its tournament quotient, with module vertex m. Extend both tournaments by the special vertices z (source) and x (sink) in the flat switching representative. Suppose P=(p_1,…,p_s) orders M, has α=0 on each consecutive triple, and has p_1→p_2 and p_{s−1}→p_s whenever the respective edges exist. If C is a compatible zero connector in Q∪{x,z}, then replacing its unique occurrence of m by the entire block P produces a compatible zero connector in T∪{x,z}. The same block substitution preserves a cover by at most two fully ported shore-zero paths. Consequently, a smallest-order tournament without such a connector has no proper nontrivial homogeneous module that has a fully ported zero Hamiltonian path; especially no homogeneous pair and no transitive module.

Proof:
Let α(a,b,c)=t(a,b)+t(b,c)+t(c,a) mod 2, with t(a,b)=1 exactly when a→b. By homogeneity, every outside vertex u (including x,z) has the same orientation to every member of M as to m. Thus α(u,v,p_1)=α(u,v,m) and α(p_s,u,v)=α(m,u,v) for any outside u,v. For any two consecutive vertices p_i→p_{i+1} of P and any outside u, put e=t(u,p_i)=t(u,p_{i+1}). Then α(u,p_i,p_{i+1})=e+1+(1−e)=0 mod 2 and α(p_i,p_{i+1},u)=1+(1−e)+e=0 mod 2. In the expansion of C, unchanged triples retain their labels; the only new triples are internal triples of P or precisely the displayed types. Therefore every new triple has label 0. If the first or last pair of C involved m, its replacement pair has the same orientation for a boundary crossing or is an endpoint edge of P; in either case it remains forward. If s=1 the conclusion is identity. This proves connector substitution. For a two-path cover of Q, replace m in the one path containing it; the same local verification preserves that path's zero word and its two forward ports, while the other path is unchanged. Finally suppose T has minimum cardinality among tournaments without a connector and has a proper nontrivial module M with the stipulated P. The quotient Q has fewer vertices and hence possesses a connector, which expands to T, a contradiction. Every two-element tournament has a forward path, as does every transitive tournament. This cardinality-minimal conclusion does not claim that an arbitrary fixed-shore inclusion-minimal obstruction remains minimal after contracting a module.
