# Size-independent insertion law for compatible monochromatic connectors

Let α(u,v,w) be the reversal-odd ternary color determined by the switched tournament. A *compatible connector* is an order with every consecutive triple of color zero and both exposed endpoint pairs forward. When such a connector of length m is inserted at the single color transition in a complementary-shore good order with word 0^p1^q, the two candidate expanded status words, using the connector and its reversal, are 0^(p−1)e_p0^me_(p+2)1^(q−1) and 0^(p−1)e_p1^me_(p+2)1^(q−1). Here e_p and e_(p+2) are the boundary parity bits of the outside order; interior triples of the inserted connector are automatically monochromatic. Hence the boundary obstruction depends on two bits and is independent of m. In particular, for the minimal outside word 01 one of the two orientations closes. This establishes a size-independent insertion mechanism: once a spanning compatible connector has been constructed on the shore together with the two distinguished coordinates, inserting it into a good order of the complementary shore establishes the claimed NOR closure. The existence of that spanning connector is a separate unproved task.
