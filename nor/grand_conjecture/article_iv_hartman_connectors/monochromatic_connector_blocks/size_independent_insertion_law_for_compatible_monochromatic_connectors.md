# Size-independent insertion law for compatible monochromatic connectors

## Composition

A compatible connector of size M contributes the constant packet eta^M and two uniform outside scan bits at an interior insertion. The central-gap (1,0) residue is a local formula, not the global frontier: the neighboring-gap theorem supplies spanning closure at all sizes, including M=2.

## Development

A compatible connector is a monochromatic zero order whose first and last ordered pairs are forward in the switching-normalized tournament. Its reversal is monochromatic one with both endpoint pairs backward. If such a connector of size m is inserted at the unique switch of an outside-shore good order with word 0^p1^q, the two candidate full words are 0^{p-1}, e_p, 0^m, e_{p+2}, 1^{q-1} and 0^{p-1}, e_p, 1^m, e_{p+2}, 1^{q-1}, where e_i are the adjacent switching parities of the outside-shore path. Thus the connector size does not enter the boundary calculation. For a nonminimal bichromatic outside word, the only simultaneous obstruction is the parity pair (1,0); for the minimal word 01 one orientation always closes. Therefore a compatible monochromatic connector on all of A union {x,z} would reduce global closure to the same two-bit parity residue as the five-coordinate seed.
