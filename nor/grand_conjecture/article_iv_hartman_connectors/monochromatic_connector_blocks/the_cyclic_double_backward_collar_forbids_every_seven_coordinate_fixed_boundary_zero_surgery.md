# The cyclic double-backward collar forbids every seven-coordinate fixed-boundary zero surgery

Let p,q,a,b,c,x,z be seven distinct coordinates in a normalized flat split, with a→b→c→a, z dominating all shore labels, all shore labels dominating x, each of a,b,c dominating p and being dominated by q. Keeping p first and q last, no permutation of the five internal labels a,b,c,x,z has all consecutive ternary colors zero. This is proved by the tournament cocycle table: x,z adjacent forces the order x,z and leaves an unavoidable cyclic-triple defect; if separated, inspection of the number of triangle vertices between the special coordinates yields a forbidden triple or contradictory required orientations of adjacent triangle edges. This is a fixed-collar impossibility theorem. It does not preclude a connector obtained by moving p or q or using more shore vertices, and it cannot defeat the unconditional one-path deletion closure lemma.
