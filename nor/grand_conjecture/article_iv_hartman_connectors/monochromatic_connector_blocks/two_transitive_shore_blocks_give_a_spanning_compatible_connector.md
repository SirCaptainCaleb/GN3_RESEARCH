# Two transitive shore blocks give a spanning compatible connector

## Composition

Two nonempty transitive shore classes, ordered forward, form the compatible connector L,x,z,R. The homogeneous-cut insertion theorem then gives spanning NOR closure. This is a sufficient structural class, with no size bound on either block.

## Development

Use the switching-normalized split B -> z -> A -> x. Suppose A=L disjoint-union R, with L and R both nonempty transitive subtournaments. Order each part in its tournament dominance order. Then the coordinate order L,x,z,R is monochromatic zero. Windows inside L and R are transitive. The final two vertices of L followed by x are transitive because every A-vertex dominates x. The last vertex of L followed by x,z has color zero from the pair signature. Likewise x,z followed by the first vertex of R has color zero, and z followed by the first two vertices of R is transitive because z dominates A. The first ordered pair of the full connector is forward, and the last ordered pair is forward; this remains true when one side has size one because L dominates x and z dominates R. Hence L,x,z,R is a compatible spanning connector. Its reversal is the complementary monochromatic one connector. Therefore every two-coloring of the shore tournament into transitive classes closes the connector-construction problem.
