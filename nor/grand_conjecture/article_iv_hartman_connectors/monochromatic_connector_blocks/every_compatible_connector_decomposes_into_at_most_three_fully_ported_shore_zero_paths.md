# Every compatible connector decomposes into at most three fully ported shore zero paths

Any compatible zero connector on R∪{x,z} canonically decomposes into at most three maximal contiguous shore blocks, each an actual fully ported zero path: special-adjacent extreme shore edges are forced forward by α(s,u,v)=α(u,v,s)=1−t(u,v), and outer pairs by connector compatibility. If x,z are separated around one middle shore vertex u, the special order must be z,u,x because α(x,u,z)=1. In the fully three-block case C=P,s,Q,t,R, the mixed windows additionally force the two reverse bridges first(Q)→last(P) and first(R)→last(Q), using α(u,s,v)=t(u,v) for either special s. Therefore every one-vertex-deletion compatible connector of a minimal obstruction yields an actual ≤3-path ported shore cover, equipped with the reverse-bridge constraints when split three ways. Single-path deletion covers close immediately by P,x,z,a (§50), and two-path deletion covers with a singleton close by pairing the singleton with the omitted vertex. The unresolved deletion witnesses necessarily have either two shore blocks each of size≥2 or three blocks with witnessed reverse bridges.
