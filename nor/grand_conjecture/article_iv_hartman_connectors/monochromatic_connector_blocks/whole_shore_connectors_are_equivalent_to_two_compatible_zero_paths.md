# Whole-shore connectors are equivalent to two compatible zero paths

A *fully ported zero path* on a shore subset is an order whose consecutive ternary labels are zero and whose first and last ordered pairs point forward whenever present. If A is partitioned into two nonempty fully ported zero paths P,Q, then concatenating P,x,z,Q yields a compatible zero connector on A∪{x,z}: the special-coordinate identities control the crossing triples and the port hypotheses control both external endpoint pairs. Conversely, a compatible zero connector having x,z consecutive and in this order splits into such paths P,Q by cutting at x,z. The equivalence concerns this adjacent-special normal form; it is not an equivalence for arbitrary connector orders in which x and z move independently.
