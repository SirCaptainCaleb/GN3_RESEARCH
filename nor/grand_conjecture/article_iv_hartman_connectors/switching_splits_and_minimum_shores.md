# Switching splits and minimum-shore structure

Suppose the NOR conjecture fails in the minimum-counterexample setting established by the preceding reduction, and let x,z be a missing protected shortcut with a minimum nonempty signature shore. For a coordinate u distinct from x,z, write d(u)=α(x,z,u), and partition the remaining coordinates into A=d^(-1)(0) and B=d^(-1)(1). The representing tournament admits the flat switching normalization B→z→A→x, with all edges directed from B to A. This turns the missing-shortcut configuration into a distinguished tournament shore problem.

Minimality of A is stronger than mere nonemptiness. If x or z lacked a protected shortcut to u∈A, the corresponding signature shore would be properly smaller. Likewise, for distinct u,v∈A, the pair-signature restriction outside A would force a smaller nonempty shore if uv were both nonclonal and missing a protected shortcut. Consequently the protected adjacency graph on A∪{x,z} contains every edge apart from the distinguished edge xz, whose absence is the starting hypothesis.

The tournament T induced by A is not transitive: the minimum-shore argument gives every shore vertex both an in-neighbor and an out-neighbor. It therefore contains a directed triangle u→v→w→u. The flat pair-signature identity gives α(x,a,b)=α(z,a,b) for every a,b∈A, so this triangle has a common protected A3 face table with either special vertex. The consequence needed later is precisely the existence of a canonical five-coordinate connector seed; the structural reductions do not imply that every shore vertex can be inserted into one connector with the required endpoint ports. That distinction is the source of the subsequent whole-shore construction problem.
