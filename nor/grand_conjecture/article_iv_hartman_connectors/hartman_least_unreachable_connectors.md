# Hartman least-unreachable connectors and polarity sheets

The topological objective is a reversible full-state connector complex to which the least-unreachable labeling principle applies. States retain their ordered connector, both endpoint ports, absorbed support, and initial polarity; the one-change automaton conserves that polarity. Directed prefix prisms fail the required reversibility test.

The local coherence calculus is now explicit. Two absorptions in the same gap have a rank-two filler, and separated gaps commute. Adjacent gaps leave one directed-triangle obstruction and one isolated ternary defect. Its internal connector cycle is reversible locally; the support-increasing two-for-one exchange succeeds exactly when both outer incidences hold. One-sided hole transport is therefore a conditional candidate, not an established legal global repair algorithm. A decreasing endpoint-distance potential proves termination only after every proposed step is realized with its ports and outside word intact.

The precise Hartman frontier has three independent obligations: component-wise union closure of absorbed supports, coherent choices on proper supports, and boundary exclusion or attachment for the chosen realization. Individual reachability does not define the least missing label: a component may cover every individual coordinate without reaching their union. Parity orientation alone does not supply higher coherence. The two-polarity criterion is a conditional closure theorem whose full-state, union-closure, selection, and boundary hypotheses remain to be proved. Preserve this distinction when using barycentric boundary data.

An enlarged monochromatic state space with compatibility as a boundary target is a useful alternative to preserving ports at every intermediate step, but then both compatible targets must be reached at a spanning state. Separated special vertices are transposable twins away from the adjacent/distance-two cases. Five-packet cyclic rotations have an exact two-bit law but change exterior collars. A blocked equal-zero A2 packet allows frozen-collar deletion; the equal-one packet does not, and reversal cannot rescue its deletion. Pairwise shared-wall thickness restrictions remain conditional on common internal-edge normalization.

A gauge-invariant special-vertex parity restricts perfect alternation to one sheet. A realized ambient one-step special rotation flips this parity, but nonalternation alone need not yield a thick wall. Endpoint hole exchanges are available at shore endpoints under exact equal-incidence tests. These are conditional global repair interfaces: special vertices must be retained, exterior windows checked, and the equal-one deletion barrier remains unresolved.

The equal-one special-cell barrier is now classified by one outer shore edge: the transitive branch has exact one-sided zero rotations, and the cyclic branch admits no five-coordinate permutation retaining either ordered collar. The latter needs additional support or both-side boundary change. Rank-two alternate-gap arguments must track the inserted vertex's normalization: a neighboring 0011 insertion may reverse its mutual edge. The purported one-step outer exchange needs H→b and its changed first exterior collar. Relocated x,z fillers likewise need the two stated fixed-representative incidences and a legal removal at their former location. These preserve the common abstraction of an ordered packet with an explicit collar.
