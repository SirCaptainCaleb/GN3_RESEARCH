# Hartman least-unreachable connectors and polarity sheets

A Hartman-style connector argument begins with a graph of *actual* monochromatic connector orders, not a formal adjacency graph of candidate labels. Two states are adjacent only if a prescribed reversible local modification preserves the color of every consecutive ternary window, including those crossing the modified packet. The support of a state is the set of shore coordinates represented in its order. A successful terminal state must have full shore support and compatible exposed endpoint pairs.

The state space can be enlarged by dropping endpoint compatibility as an invariant of intermediate states. This allows the distinguished coordinates x,z to move independently and permits intermediate zero orders whose first or last pair is not yet forward. The enlargement is sound only for fully checked moves: a local A2 rotation may alter exterior triple windows, so the packet identity alone never proves that the move is legal in the complete connector.

The topological labeling proposal uses, for each repair component, its least unreachable boundary target. Component invariance follows from defining reachability by actual reversible edges, but a second logical requirement remains. Reachability of each individual shore coordinate does not imply reachability of their union in a single state. To infer spanning support one needs a union-closure theorem for represented supports in each oriented component, or an alternative exchange that explicitly combines all separately witnessed coordinates. The known same-gap and separated-gap absorption fillers establish only local parts of such a theorem; adjacent-gap obstructions and endpoint compatibility still require global handling.

Accordingly the proposed least-unreachable/Sperner extraction remains conditional. A complete proof must identify a finite repair complex and its boundary map, prove both the component and boundary conditions, obtain a forced successful component, and turn its support into one full compatible connector. Without those obligations, topology supplies neither a proof nor a valid claim that all coordinates have been simultaneously absorbed.
