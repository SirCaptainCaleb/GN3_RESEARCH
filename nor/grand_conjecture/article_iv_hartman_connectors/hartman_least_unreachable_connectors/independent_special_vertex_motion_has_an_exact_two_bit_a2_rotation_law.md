# Independent special-vertex motion has an exact two-bit A2 rotation law

## Composition

The two cyclic motions of a special vertex in a five-coordinate zero packet have an exact two-bit criterion: a motion exists locally iff the exterior comparison bits differ. Equal bits block both. The rotations change exposed packet pairs, so exterior windows and global endpoint ports still require verification.

## Development

Let a monochromatic-zero connector contain five consecutive coordinates p,a,x,b,c with p,a,b,c in A. The old zero windows imply p->a, b->a, and b->c in the fixed switching-normalized tournament. Cyclically rotate the central zero triple (a,x,b). The order p,b,a,x,c is monochromatic zero exactly when p->b and c->a. The opposite cyclic rotation p,x,b,a,c is monochromatic zero exactly when b->p and a->c. Thus one of the two motions of x exists exactly when the two exterior comparison bits t(p,b) and t(a,c) are opposite. If they are equal, both cyclic motions are blocked. The same statement holds with z in place of x. Hence independent motion of a special vertex is governed by a two-bit local barrier, not by adjacent swaps.

Elevation audit: this is an exact law for the five-coordinate packet. Each rotation changes the packet's first or last ordered pair, so external ternary windows are not automatically frozen. A legal move in an ambient monochromatic or compatible connector requires separate exterior-window and endpoint checks.
