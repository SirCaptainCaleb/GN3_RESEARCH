# Hartman closure requires union closure of absorbed supports

Consider a reversible repair graph whose vertices are realizable monochromatic connector orders, with a support equal to the shore coordinates already absorbed. A connected component may contain, for every shore vertex a, some state whose support contains a without containing a single state whose support is all of A. Therefore single-coordinate reachability does not justify an assertion of spanning closure. A sufficient additional hypothesis is support union closure within each oriented component: whenever supports S,T are represented in that component, a state with support containing S∪T is represented there. Finite iteration then converts reachability of every singleton into reachability of full support. In a Hartman-style least-unreachable argument this hypothesis must be proved by admissible connector exchanges before topological boundary labels can be converted into a spanning witness.
