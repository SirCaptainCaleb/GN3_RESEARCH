# The one-skeleton is not enough

The chamber graph of the type-A Coxeter complex records adjacent transpositions, but its edges alone do not control the extension of an antipodal labeling across higher-dimensional cells. For example Q(v_1,…,v_n)=(v_1,v_n) reverses its ordered coordinate pair under permutation reversal, while an adjacent swap changes at most one endpoint unless that swap touches an extreme position. This local rook-type compatibility does not imply a desired complementary chamber edge. A topological argument therefore has to build a genuine equivariant cellular map or verify all commuting-square and braid-hexagon compatibility relations. In particular, an edge-labeling argument that assumes such a map exists without checking its higher-dimensional cells is incomplete.
