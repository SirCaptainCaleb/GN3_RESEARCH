# Bourgin-Yang turns target compression into a large zero locus — preserved pre-item development

## Development


A second transferable principle from Article VII is the dimension form of Bourgin--Yang.

Let
\[
f:S^d\to W
\]
be a continuous odd map into a real vector space \(W\) of dimension \(q\le d\). Then
\[
\dim f^{-1}(0)\ge d-q.
\]

For NOR, the useful design problem is therefore to compress a one-change obstruction into a low-dimensional reversal-odd statistic on the Coxeter sphere of coordinate orders.

If such a statistic takes values in a proper subspace \(W\), Bourgin--Yang produces not merely one balanced carrier but a positive-dimensional family of zeros. A minimal carrier of any zero gives a positive convex dependence among the chamber labels occurring on that face.

This suggests a two-stage program:
1. construct a low-rank odd statistic from the first and last obstruction fronts, transition positions, or another compressed one-change defect;
2. use the dimension of the zero locus to obtain enough freedom to vary chambers or pole charts while retaining balance.

A guardrail from Article VII remains essential: a positive-dimensional zero set does not automatically imply many combinatorially distinct recurrences. One must separately prove that motion in the zero locus changes the relevant chamber support, pole, or defect configuration.
