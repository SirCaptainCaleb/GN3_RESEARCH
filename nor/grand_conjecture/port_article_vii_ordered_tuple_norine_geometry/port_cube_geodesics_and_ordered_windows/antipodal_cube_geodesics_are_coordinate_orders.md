# Antipodal cube geodesics are coordinate orders

Let V be an n-element set and Q_V its Boolean cube. A geodesic from S to V\S has length n and therefore changes each coordinate precisely once; its successive flip directions form a permutation π=(v_1,…,v_n) of V. Conversely any S and π determine such a geodesic by successively taking symmetric difference with {v_i}. Thus an antipodal geodesic is specified by an initial cube vertex and a coordinate permutation. This decomposition is canonical and shows that requiring a property of every antipodal geodesic is different from restricting to monotone geodesics between the particular poles ∅ and V. In the translation-invariant coordinate sector the color word depends on π alone; for general cube-window colorings it may also depend on S.
