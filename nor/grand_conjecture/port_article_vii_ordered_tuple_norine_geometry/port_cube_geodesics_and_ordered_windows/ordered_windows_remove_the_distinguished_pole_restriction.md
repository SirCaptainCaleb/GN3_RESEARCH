# Ordered windows remove the distinguished-pole restriction

Fix k≥2 and a coloring χ of k consecutive cube vertices satisfying χ(\bar X_{k−1},…,\bar X_0)=1−χ(X_0,…,X_{k−1}). Given an antipodal cube geodesic, its consecutive k-vertex windows are defined regardless of the initial vertex. For a translation-invariant coloring the window with flip directions (v_i,…,v_{i+k−2}) has color h(v_i,…,v_{i+k−2}), and changing the initial pole does not alter this directed-coordinate word. By contrast, an up-then-down path defined relative to Boolean rank has a prescribed visit to V, and a down-then-up path visits ∅. These are special antipodal paths rather than a representation of all paths. The distinction prevents a rank-oriented argument from being asserted for arbitrary geodesics without a proof that pole choice is harmless for its coloring.
