# Article IV — Hartman connectors and least-unreachable repair topology

Article IV turns the flat switching residue into a compatible monochromatic connector problem. A connector spanning A∪{x,z} closes the full homogeneous-cut instance by neighboring-gap insertion. Universal bases cover every shore of size at most six; a minimal whole-shore obstruction is deletion-critical. Exact ported zero paths, square-path gauges, and collective six-incidence collars form the local interfaces.

The cross-article combination supplies explicit absorbing boundary targets. With adjacent x,z, an empty or singleton shore cap and the opposite port forward absorbs any missing shore vertex while completing both ports. An empty cap absorbs a missing pair; a singleton cap absorbs a transitive missing pair. Therefore one- and two-deletion witnesses of these types yield a spanning connector and full NOR closure. These excluded boundary classes follow from actual legal moves and can serve the Hartman boundary analysis.

The special-cell obstruction is sharper. Equal-zero rotation barriers admit collar-preserving deletion. In the equal-one branch, a single outer shore edge distinguishes two exact one-sided rotations from a rigid directed-four-cycle configuration. The rigid branch requires more support, both-side collar changes, or the second special vertex. Every motion retains an explicit exterior-collar obligation.

Rank-two growth also requires consistent certificates. Neighboring alternative insertion positions can change a vertex's normalization and its mutual edge; separated absorptions still commute. The candidate one-step two-hole exchange needs its independent incidence and external-collar conditions. Relocated special-pair fillers require their fixed-representative incidences and a legal removal at the former location. These conditions align the connector calculus with Articles I–III's ordered-packet realization interface.

The current global target is to reach an absorbing boundary class or prove collective spanning growth. A Hartman argument still needs reversible full-state moves, a valid support-union mechanism, and coherent boundary realization of both final port targets. The new boundary theorems and barrier classification give concrete cases and exact remaining prerequisites for that proof.

The GN3N article-only transfer supplies two further complete closure criteria. Four pairwise-compatible deletion covers of the shore by at most two fully ported zero paths reconstruct a whole-shore ported cover; P,x,z,Q (or z,P,x for one class) then spans. Selected two-path deletion supports form a forest or a unique spanning odd cycle. When their graph is a spanning path, its terminal supports are complementary and their ported witnesses close the instance. These proofs use locality and set incidence, so apply beyond the boundary-tournament subclass. The remaining task is to select such ported covers and force coherence or the spanning-path geometry. Unrestricted deletion connectors do not supply those stronger witnesses automatically.

More generally, 2r+2 mutually consistent NOR-good deletion orders glue to a good order: a forbidden 010/101 color subsequence can be witnessed by an adjacent change-pair and a later return-color window, using at most 2r+1 coordinates. Thus eight coherent deletion orders suffice in the ternary case. The topological opportunity is to force witness coherence while retaining ports and exact closure certificates; GN3N's symmetric positive-deficiency zeros require independent exclusion before they can prove NOR.

Latest closure elevation: the two-path ported shore-cover condition is closed under ordered tournament domination joins, so all shores whose strongly connected components have size at most six close. A single fully ported zero Hamiltonian path on any shore deletion A\{a} yields the full connector P,x,z,a immediately (§50), and a singleton in either class of a two-path deletion cover can be paired with the omitted coordinate to close. Thus a genuine unclosed ported deletion cover must consist of two paths of length at least two. Three coherent two-path deletion covers localize their sole possible order-gluing failure to a cyclic three-label packet of color 1. Its seven-label double-backward fixed-collar configuration has no packet-only zero permutation preserving both outer endpoints; resolving that relative barrier demands moved exterior collars or additional shore support. The one-path specialization of this packet always closes by the direct omitted-coordinate construction. The outstanding global problem is selecting ported two-path deletion witnesses and forcing coherent gluing, support-path incidence, or a genuine admissible global exchange.

Latest whole-shore closure (§§43–50). An extreme shore module carrying one fully ported zero Hamiltonian path absorbs into every compatible connector on its complement, including connectors with independently moving x,z. In particular minimal obstructions have no universal shore source or sink and no proper extreme transitive module. Ported two-zero-path covers glue across ordered tournament joins. Hence shores with strong components of size at most six close for arbitrary total shore size. In a minimal obstructed shore with multiple strong components, at least two components lack a ported zero Hamiltonian path.

Deletion-interface sharpening (§50): a single fully ported zero Hamiltonian path P on A minus {a} immediately closes via P,x,z,a. Likewise a two-path deletion cover with a singleton path q closes by pairing a,q into a forward two-vertex zero path, with the other path unchanged. Thus EVERY fully ported two-path deletion cover of a minimal obstruction has two paths, each of size at least two. In particular the coherent three-deletion cyclic-packet single-path residual is no global obstacle: earlier seven-coordinate fixed-collar impossibility remains a valid local no-go but a deletion path bypasses it. General closure still requires production/selection/coherence of nontrivial ported two-path deletion covers or collective whole-shore absorption.

Canonical deletion-cover extraction (§51). Removing x,z from ANY compatible connector on a proper shore leaves at most three maximal contiguous shore blocks, each automatically a fully ported zero path: the special-adjacent extreme shore edges are forced forward by the zero ternary windows. Hence every omitted-vertex witness in a minimal connector obstruction yields an actual at-most-three-path ported shore cover. One-block covers close by P,x,z,a; two-block covers with a singleton close by pairing the missing vertex with that singleton. Every surviving deletion witness therefore consists of either two ported blocks of size at least two each, or three ported blocks. The route toward full closure is to eliminate/repartition the three-block case, or cohere/select the two-block deletion family so that its global support graph has a spanning path or four pairwise coherent covers. Hartman union-closure and compatible outside collars are still independent unsolved obligations.

Refined three-block extraction: When the special vertices are separated in a compatible deletion connector P,s,Q,t,R with three shore blocks, the two crossing zero windows force the reverse bridge orientations first(Q)→last(P) and first(R)→last(Q). These are certified incidence constraints on any remaining three-path deletion state, offering a concrete target for admissible block merger or omission exchange.

Full-state deletion gluing (§52) now applies directly to the compatible connectors supplied by minimality, including their x,z positions and up to three shore blocks. Four pairwise common-order-compatible deletion connectors reconstruct a spanning compatible connector by ternary locality. Two common-order-compatible deletion connectors already close when their omitted labels occupy the same gap (the alternating cocycle gives a zero two-label insertion) or gaps separated by at least two common coordinates. Adjacent gaps leave precisely one new triple; its zero color closes. Therefore every compatible pair surviving in an obstruction occupies adjacent gaps and exposes a single color-one triple. This provides a direct coherence target on actual full connector states alongside the two-path shore-cover route. A topological extraction may seek four distinct omission labels in a simplex of genuinely order-compatible connector states; boundary realization and forced existence of that simplex remain mathematical obligations.
