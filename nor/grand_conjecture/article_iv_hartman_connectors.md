# Article IV — Hartman connectors and least-unreachable repair topology

# From protected shortcut failure to whole-shore connector construction

Fix the reversal-odd ternary coloring α on ordered triples associated with the representing tournament of a NOR instance. An order is NOR-good if the sequence of colors of its consecutive triples has at most one change. The objective of this Article is to exhibit an antipodal NOR-good spanning order. The arguments below operate in the minimum-counterexample regime established by the prior reductions; each use of that regime is conditional until the resulting obstruction has been eliminated.

Suppose a protected shortcut required by the existing construction cannot be realized between two distinguished coordinates x,z. For each other coordinate u define its pair signature d(u)=α(x,z,u), and let A and B be the shores d^(-1)(0) and d^(-1)(1). The flat tournament identities permit a switching normalization B→z→A→x (in particular B→A). Choose the nonempty unresolved shore A with minimum size. The minimum-shore argument then shows that every pair among A∪{x,z}, except the distinguished pair xz, has the required protected adjacency. It also forces the tournament induced on A to have both an entering and leaving edge at every vertex, hence a directed triangle u→v→w→u. The same flatness identity gives α(x,a,b)=α(z,a,b) for any a,b∈A. These statements explain why local connector construction is possible, but they are not yet a spanning argument.

A compatible zero connector on a set U containing x,z is a linear order on U for which all consecutive ternary colors vanish and whose two exposed directed endpoint pairs point forward in the fixed switched tournament. The induced complement order on B is chosen NOR-good under the minimum-counterexample hypothesis. The size-independent connector insertion calculation shows that if a compatible zero connector covers U=A∪{x,z}, inserting it, or its reversal, at the color-change cut of that complement order gives a NOR-good spanning order. Only two crossing ternary windows are new; their two parity bits determine whether the first or reversed insertion works. For the reduced complementary status word 01, one orientation succeeds regardless of the connector length. Thus the existence of one full compatible connector is a sufficient *closure certificate*, while the calculation alone does not produce that connector.

The directed triangle supplies an actual five-coordinate certificate: (u,x,z,v,w) is a compatible zero connector on {u,v,w,x,z}. Each of its three consecutive ternary colors vanishes by the common special-coordinate identities and the directed edge v→w, and both exposed ports are forward. The proof cannot conclude by absorbing every remaining shore label separately. An absorption of label a and an absorption of label b can occur in incompatible orders and can change different endpoint pairs. What is needed is a single monochromatic connector whose support is all of A and whose final two endpoint ports are simultaneously compatible.

The simplest sufficient construction has two disjoint fully ported zero paths P,Q partitioning A. A fully ported zero path has color zero on every consecutive triple and forward first and last directed pairs where defined. The concatenation (P,x,z,Q) is then a compatible zero connector. Conversely, such a decomposition follows from a full connector that happens to place x,z consecutively in that order. The converse must not be applied to general repair states: permitting x,z to move independently enlarges the connector class, and an argument confined to adjacent-special orders would require an additional normalization theorem to be exhaustive.

The one-deletion lemma gives a sharper closure criterion. If for some a∈A the deletion shore A\{a} has a fully ported zero Hamiltonian path P, then (P,x,z,a) is already a full compatible zero connector. The crossing triple between P and x vanishes by the terminal port of P; the two final triples vanish by the special-pair identities; and the initial and terminal pairs are forward. In particular, any unresolved minimum shore cannot admit this one-path deletion certificate. This criterion takes precedence over attempts to repair a cyclic deletion packet with fixed outer endpoints: even though a seven-coordinate cyclic double-backward collar can prohibit every fixed-boundary zero surgery, that obstruction concerns a strictly restricted family of moves, not the existence of a full connector after moving the outer collar.

Coherent families of full deletion connectors give another exact route. If four deletion connectors indexed by distinct shore labels agree on all relative pair comparisons where both are defined, their pair comparisons determine a total order of the full vertex set. Every triple is witnessed in some deletion domain, and the endpoint ports inherit compatibility, so this order is a full connector. Two coherent deletion witnesses can likewise be glued when their missing vertices occupy nonadjacent insertion gaps or a common gap satisfying the cocycle completion. Adjacent distinct gaps expose a possible bad triple (d,w,e), and three coherent deletions can leave a cyclic packet of omitted labels. These are precise obstructions to the corresponding gluing operations; they do not establish a global impossibility. The present deletion theory consequently reduces closure to forcing a suitable coherent family, finding one fully ported deletion path, or repairing the residual adjacent-gap/cyclic configuration by an admissible move.

The protected-shortcut ladder is a complementary local mechanism, not an independent solution of that final task. For a middle order W=(w_1,...,w_m), define X_i=α(x,w_i,w_(i+1)), Z_i=α(z,w_i,w_(i+1)), and R_i=α(x,z,w_i). Flat ternary parity yields R_(i+1)⊕R_i=X_i⊕Z_i. At a rail disagreement the four-coordinate carrier (x,w_i,w_(i+1),z) realizes the local shortcut, and the crossed-endpoint residue closes on its first four coordinates. The missing step in general is therefore not the algebraic production of that local carrier; it is the integration of an actual protected witness into an order with globally admissible collars.

Hartman's least-unreachable approach similarly isolates a genuine global requirement. Let repair states be actual monochromatic connector orders, joined only by explicitly legal reversible moves. Define their supports by the shore labels they contain. Reaching a state with each individual shore label somewhere in a connected component does *not* imply reaching one state containing every label. A topological extraction requires a proved support-combination operation (for example, componentwise union closure), as well as its domain, boundary labels, invariance, and an admissible path from the topologically forced event to the final endpoint-compatible state. Endpoint compatibility need not remain invariant at intermediate states, but the terminal extracted state must satisfy it; independent motion of x,z is allowed only after checking all affected ternary windows. Thus the topological route presently supplies a conditional framework rather than the missing global connector.

Combining the established statements yields the following exact frontier. Assume the minimum-counterexample and flat switching reductions, fix a minimum nonempty shore A in B→z→A→x, and use the protected-completeness and common-triangle lemmas to obtain a five-coordinate compatible connector. Prove that this initial configuration admits a *single* compatible zero connector on all of A∪{x,z}; equivalently, force any one of the demonstrated sufficient certificates, or construct an admissible terminating collective repair that reaches such a connector without requiring x,z to remain adjacent throughout. The insertion theorem then yields a NOR-good spanning order and discharges the original counterexample. None of the presently stated local absorption, fixed-collar obstruction, coherent deletion, or topological component results establishes this global step on its own.

This is the Article's current proof boundary: local protected completeness and multiple conditional connector-closure mechanisms are available, but unconditional whole-shore connector existence with compatible final ports has not been derived. Any future proof must either close that gap or replace the conditional insertion route with a different fully justified spanning construction.
