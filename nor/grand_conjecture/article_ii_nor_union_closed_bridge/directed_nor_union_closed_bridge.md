# Directed NOR and union-closed structures

Article II isolates the useful union-closure mechanism from its failed reductions. A union-closed fixed-tail feasible family saturates the union of its supports; covering all but at most one exterior coordinate gives a spanning NOR order by endpoint extension, in every coordinate arity. Opposite saturated sectors give a spanning converging fork under their stated coverage hypotheses. This is a support-union theorem, not an implication from a Frankl heavy-element conclusion.

The obstructions explain the remaining burden. Local transitive tournaments can fail support union closure, punctured Boolean families can miss only the top, and ordered tight witnesses can resist merging. The doubled-cube lift can force three changes despite balanced frequencies. A common global edge order also requires an acyclic comparison system and cannot be assumed for general ternary labels. Preserve these failures as tests for any claimed bridge.

The protected-root continuation is now routed to Article III, and the flat connector continuation to Article IV. The earlier special-blocker flat-closure claim is withdrawn; arbitrary blocking does not force that scan. Article IV's component-wise union closure is a related saturation requirement for reversible full-state connector components, with no proved equivalence to these fixed-tail families.

Recent topological results sharpen local attachment. Omitted-coordinate exchange roots are distinct from physical window-slide roots; a dictionary is needed before importing exchange cycles as Radon cancellations. Actual protected-root blow-ups have a common strict halfspace locally, so isolated inert zeros have index zero. Global patching of overlapping stars and positive-dimensional zero loci remain open. Opposite physical roots in one cell require a tied block containing their full ordered packet. Their physical endpoints are r positions apart, so freezing to an adjacent-swap edge cannot retain that same certificate. These local topology results support, rather than replace, the ordered-witness realization problem.

The cross-article realization interface is now explicit: reverse the contiguous r+1-coordinate descent packet. This retains 10 and reverses its physical root while preserving full support; only the r−1 crossing windows on each side remain to be checked. Article I's full-support interval calculus therefore supplies a concrete packet move for the physical-root program. The root vector, its ordered packet, and its exposed collar form one certificate.
