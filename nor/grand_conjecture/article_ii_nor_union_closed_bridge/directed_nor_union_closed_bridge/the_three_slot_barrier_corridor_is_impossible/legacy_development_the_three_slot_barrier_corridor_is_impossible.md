## The three-slot barrier corridor is impossible

Work in the doubly extremal threshold state of subsection 155. Thus the target-compatible band has globally maximum length B, and among such states the distance from the target switch to the nearest unresolved full-curvature boundary is minimum.

Assume the target switch is flat and the nearest full-curvature boundary lies exactly three transition slots to the right.

Repair the flat target switch toward that boundary.

The audited transport law gives only two possibilities.

1. **Distance three.**  
   The transported switch lands exactly on the occupied full-curvature boundary transition. The two switches annihilate, lowering cyclic variation by two and yielding a spanning one-change order after the usual cut. This closes NOR.

2. **Distance two.**  
   The transported switch remains strictly inside the already target-compatible monochromatic band, one transition slot before the full boundary. Move the proposed cut together with the transported switch. No unresolved boundary is crossed, so the same matched band remains target-compatible and B is unchanged. But the cut-to-boundary distance drops from three to one, contradicting the secondary extremality.

Both alternatives are impossible in a counterexample.

Therefore a doubly extremal bad threshold state cannot have a flat target switch whose nearest full-curvature boundary is at distance three.

### Residual local frontier

After outward combing and secondary extremality, only the following cases remain:

- the target switch itself is fully curved;
- a full-curvature boundary lies one transition slot from the flat target switch;
- a full-curvature boundary lies two transition slots away.

In the distance-two case, a 2-step repair would collide and close NOR, so the repair selector is forced to distance three and jumps across the barrier. Thus the unresolved flat-target geometry is confined to five- and six-coordinate packets.
