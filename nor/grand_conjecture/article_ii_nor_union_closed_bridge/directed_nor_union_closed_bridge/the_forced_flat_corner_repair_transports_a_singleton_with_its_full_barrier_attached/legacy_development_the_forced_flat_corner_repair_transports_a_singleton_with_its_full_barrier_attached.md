## Development

Normalize the Phi-maximal profile near the q|2|1 corner by consecutive statuses 1,0,0,1,0. Let Q={x,u,a,b} be the fully-curved q|2 transition tetrahedron, so alpha(x,u,a)=1 and alpha(u,a,b)=0. Let D={a,b,c,d} be the unique flat 2|1 transition tetrahedron, with alpha(a,b,c)=0 and alpha(b,c,d)=1. The counterexample-forced left repair of D swaps a,b and must take the distance-3 branch. Full curvature of Q gives alpha(x,u,b)=0 and alpha(x,a,b)=1; alternation gives alpha(u,b,a)=1 and alpha(x,b,a)=0. Hence in the repaired order (...,x,u,b,a,c,d,...) the same underlying tetrahedron Q, now ordered (x,u,b,a), has consecutive statuses 0,1 and off-face values 1,0, so it is again fully curved. Thus the new singleton created inside the old q-run carries the old q|2 full barrier as its trailing boundary. The first repair therefore does not dissolve the curvature obstruction; it moves a singleton-plus-full-wall packet one step into the q corridor. The remaining escape problem is to show this packet cannot traverse the corridor and return without colliding with the fixed p|q barrier or lowering variation.
