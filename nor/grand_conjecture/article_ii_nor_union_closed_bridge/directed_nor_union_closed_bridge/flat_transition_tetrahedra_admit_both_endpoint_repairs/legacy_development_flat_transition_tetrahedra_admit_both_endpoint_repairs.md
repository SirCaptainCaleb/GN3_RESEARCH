## Development

Work in the pure orientation sector with tetrahedral coboundary zero. Let four consecutive coordinates a,b,c,d carry a transition x=alpha(a,b,c), y=alpha(b,c,d)=1-x. Put u=alpha(a,b,d) and w=alpha(a,c,d). Since delta alpha on this four-set is zero, x xor u xor w xor y=0, hence u xor w=1. The fully curved pattern is u=y and w=x. If the tetrahedron is not fully curved, the only remaining binary possibility is u=x and w=y. Therefore both endpoint repair criteria hold simultaneously: swapping c,d removes the central transition because u=x, and swapping a,b removes it because w=y. Consequently in the coboundary-flat sector every transition is either a pinned fully curved universal switch or a flat switch admitting repairs in both directions. By endpoint-repair monotonicity, on a minimum-variation cyclic order each such flat switch can be moved left or right by a full-support adjacent swap without increasing variation; any strict decrease closes NOR. Thus the residual flat-sector problem is a system of bidirectionally mobile switches separated by sparse fully curved barriers.
