## Three of four transitions are curved in an extremal perfect-blocker carrier

Continue in the coboundary-flat pure-orientation sector. Let C maximize
[
Phi=sum r_i^2
]
among four-change cyclic orders, and suppose its canonical run profile is
[
1,p,q,2,
qquad p,qge2.
]

The previous results give:
- the transition q|2 is fully curved by the singleton-trap theorem;
- the transition p|q is fully curved by the double-barrier theorem.

### Theorem

The transition
[
1mid p
]
is also fully curved.

### Proof

Assume 1|p is flat. Then its left endpoint repair is available and cannot decrease variation from 4 to 2.

Moving left from the transition 1|p, the immediately preceding run has length 1, and the run before that has length 2.

The corrected transport distance is either 2 or 3.

- If the distance is 3, the transported switch lands exactly on the transition q|2, which is already occupied. The repair would annihilate two switches and produce cyclic variation 2, contradicting counterexamplehood.

- Therefore the transport distance must be 2. It crosses the singleton run and lands one status inside the preceding length-two run. The local run lengths
  [
  (2,1,p)
  ]
  become
  [
  (1,1,p+1).
  ]
  Hence
  [
  DeltaPhi
  =
  1+1+(p+1)^2-(4+1+p^2)
  =
  2(p-1)>0,
  ]
  because p>=2.

This contradicts the maximal choice of C. Therefore 1|p is fully curved. QED.

### Corollary

In a Phi-maximal canonical four-change carrier with p,q>=2, three transitions are pinned universal switches:
[
1 oxed{m full} p oxed{m full} q oxed{m full} 2.
]
Only the remaining short-corner transition
[
2mid1
]
can possibly be flat and mobile.

Thus any closed equality component of the flat-sector repair graph has an extremal representative with at most one mobile switch. If that final switch is also fully curved, all four transitions are universal-switch gadgets. If it is flat, every available repair must leave the three-barrier regime and later return without ever creating a collision. This is now the precise residual escape problem.
