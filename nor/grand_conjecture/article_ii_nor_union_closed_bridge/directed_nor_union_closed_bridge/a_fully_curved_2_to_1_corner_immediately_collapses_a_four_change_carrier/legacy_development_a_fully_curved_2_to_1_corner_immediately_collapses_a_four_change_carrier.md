# A fully-curved 2-to-1 corner immediately collapses a four-change carrier — preserved pre-item development

Normalize the local cyclic word around a length-two run followed by a singleton as 0,0,1,0, where the first two 0s are the entire length-two run and the final 0 begins the next long run. Write the relevant coordinates as ...,u,a,b,c,d,... with alpha(u,a,b)=0, alpha(a,b,c)=0, alpha(b,c,d)=1, alpha(c,d,e)=0. Suppose the transition tetrahedron {a,b,c,d} at the 2|1 corner is fully curved. Then full curvature gives alpha(a,c,d)=0. Swap a,b. Alternation gives alpha(u,b,a)=1 and alpha(b,a,c)=1, while alpha(a,c,d)=0 and the following 0-run is untouched. Hence the local packet becomes 1,1,0,0. The run preceding the old length-two run already has color 1, so the two new 1s merge into it; the old length-two 0-run disappears completely, and the remaining 0s merge into the following run. Cyclic variation drops from four to two, giving a spanning one-change order after cutting. Therefore the residual 2|1 corner of a Phi-maximal canonical 1,p,q,2 carrier cannot be fully curved. Together with the previous three-barrier theorem, every surviving Phi-maximal flat-sector counterexample has exactly three fully-curved transitions and the unique remaining 2|1 transition flat.
