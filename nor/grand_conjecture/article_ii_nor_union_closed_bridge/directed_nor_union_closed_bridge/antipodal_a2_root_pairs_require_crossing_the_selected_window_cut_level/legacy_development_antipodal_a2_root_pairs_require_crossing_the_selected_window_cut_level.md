## Antipodal A2 root pairs require crossing the selected window cut level

Work in ternary arity in the switch-prism root construction. Let a minimal zero carrier project to an A2 Coxeter face with tied block B={a,b,c}. The three coordinates of B occupy three consecutive positions in every refining permutation.

Any active selected root whose endpoints both lie in B must come from the unique ternary window occupying exactly those three tied positions. Thus all active A2 roots refer to the same physical window position i; only the internal order of B changes.

For reversed block orders (a,b,c) and (c,b,a), the selected roots are antipodal, e_a-e_c and e_c-e_a. Reversal oddness makes the observed window colors opposite. At a fixed cut level k, however, the threshold target q_i(k) for that physical window is independent of the chamber order. Therefore exactly one of the reversed windows can violate the target at that fixed cut.

Consequently an antipodal root pair cannot coexist in a single horizontal cut slice. Any minimal zero dependence of antipodal-pair type must cross the vertical cut wall at the same physical window position i, where the prescribed threshold sign changes.

Thus the minimal A2 obstruction splits into:
1. directed-root-triangle cancellation, which may live in one horizontal A2 slice;
2. antipodal-pair cancellation, which is necessarily a mixed braid-times-switch obstruction localized to one A2 block and the cut wall of its physical window.

This reduces the antipodal branch to a small local prism cell rather than a generic permutahedral obstruction.
