# Sperner endpoint labels require ordered-tail and switch compatibility — preserved pre-item development

The completed-cube macroface B_0=sd(Delta^{n-1}) gives a legal Sperner labeling from every choice function l(S) in S. Its fully labeled maximal chain is unique, not merely guaranteed to exist. Indeed the final element of the associated permutation must be l(V); the preceding element must be l(V minus {l(V)}), and so on. Conversely these successive deletions construct the fully labeled chain. Thus this particular Sperner interface is a deterministic elimination order. This does not invalidate the simplex construction; it identifies the mathematical work that a NOR labeling must perform.

An explicit failure of endpoint labels. On V={1,2,3,4,5}, define h(a,b,c)=1_{a>c}, except flip h(2,3,4) to 1 and its reversal h(4,3,2) to 0. Reversal oddness is preserved. Let l(S)=max S. For every nonempty S there exists a NOR order of S ending at l(S): for |S|<=4 the window word has length at most two, hence automatically at most one change; for S=V use (2,1,3,4,5), whose word is 000. Therefore l is a legal Sperner label selecting an attainable good endpoint on every subset. Nevertheless its unique fully labeled chain gives (1,2,3,4,5), whose word is 010. Endpoint attainability on subsets does not supply witness compatibility.

A state retaining the missing information. For a partial order P of at least three vertices, retain (S,a,b,c,t), where S is its support, (a,b) its final ordered pair, c its last window color, and t in {0,1} its number of changes. Appending x outside S computes d=h(a,b,x), changes the tail to (b,x), and changes the switch count to t+1_{d!=c}; admit the move precisely when this is at most one. For a two-vertex order the first appended vertex initializes c with t=0. These transitions characterize NOR orders, and continuation feasibility is independent of the earlier ordering once this state is fixed.

Proposed topology interface, not a closure claim. Use a subdivision whose face vertices retain ordered prefixes or ordered tail data, rather than labeling support subsets alone. The local continuation labels should distinguish the no-switch state from the already-switched state and the two ordered tail coordinates. A simplex theorem or connector argument must then produce an actual sequence of compatible transitions, not a collection of separately attainable endpoints. Endpoint attainability alone has been ruled out as a sufficient extraction condition by the displayed five-coordinate example; the example is a failure of that selector method, not a NOR counterexample. No labeling with the required global boundary condition and extraction theorem is established here.

Concrete candidate labeling for the refined interface. At a fixed terminal state (a,b,c,t), take the simplex on the remaining coordinates. For each nonempty face R choose x=min R in an auxiliary fixed coordinate order and label its barycenter +x if t=0 or h(a,b,x)=c, and -x otherwise. The absolute label lies in R, so the face condition is automatic. The sign distinguishes a permissible continuation from a move creating a second change. On a positive move the next fiber has tail (b,x), last color h(a,b,x), and the updated switch count. This gives a concrete signed labeling to test in a connector construction. It is not yet a Tucker labeling: antipodal compatibility and the gluing between different terminal-state fibers are not established. Ignoring signs reduces to ordinary Sperner and loses the continuation condition. Those two requirements, plus an extraction of a compatible spanning path, are the outstanding proof obligations.
