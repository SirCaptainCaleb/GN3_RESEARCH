# A six-coordinate weave kills the flat antipodal backtrack — preserved pre-item development

Closure of the d=e=3 antipodal backtrack in the corrected arbitrary-scan flat dynamics. Let a=v_{p+1}, b=v_{p+2}, y=v_{p+3}, c=v_{p+4}, d=v_{p+5}, with omitted coordinate x. A distance-three right replacement means replacing y by x gives bridge 000: alpha(a,b,x)=0, alpha(b,x,c)=0, alpha(x,c,d)=0. The original carrier has the corresponding bridge 111: alpha(a,b,y)=alpha(b,y,c)=alpha(y,c,d)=1. Exact distance-three return implies the pair-crossing values c_t=alpha(x,y,t) alternate along t=a,b,c,d. If the central pair insertion x,y between b and c had c_b=0,c_c=1, the full local packet would be 0,0,1,1 between the old 0-prefix and 1-suffix, immediately closing NOR. Hence counterexamplehood forces c_b=1,c_c=0, and flat transport then gives c_a=0,c_d=1. Now consider the spanning order obtained by replacing the old block a,b,y,c,d with a,x,b,y,c,d, i.e. insert x between a and b while keeping y. Its internal statuses are alpha(a,x,b)=1, because alpha(a,b,x)=0; alpha(x,b,y)=alpha(b,y,x)=? More directly alpha(x,b,y)=alpha(b,y,x) by cyclic invariance, and alpha(b,x,y)=c_b=1, so alpha(x,b,y)=1 as the cyclic order of (b,x,y); alpha(b,y,c)=1; alpha(y,c,d)=1. Thus from alpha(a,x,b) onward the entire old 1-suffix is monochromatic 1. The only changed left reconnection is alpha(r,a,x), where r precedes a in the old 0-prefix. If it is 0, the new full order has the original 0-prefix followed by 0,1,1,...; if it is 1, the switch simply occurs one window earlier. Either way the full order has at most one change. Therefore the d=e=3 antipodal backtrack cannot occur in a counterexample. Combined with the five-set weave eliminating the d=e=2 A2 cycle and reversal-minimality terminating residual drift, there is no recurrent branch left in the arbitrary-scan flat replacement dynamics, subject only to handling the short p=2 or q=2 boundary case outside the p,q>=3 replacement theorem.
