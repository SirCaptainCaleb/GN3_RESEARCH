## Clarification: distinguish union closure from Frankl's conclusion

The current Article II results establish a connection between NOR and union-closure structure, not a reduction from the Union-Closed Sets Conjecture to NOR.

For a maximal monochromatic tight path, a minimal local fixed-tail obstruction has support family 2^U minus {U}. Thus it is one missing top away from union closure. If that missing union U were feasible, the corresponding tight witness would splice to the maximal path and close NOR locally.

This does **not** mean that Frankl's heavy-element conclusion supplies U or implies NOR. The Union-Closed Sets Conjecture assumes union closure and concludes that some element occurs in at least half the sets. Our punctured Boolean obstruction is not union-closed. Its coordinate frequencies are already forced by its shape: each coordinate occurs in 2^{|U|-1}-1 of the 2^{|U|}-1 sets, so its signed half-frequency bias is -1. That frequency fact is descriptive, not a mechanism for recovering the missing top.

Accordingly:
- supported: union closure of the relevant NOR support family would be strong enough to merge feasible branches and yield the missing witness;
- unsupported: the truth of Frankl's conjecture, by itself, implies NOR;
- unsupported: the heavy-element conclusion currently gives closure-relevant information beyond what is already forced by the punctured-cube structure.

Any future claim of a Frankl-to-NOR implication must exhibit a new argument that explicitly uses the heavy-element conclusion, rather than merely the definition of union closure.
