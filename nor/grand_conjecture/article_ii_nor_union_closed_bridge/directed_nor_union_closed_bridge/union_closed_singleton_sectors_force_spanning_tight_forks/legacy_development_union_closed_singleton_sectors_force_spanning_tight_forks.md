## Union-closed singleton sectors force spanning tight forks

Fix coordinate arity \(r\ge2\), a reversal-antisymmetric binary label \(h\), a color \(\sigma\in\{0,1\}\), and an ordered terminal \((r-1)\)-tuple \(S\). Write
\[
\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}=\{A:\text{some ordering of }A,S\text{ is }\sigma\text{-tight}\}
\]
and define its singleton sector
\[
X_{\sigma}(S)=\{x\notin S:h(x,S)=\sigma\}.
\]
Thus \({x}\in\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}\) exactly when \(x\in X_{\sigma}(S)\).

### Lemma 1: union closure saturates the singleton sector
If \(\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}\) is union-closed, then every subset of \(X_{\sigma}(S)\) belongs to \(\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}\).

Indeed every singleton \({x}\), \(x\in X_{\sigma}(S)\), is feasible, and any finite union of these singleton feasible sets is feasible. Hence
\[
2^{X_{\sigma}(S)}\subseteq\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}.
\]
In particular the whole sector \(X_{\sigma}(S)\) has a \(\sigma\)-tight ordering ending in \(S\).

This is the exact Frankl matching mechanism inside NOR support systems. More generally, for any nonempty accessible union-closed family, accessibility supplies a feasible singleton \({x}\), and the map \(A\mapsto A\cup\{x\}\) injects the sets omitting \(x\) into the sets containing \(x\). Thus \(x\) occurs in at least half the family. Here union closure yields the stronger Boolean-sector statement because every singleton in \(X_{\sigma}(S)\) is explicitly feasible.

### Theorem 2: paired union closure plus singleton-sector coverage closes directed NOR
Suppose both \(\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}\) and \(\mathcal F_{\sigma,S^{\rm rev}}\) are union-closed and
\[
X_{\sigma}(S)\cup X_{\sigma}(S^{\rm rev})=V\setminus S.
\tag{1}
\]
Then the directed NOR conclusion holds.

Choose a partition \(V\setminus S=A\dot\cup B\) with
\[
A\subseteq X_{\sigma}(S),\qquad B\subseteq X_{\sigma}(S^{\rm rev});
\]
this is possible by (1), assigning coordinates in the overlap arbitrarily. By Lemma 1, \(A\in\mathcal F_{\sigma,S}\) and \(B\in\mathcal F_{\sigma,S^{\rm rev}}\). Hence there are \(\sigma\)-tight branches \(A,S\) and \(B,S^{\rm rev}\) whose vertices outside the common center partition the ground set. They form a spanning converging tight fork, so there is a spanning coordinate order whose color word changes at most once. \(□\)

### Corollary 3: the residual obstruction is either a square defect or a polarized pair
Fix \(S\) in a counterexample. If all four support families
\[
\mathcal F_{0,S},\quad\mathcal F_{1,S},\quad\mathcal F_{0,S^{\rm rev}},\quad\mathcal F_{1,S^{\rm rev}}
\]
are union-closed, then there are exterior vertices \(x_0,x_1\notin S\) satisfying
\[
h(x_0,S)=h(x_0,S^{\rm rev})=0,\qquad
h(x_1,S)=h(x_1,S^{\rm rev})=1.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed Theorem 2 cannot apply with \(\sigma=0\), so some exterior vertex lies outside both zero singleton sectors; this is \(x_1\). Likewise failure for \(\sigma=1\) gives \(x_0\).

By reversal antisymmetry, (2) also gives
\[
h(S,x_1)=0,\qquad h(S,x_0)=1.
\]
Consequently
\[
(x_0,S,x_1)\text{ is a two-window }0\text{-tight path},
\qquad
(x_1,S,x_0)\text{ is a two-window }1\text{-tight path}.
\]
Call \(x_0,x_1\) an opposite polar pair for the center \(S\).

Thus every center in a counterexample satisfies a sharp dichotomy: either one of its four oriented/color feasible-support families fails union closure, hence has a minimal top-missing square with the antimatroid side structure already proved in Article I, or the center carries an opposite polar pair and therefore lies inside monochromatic tight bridges of both colors.

### Significance
This converts Article II's Frankl analogy into a direct NOR mechanism. Union closure does not merely provide a frequent coordinate: it fills the entire Boolean algebra generated by feasible singleton extensions. The only remaining obstruction to turning the two oriented antimatroids into a spanning fork is failure of singleton-sector coverage. If coverage fails for both colors, the failure itself produces an opposite polar pair and a rigid two-window monochromatic bridge through the center.

A closure route can therefore split cleanly into two cases: eliminate minimal top-missing squares by witness synchronization, or propagate/recenter the opposite polar bridges until a singleton-sector cover appears.
