## Development

Exact braid-hexagon normal form for the flat antipodal root pair. In the d=e=3 backtrack use local coordinates a,b,x,y,c,d with x-bridge 000, y-bridge 111, and forced pair-crossing values alpha(x,y,a),alpha(x,y,b),alpha(x,y,c),alpha(x,y,d)=0,1,0,1. Keep a before the protected triple and c,d after it. For the six permutations of (b,x,y), the four local statuses are: bxy -> 0101; byx -> 1010; xby -> 1011; xyb -> 010t; ybx -> 0100; yxb -> 101t, where t=alpha(b,c,d). Hence every vertex of the A2 braid hexagon has at least two changes, independently of t. The antipodal pair is therefore a genuine caged Coxeter residue, not a missed local permutation. However it has two canonical protected exits obtained by moving one residual coordinate across an outer boundary: the left bubble order x,a,b,y,c,d has internal word 0111, while the right bubble order a,b,x,c,d,y has internal word 0001. The left exit preserves the ordered right boundary pair (c,d) and exports uncertainty only through the two left reconnection windows; the right exit preserves the left boundary pair (a,b) and exports uncertainty only through the two right reconnection windows. Thus the antipodal Radon two-cycle is exactly a Hex-type carrier: no good chamber in the braid hexagon, with two opposite threshold-compatible boundary exits. A closure argument should connect these exits through adjacent protected cells or show that repeated exported uncertainty reaches an endpoint. This is also the natural base cell for the proposed blow-up of inert protected exchanges.
