## Development

Let O=(v_1,...,v_m) be a one-change deletion order in the coboundary-flat pure-orientation sector with word 0^p1^q, p,q>=2, and let x be an omitted vertex which is a perfect insertion blocker for O. The unique blocker scan is s_i=alpha(x,v_i,v_{i+1})=1^(p+1)0^q. Prepending x gives the endpoint-singleton word 1,0^p,1^q. On the first tetrahedron Q={x,v_1,v_2,v_3}, the consecutive face values are alpha(x,v_1,v_2)=1 and alpha(v_1,v_2,v_3)=0, while the scan gives alpha(x,v_2,v_3)=1. Coboundary flatness on Q forces alpha(x,v_1,v_3)=0. Thus the four faces have the fully-curved pattern 1,0,1,0 (with the two appropriate opposite face pairs), so Q is fully curved. Therefore in the flat sector a perfect blocker necessarily creates a universal-switch barrier at the endpoint where it is prepended. The reversed/appended statement is analogous. Combined with the minimum-counterexample threshold-defect-one normal form, every codimension-one deletion witness carries a canonical full-curvature endpoint barrier; this is the local geometric meaning of perfect insertion blocking.
