# A singleton threshold defect shifts right unless its curvature type is full-flat — preserved pre-item development


Work in the coboundary-flat ternary sector on five consecutive coordinates 0,1,2,3,4 with local threshold-defect pattern

alpha(0,1,2)=0,
alpha(1,2,3)=1,
alpha(2,3,4)=0.

Thus the middle status is a singleton wrong-color defect inside a constant-0 target side. Let Q_L={0,1,2,3} and Q_R={1,2,3,4} be its two transition tetrahedra.

There are four curvature possibilities.

If Q_R is fully curved, then its face pattern gives

alpha(1,2,4)=0.

Swapping the last two coordinates gives the order

(0,1,2,4,3)

with statuses

alpha(0,1,2)=0,
alpha(1,2,4)=0,
alpha(2,4,3)=1.

Hence the defect moves one rank to the right. This works whether Q_L is flat or fully curved.

If both Q_L and Q_R are flat, then Q_L gives alpha(0,1,3)=0. Swapping the middle pair gives

(0,1,3,2,4)

with statuses

alpha(0,1,3)=0,
alpha(1,3,2)=0,
alpha(3,2,4)=1,

so again the defect moves one rank to the right.

Therefore three of the four curvature types admit a rightward one-sided transport preserving the ordered left boundary pair (0,1):

flat/full,
full/full,
flat/flat.

The only local curvature type not covered is

full/flat.

Thus a right-moving singleton threshold defect can be locally blocked only when the transition behind it is fully curved and the transition ahead of it is flat.

This isolates the exact residual one-particle transport obstruction in the flat sector.
