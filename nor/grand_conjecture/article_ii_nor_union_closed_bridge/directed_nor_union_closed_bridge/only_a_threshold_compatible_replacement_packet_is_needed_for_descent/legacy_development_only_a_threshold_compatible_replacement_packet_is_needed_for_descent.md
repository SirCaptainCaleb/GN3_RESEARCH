# Only a threshold-compatible replacement packet is needed for descent — preserved pre-item development

## Development

Sharpening of the general protected-descent target. In the setup of the protected replacement O'=(v_1,...,v_{p-1},x,v_{p+1},...,v_m), let B_1,...,B_r be the r new windows crossing x, in their natural left-to-right order. It is unnecessary to prove B_1=...=B_r=1. It suffices that the bridge packet is threshold-compatible: B=0^a1^(r-a) for some a<r. Then O' has word exactly 0^(p-r+a)1^(q+r-a), hence is a genuine one-change deletion carrier and its first run has decreased by r-a>=1. The full order obtained by moving v_p to the front differs from O' by one initial window; if that window is 0, NOR closes, while in a counterexample it is forced to 1. Deleting v_p leaves the strictly improved carrier O'. Iterating any rule that supplies such a compatible packet therefore terminates. Thus the general coordinate conjecture reduces to a weaker protected bridge lemma: from a minimum-counterexample deletion carrier, extract a replacement packet whose colors change at most once in the SAME 0-to-1 orientation and which contains at least one second-phase window. The flat ternary proof realizes the extreme case a=0. This is exactly the local carrier-to-path statement suggested by the second wisdom pass: preserve the deletion order of every other coordinate and extract phase compatibility only across the r crossing windows. A monochromatic packet or a global spanning carrier is unnecessary.
