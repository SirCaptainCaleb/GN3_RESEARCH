# Perfect blockers create an almost complete full curvature tube — preserved pre-item development

## Perfect blockers create an almost-complete full-curvature tube

Work in a minimum coboundary-flat pure-orientation ternary counterexample. Let
[
O=(v_1,ldots,v_m)
]
be a one-change deletion carrier with status word
[
w_i=alpha(v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2})=0^p1^q,
qquad p,qge3,
]
and let (x) be the omitted perfect blocker. Its scan is
[
s_i=alpha(x,v_i,v_{i+1})=1^{p+1}0^q.
]

For each (i) with (1le ile m-2), consider
[
Q_i={x,v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}}.
]

### Theorem

Every (Q_i) is fully curved except possibly the unique central tetrahedron (Q_{p+1}).

More precisely:
- for (1le ile p), (Q_i) is fully curved;
- for (p+2le ile p+q), (Q_i) is fully curved.

### Proof

For (ile p),
[
s_i=1,qquad w_i=0,qquad s_{i+1}=1.
]
Thus in the ordered quadruple
[
(x,v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2})
]
the two consecutive statuses are opposite, (1,0), while the face
[
alpha(x,v_{i+1},v_{i+2})=s_{i+1}=1
]
equals the first status. In a coboundary-flat transition tetrahedron, this is exactly the fully-curved alternative; the flat alternative would make that face equal the second status.

For (ige p+2), the scan is already in its zero phase while the deletion word is in its one phase:
[
s_i=0,qquad w_i=1,qquad s_{i+1}=0.
]
The identical criterion gives full curvature again.

Only (i=p+1) is exceptional:
[
s_{p+1}=1,qquad w_{p+1}=1,qquad s_{p+2}=0,
]
so the first two displayed faces do not form a transition and the preceding criterion does not apply.

### Off-diagonal face values

On every full tube tetrahedron the remaining face is forced:
[
alpha(x,v_i,v_{i+2})=w_i.
]
Indeed for a fully-curved ordered quadruple whose consecutive colors are (s_i, w_i=1-s_i), the opposite face (alpha(x,v_i,v_{i+2})) equals the second color (w_i).

At the exceptional index, coboundary flatness alone gives
[
alpha(x,v_{p+1},v_{p+3})
=
s_{p+1}oplus s_{p+2}oplus w_{p+1}
=
0.
]

### Significance

A perfect blocker is not merely accompanied by one fully-curved endpoint tetrahedron. It creates a full-curvature tube along the entire deletion carrier, broken at only one central four-set adjacent to the genuine switch.

Thus the flat-sector closure problem can be recast as joining two long full-curvature tubes across one exceptional central packet. Any global splice or interval-reversal argument may use the forced off-diagonal identities on both sides instead of treating remote reconnection triangles as free.
