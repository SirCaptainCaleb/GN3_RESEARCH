# Every double-full singleton has two one-sided monotone resolutions

## Composition

A 010 packet bounded by fully curved tetrahedra has two one-sided resolutions, 001 preserving the left boundary pair or 100 preserving the right. The exported reconnection risk is handled by threshold-band combing, which closes or reaches a fully-curved protected root. Local passage is not global root extraction.

## Development

Let five consecutive coordinates (0,1,2,3,4) have ternary word 010, and assume both transition tetrahedra are fully curved. Full curvature of the left 0-to-1 transition forces alpha(0,2,3)=0. Full curvature of the right 1-to-0 transition forces alpha(1,2,4)=0. Therefore swapping only the last pair gives the order (0,1,2,4,3), whose statuses are alpha(0,1,2)=0, alpha(1,2,4)=0, and alpha(2,4,3)=1-alpha(2,3,4)=1. Its word is 001 and the ordered left boundary pair is unchanged. Symmetrically, swapping only the first pair gives (1,0,2,3,4), with statuses 1,0,0, hence word 100, and the ordered right boundary pair is unchanged. Thus every double-full singleton admits a one-change local resolution exporting all reconnection risk to either chosen side. The residual five-set holonomy bit is irrelevant for this one-sided monotone resolution; it only controls stronger two-sided monochromatic passage. Combining either resolution with threshold-band combing on the exported side gives a finite dichotomy: reach a globally one-change state, or stop at a fully-curved protected-root barrier.
