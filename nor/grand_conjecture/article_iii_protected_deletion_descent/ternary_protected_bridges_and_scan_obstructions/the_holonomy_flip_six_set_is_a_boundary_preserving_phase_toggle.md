# The holonomy-flip six-set is a boundary-preserving phase toggle

## Composition

Two full-support orders at the special holonomy flip, (x,a,b,d,c,e) and (x,a,d,b,c,e), have internal words 1111 and 0000 and share exactly the same ordered attachment pairs (x,a) and (c,e). Hence replacing one by the other changes only the four internal statuses and leaves every exterior-crossing ternary window unchanged.

This is a genuine boundary-preserving phase toggle relative to the new attachment pairs. It does not say those pairs coincide with the original carrier pairs; the old-boundary preservation claim is separately false.

## Development

Boundary-preserving phase toggle at a holonomy flip. Combine the color-1 weave (x,a,b,d,c,e) from the preceding holonomy-flip subsection with the color-0 weave (x,a,d,b,c,e) from the full-support zero-exit calculation. The first order has internal status word 1111; the second has 0000. Crucially, both orders have exactly the same ordered first pair (x,a) and the same ordered last pair (c,e). Therefore replacing one weave by the other inside any larger coordinate order leaves every ternary window meeting the exterior unchanged: all windows wholly outside are untouched, the two left reconnection windows depend only on the ordered pair (x,a), and the two right reconnection windows depend only on (c,e). Only the four internal statuses change, and they change simultaneously from 1111 to 0000 or vice versa. Thus every 1-to-0 holonomy interface in the special perfect-blocker tube contains a boundary-preserving binary phase-toggle gadget on six physical coordinates. Any obstruction persisting for both toggle choices is therefore purely an exterior reconnection obstruction; it cannot use the internal curvature or the residual old five-set bit. In threshold-band language, whenever the six-block lies entirely on a constant-color side of a proposed target, choose the matching toggle value and the entire four-window interior becomes compatible without altering either boundary packet. This isolates the remaining difficulty to the two fixed reconnection packets adjacent to the common boundary pairs.
