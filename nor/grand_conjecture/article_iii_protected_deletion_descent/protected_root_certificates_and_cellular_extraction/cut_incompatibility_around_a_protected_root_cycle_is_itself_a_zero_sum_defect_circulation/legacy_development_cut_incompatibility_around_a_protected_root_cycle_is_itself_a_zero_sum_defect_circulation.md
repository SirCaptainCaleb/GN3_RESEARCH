# Cut incompatibility around a protected root cycle is itself a zero-sum defect circulation — preserved pre-item development

Let rho_i=e_{x_{i+1}}-e_{x_i} be a directed physical protected-root cycle with cuts L_i. Canonical orientation gives x_{i+1} in L_i and x_i outside L_i, while the preceding root gives x_i in L_{i-1}. The root rho_i determines the predecessor cut that would make the physical cycle a literal Johnson exchange: hat L_{i-1}=L_i minus {x_{i+1}} plus {x_i}. Define the cut-defect vector D_i=1_{L_{i-1}}-1_{hat L_{i-1}}=1_{L_{i-1}}-1_{L_i}+rho_i. Then sum_i D_i=0 because the cut-indicator differences telescope and sum_i rho_i=0. Moreover D_i=0 exactly when the desired cycle exchange L_{i-1} to L_i replaces x_i by x_{i+1}. Each nonzero D_i is a difference of two p-cuts and decomposes into Johnson edge vectors; its half L1 norm is the Johnson distance d_J(L_{i-1},hat L_{i-1}). Hence Delta=sum_i d_J(L_{i-1},hat L_{i-1}) is an integer measure of failure of cut/root compatibility. A closure proof may minimize Delta among protected root cycles: Delta=0 gives the required composable Johnson cycle, while Delta>0 supplies a secondary zero-sum family of cut-exchange defects that should yield a smaller carrier or a threshold improvement. This reframes the first-ejection problem as an induction on cut defect rather than a coordinate-choice assertion.
