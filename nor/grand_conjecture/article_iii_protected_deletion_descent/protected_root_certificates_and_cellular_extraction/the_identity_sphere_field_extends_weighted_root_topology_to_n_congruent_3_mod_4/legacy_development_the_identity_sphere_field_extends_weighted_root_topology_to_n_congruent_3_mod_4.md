## The identity sphere field extends the formal weighted-field obstruction to n congruent 3 mod 4

### Transversality lemma

For every (xin S(W)),
[
x
otinoperatorname{span}{F_{10}(x),F_{01}(x)}.
]

Let (M_-) and (M_+) be the nonempty sets of coordinates attaining the global minimum and maximum of (x). Every nonzero summand of either weighted field is a middle simple root
[
e_{v_{i+1}}-e_{v_{i+2}}.
]
Such a surviving term has zero coordinate on every member of (M_-cup M_+). If an extremum is unique it occupies rank 1 or n and is never a middle-root endpoint. If it belongs to a tied extremal block and could occupy a middle-root rank, one of the tied adjacent gaps occurs in the local vanishing weight, so that term is zero. Hence the entire span of the two weighted fields vanishes on (M_-cup M_+).

Since (x
eq0) is centered, its minimum is strictly negative and its maximum strictly positive. Thus (x) does not lie in that span.

Consequently, wherever (F_{10},F_{01}) are independent, the triple
[
(I,F_{10},F_{01})
]
is a pointwise 3-frame.

### Formal projective obstruction

All three fields are odd. If they formed a 3-frame everywhere, they would descend to three independent sections of
[

]

[

### Audit qualification

This is a correct characteristic-class extension, but it is **not** a physical-root extraction theorem. The subsequent gap-weight audit proves that (F_{10}) and (F_{01}) have coloring-independent common zeros on cooriented braid faces. Therefore the forced failure of the formal 3-frame may occur entirely on that universal zero locus and supplies no new coloring-sensitive obstruction by itself.

Accordingly this result should be used only as topology infrastructure: it shows that adjoining geometric odd sections such as the identity field can change the relevant Stiefel--Whitney parity class once a zero-free, coloring-sensitive carrier is available. The actual-root face carrier and its fixed-point alternative are the current extraction route.
