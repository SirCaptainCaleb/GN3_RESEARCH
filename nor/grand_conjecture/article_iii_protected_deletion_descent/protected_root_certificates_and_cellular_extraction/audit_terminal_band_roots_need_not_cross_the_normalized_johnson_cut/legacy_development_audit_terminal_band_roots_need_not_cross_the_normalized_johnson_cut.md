## Development

Article III currently uses two distinct classes of protected physical roots, and they must not be conflated.

A canonical switch root comes from reinserting the omitted coordinate at the switch of a one-change deletion witness. The switch-root theorem proves that its two consecutive ternary windows both contain the inserted coordinate and that the dropped endpoint lies strictly on the left of the normalized phase cut while the entering endpoint lies strictly on the right. Therefore this root genuinely crosses the fixed p-cut. The cut-projection and Johnson/hypersimplex theorems apply to this class.

A terminal threshold-band root is different. Let the maximal target-compatible band have a fully-curved boundary supported by four consecutive coordinates (a,b,c,d). The boundary transition gives the actual slide root e_a-e_d. If the boundary has already been combed outward away from the target switch, all four coordinates may lie strictly on the same side of the original p-cut. In that case the root does not cross the normalized phase cut at all.

Hence the data (rho, original p-cut, band side) are not automatically a faithful Johnson-edge state for an arbitrary terminal barrier. Root 43 is valid only under its explicit hypothesis that the root endpoints lie on opposite sides of the stated cut. Root 17 likewise concerns the fixed-cut canonical carrier. The side-lifted barrier theorem 36 applies to terminal band roots but does not itself supply a cut they cross.

A four-coordinate slide root e_a-e_d does cross each physical rank cut lying between the positions of a and d, but choosing one of those local cuts is extra provenance. In particular the physical root alone and the left/right band-side bit do not determine which local cut should be used, nor do they identify it with the original minimum-phase cut.

Therefore the local-to-global handoff needs an explicit bridge theorem of one of the following forms:

1. transport a terminal fully-curved barrier root back to a canonical switch root while preserving the protected witness;
2. attach to each terminal barrier an actual local cut crossed by the root, and formulate the global carrier on a graded family of Johnson layers rather than one fixed p-layer; or
3. prove that in the globally extremal state relevant to extraction every terminal barrier necessarily lies adjacent enough to the threshold that its root crosses the normalized phase cut.

Until such a theorem is proved, terminal barrier roots may be used in physical root-space circuits and side-lifted arguments, but not automatically in fixed-p cut moments, Johnson successors, short-shore potentials, or hypersimplex-edge realizability.

This audit sharpens the present frontier: the missing theorem is not only realization of a forced Johnson neighbor. It is first the acquisition or preservation of legitimate cut-crossing provenance for the roots produced by threshold-band termination.
