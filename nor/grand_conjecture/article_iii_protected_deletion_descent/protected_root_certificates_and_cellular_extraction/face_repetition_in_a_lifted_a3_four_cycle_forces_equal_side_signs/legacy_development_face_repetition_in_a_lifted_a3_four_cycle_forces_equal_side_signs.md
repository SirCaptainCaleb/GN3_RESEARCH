## Face repetition in a lifted A3 four-cycle forces equal side signs

Consider an honest side-lifted positive zero whose physical support is a directed simple four-cycle

u_0 -> u_1 -> u_2 -> u_3 -> u_0

inside one ternary A3 block.

Each lifted root is carried by an ordered ternary window, hence by one triangular facet of the four-coordinate tetrahedron. The carrying facet contains the two root endpoints plus the window middle coordinate.

### Lemma

If two cycle edges are carried by the same triangular facet, then they are adjacent edges of the physical four-cycle and their side signs are equal.

### Proof

Two nonadjacent edges of a simple four-cycle have four distinct endpoints, so no three-element facet can contain both. Thus any two roots on one facet are adjacent cycle edges, say

u -> v,
v -> w.

On the physical triangle {u,v,w}, these two directed edges belong to the same directed 3-cycle orientation: the cycle containing u->v and v->w is

u -> v -> w -> u.

By the face-orientation theorem, all violating roots on one fixed side of the switch occupy one directed triangle orientation of that face, while the opposite side occupies the reverse orientation. Since u->v and v->w lie in the same orientation class, their violation side signs must be equal.

QED.

### Consequences for a side-balanced four-cycle

A balanced four-cycle has two + and two - labels.

- If the side pattern alternates +,-,+,- around the physical cycle, no adjacent pair has equal sign. Hence NO carrying facet can repeat. The four labels occupy the four distinct facets of the A3 tetrahedron, one label per facet.

- If the side pattern is ++-- up to cyclic rotation/reversal, any repeated facet can occur only on one of the two same-sign adjacent pairs. Opposite-sign adjacencies necessarily use different facets.

Thus the alternating balanced four-cycle has an especially rigid one-label-per-facet normal form.
