## Honest lifted circuits have physical cycle rank exactly two

Work with a support-minimal positive dependence of honest ternary switch-prism labels
[
sum_{ein E}lambda_e(ho_e,s_e)=0,qquad lambda_e>0,
]
where (ho_e=e_{u_e}-e_{v_e}) is a directed type-A root and (s_ein{pm1}).

Assume this is genuinely multi-cycle, so no directed physical cycle contained in the support has side sum zero; otherwise that cycle alone gives a proper positive lifted subdependence.

Let (G) be the directed multigraph formed by the physical roots in the support. Let (U) be its physical vertex set and let (c) be the number of weakly connected components.

### Rank calculation

The physical incidence columns (ho_e) have rank
[
|U|-c.
]

The side row (s=(s_e)) is NOT in the row span of the physical incidence matrix. Indeed, a row-span vector is a vertex potential difference on edges and therefore has zero sum around every directed cycle. But by the genuine multi-cycle assumption every directed cycle occurring in the positive circulation decomposition has nonzero side sum.

Therefore adjoining the side coordinate raises the column rank by exactly one:
[
operatorname{rank}{(ho_e,s_e):ein E}=|U|-c+1.
]

Because the displayed dependence is support-minimal, its columns form a linear circuit. Hence
[
|E|=operatorname{rank}+1=|U|-c+2.
]

Thus the physical support graph has cyclomatic number
[
eta(G)=|E|-|U|+c=2.
]

### Positivity removes bridges

The physical equation
[
sum_elambda_eho_e=0,qquad lambda_e>0,
]
is a strictly positive circulation. Every support edge therefore lies on a directed cycle. In particular the underlying support graph has no bridge.

So a genuinely multi-cycle support-minimal honest lifted zero is a bridgeless graph of total cycle rank exactly two.

### Graph classification

This leaves exactly the standard bicyclic possibilities componentwise.

1. If (c=2), each component has cycle rank one and no bridges. Hence each component is a simple cycle (allowing the usual two-edge multigraph degeneracy if parallel physical roots occur). Thus the support is two disjoint cycle components.

2. If (c=1), the support is connected, bridgeless, and bicyclic. After suppressing degree-two vertices its core is one of:
   - a figure-eight: two cycles meeting at one articulation vertex;
   - a theta graph: two branch vertices joined by three internally disjoint paths.

No other connected shape is possible: a barbell/handcuff with a connecting path has bridge edges, forbidden by the positive circulation.

### Audit of the earlier two-cycle intersection statement

Subsection 188 correctly proves that a multi-cycle support-minimal lifted zero can be generated by two physical cycle flows of opposite side imbalance. However its later full-support intersection count implicitly treats those two cycle supports as edge-disjoint.

That is valid for the disconnected case and for a figure-eight decomposition, but it misses the theta possibility, where two generating directed cycles share one whole path. Counting (|A|+|B|) as the number of support labels then overcounts the shared path.

The safe global statement is therefore the cycle-rank classification above, not merely “two disjoint cycles or two cycles meeting in one vertex.”

### Full-support dimension count

If (U=V) has (n) physical coordinates:

- disconnected two-cycle support has (c=2), hence (|E|=n). Its lifted span has dimension (n-1) inside the (n)-dimensional target (Woplusmathbb R), so it still has a transverse direction available in principle;

- connected figure-eight or theta support has (c=1), hence (|E|=n+1). Its lifted columns form a full-dimensional circuit in the (n)-dimensional honest target. This is the genuine degree-saturated multi-cycle analogue of the bare Hamiltonian simplex.

Thus the final honest-lifted topology should split at connectedness:
the disconnected two-cycle case is a transverse-label problem, whereas the connected figure-eight/theta cases require combinatorial extraction or additional provenance, since bare side-lift dimension alone cannot remove their local degree.
