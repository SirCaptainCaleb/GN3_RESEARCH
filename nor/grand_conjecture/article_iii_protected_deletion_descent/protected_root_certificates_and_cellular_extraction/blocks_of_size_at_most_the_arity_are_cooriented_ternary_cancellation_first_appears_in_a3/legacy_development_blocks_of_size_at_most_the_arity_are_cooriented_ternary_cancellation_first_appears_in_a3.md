## Development

Coxeter blocks of size at most the window arity are strictly cooriented. Consider a permutohedral face obtained by freely permuting one consecutive block B of b coordinates while all exterior coordinates retain fixed positions, and let the ordered-window arity be r. Assume b<=r. Average the rank functions over all b! chambers of the face. Every coordinate of B then has the same averaged rank mu, the midpoint of the block positions, while every exterior coordinate keeps its fixed rank. An actual slide root has endpoints r positions apart in its carrying chamber. Because b<=r, both endpoints cannot lie in B: the diameter of the block is b-1<r. If neither endpoint lies in B, its averaged rank gap remains exactly r. If the earlier endpoint lies in B and the later endpoint is exterior, the smallest possible averaged gap is r-(b-1)/2>0; symmetrically the same bound holds when the entering endpoint lies in B. Hence one averaged-rank functional is strictly positive on every actual window-slide root from every chamber of the entire face. No positive physical-root dependence can be supported in such a Coxeter block. Conversely, when b=r+1 the face already contains opposite slide roots: for any two block coordinates a,b, choose a chamber placing a first and b last in the block, yielding e_a-e_b as the length-r slide root across the block, and the chamber with b first and a last yields e_b-e_a. Thus block size r+1 is the first size at which strict coorientation is impossible. In ternary arity r=3, every A2 face (three tied coordinates, six chambers) is zero-free for positive physical slide roots; the first possible cancellation face is an A3 block of four tied coordinates. This supersedes the suggestion that a six-chamber A2 hexagon is the first plausible physical cancellation cell.
