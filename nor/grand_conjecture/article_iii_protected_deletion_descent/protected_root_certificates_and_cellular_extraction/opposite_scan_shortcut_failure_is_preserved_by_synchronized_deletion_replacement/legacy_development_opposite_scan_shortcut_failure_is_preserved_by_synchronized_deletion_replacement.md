## Opposite-scan shortcut failure is preserved by synchronized deletion replacement

Work in the surviving shortcut residue of §§299–301. Normalize a good middle order
O=(w_1,...,w_m)
with word 0^p1^q so that
xO
and

By §287 applied to these two deletion witnesses,

The omitted coordinate z blocks every insertion into either witness, since any successful insertion would be a spanning NOR order.

The unique switch of xO and Ox is the same physical switch inside O. The z-insertion scan on every adjacent pair in the switch collar is also the same for the two carriers: the extra coordinate x sits at the far left in xO and at the far right in Ox, outside the replacement packet.

Therefore the arbitrary-scan replacement theorem chooses the same branch and the same physical replacement coordinate in both carriers. If it replaces a coordinate y of O by z, the outputs have the form

and
O' x,
where O' is the same coordinate order on V minus {x,y} in both states.

Both outputs are genuine one-change deletion witnesses, now omitting y. The coordinate x remains literally fixed at the left endpoint of the first witness and at the right endpoint of the second.

Thus the property
P(x;y): there exists one common middle order M on V minus {x,y} such that xM and Mx are both NOR-good deletion witnesses omitting y
is invariant under every successful arbitrary-scan replacement of the omitted coordinate.

### Consequence

Starting from a failed shortcut pair (x,z) in the opposite-scan residue, the entire flat replacement trajectory can be run synchronously while preserving x at both possible global endpoints.

At each stage only the other omitted coordinate changes. Hence the unresolved shortcut dynamics is a finite-state motion on the second coordinate y with x fixed, until either:
- a spanning NOR order is produced; or
- the audited replacement dynamics exits through a fully-curved protected root.

In particular no successful flat replacement destroys the endpoint flexibility created by shortcut failure.
