## A fully curved ten barrier has a canonical zero deletion shadow

Work in the coboundary-flat alternating ternary sector. Let four consecutive coordinates

(u,v,w,z)

carry a fully-curved 10 transition:

alpha(u,v,w)=1,
alpha(v,w,z)=0.

For a fully-curved 1->0 tetrahedron the two off-faces are

alpha(u,v,z)=0,
alpha(u,w,z)=1.

### Canonical deletion shadow

Delete the THIRD coordinate w from the four-coordinate packet.

The two consecutive transition windows

(u,v,w), (v,w,z)

are replaced by the single ternary window

(u,v,z),

whose color is

alpha(u,v,z)=0.

Thus deleting w replaces the local word

10

by the single target-zero status

0.

By contrast, deleting the second coordinate v produces the off-face

alpha(u,w,z)=1.

Hence the two interior deletions are canonically distinguished by the curvature pattern: the third vertex is the zero-shadow deletion, the second is the one-shadow deletion.

### Fixed-phase consequence

Suppose the fully-curved 10 barrier lies entirely inside a phase whose desired color is 0, and all windows outside the four-coordinate packet already agree with the target.

Then, after deleting w, every surviving window inherited from the old order still agrees with the target and the collapsed barrier window also agrees.

So on the order with BOTH the old omitted coordinate and w removed, the full barrier disappears completely without creating a new local target defect.
