## Development

Quotient coorientation of the two residual antipodal exits. In the only caged exact-backtrack state from the protected-exit theorem, use outside order ...q,r,a,b,[x,y braid],c,d,e,f,... . The left canonical exit has exported packet alpha(q,r,x)=1, alpha(r,x,a)=0, hence its protected 10 descent carries root rho_L=e_q-e_a. The right canonical exit has alpha(d,y,e)=1, alpha(y,e,f)=0, hence its protected 10 descent carries root rho_R=e_d-e_f. Both roots avoid the inert exchanged coordinates x,y, so their classes remain nonzero in the quotient W/<e_x-e_y>. Moreover they are strictly cooriented there. Define a linear functional phi on physical coordinates with phi(x)=phi(y), phi(q)>phi(a), and phi(d)>phi(f); choose all other values arbitrarily and subtract the mean to regard phi as a functional on the type-A root space. Because phi(x)=phi(y), phi descends to the quotient by e_x-e_y. Then phi(rho_L)>0 and phi(rho_R)>0. Thus the two protected boundary exits of the antipodal braid cell lie in one open halfspace of the quotient root space and cannot themselves support a positive Radon dependence. Consequently an antipodal two-cycle zero cannot simply reappear on its two canonical boundary exits after the codimension-one quotient. Any compatible quotient zero must involve an additional protected cell/root outside this caged braid packet. This is the local algebraic base case needed by the proposed blow-up/inductive carrier strategy: the inert normal root is removed, and its two actual protected exits are nonzero and cooriented in the link.
