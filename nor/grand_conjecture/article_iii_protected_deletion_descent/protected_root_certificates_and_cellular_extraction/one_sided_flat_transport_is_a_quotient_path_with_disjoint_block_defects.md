# One-sided flat transport is a quotient path with disjoint block defects

## Composition

An audited one-sided flat transport has strictly nested central cuts. Their difference blocks turn its physical roots into the edges of a directed path after quotienting within-block differences. Distance-three steps contribute defects in pairwise disjoint blocks. The quotient path roots are linearly independent, so a single transport flag supports no positive root dependence. A forced topological zero must involve interactions between distinct realized trajectories.

## Development

## One-sided flat transport is a quotient path with disjoint block defects

Work in the alternating ternary sector along one audited rightward flat-transport trajectory. Let

C_0 proper-subset C_1 proper-subset ... proper-subset C_m

be the canonical central cuts of the successive transported barriers, as in the central-cut chain theorem. Define the ordered partition blocks

D_0=C_0,
D_j=C_j minus C_{j-1} for 1<=j<=m,
D_{m+1}=V minus C_m.

Let the barrier root at stage j be

rho_j=e_{s_j}-e_{t_j}.

Then

s_j in D_j,
t_j in D_{j+1}.

Indeed the source endpoint of the new barrier lies among the coordinates added when the central cut advances, while its target lies beyond the new cut.

For a distance-two transport, the old target and the next source coincide:
t_{j-1}=s_j.

For a distance-three transport, t_{j-1} and s_j are two distinct coordinates of the same new block D_j.

Hence after quotienting physical coordinates by identifying all coordinates inside each D_j, every rho_j becomes the path root

e_{D_j}-e_{D_{j+1}}.

Thus the entire one-sided transport trajectory projects to the literal directed path

D_0 -> D_1 -> ... -> D_{m+1}

on the ordered-partition quotient.

### Exact block-defect identity

For each internal block put

delta_j=e_{s_j}-e_{t_{j-1}},
1<=j<=m.

Then telescoping gives

sum_{j=0}^m rho_j
=
e_{s_0}-e_{t_m}
+
sum_{j=1}^m delta_j.

A distance-two transport has delta_j=0. A distance-three transport has nonzero delta_j supported entirely inside D_j.

The nonzero delta_j have pairwise disjoint supports because the transport blocks D_j are disjoint.

Therefore a transport trajectory splits exactly into:

1. one global quotient path from D_0 to D_{m+1};
2. independent within-block defects, one for each distance-three transport.

### Linear independence

The roots rho_0,...,rho_m are linearly independent.

Proof: quotient the type-A root space by the subspace generated by all within-block differences e_u-e_v with u,v in one D_j. The images of rho_j are the path roots e_{D_j}-e_{D_{j+1}}. These are linearly independent: in any relation, the D_0 coordinate forces the first coefficient to vanish, then successively every coefficient vanishes.

Hence no nontrivial positive root dependence can be supported inside one one-sided flat-transport trajectory.

### Consequence for topology

Any Radon/fixed-point zero involving transport roots must mix at least two distinct transport trajectories or occur where trajectories branch, rejoin, or interact through a local braid/commuting residue. A single monotone transport flag is zero-free.

This localizes the remaining topological coherence problem away from arbitrary long transport chains and toward the gluing cells between such chains.
