# Audit: raw antipodal terminal-root separation is vacuous without an orientation slice — preserved pre-item development

## Development

The graded central-cut model for terminal barriers is valid, but its finite-separation branch needs one qualification.

If the terminal-state family is closed under complement-reversal, then every state (C,rho) occurs together with (C^c,-rho). Hence the raw physical-root family automatically contains the positive two-term dependence
rho+(-rho)=0.
Therefore Gordan/Farkas applied to the FULL antipodal family can never yield a functional w with w(rho)>0 for every terminal root. The positive-dependence branch is then automatic and may carry no closure information: it can be merely a reversal pair.

Consequently the global cut potential in the graded cube must be used in one of three non-vacuous ways.

1. Orientation slice. Choose one representative from each antipodal pair by an independently defined orientation rule, then apply separation only to that slice. The orientation rule must be compatible with the local closure argument; arbitrary sign choices are useless.

2. Lifted labels. Retain an additional odd provenance coordinate, such as the established barrier-side lift, so that same geometric reversal pairs are not automatically a physically trivial cancellation in the quantity being separated.

3. Quotient/even observable. Let odd topology force a physical root zero on the antipodal domain, then study reversal-invariant provenance on the zero set. The centered-cut tensor
T(C,rho)=u_C rho^T
is exactly of this type, since both factors change sign under reversal.

Thus the graded cut cube repairs cut provenance but does not by itself repair antipodal cancellation. The meaningful fixed-point architecture is not:
'antipodal terminal roots -> separate all raw roots'.
It is:
'antipodal topology -> physical root zero, followed by quotient/even cut analysis',
or a rigorously defined oriented slice before separation.

This also explains why the terminal central-cut theorem and the side-lift theorem are complementary rather than interchangeable: the central cut records WHICH exchange occurs, while the side lift or quotient structure prevents the reversal two-cycle from being mistaken for a useful global circulation.
