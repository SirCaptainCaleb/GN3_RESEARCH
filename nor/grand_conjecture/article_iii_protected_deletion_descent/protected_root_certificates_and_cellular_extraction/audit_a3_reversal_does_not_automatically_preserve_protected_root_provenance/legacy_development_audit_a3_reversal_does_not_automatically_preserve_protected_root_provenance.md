## Audit: A3 reversal gives an opposite raw descent, not automatically an opposite protected root

The local reversal calculation in the preceding A3 analysis is correct at the level of ternary chamber labels, but its protected-root consequence requires an additional provenance hypothesis.

Let a canonical protected root arise from a minimum-first-phase deletion witness with word 0^p1^q and omitted coordinate x. After canonical insertion, an internal 10 slide gives a physical root rho=e_a-e_c. If the corresponding four-coordinate A3 block is reversed, ternary reversal-oddness indeed turns the local 10 descent into another raw 10 descent carrying -rho.

However, complement-reversal sends the underlying one-change deletion word 0^p1^q to 0^q1^p. The protected-root carrier used in this section is normalized by the minimum first-phase length p. Thus the reversed chamber belongs automatically to the same canonical protected family only when the reversed deletion witness is itself admissible for that normalization. In particular, if q>p, reversal alone does not show that the opposite raw descent is a canonical protected root obtained from a minimum-p witness.

Therefore the implication
"protected A3 root rho exists => the same protected carrier contains -rho"
is invalid without extra hypotheses.

What is unconditional is only that every internal ternary A3 10-descent has an opposite raw 10-descent in the reversed chamber.

Consequently, support-minimal positive dependences of protected A3 roots must still be treated as directed endpoint cycles of length 2, 3, or 4. A protected two-cycle is genuine additional structure: both opposite roots must carry independent protected provenance, for example two admissible minimum-phase witnesses, rather than being supplied for free by chamber reversal.

This restores the endpoint-cycle reduction as the correct protected-root frontier. The reversal pair remains useful as a symmetry test and as a possible blow-up locus when both sides are protected, but it cannot be quotiented out universally.

### Strategic consequence

The topological extraction problem should retain provenance labels through the A3 carrier. The relevant object is not the raw chamber-root map but the subcomplex/state system of admissible protected roots. Any fixed-point or Radon argument that enlarges to all raw A3 descents risks introducing artificial opposite pairs absent from the canonical carrier. A successful equivariant construction should either preserve minimum-phase provenance under its involution or explicitly enlarge the state space and prove that the added states admit zero-free fills.
