# The barycentric center zero carries the entire boundary degree — preserved pre-item development

## The barycentric center zero carries the entire boundary degree

Continue with the all-refinements actual-root carrier on the centered permutahedron P in a minimum counterexample.

Root §139 proves that the carrier is nonzero on every proper face and that its normalized boundary map is odd-homotopic to the inward radial map.

One might try to blow up the tautological top-face center zero and hope that the nonzero boundary degree forces another zero. For the standard barycentric carrier this does not work.

### Cone structure of barycentric subdivision

The barycentric subdivision sd(P) is the simplicial cone from the barycenter o of the top-dimensional face P over sd(boundary P).

Every maximal barycentric simplex is a flag

F_0 < F_1 < ... < F_{d-1} < P,

and therefore contains the top-face barycenter o.

For the all-refinements odd carrier, the top-face label is the average over the full reversal-closed permutahedron. Reversal pairs every actual 10 root with its opposite, so

g_P=0.

Thus the PL carrier sends the cone apex o to 0.

Let H denote the PL carrier on the boundary link sd(boundary P). On each cone simplex, affine interpolation gives

F((1-r)o+r y)=r H(y)

for 0<=r<=1 and y in the corresponding boundary-link simplex.

### Local link map equals the outer boundary map

For every sufficiently small r>0,

F(r,y)/||F(r,y)||
=
H(y)/||H(y)||.

Hence the normalized map on the link of the central zero is IDENTICAL to the normalized carrier on the barycentric boundary link.

In particular the local index/degree of the center zero equals the global boundary degree.

Under the minimum-counterexample inward-normal theorem, this degree is the degree of the inward radial map (up to the fixed orientation convention), and is nonzero.

### Consequence

The central zero is not merely a nuisance fixed point that can be removed by a small blow-up.

For this barycentric cone extension it carries the entire topological degree. Excising a small ball around the center leaves an annulus whose normalized maps on the inner and outer boundaries have the same degree, exactly as they must if there are no other zeros.

Therefore topology does NOT force a second noncentral zero.

### Switch-prism analogue

The same warning applies whenever the switch-prism carrier is built as an equivariant cone/affine extension with the fixed central state labeled by zero. If the inner link map reproduces the outer boundary class, blowing up the fixed center only exposes the same degree; it does not create a new compatible event.

### Strategic implication

A useful fixed-point proof must genuinely CHANGE the center mechanism. Plausible options remain:

1. restrict to a free realized-state subcomplex with no fixed center;
2. replace the top-cell cone by a nontrivial filling whose center/link labels retain compatible witness data;
3. use a relative Sperner/Tucker carrier on actual repair states, where elementary squares and A3 cells already have constructive extraction;
4. prove an independent combinatorial theorem that the central convex cancellation has a localized compatible representation.

The minimum-counterexample boundary theorem is still valuable: it says every proper-face state is certified zero-free. But the naive center blow-up of the standard barycentric carrier cannot by itself advance closure.
