## Two independent closure routes meet at one two-window reconnection kernel

Recent Article III work has reduced two apparently different obstructions to the same bounded interface problem.

### Route A: reversal of a minimum first phase

For a globally minimum-first deletion witness with word 0^p1^q, p>=4, reversing the full coordinate interval supporting the first phase gives

1^p, gamma, delta, 1^(q-2)

after deleting the omitted endpoint coordinate. Hence every unresolved outcome is determined by the two crossing bits (gamma,delta); the long phase has disappeared from the problem.

### Route B: the terminal width-two full-full corridor

A globally maximal-band width-two full-full corridor first reduces to the rigid six-coordinate pattern of the current width-two classification. That rigid pattern has the right-preserving weave

(2,3,0,1,4,5),

whose internal word is 0,1,1,1. The weave preserves the entire right exterior, including the first crossing window on that side. Therefore every unresolved outcome is confined to the two left crossing windows. Again the interior obstruction has disappeared and only two interface bits remain.

By reversal, the same statement holds with left and right exchanged.

### Unified reduction

Thus the long minimum-first endpoint branch and the rigid width-two barrier branch both terminate, before any further topology is used, at a two-window reconnection kernel:

- one exterior tail is preserved exactly;
- the replacement interior is already one-change compatible;
- every remaining failure is encoded by two consecutive crossing-window values at the opposite boundary.

This is stronger than saying that both routes have bounded support. They have the same kind of boundary data: two adjacent ternary windows joining a certified monotone interior to an unchanged monotone exterior.

Consequently the next local theorem should be formulated directly for this two-window interface rather than separately for long endpoint bands and width-two full-full corridors. A boundary-safe resolution of the four possible two-bit states would simultaneously discharge both closure routes.

This subsection claims the exact merger of the two active frontiers into one finite symbolic kernel, with full exterior provenance retained on one side.
