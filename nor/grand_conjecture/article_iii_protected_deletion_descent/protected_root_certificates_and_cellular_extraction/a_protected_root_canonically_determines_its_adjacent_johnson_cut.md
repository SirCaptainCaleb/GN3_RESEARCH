# A protected root canonically determines its adjacent Johnson cut

## Composition

For a protected root rho=e_a-e_c crossing a physical first-phase cut C, the adjacent Johnson cut is forced: C'=(C\{a}) union {c}, and 1_C-1_C'=rho. Thus root extraction contains no choice of cut exchange. The remaining issue is realizability of this specific Johnson edge as a legal protected threshold/deletion transition; failure must instead be converted into band improvement or carrier reduction. Opposite-root opposite-side pairs should therefore be interpreted as failed realizations of the same short cut exchange in opposite directions.

## Development

Let C be the physical first-phase p-cut of a protected barrier state, and let its normalized physical root be rho=e_a-e_c with a in C and c outside C. Define C prime=(C minus {a}) union {c}. Then the hypersimplex edge identity is 1_C-1_Cprime=e_a-e_c=rho. Thus the faithful data (rho,C) already determine a unique adjacent Johnson cut Cprime; root extraction does not need to choose which cut exchange to attempt. The only missing step is realizability: prove that the forced exchange C to Cprime is represented by a legal protected threshold/deletion state, or else that failure yields a monotone band improvement or a smaller protected carrier. The side sign records which boundary failed to realize this forced exchange. Consequently an opposite-root opposite-side two-cycle should be viewed as two failed realizations of the same Johnson edge in opposite directions, not merely as cancellation in root space.
