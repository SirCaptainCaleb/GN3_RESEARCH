# The s=0 width-two types have a flat-entry protected-root handoff — preserved pre-item development

## Two nonexceptional width-two types have an automatic flat-entry protected-root handoff

Continue with §265. For the two six-set types with s=0, the left-pair-preserving replacement
(a,b,c,f,e,d)
has internal word 0001. Lexicographic extremality forces its two right crossing bits to be 01, and the next untouched trailing status is 0.

Thus the relevant boundary word is 1,0,1,0.

Let g be the first exterior coordinate on the right. The first crossing bit is
alpha(e,d,g)=0,
while the preceding internal status is
alpha(f,e,d)=1.

In the original order, the right exterior lay in the trailing zero phase, so
alpha(e,f,g)=0.
By alternation,
alpha(f,e,g)=1.

For the ordered transition (f,e,d,g) with statuses 1,0, this is exactly the flat off-face value. Coboundary flatness supplies the companion flat off-face. Hence this first 1->0 transition is flat.

Apply the outward flat repair toward the right. It preserves the already-compatible left side and strictly enlarges the one-change threshold band. Continue with §151. The process terminates in either:
1. a spanning one-change order; or
2. a fully-curved protected physical root on the exported right side.

Thus both s=0 width-two types have a strict protected-root handoff after the forced 01 kernel.

### Scope correction

For the third nonexceptional type (r,s)=(0,1), §265 uses the replacement
(a,b,f,e,c,d).
Its first exported transition is (e,c,d,g). The relevant off-face alpha(e,c,g) is not determined by the old consecutive trailing-zero crossing. Therefore the automatic-flat argument above does not apply to that type.

No flatness claim is made for (r,s)=(0,1) here. That type is instead subsumed by the uniform strong-weave reduction proved separately after §266.
