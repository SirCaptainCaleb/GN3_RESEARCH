# Every two-change band closes NOR or emits a protected root — preserved pre-item development

## Every two-change band closes NOR or emits a protected root

Work in the coboundary-flat alternating ternary sector. Let a full order have exactly two changes,
\[
0^A1^B0^C,\qquad A,B,C\ge1.
\]

If \(B=1\), the singleton descent of §§250,254 gives a spanning NOR order or a strict finite improvement in the two-change class; at extremality the singleton branch disappears.

If \(B=2\), §274 gives the finite dichotomy
\[
\text{spanning NOR}\quad\text{or}\quad\text{protected fully-curved root}.
\]

Assume now \(B\ge3\). Reverse the full supporting coordinate interval of the middle 1-band as in §275. The resulting full status word has the form
\[
0^{A-2},\ L_0,L_1,\ 0^B,\ R_0,R_1,\ 0^{C-2},
\]
with endpoint clipping. In particular it contains a central monochromatic zero interval of length \(B\ge3\), and every uncontrolled window lies outside that interval in two collars of width at most two.

Apply the monochromatic-band combing theorem §276 to this central zero interval.

Every available flat endpoint repair strictly enlarges the zero interval. Therefore after finitely many repairs either:

1. the zero interval reaches both global endpoints, yielding a monochromatic spanning order and proving NOR; or
2. one endpoint is blocked by a fully-curved tetrahedron, which emits an actual protected physical root.

Hence every wide two-change band satisfies the same dichotomy.

Combining all widths gives:

\[
\boxed{\text{Every full order with exactly two changes yields either spanning NOR or a protected fully-curved root.}}
\]

### Consequence for Article III

The entire two-change sector is now an extraction source rather than an independent realization branch.

Thus in a minimum counterexample, if the global minimum number of changes is \(2\), the counterexample necessarily supplies a protected fully-curved root. The remaining closure problem is to exploit the resulting family/circulation of protected roots, not to classify additional two-change geometry.

Together with §262, any minimum counterexample has minimum variation in \(\{2,\dots,9\}\); level \(2\) is completely routed into the protected-root problem.
