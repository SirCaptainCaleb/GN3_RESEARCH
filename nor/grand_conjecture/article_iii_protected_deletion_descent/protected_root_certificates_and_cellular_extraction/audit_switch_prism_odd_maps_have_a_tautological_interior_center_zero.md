## Development

The switch-prism degree constructions have an interior fixed-point caveat analogous to the older full-permutahedron center audit.

Let
X=P_V x I
with both factors centered, and involution
tau(x,t)=(-x,-t).
The center
o=(0,0)
is an INTERIOR fixed point of tau.

Suppose F:X->E is any continuous tau-odd map into a linear space:
F(tau z)=-F(z).
Then automatically
F(o)=-F(o),
hence
F(o)=0.

Therefore the zero-existence part of any odd switch-prism argument is tautological unless the construction does something further with the zero set. In particular, the proof pattern

'if F were zero-free, normalize on X; the boundary map is antipodal of odd degree and cannot extend'

is correct, but it proves no more than the fixed-center identity already forces.

### Consequence for the honest side-lift carrier

For root §88 the target is W plus R and the state labels are
(rho,s).
The extra interval dimension correctly matches the target dimension, and any NONTRIVIAL zero would satisfy exact physical-root cancellation and side balance.

However topology alone does not force such a nontrivial zero away from the prism center. The guaranteed zero may be the global centrally symmetric average. A support-minimal convex representation of that center can still involve a large full-dimensional carrier and need not be one of the A2/A3 local zeroes already extracted.

Thus the A2/A3 extraction theorems remain valid conditional statements about a lifted zero localized there, but the degree theorem does not by itself guarantee localization to such a proper cell.

### Consequence for the distance-lifted all-descent carrier

The issue is even more explicit in root §100. Its physical component is independent of the switch parameter k. The scalar component is average transition position minus k. At the global permutahedron center, reversal pairs every descent/root with its opposite, so the physical average vanishes. Choosing the centered switch parameter gives zero scalar average as well. Hence the prism center is an explicit zero of the averaged carrier.

The theorem that every zero lies over a Coxeter face containing a block of size at least six is compatible with this: the center lies over the full permutahedron face, whose unique block has size n.

Therefore the large-block zero forced by §100 may be precisely the trivial global center zero. Its existence is not yet an extraction theorem.

### Correct topological obligation

A useful switch-prism topology must add one of the following:

1. a boundary/tangential field whose zero cannot be the interior fixed center;
2. a carrier restriction with free involution and no fixed interior point;
3. a support-minimality/normal-cone theorem proving that the central zero descends to a proper compatible face;
4. an independent combinatorial reason that the center zero admits a localized realized-state representation with extractable provenance.

Until then, the honest lifted and distance-lifted prism constructions should be used as organizational carriers and conditional zero-extraction frameworks, not as standalone proofs that a nontrivial protected circuit exists.

This is the switch-prism analogue of roots §§18/21 and should be kept in view when allocating closure effort.
