## Composition

Cellular antipodality forces a complementary switch-prism cell by the crosspolytope degree obstruction. It does not force a complementary edge or a same-deleted-order fiber. The b-only bubble calculation needs its fixed-other-order premise; its same-side two-defect potential is unavailable from E=1 states. Protected root certificates still require a symmetric domain, gluing, and witness extraction.

## Development

Sources: Article II §§141,150,168,174. This records the valid topological result and separates it from unproved extraction.

Let X=P_V times I be the n-dimensional switch-prism ball for ternary coordinate orders. Its boundary involution reverses the order and reflects the cut. At every bad state, label each violating window by its signed physical middle coordinate, positive on the pre-cut side and negative on the post-cut side. A state has no opposite pair within its label set; reversal sends the entire set to its negative.

Some genuine product cell contains opposite labels for the same coordinate. Indeed, if every cell's union of vertex labels avoided complementary pairs, those labels would span one face of the crosspolytope sphere. Send each vertex to its label barycenter and extend over each cell within that convex face, equivariantly on paired boundary cells. This would extend an antipodal sphere map over a ball, contradicting the odd degree of an antipodal sphere map.

This conclusion is a complementary CELL. It does not guarantee a complementary edge, a two-cell, or a pair of witnesses whose orders agree after deleting the labeled coordinate b. A path moving only b preserves the relative order of every other coordinate, so it cannot connect arbitrary witnesses in a permutohedron face.

The b-only fiber lemma remains useful when its premise is supplied. A persistent violation crossing the cut gives the checked local swap and phase transport. A same-side b-moving violation-to-satisfaction edge has two central old defects; its energy is nonincreasing, and in the equality case the pattern 0110 becomes 1001, increasing the squared-rank moment by four. But no such edge starts at a state with just one defect. Neighbor-replacement toggles, endpoint events, and restrictions to the minimum-energy locus require additional arguments.

The protected descent-root certificates offer a more focused candidate domain, because all other coordinates remain in one deletion order. To use a topological theorem, one must construct the domain, verify its symmetry and boundary conditions, glue the two certificate levels, and extract a nontrivial monotone bridge. Until then these labels are obstruction data, not a spanning-order proof.
