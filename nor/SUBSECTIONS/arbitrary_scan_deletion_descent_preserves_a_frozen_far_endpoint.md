## Arbitrary-scan deletion descent preserves a frozen far endpoint

Work in a minimum coboundary-flat alternating ternary counterexample.

Let
[
H=(v_1,ldots,v_m,z)
]
be a NOR-good deletion witness omitting (x), normalized with word
[
0^p1^q,
qquad p,qge3,
]
and suppose the coordinate (z) is required to remain literally fixed at the final endpoint. The reversed statement fixes a first endpoint.

### One replacement preserves z

The audited arbitrary-scan replacement theorem acts on one side of the unique switch:

- the left replacement changes a coordinate within the bounded switch collar;
- the right replacement changes the coordinate at the corresponding bounded position on the other side.

Every coordinate outside that replacement packet retains its relative order. In particular the full suffix beyond the packet is untouched.

Since the final 1-phase has length at least three, the final coordinate (z) lies strictly beyond every switch-collar coordinate used by either replacement. Hence (z) is never the replaced coordinate and remains the last coordinate.

### Iteration preserves z

After a good replacement, §287 applies again: the new one-change deletion witness still has both phase lengths at least three.

Therefore the same argument applies at every subsequent step of the arbitrary-scan replacement dynamics. Inductively, every carrier in the trajectory keeps (z) fixed at the final endpoint.

The phase-drift, distance-three backtrack, and distance-two (A_2) recurrence classifications of §297 are therefore valid inside this endpoint-relative subclass.

### Relative close-or-root theorem

Run the extremal arbitrary-scan dynamics of §297 starting from (H), but restrict attention to the trajectory generated by its canonical protected replacements. The frozen endpoint (z) survives every state.

Hence the finite dichotomy becomes relative:

1. a spanning NOR-good order is produced; or
2. the dynamics ejects an actual protected fully-curved root while the deletion carrier on the protected side still has (z) as its literal final endpoint.

By reversal, the same holds with a frozen first endpoint.

### Application to three-block shortcut failure

In the (L=R=1) shortcut-failure branch of §298, the good deletion witness
[
O,z
]
omitting (x) has (z) frozen at its final endpoint. Therefore its entire arbitrary-scan descent may be performed without losing (z).

Likewise, the (L=R=0) branch preserves (x) at the first endpoint.

Thus a failed certified shortcut (x	o z) does not merely return to the generic protected-root problem. It returns to a **relative protected-root problem with one desired shortcut endpoint permanently pinned at the boundary**.
