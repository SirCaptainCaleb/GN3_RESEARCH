## Development


Work in the coboundary-flat ternary sector. For a switch state (pi,k,eta), let B be the length of its maximal target-compatible band around the proposed cut. Choose a bad state maximizing B globally. By the maximal-band theorem, every unresolved boundary of that band is fully curved.

Among all globally B-maximal states, choose one minimizing the distance from the actual target switch at k to the nearest unresolved full-curvature boundary.

Suppose the tetrahedron carrying the target switch is flat. Repair it toward the chosen boundary. The audited endpoint-repair law transports the switch by distance 2 or 3 unless it collides with an occupied transition.

If the full boundary is more than three transition slots away, the transported switch remains strictly inside the already target-compatible monochromatic run. No other transition of the matched band is crossed. Move the proposed target cut together with the transported actual switch. Then the same entire band remains target-compatible, so B is unchanged, while the cut-to-boundary distance strictly decreases. This contradicts the secondary extremality.

Therefore, at a doubly extremal bad state, either

1. the tetrahedron carrying the target switch is fully curved; or
2. some unresolved full-curvature boundary lies within at most three transition slots of the target switch.

Hence the residual obstruction after outward flat combing is bounded locally: a flat target switch can survive only in a 2/3-step corridor next to a full-curvature barrier. In ternary arity these are finite 5-, 6-, or 7-coordinate packets. The remaining barrier-crossing problem can therefore be classified locally rather than on the entire repair graph.
