## Keep the width-two comparison class and account for the forced exported bit

Root §260 imports the rigid six-set theorem §221 into a lexicographically extremal global two-change band. These constructions use distinct extremal states.

The §221 rotations preserve the matched interval through the first right mismatch, which is the datum used by its threshold-band argument. Their two right reconnection windows can introduce an additional one-band. A lexicographic extremum of global words 0^A1^B0^C compares orders within that global two-change class. Applying a rotation there requires verification of its complete right reconnection.

Root §257 supplies the partial width-two constraints bce=abe=1. Further rigid values require the additional admissible-rotation argument.

For example, a consistent flat increasing-triple table on a<b<c<d<e<f is

acd=0, ace=0, acf=0,
ade=1, adf=0, aef=1,
bcd=1, bce=1, bcf=0,

It has the required 0110 full/full packet and bce=abe=1. Its bcf and bef values differ from the rigid table. Every four-set parity identity holds. The table illustrates the remaining local freedom; global extremality would need an admissible surgery to constrain it further.

There is also an exact simplification INSIDE the conditional rigid-table construction of §260. Its exported second left bit is
ell_1=alpha(p,0,q).
The original matched prefix has alpha(p,q,0)=0, and alternation gives
ell_1=1-alpha(p,q,0)=1.
Thus the exported kernel is (ell_0,1), with only ell_0 arbitrary.

The corresponding changed word is
old zero prefix | ell_0,1,0,1^4 | old zero suffix.
Its two possible kernels each carry an additional band/valley. A repair must verify either restoration to the declared global two-change family or a strict potential in an explicitly enlarged state class.

The terminal endpoint cases are already handled independently by §259's complete symbolic spanning construction. The interior rotation and attachment argument retains its full reconnection obligation.
