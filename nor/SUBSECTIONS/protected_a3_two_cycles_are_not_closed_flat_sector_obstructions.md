## Composition

In the coboundary-flat alternating ternary sector, a genuine protected A3 opposite-root pair is not a closed four-coordinate obstruction. The one-left case admits a boundary-safe full-support splice. In the two-left case, the paired witnesses either close, improve the deletion first phase by two, or emit an actual protected insertion root with an endpoint outside the A3 block. Hence a support-minimal obstruction confined to one A3 block cannot be a balanced two-cycle; the surviving primitive cases are balanced four-cycles or coupled side-imbalanced cycles.

## Development

In the coboundary-flat alternating ternary sector, combine the protected A3 reversal reduction with the two splice theorems. A genuine protected opposite-root pair projects to Johnson distance one or two. In the one-left case, the paired deletion witnesses admit a boundary-safe full-support splice; under global flatness the required boundary curvatures agree, so the pair closes NOR and cannot survive in a counterexample. In the two-left case, the paired witnesses either give a full-support one-change order, a deletion witness with first phase shorter by two, or a protected insertion root with an endpoint outside the original A3 block. Under globally minimum first phase in a counterexample only the last alternative survives. Therefore a genuine protected A3 two-cycle is never a self-contained four-coordinate obstruction: it either closes, strictly improves the deletion profile, or ejects a new protected root from the A3 block. Consequently any support-minimal side-lifted obstruction that remains confined to one A3 block cannot be a balanced two-cycle. The unresolved primitive A3 cases are balanced four-cycles or coupled side-imbalanced cycles.
