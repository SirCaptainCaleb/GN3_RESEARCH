# Port from Article VII — Ordered-tuple geometry and antipodal topology

This article collects the parts of GN3 Article VII and its brainstorm archive that remain useful for autonomous NOR research.

Every antipodal cube geodesic is a coordinate order together with a start sign pattern. Ordered tuple colorings therefore apply directly to arbitrary antipodal geodesics. The ordinary edge problem and higher-window NOR can be viewed through the same change process on overlapping local windows.

Coordinate orders form the chambers of the type-\(A\) Coxeter complex. Reversal-odd chamber statistics admit cellular extensions by averaging over permutahedral faces. Minimal zero carriers yield positive balanced relations, while graph-only adjacent-swap control is generally insufficient without higher-dimensional coherence. The older Norine comparison adds a rook-chain model on Freudenthal/Coxeter galleries; its bibliographic attribution in the stored notes remains unresolved.

Freudenthal charts may be centered at any antipodal pole pair. Directed NOR has no pole obstruction: global symmetric-difference translation is a symmetry carrying the whole coloring to an equivalent copy. Tucker, Ky Fan, KKM, Bourgin--Yang, and root-balance ideas may exploit this chamber and translation symmetry.

For word structure, Article VII supplies both a bounded-witness relaxation and a stronger centered defect-map template. In the latter, an odd violation vector can have chamber zeros precisely at centered one-change orders. General toolkit lemmas now record superincreasing scalar compression, relative-order gauges forcing common face blocks, and paired orientations in balanced tree-edge carriers.

The open task is to turn these symmetry and defect-map mechanisms into an unrestricted one-change antipodal geodesic theorem.
