## First-inward stars assemble into P5 or cross-depth P4 geometry

R774 is the provenance failure at the entrance to this era. Its turn calculation is correct: the old Q dimer (q1,q2) and first-inward dimer (q1,x) have opposite polarities and meet at q1, so R547 cuts to the raw singleton pair {q2,x}. But the proof then calls that pair an ancestry-bearing paid floor and invokes R432. Accepted correction R784 shows this is invalid: no active payment lineage was created. The repair is to use the actual geometry instead of laundering the raw pair.

R775/P852 contains the right middle-turn mechanism but its accepted statement has one finite hinge overstatement that should not be propagated. Write u=q1, v=q2, d=q_{m-2}, s=q_{m-1}. For every x the first-inward trimers are (v,u,x) and (x,s,d). Test (u,x,s). If it is tight, (v,u,x,s) is always a vertex-simple P4. When m>=5, v and d are distinct and the last star extends it to the P5 (v,u,x,s,d). When m=4, however, v=d=q2, so the historical five-term order repeats q2 and is not a path; the correct output is only the P4 (v,u,x,s). If every forward middle is bad, antisymmetry gives (s,x,u) for every x, and for each pair x,y one of (x,u,y),(y,u,x) yields a cross-depth P4 (s,x,u,y) or (s,y,u,x).

Pending corrective revision R976/P1046 records exactly this repair to C627. Until it receives independent review, use R775 only with the explicit m>=5 restriction for its P5 clause; the m=4 P4 calculation above is direct from accepted R3/R723. R739/R848 in D19 provide the complementary static signed-pair view.

## Internal-trimer puncture of P5: simultaneous endpoint exposure and nonreplay

R776 is the clean endpoint-exposure theorem for a proper P5 J=(j0,j1,j2,j3,j4). Delete its internal trimer D={j1,j2,j3} and fix one exact two-cover T. Apply R508 three times in that same T: to the left P4 with absorbable singleton {j0}, to the right P4 with {j4}, and to the whole P5 with absorbable pair {j0,j4}. If the endpoint chord {j0,j4} is absent, the two singleton obligations must both go to the true exterior, so both endpoints have exterior selected states simultaneously. If the chord is present, the pair obligation still forces at least one exterior endpoint state. R778 then tests each forced endpoint-exterior state by antisymmetry against the inward P5 neighbor. The forward orientation literally extends J to a P6; in the no-P6 branch the reverse contact is exactly the R773 nonreplay terminal-contact hypothesis, so one gets an adjacent P4 or shifted R703 Reverse-Ear packet, retaining the endpoint chord when present. R781 exploits the role-labelled inward P5 (v,u,x,s,d). A surviving reverse endpoint contact is probed first by the opposite P5 endpoint and then by the physical Q endpoint. Each R518 probe either yields a P4 or forces a more cross-depth trimer; after two no-P4 probes the resulting all-Q trimer has consecutive Q contacts in reverse nonadjacent order, so R435 fires. R786 and R787 sharpen this terminal residue further. R786 uses equality of the two no-P4 signatures plus R522 to force P4 or an all-Q P5 whose every possible order contains a descending Q contact. R787 needs only one signature bit: the old endpoint Q turn fixes that bit to zero on the first probe, hence on the second; antisymmetry then emits a specific nonadjacent all-Q reverse trimer and R435 cannot fall into the trivial adjacent-state branch. R785 separately consumes the generic R696 END branch: the exact R511 endpoint hole either reverses a selected T state or gives polarity opposite the two fixed witnesses on the same tested dimer, which R523 concatenates to a P4.

**R769 as the valid abandoned predecessor.** Before R776, the P5 puncture argument used only one R508 application. Delete D={j1,j2,j3} from a proper tight P5 J=(j0,j1,j2,j3,j4) and let E be the true exterior. With absorbable block {j0,j4}, R508 forces one selected state from a P5 endpoint to E. If it touches j0, antisymmetry on {h,j0,j1} gives either (h,j0,j1), extending J to the literal P6 (h,j0,j1,j2,j3,j4), or the reverse endpoint contact (j1,j0,h); the j4 case is dual. This proof is sound and remains a useful one-line front door. R776 strictly strengthens it by applying R508 three times in the **same** exact cover: separately to {j0}, {j4}, and {j0,j4}. It therefore distinguishes the endpoint-chord branch from simultaneous dual exterior exposure, information R769 did not retain. R769 is developed here because it is the direct predecessor of R776’s P5-puncture endpoint-exposure theorem.


## Shortcut P4/P6: exact cover switch or strict movement into the deep rail

R789 replaces the earlier looser endpoint-rotation statement R788. Test A=(u,v,d) and B=(v,d,s) around the R775 P5. If A is bad, R3 gives (d,v,u), which with (v,u,x) yields a P4 containing the exact reversal v u of the old state u v. If B is bad, the dual P4 contains s d. If both are tight, K=(u,v,d,s) is a tight P4 and attaching the old endpoint turns gives J#=(L,u,v,d,s,R), a tight endpoint-preserving P6. Delete K. Minimality supplies an exact two-cover T# of H-K, and R508 on J# forces a selected state from {L,R} into X union the untouched middle M=Q[3,m-3]; because u,s are deleted this is strict terminal cut movement. R792 applies R508 to J# and both one-end truncations in the same T#, yielding simultaneous L-FAN, R-FAN and OUTER-FAN. Hence either an endpoint already reaches M, {L,R} is selected together with an endpoint-X state, or both endpoints have selected X neighbors; coincident X neighbors give an actual cross-end trimer rail. R793 reorganizes T# into three physical cells G_L=(L,a,c), M, G_R=(b,z,R). The fragmentation identity gives t=1+sigma. At t=1 all cells are contiguous; the sole edge cannot be G_L-G_R because their concatenation would Hamilton-cover X union {L,R} and Q[1,m-1] would be the complementary spanning rail. Therefore the minimum residue is one outer gate attached to M and the other outer gate isolated. R794 applies R435: outside reversal geometry the M block is literally forward Q, so the unique transition is nearest-deep, at q3 or q_{m-3}, and the mixed gate plus that boundary vertex is a canonical tight P4. R795 removes the special three-cell normalization. Any R789 shortcut cover must cross O=X union {L,R} to M; if the original endpoint crossing lands in X, a second X-M selected state exists in the same T or else O and M would be separate Hamilton rails closing H. R797 then says that, outside R435 outputs, this two-stage geometry is either a bounded endpoint-X-middle bridge of length at most six or a nontrivial X-split between an endpoint rail and a middle rail. R799 is the single-wrap alternative: a legal wrap rotates Q[1,m-1] and, with the outer P5, gives a same-root exact cover whose long cuts both move inward; if all wrap/side seams fail, antisymmetry produces a central cross-depth dimer with two same-polarity witnesses. R801 closes the obvious dead end: universal first-inward/endpoint stars make every X-to-end seam onto K or J# bad, so genuine progress in SHORTCUT-CUT must use the deep interval or a nonterminal endpoint structure.

**Historical staging.** R788 is a valid abandoned predecessor of R789. Its useful idea is endpoint-preserving cut movement, but R789 is the exact exhaustive two-turn test and should be cited. R796 was a pending/superseded formulation of the claim that the four-Q shortcut cover must cross the outer six-set into the untouched middle. Accepted R795 is the clean proof: if an endpoint-X state exists but no X-M state does, the exact two-cover has no selected O|M crossing at all, so its two rails Hamilton-cover O and M separately; the O rail together with the old Q path Q[1,m-1] closes H. Thus the required outer-to-middle crossing is already established, and R796 contributes no additional unresolved obligation.

The bridge/split theorem R797 is then genuinely same-cover. Apply R435 to both rails of the fixed shortcut cover. Outside Reverse-Ear outputs, all Q contacts on each rail are increasing. If one rail meets both a physical endpoint and M, consecutive Q contacts isolate a literal endpoint-X-...-X-middle subpath with at most four internal X vertices. If no rail meets both, L,R must lie together on one rail and all of M on the other; the simultaneous endpoint-X and X-M obligations force a nontrivial partition of X between those rails. This is the exact geometric content of the two-stage residue.


## Branch-independent bounded deletion forces deep crossing

R802 is valid but abandoned because R803/R804 dominate it. Its argument is already branch-independent: for any tight Q of order at least seven, test A=(u,v,d) and B=(v,d,s). A failure reverses to a tight trimer on one side; if A is tight and B fails, the dual trimer appears; if both are tight they form the tight P4 (u,v,d,s). Delete the support D of the resulting 3- or 4-vertex tight path. In every exact two-cover of H-D, the untouched middle M=Q[3,m-3] must have a selected crossing to the outside. Otherwise the two cover rails separate M from the outside, making the outside Hamiltonian, while the old Q subpath through D union M is the complementary spanning path. R803 strengthens the exact shape. Let tau_M count selected M|C states. If tau_M>=2, retain multiplicity. If tau_M=1, R560 with the old Q absorber forces one complete M block on the mixed rail and a pure C second rail. Compare the M block with Q by R435. A decreasing contact gives explicit reversal/reverse trimer/cycle; otherwise all M vertices occur in the literal order q3,...,q_{m-3}, so the unique crossing is pinned at q3 or rho=q_{m-3}. R804 shows even this pinned-one residue is not quiet. For each branch and each orientation, R579 either immediately gives a reversed absorber seam containing an old adjacent Q-state, or rotates the absorber so that R573 at the unique selected crossing forces the reverse of q4q3 or rho p. Thus every exact cover arising from the bounded deletion has either at least two deep crossings or explicit old-Q reversal/Reverse-Ear geometry. R836 is the earlier pending formulation of the same block-count argument and is superseded by accepted R803/R804; it remains in the ledger as historical route.

## Finite core, insertion gaps, wave collisions, and cross-pair cut normal forms

R808 isolates a branch-independent finite core for any long rail with four exterior vertices. Remove the untouched middle M=Q[3,m-3]; the remaining induced G consists of X plus six boundary Q vertices, hence has order ten. Minimality gives pc(G)<=2; pc(G)=1 would combine with M to two-cover H, so pc(G)=2. Every exact two-cover of G therefore gives a literal minimum three-cover of H with M as the third rail. This is independent of gate/seam/capture choices and explains why the late program can be finite at the boundary. R813 resolves each individual insertion gap more strongly than the earlier payment route. R583 gives a reverse-middle or terminal two-witness obstruction. Reverse-middle is already an R584 P4. For a terminal packet, probe the adjacent Q trimer with one witness via R518; either that four-set has a Hamilton P4 or the no-P4 branch makes the exact opposite polarity on the tested reverse boundary dimer, and the second old witness plus R523 gives a P4. Thus every gap has a local Hamilton P4 and there is no residual payment branch. R815 stitches repeated reverse-middle blockers on adjacent gaps: test the central turn between the two common blockers; one orientation or its reversal concatenates with the two blocker turns to one of two explicit descending-Q P5s. R874 is the analogous wave collision. Head-signed two-witness wave on D_j and tail-signed wave on D_{j+2}; probing the two adjacent Q trimers by all four witnesses either makes an immediate probe P4 or transfers both polarities to the central dimer, where R523 yields all four b_r,q_{j+1},q_j,a_s P4s. R825 is an exact combinatorial normal form for four cross-pair cut obligations in one path cover on X={a,b,c,z}. Encode selected-state signatures across the four cuts: absent an M_S edge, every useful state is one of six 2-subsets of the four obligations. An inclusion-minimal edge cover of four labels is either a complementary pair, giving AC-EXT/BZ-EXT/RL, or a three-edge K_1,3, giving one of four STAR shapes. This proof is purely path-forest combinatorics. R842 obtains those four cut obligations from a 2x2 opposite-polarity dimer rectangle via four R508 P4 carriers; it remains needs-more-work as deposited, while the R825 classifier itself is accepted. R826 attempts to convert a central four-P4 fan to at least two same-frame X-anchor contact portals; its local carrier/capture argument is valid but the theorem remains needs-more-work, so it is not used as established closure. R879 is the generic complete-width-two splice fan for any nontrivial spanning three-cover and is the accepted parent behind the source-cover seam consumers: for each ordered pair of rails, concatenation creates exactly the two boundary turns, so at least one reverse mate is tight.

**Current binary-split interface.** Historical predecessor R838 is accepted, but its claim family now has the stronger current accepted revision R870. ## Selected-depth endpoint rotation and the XET seam parent

R906 is a needs-more-work compression of the endpoint-rotation packet family. Its source case analysis is useful but its LP4 wording overclaims that every P4 produced meets the designated X set. The safe invariant output types are: a bounded labelled P4 supplied by the displayed case; an inward middle-gate sign witness t=q3 or r=q_{m-3} feeding R682 CAP/XET; or deep-dimer fixed-cover rigidity. The middle-gate XET line was then developed independently. R907 is valid abandoned: if an exact deletion cover has cross-end trimer rail (L,t,R) or its reverse and t has polarity opposite the two endpoints on the deleted middle-gate dimer S, then the five-set consisting of S plus that rail cannot Hamilton-cover, because its Hamilton P5 plus the complementary rail would close H. A short antisymmetry/P5 contradiction therefore forces a source-labelled P4 using S, t, and the opposite physical endpoint. R908 is a sharper selected-depth specialization. Combine endpoint sign and inward sign by R523 to form a P4 K; in the actual exact cross-end cover, deleting K endpoints leaves exactly two exterior blocks. R833 has four alternatives, but cross-rail orientation kills reverse-chord, one endpoint has no exterior selected neighbor, and the middle cross-rail vertex has degree two, leaving a unique reverse-seam branch. This forces (R,t,s1) or (L,t,s1) on the left, and the dual seam on the right. R909 observes that the resulting seam or the reverse-oriented cross-end rail already has two nonadjacent Q contacts in decreasing order, so R435 produces explicit Reverse-Ear geometry. These three selected-depth statements are valid but abandoned because R915 is the proper parent. R915 removes the crossed/R24 frame entirely. Let exact T contain rail (x,v,y), with x,y the same polarity on tested dimer S=(p,q) and v the opposite polarity. R523 forms P4 (x,p,q,v) in the head-endpoint/tail-middle case. Removing x,v from T leaves singleton y plus the other rail, so the R833 block count is exactly two. X-REVERSE is impossible because x has no exterior T-neighbor, reverse-chord would require v x while T selects x v, and ANTI-ENDS is impossible because v has degree two. Hence Y-REVERSE is forced, and its unique exterior neighbor is y, giving (y,v,q). The three other orientation/polarity cases are exact symmetries. R915 is valid with P986 pending review, so this development records it as pending rather than accepted. It subsumes the mechanism of R908; R909 remains the R24-specific downstream R435 consumer.

**Why R915 is the parent rather than another R24 special case.** Its proof is completely local to one exact deletion cover. ## Single-wrap to deep-crossing history

The single-wrap branch went through several valid staging results that were easy to lose because each was later abandoned as a standalone interface. Their connected content is a monotone geometric staircase.

R790 starts from a single-wrap inward P5 residue. Testing the next local insertion either exposes a Hamilton P4 on four Q vertices or propagates the wrap one step farther along the interior rail. R791 packages the possible wrap outcomes: either one gets such a four-Q P4, or the failure certificates accumulate into a two-witness short-carrier packet. R798 reorganizes the surviving wrap branch into a **same-root exact cover whose two long-rail cuts have both moved inward**. This is stronger geometric information than mere existence of another cover: the root identity is preserved while the unused deep Q interval grows on the untouched side. R800 then extracts the invariant that matters downstream: every surviving R789 branch forces a current selected crossing from the active packet into the untouched deep Q interval.

These claims were abandoned because later cut/recompletion parents obtain the needed deep crossing more directly and with cleaner hypotheses. Their useful alternative method is the constructive wrap propagation: repeated local failure does not merely produce anonymous mates; it pushes the physical cut inward until a deep selected state is exposed. No abandoned staging claim is revived as canonical, and no step alone asserts closure.

The later inward-depth program should therefore be read as preserving the physical content of this staircase while replacing its turn-by-turn route.

## Distance-two two-witness waves: repaired central fan

R822 identified the correct phenomenon but did not have a clean reconstructible route: oppositely directed two-witness waves two Q steps apart should not pass each other quietly. Accepted R874 is the exact repair and removes the source-specific baggage.

Let D_j=(q_j,q_{j-1}) be head-signed by distinct b_1,b_2 and D_{j+2}=(q_{j+2},q_{j+1}) tail-signed by distinct a_1,a_2. Apply the four-cell compiler R518 to the adjacent source trimer at q_j with each b probe and to the adjacent source trimer at q_{j+1} with each a probe. If any of the four tests yields a Hamilton P4, retain that labelled P4. Otherwise all four no-P4 signatures force the central reverse dimer D_{j+1}=(q_{j+1},q_j) to be head-signed by both b witnesses and tail-signed by both a witnesses. R523 then concatenates every opposite-polarity pair, giving all four tight P4s (b_r,q_{j+1},q_j,a_s), r,s in {1,2}.

Thus the correct invariant is a complete simultaneous 2-by-2 central fan, not a vague collision. R874 is order-free and uses only R518/R523. In the crossed R24 application the witness pairs are {b,z} and {a,c}; exact-cover seam theorems, rather than generic path-growth optimism, are the intended consumers.
