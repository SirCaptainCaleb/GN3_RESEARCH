## Same tested dimer: collision or literal P4

Fix tested oriented dimer D=(a,b). Head witness w means (w,a,b) tight; tail witness means (a,b,w) tight. R523/P538 gives the exhaustive interaction for distinct x,y on this same tested orientation. HH and TT are exactly the two-head/two-tail collision packets. HT gives the tight P4 (x,a,b,y), and TH gives (y,a,b,x).

Tested order is part of the datum. Certificates on (a,b) and (b,a) cannot be combined merely because the unordered support agrees, and no cyclic rereading of certified turns is licensed.

## Reverse Ear Lemma and contact monotonicity

Let P=(v_0,...,v_k) and K be vertex-simple tight paths. Let E be the K-subpath between consecutive K-contacts v_i,v_j, with no internal P-vertex, and suppose i>j. If E is the single adjacent reversed state with i=j+1, the old P-state is already reversed. Otherwise let x follow v_i on E and y precede v_j. Test (v_{i-1},v_i,x) and (y,v_j,v_{j+1}). A bad first seam reverses to (x,v_i,v_{i-1}); a bad second seam reverses to (v_{j+1},v_j,y). If both are tight, E followed by old P segment v_j,...,v_{i-1} forms a vertex-simple tight cycle.

Hence outside explicit reversal/reverse-trimer/proper-cycle output, P-contacts on K occur in increasing P-order. A proper tight cycle is constructive output, not contradiction.

## Exact scope of captured-crossing mirror reentry

R277 was an attempted scope correction for R155, but it overcorrected and is invalid. It claimed the carrier used at mirror reentry had to be identical to the carrier from the original capture representative.

R528 is the accepted exact fence. A valid historical capture is attached to the physical event `(x,D)` where `D={x,y}`. At the time R155 is applied, one may use **any certified graph-intrinsic tight carrier A** with `x∈A`, `y∉A`, and a genuine carrier adjacency `E={p,x}`. A may have been certified after the capture. ## Deleted-neighbor restoration does not consume a fresh witness

Head form. Assume only that (k,E) is tight, with E=(e_0,...,e_m), m>=1, and that z is distinct from k and outside E. Test the ordered triple (z,k,e_0). If it is tight, then (z,k,e_0,e_1,...,e_m) is a tight path: its first turn is the tested turn, while every remaining turn is already part of the tight path (k,E). Hence (z,k,E) is tight. If (z,k,e_0) is bad, Boundary Antisymmetry R3 makes its complete reversal (e_0,k,z) tight. This is exactly the head conclusion of R164. At no point was (z,E) used.

Tail form. Assume only that (E,k) is tight. Test (e_m,k,z). If it is tight, then (e_0,...,e_m,k,z) is tight because all earlier turns are already contained in (E,k). If it is bad, R3 makes the complete reversal (z,k,e_m) tight. This is exactly the tail conclusion of R164, again without using (E,z).

Therefore the extra fresh-extension hypothesis in R164 does not strengthen the logical disjunction it concludes. It records that z is itself a certified endpoint witness, which can matter to later ancestry-sensitive consumers, but R164's own forcing step is fresh-witness-blind.

Now specialize to either same-polarity residual-terminal collision supplied by R455. For example, in a head-signed collision let E be the reverse terminal dimer and let k=x be the retained R359 middle witness. R455 also supplies a second witness w from the opposite residual rail, so (w,E) is tight; however the generic argument above shows that the R164 restoration/contact alternative with z=w already follows from R3 and (x,E) alone. The same conclusion would hold for any distinct z outside E, whether or not z signed E. The tail-signed case is identical by the dual argument. Thus an R455-to-R164 step by itself does not consume the second R455 certificate and cannot be credited as additional linkage produced by two-witnessness. A genuine consumer must use both signed facts elsewhere or introduce additional structure.
