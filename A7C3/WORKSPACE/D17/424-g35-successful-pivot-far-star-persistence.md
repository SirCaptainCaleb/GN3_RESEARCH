# Across successful defect pivots only the fixed far-end reverse star can persist

**Workspace:** D17
**State:** established
**Key:** `g35-successful-pivot-far-star-persistence`

**Summary:** Let two consecutive G35 prefix states M_q,M_{q+1} be literal tight three-forests, related by one successful zipper pivot, and assume neither shortcut is the unique loop coincidence. The q-th closing shortcut g_q=a_q->b_m has only a near seam at a_q and a far seam at b_m. Any near-seam blocker uses g_q and disappears when the defect advances because g_q is no longer selected. By SV113367 a shortcut-cycle blocker also cannot persist across the successful pivot. Since a_{q+1}!=b_m, the pivot does not remove the selected outgoing edge of b_m, so its physical successor c is unchanged. Hence the only blocker that can recur at the next shortcut is the far seam: badness of (a_q,b_m,c) and then (a_{q+1},b_m,c) gives by R3 the tight reverse-star turns (c,b_m,a_q) and (c,b_m,a_{q+1}). Along any run of successful physical pivots, genuinely persistent seam debt is therefore one growing reverse star at the fixed far boundary; near blockers and cycle debt are one-step phenomena.

### 1. Consecutive physical defect states\nRetain the G35 zipper corridor and suppose two consecutive prefix matchings\n\n  M_q, M_{q+1}\n\nare both realized as literal spanning tight three-path forests. They are related by the successful pivot\n\n  f_{q+1}=a_{q+1}->b_{q+1}\n  replaced by\n  e_{q+1}=a_q->b_{q+1}.                                (SP.1)\n\nThe moving unmatched OUT copy is a_q,out in M_q and a_{q+1},out in M_{q+1}; the fixed far unmatched IN copy is b_m,in in both states. Assume\n\n  a_q != b_m,   a_{q+1} != b_m,                          (SP.2)\n\nso neither closure is the unique singleton-loop coincidence.\n\nFor the q-th state put\n\n  g_q=a_q->b_m.                                           (SP.3)\n\nBecause a_q is a rail terminal and b_m a rail source, adding g_q creates at most two new distinct-vertex turns: the NEAR seam\n\n  alpha_q=(p_q,a_q,b_m)                                  (SP.4)\n\nwhen a_q has predecessor p_q, and the FAR seam\n\n  beta_q=(a_q,b_m,c)                                     (SP.5)\n\nwhen b_m has successor c. All other turns are inherited from M_q.\n\n### 2. Near-seam debt is one-step debt\nIf alpha_q is bad, R3 gives its exact reverse\n\n  (b_m,a_q,p_q) tight.                                   (SP.6)\n\nBut the selected edge g_q is not present in the next closure M_{q+1}+g_{q+1}; its tail changes from a_q to a_{q+1}. Therefore the exact selected turn alpha_q is absent after the defect advances. A near-seam blocker can be newly replaced by another near-seam blocker at q+1, but the physical blocker at q itself does not persist.\n\nThis is literal support transport, not a novelty claim.\n\n### 3. Cycle debt is also one-step debt after a successful pivot\nIf both shortcut seams at q are tight but g_q closes a directed cycle, SV113367 applies. Since the pivot (SP.1) is physically successful, the cut edge f_{q+1} must lie on a different M_q rail from the b_m-to-a_q cycle rail. Consequently b_m and the new terminal a_{q+1} lie on different M_{q+1} rails. Thus the next closure g_{q+1}=a_{q+1}->b_m cannot create a directed cycle.\n\nHence shortcut-cycle debt cannot persist across two consecutive physical corridor states.\n\n### 4. The far boundary itself is fixed\nAssume b_m has a selected successor c in M_q. The only selected edge leaving b_m could be changed by the pivot (SP.1) if its tail were b_m, i.e. if\n\n  a_{q+1}=b_m.\n\nThis is excluded by (SP.2). The new edge e_{q+1} has tail a_q, not b_m. Therefore the selected outgoing edge b_m->c survives literally into M_{q+1}. In particular the far seam of the next closure is\n\n  beta_{q+1}=(a_{q+1},b_m,c).                            (SP.7)\n\nIf b_m is a singleton rail, no far seam exists at either state and this section is vacuous.\n\n### 5. Persistent failure is exactly a reverse star\nSuppose the far seam is bad at both consecutive states. R3 gives\n\n  (c,b_m,a_q) tight,\n  (c,b_m,a_{q+1}) tight.                                 (SP.8)\n\nThus repeated far-end shortcut failure does not produce unrelated trimers: it grows one graph-intrinsic reverse star at the fixed physical dimer (c,b_m), with the successive moving defect terminals as witnesses.\n\nIterating the same argument along any run\n\n  M_q,M_{q+1},...,M_s\n\nof successful physical pivots (omitting the at-most-one loop index), every blocker that genuinely persists from one closure to the next is represented by the single fixed-boundary family\n\n  (c,b_m,a_i) tight.                                      (SP.9)\n\nNear blockers use the current g_i and disappear when i advances; cycle debt disappears after one successful pivot by Section 3.\n\n### 6. G35 consequence and scope\nThe moving-defect corridor therefore has only one mechanism for carrying the SAME physical shortcut obstruction forward through successful pivots: a reverse star anchored at the fixed far endpoint. This is a transport normal form, not a seam taxonomy. It does not claim that successive closures must all fail at the far seam, nor does it yet consume the reverse star when a source-spoke terminal enters it. Its purpose is to turn persistent mixed-turn debt into one common-parent object whose witness set is ordered by the zipper traversal.\n\nNo R24, R5, payment, replay, MILP, or SAT is used.
