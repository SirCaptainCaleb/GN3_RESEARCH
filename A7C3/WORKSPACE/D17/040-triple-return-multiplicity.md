The endpoint-return program becomes substantially sharper when all three middle choices of one physical triple T={a,b,c} are compared over the same residue H-T. R395-R405 are the historical route to this viewpoint. Endpoint-return representatives trim to exact two-covers of H-T carrying restoration caps. If three caps lie on one representative, either they attach at three distinct physical ends and close H, or two share an end and force the same-tested-order collision/2+1 occupancy structures of R396/R400 and the dual saturation packet R401. R403/R404 consume the role-linked common-shadow residue, while R405 records the remaining no-interaction conclusion: every triple deletion has at least two exact cover representatives. R402 and its stronger R447 are the complementary four-endcap matrix route. These are useful predecessor mechanisms, not separate terminal branches.

The revived C347 chain R472-R482 turns the multiplicity statement into an exact finite common-residue normal form. For each middle m in T orient the unique m-middle trimer J_m and apply R359. Associate to m an exact two-cover F_m of H-T. In the endpoint-return case, trim m from a cover exposing it at an endpoint and retain the corresponding restoration cap. In the all-internal case, take the residual rails U_m sqcup V_m from the R359 currentization and retain its four shield turns with witness m. This gives three middle-labelled covers of one literal residue.

If two F_m are distinct up to rail exchange, R470/R471 supplies the order-divergence branch: either the support partition changes and R410/R176 births a balanced pair, or one common support has a physical order change, namely a reversed adjacent state, reverse trimer, or proper tight cycle. Suppose instead all three F_m coincide as U sqcup V. If all three middles are endpoint-return, the same rails carry all three restoration caps and R404 gives the common-shadow consumer. It remains to handle synchronized states with internal middles.

Exactly one internal middle is the I1 case. R475 first places the two endpoint caps on the common rails. Caps on the same end give a same-support collision. Distinct ends can be installed simultaneously to give an exact H-{x} cover, so all direct x attachments are bad and R3 supplies reverse x-shields. Most placements immediately give a role-labelled P4/P5 or an R435 order-change; the sole seam-aligned cross-rail placement was isolated rather than hidden. R476 applies R518 to the two cap endpoints: a P4 is explicit, while double no-P4 gives two reverse-boundary collisions. R478 observes that one such P4 cuts only one source adjacency and therefore cannot by itself complement the original native seam; the R522 P5 cuts both. R479 then shows the P5 cannot remain quiet: order preservation against the tail cap rail, J_x, and the head cap rail would force the unique order (r_t,t,x,h,r_h), whose first turn contradicts the retained antisymmetric x-shield. R480 removes the other P4/P5 outputs in the same way. Finally R481 consumes the same-end collision itself. At a common head cap (p,r,s), the internal shield is (s,r,x); R518 gives a P4 on these four vertices, but quiet comparison with the rail requires r before s while quiet comparison with the shield trimer requires s before r. Hence R435 must output a reversed state, reverse trimer, or proper cycle. The tail case is dual. Thus I1 has no anonymous path-sized terminal residue.

If at least two middles are internal, R482 closes the synchronized I2+ normal form to the same order-change currency. With exactly two internal labels, use the third endpoint cap and one internal shield at its rail end. R518 supplies a P4 and the rail-versus-shield orders on the shared terminal dimer are opposite, so R435 fires. With all three labels internal, choose one shield trimer C_a at a rail end and test the other two middle labels through R518. A P4 again yields the order conflict; if neither four-set has a P4, R522 gives a P5, and quiet comparison with the rail versus C_a again demands opposite orders of the same terminal dimer. Therefore every synchronized multi-internal state also produces explicit R435 geometry.

This is an exact reduction, not global closure: reverse trimers and proper cycles remain outputs requiring their own consumers. The historical revisions R472-R480 retain useful intermediate roles and cut-budget information, while R481/R482 are the clean preferred terminal descriptions of the synchronized I1/I2+ branches. Their accepted routes remain not fully reconstructible because they inherit historical dependencies, so this section does not upgrade their review semantics.
