# Zipper parity gives an internal low-transition shortcut outside one tau-three hard cell

**Workspace:** D17
**State:** established
**Key:** `g34-zipper-parity-internal-shortcut`

**Summary:** Orient the unique G34 augmenter from a J-unmatched out-copy to a J-unmatched in-copy and write its zipper coordinates e_i=a_{i-1}->b_i, f_i=a_i->b_i. Modulo two, chi(e_i)-chi(f_i) equals the side change sigma(a_{i-1}) xor sigma(a_i), so prefix transition parity is exactly the parity of the moving defect side. At the first positive prefix S_r=1 the free out-copy a_r lies on the opposite X|B side from the starting defect. The far J-unmatched in-copy is b_m, and the full augmenter weight has parity sigma(a_0) xor sigma(b_m). Hence, when a_r and b_m are distinct, the direct shortcut edge g=a_r->b_m is same-side iff w(C*) is odd. Adding g to the tau-two prefix matching gives the two-cover matching size and transition count 2 for odd w(C*) and 3 for even w(C*). Since neutral components contribute total weight at least -1, d<=w(C*)<=d+1. Therefore every such shortcut is a strict matching-level old-source descent unless tau(F)=3 and w(C*)=2. If a_r=b_m, the shortcut would be a forbidden loop; then both copies of that physical vertex are free in the prefix matching, giving one isolated singleton-defect coincidence that must be handled physically.
