# A shortcut-cycle blocker cannot survive one successful defect pivot

**Workspace:** D17
**State:** established
**Key:** `g35-shortcut-cycle-one-step-transport`

**Summary:** Let M_q be a literal tight three-forest prefix in the G35 zipper corridor and suppose its strict-descent shortcut g_q=a_q->b_m passes its seam tests but closes a directed cycle. Then b_m and a_q are respectively the source and terminal of one M_q rail R. If the next defect pivot f_{q+1}=a_{q+1}->b_{q+1} to e_{q+1}=a_q->b_{q+1} is physically realized as a literal three-forest M_{q+1}, the cut edge f_{q+1} cannot lie on R: cutting it on R would leave the suffix b_{q+1}...a_q and e_{q+1} would close exactly that suffix into a directed cycle. Hence f_{q+1} lies on another rail S. The pivot then joins R to the suffix of S, while the prefix of S ends at the new defect a_{q+1}; therefore b_m and a_{q+1} lie on different M_{q+1} rails. The next shortcut a_{q+1}->b_m is consequently cross-rail and can no longer be blocked by directed-cycle debt (nor by the loop coincidence); if its two endpoint turns are tight it is an exact strict-transition-descent two-cover. Thus cycle debt is transported away after one successful pivot and cannot persist on consecutive physical corridor states.
