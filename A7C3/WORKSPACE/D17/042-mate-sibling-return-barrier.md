# Winner-trimer return and the exact mate-sibling barrier

**Workspace:** D17
**State:** working
**Key:** `mate-sibling-return-barrier`

**Summary:** R466/R468/R469 give the exact winner-return barrier: a native winner reaches its sibling complement only through a cross-neighbor endpoint return; failure becomes a blocked-cap shield family whose native fans have no exits, except for one sharply characterized cross-fiber replay bridge.

### Proposition: winner return, blocked caps, and the exact native-fan barrier

**Setting and notation.** Retain the accepted R453/R467 all-internal source state
\[
H=J\sqcup U\sqcup V,
\qquad U=(u_1,\ldots,u_r),\quad V=(v_1,\ldots,v_s),\quad r,s\ge2,
\]
with native U-to-V splice literals
\[
A=(v_1,u_r,u_{r-1}),\qquad B=(v_2,v_1,u_r).
\]
The general R461 two-cut barrier applies to these literals. When exactly one of A,B is tight, put
\[
D=\{v_1,u_r\}.
\]
In the Unique-A branch (A tight, B bad), R467 gives r\ge3; write
\[
t=u_{r-1},\quad w=u_{r-2},\quad O_A=(w,t,u_r).
\]
In the Unique-B branch (A bad, B tight), R467 gives s\ge3; write
\[
t=v_2,\quad w=v_3,\quad O_B=(v_1,t,w).
\]
All exact covers and splice clauses below are the literal physical representatives displayed by R466/R468/R469; different exact covers are alternative representatives, not simultaneous data.

**Conclusion.**

1. **Winner-return condition.** Each native winner already realizes locally the two source-endpoint cuts required by R461. Currentizing A or B as a return trimer therefore respects the necessary cut geometry, but a one-step R358 endpoint return never produces the complement of that *same* winner. After A wins, the sibling complement \(\bar B=(u_r,v_1,v_2)\) appears in an R358 clause **iff** the relevant return rail ends \((\ldots,v_2,u_r)\). After B wins, \(\bar A=(u_{r-1},u_r,v_1)\) appears **iff** the relevant return rail begins \((v_1,u_{r-1},\ldots)\). Thus winner return is a cross-neighbor condition, not an automatic mate fork.

2. **Nonreplay exposure or blocked caps.** In the Unique-A branch, any pair-deletion cover exposing \(P=(t,z,\ldots)\) forces
\[
C_A(z)=(z,t,u_r)
\]
tight. It is exact source replay only for \(z=w\); otherwise \(C_A(z)\) collides on the retained support \((t,u_r)\) with \(O_A\). If every nonreplay exposure is absent, then for every exact triple-deletion cover \(H-(D\cup\{t\})=R\sqcup S\) with \(R=(r_0,r_1,\ldots)\) and \(r_0\ne w\), the cap \((t,r_0,r_1)\) is bad and the reverse shield
\[
S_A(R)=(r_1,r_0,t)
\]
is tight. The same physical R453 clause has other literal \(C_A(r_0)=(r_0,t,u_r)\), already collision-decorated by \(O_A\). The Unique-B branch is the exact reversal/rail dual, with terminal exposure \((\ldots,z,t)\), shield \(S_B(R)=(t,r_0,r_1)\), and dormant literal \(C_B(r_0)=(v_1,t,r_0)\).

3. **Native-fan obstruction.** The complete six-splice fans of the source, shifted-source, and triple-deletion covers have the wrong implication orientation for these blocked shields. In Unique-A no native mate literal equals \(\bar S_A(R)=(t,r_0,r_1)\); in Unique-B no native mate literal equals \(\bar S_B(R)=(r_1,r_0,t)\). Hence the blocked shields are sinks in the implication graph generated by those native fans. The collision decoration lies on the *other* literal of the blocked clause and does not by itself create an outgoing shield edge.

4. **Exceptional cross-fiber bridge.** Exact replay has one sharply characterized length-one escape, but its existence is not forced. In Unique-A, if a replay pair-deletion cover is
\[
H-D=(t,w,p_2,\ldots,p_m)\sqcup(q_0,\ldots,q_n),
\]
then the Q-to-P splice produces
\[
(t,q_n,q_{n-1})\ \text{OR}\ (w,t,q_n).
\]
A triple-deletion rail beginning \((q_n,q_{n-1},\ldots)\) has shield \((q_{n-1},q_n,t)\), whose complement is the first literal; this gives a physical length-one edge to \((w,t,q_n)\), collision-decorated by \(O_A\). Conversely, inside that replay fan this reversed terminal-dimer alignment is the only blocked-head exit. The Unique-B dual uses a replay cover ending \((\ldots,w,t)\) and a triple-deletion rail ending \((\ldots,q_1,q_0)\), giving the corresponding reversed initial-dimer bridge.

5. **Double winner.** If both A and B are tight, neither \(\bar A\) nor \(\bar B\) occurs in the complete native source fan. Both winners are already collision-decorated by the R467 source shields, so R463 contributes only the existing length-zero terminal collisions. The tight P4 formed by the double winner is retained, but no additional physical clause follows from R453/R463 alone.

**Retained mathematical information.** Keep the original source endpoint adjacencies, the native literals A,B, the winning trimer, deletion fibers, exact return-rail endpoint neighbor, old source turn \(O_A\) or \(O_B\), every blocked cap and reverse shield, the complete physical splice clause containing that shield, its collision-decorated sibling literal, and, when realized, both exact covers participating in the cross-fiber reversed-endpoint bridge.

**Limitation.** R466/R468/R469 do not prove that the required cross-neighbor return or cross-fiber replay alignment exists. They also do not rule out a shield exit created by a genuinely new external proposal. Their conclusion is therefore a precise barrier and localization theorem, not closure of the all-internal return branch.

### Proof

**R466: two-cut geometry and one-step winner return.** R461 gives the two-cut necessity. For the local winner geometry, L_1=(b_0,a_p,a_{p-1}) locally replaces the source terminal adjacency a_{p-1}->a_p by a_p->a_{p-1} and replaces b_0->b_1 by b_0->a_p. Likewise L_2=(b_1,b_0,a_p) replaces b_0->b_1 by b_1->b_0 and gives a_p predecessor b_0 rather than a_{p-1}. Hence either tight winner already contains a local ordered segment satisfying the two-cut necessity.

For the exact sibling-complement condition, first assume A=(v_1,u_r,u_{r-1}) is tight and set (a,x,c)=(v_1,u_r,u_{r-1}) in R358. If x=u_r is the initial endpoint of the return rail P=(x,d,e,...), R358 produces clause literals (d,c,x)=(d,u_{r-1},u_r) and (e,d,c)=(e,d,u_{r-1}); neither can equal bar(A)=(u_{r-1},u_r,v_1) or bar(B)=(u_r,v_1,v_2), by coordinate comparison and distinctness of the proper trimer. If x is the terminal endpoint P=(...,e,d,x), R358 produces (a,d,e)=(v_1,d,e) and (x,a,d)=(u_r,v_1,d). Again neither can equal bar(A), while (x,a,d)=bar(B) holds exactly when d=v_2, i.e. exactly when the return rail ends (...,v_2,u_r).

Now assume B=(v_2,v_1,u_r) is tight and set (a,x,c)=(v_2,v_1,u_r). If x=v_1 is initial, R358 produces (d,c,x)=(d,u_r,v_1) and (e,d,c)=(e,d,u_r). The first equals bar(A)=(u_{r-1},u_r,v_1) exactly when d=u_{r-1}; the second cannot. Thus bar(A) appears exactly when the return begins (v_1,u_{r-1},...). If x is terminal, R358 produces (a,d,e)=(v_2,d,e) and (x,a,d)=(v_1,v_2,d), neither equal to bar(A). In all four forms, equality with bar(B)=(u_r,v_1,v_2) is impossible by the fixed first/third coordinates and properness. This proves the claimed iff conditions and no-self-complement fence.

### Nonreplay endpoint exposure versus blocked caps

Unique-A: R467 gives A tight and the shifted source cover; its U^- rail is nontrivial, so r>=3 and O_A=(u_{r-2},u_{r-1},u_r) is a literal tight source turn. Let t=u_{r-1}, w=u_{r-2}. If H-D=P sqcup Q with P=(t,z,...), R168 gives |P|>=3. Adding the dimer (v_1,u_r) gives a literal three-cover. The R453 ordered splice from that dimer to P has literals (t,u_r,v_1)=bar(A) and (z,t,u_r)=C_A(z). R3 makes bar(A) bad, so C_A(z) is tight. For z!=w, C_A(z) and O_A have the same tested order on (t,u_r) and distinct witnesses, so R38 applies; z=w is literal equality with O_A.

Now put X=D union {t}. By R5, H-X has exact two-covers, and R168 makes both rails have order at least two. For an exact cover R sqcup S and R=(r_0,r_1,...), the cap (t,r_0,r_1) is tight if and only if (t,r_0,r_1,...) sqcup S is an exact two-cover of H-D exposing t initially with successor r_0. The forward implication is concatenation; the reverse follows by deleting the initial vertex t from such a cover. Hence absence of all nonreplay exposures is equivalent to badness of every cap with r_0!=w. R3 then forces S_A(R)=(r_1,r_0,t). Since A is a tight trimer on X, A sqcup R sqcup S is a literal spanning three-cover. R453 on A to R gives exactly C_A(r_0)=(r_0,t,u_r) OR S_A(R)=(r_1,r_0,t). The old turn O_A collision-decorates C_A(r_0) whenever r_0!=w. If C_A(r_0) is tight, R38 gives the advertised collision; otherwise R3 forces (u_r,t,r_0). Finally R463 applies to this physical clause whenever S_A(R) reaches any decorated sink, because C_A(r_0) itself is a length-zero decorated sink. This proves (A).

Unique-B is the exact reversal/rail dual. R467 makes V^- nontrivial, so s>=3 and O_B=(v_1,v_2,v_3) exists. Put t=v_2,w=v_3. If P=(...,z,t), the ordered P-to-(v_1,u_r) clause is (v_1,t,z)=C_B(z) OR (u_r,v_1,t)=bar(B); B tight forces C_B(z), and z!=w gives R38 with O_B. For X=D union {t}, a rail R=(...,r_1,r_0) admits terminal restoration of t exactly when (r_1,r_0,t) is tight. Failure for every r_0!=w forces S_B(R)=(t,r_0,r_1). The literal cover B sqcup R sqcup S and its R-to-B splice give S_B(R) OR C_B(r_0), with C_B(r_0) collision-decorated by O_B. The remaining assertions follow by R3 and R463 as above.

### Complete native-fan orientation and the cross-fiber replay bridge

For an ordered splice from X=(x_0,...,x_p) to Y=(y_0,...,y_q), R453 gives mate literals M_1=(y_0,x_p,x_{p-1}) and M_2=(y_1,y_0,x_p).

Unique-A native fans. Here A=(v_1,u_r,t), and every rail of H-X omits t. Therefore, in any fan on A,R',S', M_1 cannot begin with t because no component begins with t, and M_2 cannot begin with t because no component has t as its second vertex: A has second vertex u_r and the residue rails omit t. Hence no such fan contains bar(S_A(R))=(t,r_0,r_1). In the original source fan t=u_{r-1}. A mate literal can begin with t only if t=u_2, hence r=3, and then its second coordinate is u_1=w. The same calculation applies to the shifted source fan with U^-=(u_1,...,t). Since blocked shields require r_0 != w, neither fan contains bar(S_A(R)). By the definition of G_F in R463, an arc leaves a literal L only when some clause contains bar(L). Thus every S_A(R) is a sink in the union of these physical fans. The Unique-B proof reverses the coordinate order: bar(S_B(R)) ends in t; a triple-deletion fan cannot end in t, and the sole short source/shifted possibility has penultimate coordinate w, excluded by r_0 != w.

Replay bridge, Unique-A. Let E=(v_1,u_r), P=(t,w,p_2,...,p_m), and Q=(q_0,...,q_n). R168 gives n>=2. The Q-to-P splice proposal has complete holes (q_{n-1},q_n,t) and (q_n,t,w), hence its mate clause is
L_1=(t,q_n,q_{n-1}) OR L_2=(w,t,q_n).
If a triple-deletion rail begins (q_n,q_{n-1},...), its R468 shield is S=(q_{n-1},q_n,t)=bar(L_1). The labeled arc S->L_2 is therefore physical. Since q_n lies in H-D while u_r lies in D, q_n != u_r; L_2 and O_A=(w,t,u_r) have the same tested polarity on oriented support (w,t) and distinct witnesses, so L_2 is collision-decorated. Conversely the only mate literals beginning t in the replay fan arise when P is the right component. Splicing E-to-P gives (t,u_r,v_1), which cannot equal (t,r_0,r_1) because r_0 is in H-X and u_r is deleted. Splicing Q-to-P gives L_1, so equality is exactly r_0=q_n,r_1=q_{n-1}. Finally Q itself cannot be reversed into the required rail: because n>=2, its last tight turn reverses to a bad first turn by R3.

Replay bridge, Unique-B. Write P=(p_0,...,p_{m-2},w,t) and Q=(q_0,...,q_n). The P-to-Q splice has holes (w,t,q_0) and (t,q_0,q_1), hence mate clause L_1=(q_0,t,w) OR L_2=(q_1,q_0,t). A triple-deletion rail ending (...,q_1,q_0) has shield S=(t,q_0,q_1)=bar(L_2), giving the physical arc S->L_1. Because q_0 != v_1, L_1 is collision-decorated by O_B=(v_1,t,w). Enumerating the replay fan literals ending in t gives the stated converse, and R3 plus n>=2 excludes obtaining the alignment by simply reversing Q.

Double winner. The native U-to-V clause has tight literals A=(v_1,u_r,u_{r-1}) and B=(v_2,v_1,u_r). Inspecting all six ordered source-component pairs shows no mate literal equals bar(A)=(u_{r-1},u_r,v_1) or bar(B)=(u_r,v_1,v_2): the reverse ordered pair uses the initial endpoint(s) of U and terminal endpoint(s) of V, while every pair involving J contains a J vertex. Hence no native-fan arc leaves A or B. R467 already decorates A by (x,u_r,u_{r-1}) and B by (v_2,v_1,x). R463 with length-zero paths therefore reproduces exactly those two R38 collisions. Nothing in R453 or R463 compiles the concatenated tight P4 into an additional clause. This proves all parts.
