## Proof

R1036 gives the corrected literal first-loss switch whenever both adjacent closures exist:

`{f_r,g_{r-1}} <-> {e_r,g_r}`,

where the old pair is same-side and the new pair is crossing. In the non-source-rooted v-case, the physical `X` endpoint of `e_r` is the unique non-spoke vertex `v`.

Take the OUT-`v` orientation. Then

`e_r=a_{r-1}->b_r=v->b_j`

with `b_j∈B`. Same-sidedness of

`f_r=a_r->b_r`

forces `a_r∈B`. R1047 says every selected `B-B` edge in the canonical `J` is a consecutive edge of the displayed spectator path. Since `f_r` is oriented into `b_j`, it is exactly

`f_r=b_{j-1}->b_j`.

Now the other old same-side edge is

`g_{r-1}=a_{r-1}->b_m=v->b_m`.

Since this edge is same-side and `v∈X`, we have `b_m∈X`. The non-loop hypothesis gives `b_m≠v`. R1037 identifies

`X={v,p,q,r}`

with `p,q,r` precisely the three source spokes. Therefore

`b_m=t∈{p,q,r}`,

and

`g_{r-1}=v->t`.

The remaining new crossing edge is

`g_r=a_r->b_m=b_{j-1}->t`.

The four physical vertices are pairwise distinct: `b_{j-1}≠b_j` because they are consecutive vertices of the spectator path, `B∩X=∅`, and `t≠v` by nonloopness. Hence this is a genuine four-vertex `K_{2,2}` switch, not a collapsed or looped packet.

The source spoke is unique because it is the fixed far endpoint `b_m` of the canonical zipper switch.
