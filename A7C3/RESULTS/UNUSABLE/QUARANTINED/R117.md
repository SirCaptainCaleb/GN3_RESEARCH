# R117 — Zero-excess paired-anchor polarity rectangle

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the canonical zero-excess terminal six-cycle of R116. Choose a private vertex p in {alpha,beta,gamma} whose two incident terminal dimers have opposite R112 polarities, as guaranteed by R116. Let Q_i,Q_j be the two shields incident with p. Then Q_i and Q_j share exactly one further wing vertex r, and the terminal dimer of Q_i incident with r and the terminal dimer of Q_j incident with r also have opposite polarities. Thus the two shields form a four-dimer rectangle consisting of two distinct opposite-polarity stars, one centered at p and one centered at r. All four dimers are pairwise distinct except for their prescribed star-center incidences, and every one carries both distinct witnesses delta and epsilon via R114. Concretely: if p=alpha then (Q_i,Q_j,r)=(Q_x,Q_y,z) and the two stars are {alpha,y},{alpha,x} and {z,beta},{z,gamma}; if p=beta then (Q_i,Q_j,r)=(Q_x,Q_z,y) and the stars are {beta,z},{beta,x} and {y,alpha},{y,gamma}; if p=gamma then (Q_i,Q_j,r)=(Q_y,Q_z,x) and the stars are {gamma,z},{gamma,y} and {x,alpha},{x,beta}.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; simultaneous zero-excess alternative; canonical R101 zero-packet representatives; accepted R116.

## Scope
Canonical signed-incidence strengthening of the R116 private-anchor output.

## Applicability
Use when attacking the shared-anchor interaction problem. Any local argument at the parity-forced private star has a parallel opposite-polarity star on the same two shields, with the same two common marker witnesses.

## Persistence
All four physical dimers, both anchors, their shield incidences, polarities, and delta/epsilon witness certificates are graph-intrinsic once the canonical R101 zero-packet representatives are fixed.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that either opposite-polarity star already forms the terminal signed dimers of one tight trimer in the required orientations; does not couple payment descendants; does not itself force a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
R116 lists the canonical terminal dimers and states that the two terminals of each shield Q_k have opposite polarity. If p=alpha, the incident shields are Q_x and Q_y. Write s for the polarity of {alpha,y} in Q_x. Then {z,beta} has polarity -s. Since {alpha,x} in Q_y has polarity -s by the choice of alpha as a sign-flip private vertex, the other Q_y terminal {z,gamma} has polarity s. Hence the z-centered pair {z,beta},{z,gamma} is also opposite-polarity. The four dimers are distinct by the R116 six-cycle list, and R116 imports from R114 both delta and epsilon as witnesses on each. The beta and gamma cases are the cyclic relabelings: for beta use Q_x,Q_z with second anchor y; for gamma use Q_y,Q_z with second anchor x. This proves the rectangle statement.
