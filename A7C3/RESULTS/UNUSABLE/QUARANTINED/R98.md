# R98 — Minimum-overlap three-butterfly transversal-or-hub dichotomy

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let F be the family of supports of global-longest tight five-paths in the standing order-ten smallest counterexample. Choose A,B in F with minimum intersection size |A∩B|=3, and write S=A∩B, U=A\B, V=B\A, R=V(H)\(A∪B). Then exactly one of the following structural alternatives is available: (i) there exists C in F meeting R and omitting some vertex of S, in which case C has the exact block profile |C∩S|=2, |C∩U|=|C∩V|=|C∩R|=1; or (ii) no such C exists, every support meeting R has the form S∪{w,r} with w∈U∪V and r∈R, and for each s∈S every support D∈F with s∉D is R-free, contains exactly S\{s}, has three wing vertices meeting both U and V, and contains every wing vertex w that occurs in any R-touching support S∪{w,r}.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; A,B are a globally minimum-overlap pair in F and |A∩B|=3.

## Scope
Pure longest-support incidence statement in the minimum-overlap-three branch.

## Applicability
Use before opening representative-dependent crossing or return machinery. The first alternative is the four-block transversal branch; the second is the rigid support-incidence kernel.

## Persistence
The conclusion depends only on stable support membership and therefore persists across representative changes.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the transversal branch already yields overlap descent, nor that the hub kernel is impossible; it only classifies support incidences forced by minimum overlap, union F=V(H), and intersection F=empty.

## Proof
Write a=|C∩S|, u=|C∩U|, v=|C∩V|, r=|C∩R| for C∈F. Since A,B are a minimum-overlap pair with |A∩B|=3, every C∈F satisfies |C∩A|=a+u≥3 and |C∩B|=a+v≥3. Also |S|=|R|=3, |U|=|V|=2, and a+u+v+r=5.

Suppose first that C meets R and omits some vertex of S, so r≥1 and a≤2. The inequalities exclude a=0 because u,v≤2. If a=1 then u=v=2, already using all five vertices and forcing r=0, contradiction. Hence a=2. Then u,v≥1, and because r≥1 while a+u+v+r=5, necessarily u=v=r=1. This is alternative (i).

Now assume no support meeting R omits a vertex of S. Because union F=V(H) by R13, there is at least one support meeting R, and every such support contains all three vertices of S. Any R-touching support C therefore uses its remaining two vertices outside S. It cannot use two vertices of R: if C=S∪{r1,r2}, then because intersection F is empty by R13, choose any s∈S and some D∈F with s∉D. By the no-transversal assumption D cannot meet R, since D would then meet R while omitting S. Thus D∩C⊆D∩S has size at most two, contradicting the global minimum intersection three. Hence every R-touching support has exactly one R vertex and exactly one wing vertex, i.e. C=S∪{w,r} with w∈U∪V.

Fix s∈S and any D∈F with s∉D, which exists by intersection F=empty. Again D is R-free by the no-transversal assumption. Put a_D=|D∩S|≤2. Compare D with an arbitrary hub support C=S∪{w,r}. Since D is R-free, |C∩D|=a_D+1_{w∈D}≥3. Therefore a_D cannot be at most one, so a_D=2, and necessarily w∈D. Thus D∩S=S\{s}. Since |D|=5 and D is R-free, D has exactly three vertices in U∪V. The minimum-overlap inequalities against A and B give |D∩U|≥1 and |D∩V|≥1. Finally, because the hub support C was arbitrary, D contains every wing vertex w that occurs in any R-touching support. This proves alternative (ii) and the dichotomy.
