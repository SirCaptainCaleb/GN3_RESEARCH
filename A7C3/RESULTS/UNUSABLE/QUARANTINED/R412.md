# R412 — Fixed-support endpoint universality reduces to a singleton root or a collision-saturated rail

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the fixed-support factorization branch of R411 for a pair deletion H-{a,c}=P sqcup Q in a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample H: every vertex of P and Q is a physical endpoint of some Hamilton tight path on its own fixed support, and endpoint choices on the two supports may be combined independently. Call a deleted vertex t attachable to a support S if some Hamilton tight path on S has an endpoint at which direct attachment of t is tight. Then either (I) at least one of a,c is attachable to at least one support; choosing such an attachment, say c to P, and any Hamilton path on Q gives a literal exact two-cover of H-a and therefore a spanning singleton-root three-cover H={a} sqcup P_c sqcup Q that retains the fixed pair-deletion support partition away from the attached c; or (II) neither a nor c is attachable to either P or Q. In case (II), both deleted vertices are blocked at every endpoint of every Hamilton path on both fixed supports. Since |P|+|Q|=|H|-2>=9, one support, say P, has order at least five. Choose any Hamilton tight path P_0=(p_1,...,p_m) on P. The two direct a-attachments at its ends are bad, so boundary antisymmetry gives opposite-polarity signed certificates with common witness a on the two disjoint reverse terminal dimers {p_1,p_2} and {p_{m-1},p_m}. They form a direct balanced mass-four pair; the interval between the inner terminal vertices invokes R174 and yields, unless a spanning two-cover occurs, an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor on the two physical endpoints {p_1},{p_m}. Hence R249 enters a floor-aligned fixed-turn lineage. Moreover both a and c are blocked at every endpoint, so every vertex x of P or Q is the endpoint of some Hamilton path whose terminal dimer carries a same-polarity R38 collision with the two witnesses a,c. Thus the no-attachment branch is simultaneously a support-wide collision saturation and a paid-floor front door.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; R411 fixed-support factorization holds for pair {a,c} with supports P,Q; accepted R3 and R38.

## Scope
Beyond-order-ten structural reduction inside the no-cross-state fixed-support endpoint-universal branch.

## Applicability
Use after R411. Either one deleted vertex can be absorbed into one fixed rail support, producing a singleton root for the other while retaining the support partition, or both deleted vertices are globally blocked from both supports and a long-support terminal pair pays directly to an endpoint floor.

## Persistence
Attachability is existential over Hamilton paths on one fixed support. The singleton-root cover in case (I) is one chosen representative. In case (II), the collision certificate for each x may arise from a different Hamilton path P_x; the physical witness pair {a,c} and support vertex x are fixed, but the terminal neighbor may vary with x.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the vertex-indexed collision supports are distinct, simultaneous, or closing. Does not prescribe both endpoints of a Hamilton path. The singleton-root branch does not automatically inherit maximin properties.

## Proof
Let B be the bipartite graph with left side {a,c}, right side {P,Q}, and tS an edge iff t is attachable to S. If B had a perfect matching, say aP and cQ, choose a Hamilton path on P with a tight a-endcap and independently a Hamilton path on Q with a tight c-endcap. Attaching a and c gives two disjoint tight paths spanning H, contradiction; the swapped matching is identical. Thus B has no perfect matching. For a 2x2 bipartite graph, either some left vertex has degree zero, or the union of the neighborhoods of both left vertices has size one. In the first case, say deg(a)=0. If deg(c)>0, choose an edge cS and its witnessing Hamilton path; attach c there and combine with any Hamilton path on the other fixed support, producing an exact two-cover of H-a and hence the singleton root. If deg(c)=0 too, either support is blocked by both witnesses. In the second Hall-failure case, both a,c have all neighbors contained in one support, so the other support is blocked by both. Now let S be a support blocked by both a,c and fix x in S. R411 supplies a Hamilton path S_x with x as a physical endpoint. The direct attachment turn for a at that endpoint is bad by nonattachability, and so is the direct attachment turn for c. Their exact reverses are tight and use the same tested orientation of the x-terminal dimer with distinct witnesses a,c, hence are same-polarity certificates. R38 gives the collision package. Repeat separately for every x in S.
