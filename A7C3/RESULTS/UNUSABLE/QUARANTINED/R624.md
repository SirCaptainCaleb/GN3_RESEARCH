# R624 — Shallow crossed covers yield only tautological X-local R358 mate clauses

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Retain the crossed phase and the four shallow singleton-deletion covers of R621. Puncturing a left-enlarged-root shallow cover at L, or a right-enlarged-root shallow cover at R, produces an exact pair-deletion two-cover in which the middle of a natural M_S gate trimer is exposed as an endpoint, so R358 applies. However the resulting four two-hole mate clauses are respectively: for root z, (z,c,a) OR (b,z,c); for root b, (b,a,c) OR (z,b,a); for root c, (b,c,a) OR (z,b,c); for root a, (z,a,c) OR (b,z,a). Under the transitive matching-height rule M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}, the first literal is already tight in the z and b clauses, while the second literal is already tight in the c and a clauses. Hence all four clauses are satisfied by pre-existing X-local mathematics and force no new turn. Therefore the direct strategy 'puncture the R621 shallow cover at the opposite Q endpoint and consume it with R358' is a sterile route unless additional non-X information is coupled to the same physical two-hole proposals.

## Hypotheses
H is in the crossed R621 frame with m>=4. Use the four shallow covers and gate polarities from R621, accepted R358, and the accepted matching-height rule of R534.

## Scope
R24 strategy fence. Crossed middle-gate phase. Rules out the most immediate shallow-cover endpoint-return mate attack as a source of new information.

## Applicability
Use whenever the R621 shallow covers suggest deleting L or R and applying R358 to the exposed gate-trimer middle. Do not spend further effort on those four isolated mate clauses: each is already discharged by the fixed X orientation. Any useful mate attack must involve an additional proposal, a clause cycle, Q-interior data, or another ancestry-bearing interaction.

## Persistence
Retain the four explicit punctured pair-deletion covers, their gate trimers, the four literal R358 clauses, and the matching-height comparison certifying which literal in each clause is already tight.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say the punctured pair-deletion covers are useless for other comparisons. Does not rule out combining these clauses with additional physically certified clauses in an R173 cycle. Does not weaken R621, R622, or R623. Does not address deep-cover punctures or mixed pair deletions.

## Proof
Write L=q_0 and R=q_m. We inspect the four roots.

Root z. R621 gives the shallow exact cover H-z=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (R,a,c,b). Delete L, an endpoint of the Q rail, to obtain the literal two-cover H-{z,L}=Q[1,m-1] sqcup (R,a,c,b). Accepted pair-deletion exactness R5 gives pc(H-{z,L})=2, so this displayed two-cover is exact. The left fully incoming M_S gate {b,z} gives the tight turn J=(z,b,L). In the displayed return the middle b is the tail endpoint of P=(R,a,c,b). Apply the tail form of R358 with outer pair {z,L}: with u=c and v=a it gives the clause (z,c,a) OR (b,z,c). By R534's matching-height rule, M(zc)=M_R>M(ca)=M_S, so (z,c,a) is already tight. The clause adds nothing.

Root b. R621 gives H-b=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (R,c,a,z). Delete L to obtain H-{b,L}=Q[1,m-1] sqcup (R,c,a,z), exact by R5. The same fully incoming gate gives J=(b,z,L), with middle z the tail endpoint of the P4. R358 gives (b,a,c) OR (z,b,a). Since M(ba)=M_R>M(ac)=M_S, (b,a,c) is already tight.

Root c. R621 gives H-c=(a,b,z,L) sqcup Q[1,m]. Delete R to obtain H-{c,R}=(a,b,z,L) sqcup Q[1,m-1], exact by R5. The right fully outgoing M_S gate {a,c} gives J=(R,a,c). Here middle a is the head endpoint of P=(a,b,z,L). The head form of R358 gives (b,c,a) OR (z,b,c). Since M(zb)=M_S>M(bc)=M_L, (z,b,c) is already tight.

Root a. R621 gives H-a=(c,z,b,L) sqcup Q[1,m]. Delete R to obtain H-{a,R}=(c,z,b,L) sqcup Q[1,m-1], exact by R5. The same outgoing gate gives J=(R,c,a), with middle c the head endpoint. R358 gives (z,a,c) OR (b,z,a). Since M(bz)=M_S>M(za)=M_L, (b,z,a) is already tight.

Thus every isolated R358 clause produced by this shallow-cover puncture is already satisfied by a known X-only turn. No new orientation is forced, proving the route fence.
