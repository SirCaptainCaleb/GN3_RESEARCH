# R127 — Minimum longest-five support overlap three is impossible

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
In the standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case, the global-longest five-support family F cannot contain a globally minimum-overlap pair A,B with |A∩B|=3.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case and the accepted global-longest support family F.

## Scope
Parent-level elimination of the entire minimum-overlap-three butterfly branch, including both transversal and hub descendants and all zero-/positive-excess subbranches.

## Applicability
Use immediately after obtaining a candidate globally minimum-overlap pair of size three. All conditional descendants R98-R125 become unnecessary for closure once this theorem is accepted, though they remain valid conditional mathematics.

## Persistence
The contradiction is graph-intrinsic and independent of representative choice.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not exclude minimum overlap one or two and therefore does not by itself close the order-ten smallest-counterexample case unless those smaller overlaps are separately excluded.

## Proof
Suppose A,B∈F are a globally minimum-overlap pair with |A∩B|=3, and let S=A∩B. By universal small-deletion exactness R5, H-S has an exact two-cover. By the accepted common-triple cap R105, every such exact cover has profile 4+3; write its components as a tight four-path Q and a disjoint tight trimer K. But R126 applies to Q and K and forces a tight path of order five inside V(Q)∪V(K)=V(H)\\S. This contradicts the first conclusion of R105 that H-S contains no tight path of order five. Therefore no globally minimum-overlap pair with overlap three can exist.
