# R105 — Common-triple deletion longest-path cap

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let A,B be globally minimum-overlap longest-five supports with |A∩B|=3 and S=A∩B. Then H-S has no tight path of order five. Hence every exact two-cover of H-S has the 4+3 profile from R99, and if Q=(q1,q2,q3,q4) is its four-path and K its three-path, then for every k in K the direct endpoint triples (k,q1,q2) and (q3,q4,k) are bad, so by boundary antisymmetry the reverse triples (q2,q1,k) and (k,q4,q3) are tight.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; A,B are a globally minimum-overlap pair with overlap three.

## Scope
Minimum-overlap common-triple deletion frame.

## Applicability
Use in either zero- or positive-excess faces of R99.

## Persistence
The path-order-five obstruction is support-level; endpoint turns are relative to the chosen four-path ordering.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
No claim about internal attachments or immediate two-cover closure.

## Proof
Suppose H-S contained a tight path P of order five. By R9, order five is globally longest, so supp(P) belongs to the longest-five support family. Since supp(P) is disjoint from S and A=S union (A-S) with |A-S|=2, we have |supp(P)∩A| at most 2, contradicting that A belongs to a globally minimum-overlap pair of value 3. Thus H-S has no tight five-path. By R99, every exact two-cover of H-S has profile 4+3. Let Q=(q1,q2,q3,q4) be its four-path and K its three-path. For k in K, if (k,q1,q2) were tight then (k,q1,q2,q3,q4) would be a forbidden five-path in H-S; similarly if (q3,q4,k) were tight then (q1,q2,q3,q4,k) would be a forbidden five-path. Both triples are bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 gives the tight reversals (q2,q1,k) and (k,q4,q3).
