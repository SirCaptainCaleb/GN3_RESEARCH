# R336 — Outer-pair endpoint return yields a literal rooted two-hole mate clause

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the literal first-contact mirror-trimer branch of accepted R329, with tight current contact trimer J=(p,x,z). Choose an exact two-cover P sqcup Q of H-{p,z} in which the retained captured anchor x is an endpoint of P; by R168, |P|>=3. If P is ordered P=(x,a,b,...) from that endpoint, then the spanning two-cover proposal P*=(p,x,z,a,b,...) together with Q has exactly two uncertified turns, (x,z,a) and (z,a,b). Since H has no spanning two-cover, at least one is bad, so boundary antisymmetry gives the literal mate clause (a,z,x) tight OR (b,a,z) tight. Dually, if P=(...,b,a,x), then the proposal (...,b,a,p,x,z) together with Q has exactly the two holes (b,a,p),(a,p,x), yielding the clause (p,a,b) tight OR (x,p,a) tight. Thus every endpoint branch of the outer-pair return produces an explicit two-hole R173-admissible candidate clause anchored on one boundary side of the role-sensitive contact trimer.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; the R329 branch-(ii) current trimer J=(p,x,z) is given; P sqcup Q is an exact two-cover of H-{p,z}; x is an endpoint of P.

## Scope
Order-free literal candidate-cover lemma inside the rooted first-contact mirror-trimer normal form.

## Applicability
Use after the endpoint branch of the outer-pair deletion return. It converts endpoint exposure of the captured anchor into a physical two-hole mate clause suitable for R173 chains or further seam classification.

## Persistence

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
Suppose first P=(x,a,b,...) with |P|>=3. The sequence (p,x,z,a,b,...) uses the already tight turn (p,x,z) from J, then requires only the new seam turns (x,z,a) and (z,a,b); all later turns are inherited from P starting with (a,b,...). Together with Q this would be a spanning two-cover of H if both seam turns were tight. Hence at least one is bad. R3 says the exact reverse of a bad turn is tight, giving (a,z,x) or (b,a,z). If instead P=(...,b,a,x), the candidate (...,b,a,p,x,z) inherits all earlier P turns and J, with only (b,a,p) and (a,p,x) new. Their exact reversals are (p,a,b) and (x,p,a).
