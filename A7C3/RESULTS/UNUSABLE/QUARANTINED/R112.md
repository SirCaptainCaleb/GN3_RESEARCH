# R112 — Transversal zero-excess shield four-family common-mate lift

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the simultaneous zero-excess transversal kernel of R102. For each i in {x,y,z}, fix an ordering Q_i=(q_{i1},q_{i2},q_{i3},q_{i4}) of its tight four-path component, and let M={delta,epsilon}. For each w in M, the three oriented left endpoint dimers L_i(w)=(q_{i2},q_{i1}) are graph-intrinsic signed supports of one common polarity with common witness w, while the three oriented right endpoint dimers R_i(w)=(q_{i4},q_{i3}) are graph-intrinsic signed supports of the opposite polarity with the same witness w. For each fixed w, there exists one graph-intrinsic opposite-polarity support E_L(w) that forms a balanced pair with every L_i(w), and separately one graph-intrinsic opposite-polarity support E_R(w) that forms a balanced pair with every R_i(w). Hence the zero-excess equality kernel carries four three-shield common-mate support families, indexed by the two common marker witnesses and the two endpoint sides.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case and the R102 simultaneous zero-excess transversal kernel, with one fixed ordering chosen for each of Q_x,Q_y,Q_z.

## Scope
Graph-intrinsic signed-support compilation directly from the three R102 shields. This is a pre-payment statement and does not require the R50 pivot/reverse classification or the R51/R52 fold machinery.

## Applicability
Use before opening local return or payment descendants. It provides the common-mate fan interface directly in the zero-excess kernel and is a bridge from the common delta/epsilon endpoint shields to support-family actualization.

## Persistence
For a fixed ordering of each Q_i, the reverse endpoint turns and resulting signed dimers are graph-intrinsic by R102. The common opposite supports supplied by R40 are graph-intrinsic pre-payment certificates.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The three Q_i need not be simultaneously current. The supports within one family need not be distinct. E_L(w) and E_R(w), and the supports for delta versus epsilon, need not coincide. No coupling of later paid descendants is asserted, and no spanning two-cover follows from this lemma alone.

## Proof
Fix w in {delta,epsilon}. For i in {x,y,z}, write the chosen R102 four-path as Q_i=(q_{i1},q_{i2},q_{i3},q_{i4}). R102 says that the direct endpoint attachments (w,q_{i1},q_{i2}) and (q_{i3},q_{i4},w) are bad and therefore, by boundary antisymmetry, the reverse turns (q_{i2},q_{i1},w) and (w,q_{i4},q_{i3}) are tight. In a tight trimer, its first terminal oriented dimer has one polarity with the third vertex as witness, while its second terminal oriented dimer has the opposite polarity with the first vertex as witness. Consequently L_i(w)=(q_{i2},q_{i1}) is a signed dimer support of the same polarity for all three i with common witness w, and R_i(w)=(q_{i4},q_{i3}) is a signed dimer support of the opposite polarity for all three i with the same witness w. These signs are graph-intrinsic once the Q_i orderings are fixed, exactly as recorded in R102.\n\nConsider first {L_x(w),L_y(w),L_z(w)}. Each support has two vertices, so their union has size at most six. Because w is the sign witness, w lies outside every support. Thus the union of the three supports together with w uses at most seven of the ten vertices. At least three vertices remain. Choose any two of those spare vertices as a physical dimer J. Then J is a nontrivial marker path disjoint from every L_i(w) and from the common witness w. R40 applies to the three graph-intrinsic supports with common polarity and common witness and yields one opposite-polarity support E_L(w) such that E_L(w) forms a balanced pair with each L_i(w). The identical counting argument applied to {R_x(w),R_y(w),R_z(w)} yields one common opposite support E_R(w). Repeating for w=delta and w=epsilon gives four three-shield common-mate families. No payment descendants are coupled, in accordance with the fence in R40.
