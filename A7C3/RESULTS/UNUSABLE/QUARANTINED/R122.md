# R122 — Positive-excess wing-interleaving or six-exterior shield dichotomy

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let A,B be a globally minimum-overlap pair of global-longest five-supports with |A∩B|=3, and use the R99 blocks S=A∩B, U=A\\B, V=B\\A, R=V(H)\\(A∪B). Let Q=(q1,q2,q3,q4) and K be the 4- and 3-vertex components of an exact two-cover of H-S having positive R99 excess. Then exactly one of the following structural alternatives holds. (I) supp(Q)=U∪V and supp(K)=R; the positive excess is entirely wing-interleaving, i.e. the Q ordering has at least two U/V class transitions rather than the zero-excess one-transition concatenation. (II) One wing W∈{U,V} satisfies |supp(Q)∩W|≤1. For the corresponding longest support L=S∪W, every five-set supp(Q)∪{s}, s∈S, is non-Hamiltonian, since any tight five-path on it would be global-longest and would meet L in at most two vertices. Hence for every s∈S the direct endpoint turns (s,q1,q2) and (q3,q4,s) are bad and the reverse turns (q2,q1,s) and (s,q4,q3) are tight. Combining this with R105, the same two reverse endpoint turns are tight for every k∈K. Thus every one of the six exterior vertices S∪supp(K)=V(H)\\supp(Q) is a witness for both terminal reverse-turn systems of Q.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; R99 minimum-overlap-three common-triple frame; an exact H-S two-cover with positive excess.

## Scope
Support-level and endpoint-turn compression of the positive-excess R99 branch.

## Applicability
Use as the parent split for the remaining transversal positive-excess branch. Attack the pure U/V interleaving face separately; otherwise exploit the six-exterior terminal shield without reopening arbitrary return trees.

## Persistence
The support dichotomy and non-Hamiltonicity of Q∪{s} are physical support facts. The endpoint reverse turns are graph-intrinsic relative to the displayed Q ordering.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the six witness certificates can be paid simultaneously, does not by itself force overlap descent, and does not classify the U/V interleaving face beyond its transition count.

## Proof
Let Q and K be the displayed 4+3 exact cover of H-S. If |Q∩U|=|Q∩V|=2, then |Q|=4 forces supp(Q)=U∪V, hence supp(K)=R. R99 says zero excess in this support grouping occurs exactly for a one-transition concatenation of the U and V wing dimers. Since the present cover has positive excess, its Q ordering has at least two U/V transitions. This is alternative (I).\n\nOtherwise one of U,V, call it W, satisfies |Q∩W|≤1. Put L=S∪W, so L is A or B and is a global-longest five-support. Fix s∈S. If supp(Q)∪{s} supported any tight five-path, then by the order-ten longest-path value R9 it would itself be a global-longest support. But its intersection with L is contained in (Q∩W)∪{s}, hence has size at most 2, contradicting that A,B were chosen with globally minimum longest-support overlap 3. Thus supp(Q)∪{s} is non-Hamiltonian. In particular neither displayed endpoint extension (s,q1,q2,q3,q4) nor (q1,q2,q3,q4,s) can be tight. Therefore the endpoint turns (s,q1,q2) and (q3,q4,s) are bad; boundary antisymmetry R3 yields (q2,q1,s) and (s,q4,q3) tight. This holds for every s∈S. Independently, R105 gives (q2,q1,k) and (k,q4,q3) tight for every k∈K. Since Q,K,S are disjoint and exhaust the ten vertices, S∪K is exactly the six-vertex exterior of Q. Hence both terminal reverse-turn systems of Q have all six exterior vertices as witnesses, proving (II).
