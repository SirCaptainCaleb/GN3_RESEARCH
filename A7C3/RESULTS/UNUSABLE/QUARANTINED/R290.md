# R290 — R239 historical captures aggregate at an arbitrary fixed exterior anchor

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the non-Hamiltonian five-exterior equality face of R239, with retained global-longest carrier K, exterior E, and captured carrier-vertex set C of size at least N=max(0,ceil(4|K|/5)-26). Fix distinct exterior vertices d,y in E. For every b in C, the historical R239 capture event at b remains valid on its original carrier crossing, and independently the unique d-middle tight trimer on {b,d,y} gives by R41 a signed certificate on the fresh spoke {b,d} with the same witness y. Thus the same index set C simultaneously labels historical carrier captures and a fixed-anchor common-witness spoke-sign fan of size at least N. No capture ancestry is attached to the spokes.

## Hypotheses
Hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the R239 five-exterior equality face; d,y distinct exterior vertices; accepted R3,R41,R239.

## Scope
Safe co-indexing replacement for the invalid R259 capture-aggregation theorem.

## Applicability
Use when a later argument needs to compare where captures occur with a fresh sign fan, while respecting that the two resources come from different certificates.

## Persistence
Each R239 capture remains graph-intrinsic historical ancestry on its original crossing. The fresh d-middle trimer and spoke sign are graph-intrinsic. The common index b is physical and persistent.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not invoke R155; does not reenter a capture onto {b,d}; does not claim source deletion frames or fresh trimers are simultaneous; does not attach capture ancestry to any spoke.

## Proof
R239 already supplies the historical capture event at each b in C. Because b lies on K and d,y lie in E, the three vertices are distinct. R3 gives exactly one tight d-middle orientation of {b,d,y}, and R41 makes {b,d} a signed terminal support with witness y. No transport theorem is used. Repeating over C gives a fan of the same cardinality N, co-indexed with but not descended from the capture events.
