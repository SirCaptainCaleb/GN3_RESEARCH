# R100 — Transversal branch symmetric minimum-overlap triangle

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
In the minimum-overlap-three butterfly of R98, assume the transversal alternative and choose C∈F with profile |C∩S|=2 and |C∩U|=|C∩V|=|C∩R|=1. Then |A∩C|=|B∩C|=3, so A,B,C are pairwise globally minimum-overlap supports. Their triple intersection T=A∩B∩C has size 2 and their union has size 8. Equivalently, there are uniquely determined vertices x,y,z,alpha,beta,gamma outside T and two exterior vertices delta,epsilon such that A=T∪{x,y,alpha}, B=T∪{x,z,beta}, C=T∪{y,z,gamma}, with {delta,epsilon}=V(H)\\(A∪B∪C). For each of the three minimum-overlap pairs, the third support is again a (2,1,1,1) transversal in that pair's butterfly; its private vertex is the unique non-exterior vertex in that pair's R-block.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; A,B are globally minimum-overlap with |A∩B|=3; C is a transversal supplied by alternative (i) of R98.

## Scope
Pure support-incidence normalization of the transversal branch.

## Applicability
Use to rotate the butterfly among the three supports and obtain three equivalent common-triple deletion frames.

## Persistence
All coordinates are physical support-membership coordinates and persist across representative changes.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert existence of a fourth support, overlap descent, or a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
By R98 alternative (i), C contains exactly two vertices of S=A∩B, one vertex of U=A\\B, one vertex of V=B\\A, and one vertex of R=V(H)\\(A∪B). Hence |A∩C|=2+1=3 and |B∩C|=2+1=3. Since A,B were chosen with globally minimum support intersection 3, the three pairs A,B; A,C; B,C are all globally minimum-overlap pairs.\n\nThe triple intersection A∩B∩C is C∩S, so it has size two; call it T. Because each pairwise intersection has size three, there is exactly one vertex x in (A∩B)\\C, exactly one y in (A∩C)\\B, and exactly one z in (B∩C)\\A. Each support has size five, so after accounting for T and its two pair-only vertices, each has exactly one private vertex: alpha in A\\(B∪C), beta in B\\(A∪C), gamma in C\\(A∪B). Thus A=T∪{x,y,alpha}, B=T∪{x,z,beta}, C=T∪{y,z,gamma}. The union therefore has 2+3+3=8 vertices, leaving exactly two exterior vertices delta,epsilon.\n\nNow consider the pair A,C. Its common triple is T∪{y}; its two wings are {x,alpha} and {z,gamma}; its R-block is {beta,delta,epsilon}. The third support B=T∪{x,z,beta} contains exactly two vertices of the common triple T∪{y}, one vertex x of the first wing, one vertex z of the second wing, and the unique non-exterior R-vertex beta. Hence B is a (2,1,1,1) transversal for the A,C butterfly. The same argument cyclically shows that C is the transversal for A,B and A is the transversal for B,C. This proves the stated symmetric normalization.
