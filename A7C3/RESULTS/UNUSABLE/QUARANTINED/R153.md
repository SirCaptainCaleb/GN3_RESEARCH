# R153 — Universal two-orientation dimer common shadow

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let D={x,y} be any physical vertex pair. There exists one fixed exact two-cover H-D=U⊔V such that both U⊔V⊔(x,y) and U⊔V⊔(y,x) are minimum three-cover representatives of H. Thus the two opposite orientations of the same physical dimer can be compared over literally identical residual rails U,V. Relative to the four-component shadow W=U⊔V⊔(x)⊔(y), inserting either dimer has gain one and cycle debt zero.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the standing project sense; x,y are distinct vertices and D={x,y}.

## Scope
Universal arbitrary-pair common-shadow lemma. It is not restricted to endpoint-transfer edges, special markers, pair-deletion rigidity, or an already chosen fan. It refines R5 by retaining one chosen exact residual two-cover as a shared literal shadow for both dimer orientations.

## Applicability
Use whenever two opposite orientations of one physical pair must be tested or compared without changing the residual rails. This supplies a lawful same-shadow bridge for mirror arguments, orientation classification, sign tests, and pair-deletion consumers.

## Persistence
The residual rails U,V are literally the same in the two displayed minimum covers. The selected dimer orientation differs. Any graph-intrinsic fact about U,V remains true across the comparison; unrelated current facts are not preserved unless encoded in U or V. The common-shadow guarantee is existential in the choice of one exact H-D two-cover, not universal over every exact complement.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not imply that both dimer orientations are simultaneously selected in one cover; does not give nontriviality or size bounds for U,V beyond R5; does not supply pair-deletion rigidity; does not preserve arbitrary ancestry or roles; does not imply a progress measure, capture, protection, or spanning two-cover. Special common-shadow theorems remain nonredundant when they retain extra labels or ancestry.
