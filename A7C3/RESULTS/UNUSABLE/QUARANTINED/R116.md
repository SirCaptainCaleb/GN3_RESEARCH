# R116 — Zero-excess canonical terminal six-cycle and private-anchor parity

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the simultaneous zero-excess transversal kernel, and choose Q_x,Q_y,Q_z to be the canonical zero-packet four-path representatives supplied by R101. Then their terminal physical dimers are exactly Q_x:{y,alpha},{z,beta}; Q_y:{x,alpha},{z,gamma}; Q_z:{x,beta},{y,gamma}. These six physical dimers are pairwise distinct and form the incidence six-cycle Q_x-alpha-Q_y-gamma-Q_z-beta-Q_x, where each edge records the unique terminal dimer incident with that shield and private vertex. Under the R112 signed-terminal-dimer polarities, the two edges incident with every Q_i have opposite polarity. Consequently an odd number of the three private vertices alpha,beta,gamma have opposite-polarity incident dimers; in particular at least one private vertex p is the common physical anchor of two distinct opposite-polarity terminal dimers. By R114, each of those two dimers also carries same-polarity signed certificates with both distinct witnesses delta and epsilon. Thus the canonical equality kernel contains a doubly-witnessed opposite-polarity private-anchor pair.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; simultaneous zero-excess alternative of R101; canonical zero-packet representatives Q_x,Q_y,Q_z; accepted R112 and R114 signed-support conclusions.

## Scope
Canonical representative-level endpoint geometry and sign parity in the transversal zero-excess branch. This intentionally uses the named wing-concatenation representatives from R101, not arbitrary alternate tight four-path orderings on the same W_i supports.

## Applicability
Use before R115 repetition branching. It collapses the canonical zero packets directly to the no-repeat physical-dimer case and exposes at least one private vertex carrying two opposite-polarity, delta/epsilon-doubly-witnessed dimers. The next target is a shared-anchor interaction theorem at that private vertex.

## Persistence
The six physical terminal dimers are fixed by the canonical R101 zero-packet representatives. Their R112 polarities and R114 witness certificates are graph-intrinsic once those representative orderings are fixed.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not invalidate R115 for alternate tight four-path orderings on the same four-set supports. Does not assert that the two opposite-polarity dimers at the private anchor are terminal dimers of one tight trimer, does not couple later payment descendants, and does not by itself force a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
In the x-frame, R101 says that the canonical zero-excess four-component Q_x is obtained by concatenating the tight wing dimer on {y,alpha} with the tight wing dimer on {z,beta} using exactly one inter-wing transition. Hence its two terminal physical dimers are {y,alpha} and {z,beta}. Cyclically, the terminal physical dimers of Q_y are {x,alpha} and {z,gamma}, and those of Q_z are {x,beta} and {y,gamma}. All six pairs are distinct. Regrouping them by their private vertex yields alpha incident with the Q_x dimer {y,alpha} and Q_y dimer {x,alpha}; gamma incident with the Q_y dimer {z,gamma} and Q_z dimer {y,gamma}; beta incident with the Q_z dimer {x,beta} and Q_x dimer {z,beta}. Thus the shield/private incidence graph of these six dimers is the six-cycle Q_x-alpha-Q_y-gamma-Q_z-beta-Q_x. Fix the R112 polarity labels on these six oriented terminal dimers. At every shield vertex Q_i the two incident terminal dimers have opposite polarity, so passage through each of Q_x,Q_y,Q_z flips sign. Traversing the whole six-cycle returns to the initial dimer polarity; therefore the total number of sign flips over all six vertices is even. The three shield vertices contribute exactly three flips, hence the three private vertices contribute an odd number of flips. Therefore one or three of alpha,beta,gamma have opposite-polarity incident dimers, and in particular at least one does. Finally R114 applies to every chosen terminal dimer and says that its delta-indexed and epsilon-indexed certificates are the same oriented physical support with the same polarity and distinct witnesses. Thus each of the two opposite-polarity dimers at the selected private anchor carries both witnesses delta and epsilon. This is the claimed doubly-witnessed opposite-polarity private-anchor pair.
