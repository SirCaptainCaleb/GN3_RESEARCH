# R121 — Zero-excess paired-anchor marker bridge forces overlap descent

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
In the canonical simultaneous zero-excess transversal kernel, the paired-anchor rectangle of R117 together with the alignment theorem R120 is impossible. More precisely, choose the parity-forced private anchor p and its companion anchor r from R117, and orient the two R120 middle trimers as J_p=(u,p,v) and J_r=(a,r,b). For either common marker witness w in {delta,epsilon}, R120 gives the four tight witness turns (u,p,w), (w,p,v), (a,r,w), (w,r,b). Boundary antisymmetry on the middle triple {p,w,r} then yields a tight five-path: either (u,p,w,r,b) or (a,r,w,p,v). Its support is global-longest, but according as p=alpha,beta,gamma it meets A,B,C respectively in exactly two vertices, contradicting the global minimum overlap three of R100. Hence the simultaneous zero-excess alternative cannot occur.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; R100 transversal minimum-overlap triangle; simultaneous zero-excess alternative; accepted R117 paired-anchor rectangle and R120 sign alignment.

## Scope
Direct closure of the canonical zero-excess transversal equality kernel by a support-level marker bridge. No payment, actualization, collision descent, or representative coupling is used.

## Applicability
Use after the R101 zero-packet enters simultaneous zero excess. This eliminates that branch outright, leaving the positive-excess alternative as the remaining R101 transversal branch.

## Persistence
The contradiction is support-level: the constructed order-five path is graph-intrinsic, and its overlap-two relation with the relevant one of A,B,C is physical support incidence.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not address the positive-excess branch and does not by itself prove C2. It uses one common witness only; no simultaneous use of delta and epsilon is asserted.

## Proof
Choose the parity-forced private anchor p from R117 and let r be its companion anchor on the same two shields. By R120 choose tight middle-trimer orientations J_p=(u,p,v) and J_r=(a,r,b), where {u,v} are the two outer vertices of the p-star and {a,b} the two outer vertices of the r-star. Fix w=delta (the same proof works with epsilon). Because R120 says the pre-existing rectangle polarities align componentwise with the terminal polarities induced by these trimers, and because w is a same-polarity witness on every rectangle dimer, the exact ordered-turn semantics give (u,p,w) and (w,p,v) tight at p, and (a,r,w) and (w,r,b) tight at r.\n\nApply R3 to the three distinct vertices p,w,r. If (p,w,r) is tight, then u,p,w,r,b is a tight path of order five, using consecutive tight triples (u,p,w), (p,w,r), (w,r,b). If (p,w,r) is bad, R3 makes its complete reversal (r,w,p) tight, and then a,r,w,p,v is a tight path of order five, using (a,r,w), (r,w,p), (w,p,v). In either case all five vertices are distinct by the R117 rectangle geometry and because w is one of the exterior marker vertices. By R9 the resulting five-path is global-longest.\n\nNow use the concrete R117 cases together with the R100 coordinates A=T∪{x,y,alpha}, B=T∪{x,z,beta}, C=T∪{y,z,gamma}. If p=alpha then r=z, the p-outers are x,y and the r-outers are beta,gamma. Either constructed support therefore has the form {alpha,z,w,o_p,o_r} with o_p in {x,y} and o_r in {beta,gamma}; it meets A in exactly {alpha,o_p}, hence in two vertices. If p=beta then r=y, the p-outers are x,z and the r-outers are alpha,gamma, so either support meets B exactly in {beta,o_p}, again two vertices. If p=gamma then r=x, the p-outers are y,z and the r-outers are alpha,beta, so either support meets C exactly in {gamma,o_p}, again two vertices. Each contradicts R100, since A,B,C are globally minimum-overlap longest-five supports with minimum overlap three, so no global-longest five-support can meet any of them in only two vertices. Therefore the simultaneous zero-excess transversal kernel is impossible.
