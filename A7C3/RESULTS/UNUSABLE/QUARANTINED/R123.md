# R123 — Transversal common-triple universal four-witness shield

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
In the R100 transversal coordinates, consider the x-frame S_x=T∪{x} for the pair A,B, and let Q=(q1,q2,q3,q4) and K be the 4- and 3-vertex components of any exact two-cover of H-S_x, with no assumption on R99 excess. Then for every w in {x}∪supp(K), both direct endpoint turns (w,q1,q2) and (q3,q4,w) are bad, while the reverse turns (q2,q1,w) and (w,q4,q3) are tight. Thus the two terminal reverse-turn systems of Q have the same four graph-intrinsic witnesses {x}∪supp(K). Cyclically the same statement holds in the y- and z-frames, with distinguished witnesses y and z respectively. In the simultaneous zero-excess face, K is the canonical R-block trimer and this recovers the corresponding four-witness portion of R102.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; R100 transversal minimum-overlap triangle; any exact two-cover of one of its common-triple deletion frames.

## Scope
Excess-independent endpoint shield for the transversal triangle.

## Applicability
Use as the common parent endpoint package in both positive- and zero-excess transversal analysis. In the remaining positive-excess branch, combine it with class-transition information rather than rebuilding witness turns case by case.

## Persistence
The distinguished witness and K support are physical for the displayed deletion cover; reverse turns are graph-intrinsic relative to the displayed Q ordering.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify K across different exact covers, does not assert simultaneous currentness across the three deletion frames, and does not by itself force a five-path or spanning two-cover.

## Proof
Work in the x-frame. By R105, for every k∈K the turns (k,q1,q2) and (q3,q4,k) are bad and (q2,q1,k),(k,q4,q3) are tight. We show the same for x. Let U_x={y,alpha} and V_x={z,beta}. If supp(Q)=U_x∪V_x, then supp(Q)∪{x} intersects C=T∪{y,z,gamma} exactly in {y,z}, since x is not in C and T is deleted. Any tight five-path on supp(Q)∪{x} would be global-longest by R9 and would therefore contradict the globally minimum overlap three of the R100 triangle. Hence this five-set is non-Hamiltonian.\n\nIf supp(Q)≠U_x∪V_x, then because |Q|=4 at least one wing W∈{U_x,V_x} satisfies |Q∩W|≤1. Let L be A when W=U_x and B when W=V_x. Since L=S_x∪W and T⊂S_x is absent from Q while x∈S_x, the five-set supp(Q)∪{x} meets L in at most {x} together with Q∩W, hence in at most two vertices. Again any tight five-path on that support would be global-longest and contradict minimum overlap three. Thus supp(Q)∪{x} is non-Hamiltonian in all cases. In particular the two endpoint extensions (x,q1,q2,q3,q4) and (q1,q2,q3,q4,x) fail, so (x,q1,q2) and (q3,q4,x) are bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 gives (q2,q1,x) and (x,q4,q3) tight. Together with the K-witness conclusions from R105, the common witness set is {x}∪K. The y- and z-frame statements follow by the R100 cyclic symmetry.
