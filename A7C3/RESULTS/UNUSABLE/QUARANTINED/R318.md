# R318 — Order-eleven singleton root forces a mixed global-longest bridge support

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume an order-eleven 5+5+1 singleton root H=K sqcup Q sqcup {e} in the R315/R316 residue, with ordered rails K=(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4,k_5) and Q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4,q_5). Use the left reverse P4 and leftover trimer from R317. Writing the reverse P4 in its natural tight order on support A={k_1,k_2,q_1,q_2} and the disjoint trimer support T={k_3,q_3,e}, accepted R138 forces a tight P5 support S in its six-support family. Since the global longest-path order is five, S is global-longest. Its overlap signature with the original rails and singleton is necessarily one of (|S∩K|,|S∩Q|,1_{e∈S}) in {(3,2,0),(2,3,0),(2,2,1),(3,1,1),(1,3,1)}. In particular S is distinct from K and Q, meets both rails nontrivially, and uses at most three vertices of either rail. Thus every R196 singleton root in the order-eleven residue forces a third mixed global-longest support bridging its two disjoint longest rails.

## Hypotheses
Order-eleven longest-five residue; one R196 singleton root; accepted R317 and R138.

## Scope
Finite support-exchange consequence of the equal-rail 4+4+3 normal form.

## Applicability
Use to turn a disjoint longest-support pair in the order-eleven residue into a third mixed longest support with a five-pattern overlap list.

## Persistence
K,Q,e,A,T and the resulting support S are graph-intrinsic once the root orderings are fixed. The theorem asserts existence of at least one support in the listed family, not a synchronized path representative with the root.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify which of the five overlap signatures occurs. Does not say the new support is disjoint from another prescribed support. Does not by itself produce a P6 or two-cover.

## Proof
R317 supplies a tight reverse P4 whose support is A={k_1,k_2,q_1,q_2} and a disjoint tight trimer on T={k_3,q_3,e}. Apply R138. Its candidates are A union {k_3}, A union {q_3}, A union {e}, T union the K-side terminal pair of A, T union the Q-side terminal pair of A, and T union the two outer endpoints of A. Their overlap signatures are respectively (3,2,0), (2,3,0), (2,2,1), (3,1,1), (1,3,1), and (2,2,1). At least one supports a tight P5. Since no proper tight path has order more than five in this residue, that P5 is global-longest. Every listed support contains vertices from both K and Q, so it is distinct from both original rails.
