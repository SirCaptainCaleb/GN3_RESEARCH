# R447 — Pair-deletion four-endcap matrix forces a paid floor or triple terminal collision

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample of order n>=11, let {a,c} be any physical pair, and let H-{a,c}=U sqcup V be any exact two-cover. The R402 endcap matrix admits the following finite consumer. Either H has a spanning two-cover; or (I) one deleted vertex, say a, is blocked at all four rail ends. Then accepted R402/R174 yields an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor on the two physical endpoints h,t of a literal long residual rail. Choose the blocked deleted vertex a as the third vertex of that floor and orient the unique a-middle tight turn J on {h,a,t}. R249 enters a lawful floor-aligned fixed-turn lineage for J, and R387 yields either an explicit R173-admissible mate clause anchored on J or an accepted historical contact/pair interaction localized to the same turn lineage; or (II) G_a=G_c={e} for one unique common good end position. At e the direct attachments of both a and c to the same source rail are tight. If e is a head endpoint p, two applications of R164 in opposite witness orders have strict-extension alternatives that would prepend both deleted vertices to the whole rail and close with the untouched other rail; hence in the nonclosing branch both (p,a,c) and (p,c,a) are forced. Thus both orientations of the deleted dimer {a,c} are head-signed with common witness p and R407 gives a fixed-trimer historical interaction localized at p. The tail-end case is dual, forcing both (c,a,q) and (a,c,q) and applying R407 at q. Consequently every exact pair-deletion endcap matrix compiles to a spanning two-cover, a literal mate clause/historical interaction through a blocked deleted vertex, or one endpoint-local R407 interaction at the unique common good end. The three bad ends are not asserted to satisfy the R407 bidirectional same-witness hypothesis.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample with n>=11; {a,c} is any physical pair; H-{a,c}=U sqcup V is any exact two-cover. Use accepted R402 for the endcap dichotomy, R174/R249/R387 for the blocked-end paid-floor branch, and R164/R407 for the common-good-end collision branch.

## Scope
Order-free beyond-order-ten strengthening of accepted R402. It consumes both nonclosing R402 branches: the blocked-end floor is routed immediately through a fixed turn whose middle is the blocked deleted vertex, while the common-good-end branch yields one certified R407 interaction at the unique end where both deleted vertices attach tightly. No endpoint-universality, capture, longest-path, or density hypothesis is used.

## Applicability
Use on any exact pair-deletion cover when one wants an actual downstream consumer rather than stopping at R402's paid floor or collision-web labels. The blocked deleted vertex is the canonical middle for case I. In case II, consume the unique common good end by double R164 and R407; do not infer the same packet at the three bad ends.

## Persistence
In case (I), retain the blocked deleted vertex a, the long-rail physical endpoints h,t, the ancestry-bearing floor, and the chosen a-middle fixed turn through the R249/R387 lineage. In case (II), retain the source deleted pair {a,c}, the unique common good end e, the two forced reverse-contact trimers at that end, the two same-polarity orientations of {a,c} with common endpoint witness, and the resulting R407 fixed-trimer interaction. Later descendants are lineage-relative and need not coexist.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert a single mate clause or historical interaction closes O4. Does not assert R407 packets at the three bad ends of case II. Does not assert later floor and interaction descendants coexist. Does not treat tight cycles as contradictions. Does not assume the source exact pair-deletion cover persists into the case-I fixed-turn lineage. Does not invoke opposite-polarity same-singleton collision.

## Proof
Start from accepted R402. If its immediate spanning-two-cover branch occurs, stop. Suppose case (I), say a is blocked at all four rail ends. R402 gives a literal long residual rail L with physical endpoints h,t and, through its R174 refund, an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor {h},{t}. The deleted vertex a is distinct from h,t, so orient by boundary antisymmetry the unique a-middle tight turn J on {h,a,t}. Apply R249 to this specific floor and this middle a to enter a lawful pair-bearing fixed-turn lineage whose outer target pair is exactly {h,t}. Since n>=11, R387 applies: unless H closes, the lineage yields either an explicit R173-admissible two-hole mate clause anchored on J or an accepted explicit historical contact/pair exit localized to the same fixed-turn continuation. Thus the R402 paid floor is not terminal, and the original blocked deleted vertex remains the chosen middle coordinate. Now suppose case (II), so G_a=G_c={e} for one common good end position. At that one end, direct attachment of both deleted vertices a and c to the same source rail is tight. If e is a head endpoint p with next rail vertex r, then (a,p,r) and (c,p,r) are tight. Apply R164 in head form with k=a,z=c to E=(p,r). Its strict-extension alternative (c,a,p,r) would prepend c,a to the entire source rail and, together with the untouched other rail, give a spanning two-cover of H. Hence in the nonclosing branch R164 forces (p,a,c). Swap the witness order k=c,z=a; the extension (a,c,p,r) would likewise close, so (p,c,a) is forced. Therefore both orientations (a,c) and (c,a) of the deleted physical dimer are head-signed with the same common witness p. Accepted R407 now gives a fixed-trimer historical signed-support/anchor or cross-generation pair interaction localized at p. If e is instead a tail endpoint q with predecessor r, the exact tail-dual R164 argument forces both (c,a,q) and (a,c,q), so the two orientations of {a,c} are tail-signed with common witness q and R407 applies at q. Thus case (II) yields at least one genuine endpoint-local interaction packet at the unique common good end e. No claim is made that the three bad ends also satisfy the R407 bidirectional same-witness hypothesis. Combining cases I and II consumes every nonclosing R402 branch.
