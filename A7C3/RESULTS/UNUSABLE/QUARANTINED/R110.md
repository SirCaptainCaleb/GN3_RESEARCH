# R110 — Transversal all-pivot four-family common-mate lift

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the R106 transversal minimum-overlap triangle with common exterior marker M={delta,epsilon}, and assume that each of its three exact 5+3 roots lies in the double-transfer pivot face of R50. Then for each marker vertex m in M and each sign polarity s, there are three graph-intrinsic signed terminal-dimer supports D_A(m,s), D_B(m,s), D_C(m,s), one coming from the m-folded longest path associated to each of the roots A,B,C, such that all three have polarity s and the same witness m*=M\{m}. Moreover there exists one graph-intrinsic support E(m,s) of the opposite polarity such that E(m,s) forms a balanced pair with each of D_A(m,s), D_B(m,s), D_C(m,s). Thus the all-pivot triangle carries four three-root common-mate support families, indexed by the two physical marker vertices and the two polarities.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; R106 transversal triangle; for all three common-marker roots, the R50 reverse-four-path alternative is not used and the double-transfer pivot face holds.

## Scope
Graph-intrinsic support/sign compilation in the all-pivot transversal branch. The three members of a family need not be distinct physical dimers.

## Applicability
Use before opening separate payment descendants. It synchronizes the three common-marker roots at the signed-support level and exposes a shared opposite mate for every fixed marker/polarity family.

## Persistence
The fold-derived signed terminal-dimer systems are graph-intrinsic by R51/R52, and the R40 common opposite support is a graph-intrinsic pre-payment certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The four family actualizations may use different auxiliary marker dimers and may produce different opposite supports. No simultaneous currentness of the three folded longest paths is asserted. No coupling of later paid descendants is asserted, and the theorem does not by itself close the conjecture.

Their union has size at most 6 and m* lies outside all three, so among 10 vertices at least 3 are simultaneously outside the supports and witness. ## Proof
Let the three R106 roots be indexed by i in {A,B,C}, all with common exterior pair M={delta,epsilon}, and assume all three are in the R50 double-transfer pivot face. Fix m in M and write m* for the other marker vertex. By R51, folding the i-th pivot root through m produces a globally longest folded five-path whose terminal signed-dimer system has as common witnesses the opposite three-vertex shadow together with m*. In particular m* is a named witness for that system. By R52, the two terminal physical dimers of this folded longest path have opposite polarities for every common witness. Hence, for each polarity s, choose the terminal dimer D_i(m,s) having polarity s with witness m*. R51 makes these signed systems graph-intrinsic, so for fixed (m,s) the three supports D_A(m,s),D_B(m,s),D_C(m,s) are three graph-intrinsic supports with the same polarity and the same witness m*.

Each D_i(m,s) is a physical dimer, so the union of the three supports has size at most 6. A sign witness is outside its support, hence m* is outside all three D_i(m,s). Therefore at most 7 of the 10 vertices are occupied by the three supports together with m*, leaving at least three spare vertices. Choose any two spare vertices as a physical dimer J. A physical dimer is a nontrivial marker path, and J is disjoint from every D_i(m,s) and from the common witness m*. The hypotheses of R40 now hold for the three-support family. One test of J therefore creates a single opposite-polarity support E(m,s) such that each D_i(m,s) forms a balanced pair with E(m,s).

This argument is independent for s equal to either polarity and for m equal to delta or epsilon, yielding four three-root common-mate family certificates. R40 explicitly keeps later payments of the several balanced pairs as separate descendants, so no simultaneous-payment claim is made.
