# R120 — Paired-anchor signs must align with the middle trimers

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the paired-anchor polarity rectangle R117. At either of its two anchors c, write the opposite-polarity star as {c,u},{c,v}, with both dimers carrying the witnesses delta and epsilon. Choose the unique tight orientation J_c of the reversal pair (u,c,v),(v,c,u), so J_c is a tight trimer with middle c. Then the two R117 polarities on {c,u},{c,v} agree componentwise with the two terminal polarities induced by J_c; the anti-aligned possibility is impossible. Consequently each rectangle dimer has a third same-polarity witness, namely the opposite outer vertex of its middle trimer, in addition to delta and epsilon. This holds simultaneously as graph-intrinsic sign data at both rectangle anchors. Combined with R41, the two vertex-disjoint middle trimers therefore give a synchronized two-anchor interaction whose four terminal physical dimers are all three-witness same-polarity supports.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; R100 transversal minimum-overlap triangle; R117 paired-anchor rectangle; R3 boundary antisymmetry; R9 longest-path order five; R41 central-anchor interaction.

## Scope
Orientation-sensitive closure of the align/anti-align ambiguity in the canonical zero-excess private-anchor rectangle.

## Applicability
Use as the canonical zero-excess local interface after R117. The next step may treat the four rectangle dimers as three-witness same-polarity supports tied to one exact/common two-anchor trimer cell; no opposite-sign branch remains.

## Persistence
The forbidden-five-set argument is support-level. The chosen middle-trimer orientations and resulting third-witness certificates are graph-intrinsic. R41 interaction remains a historical interaction certificate, not simultaneous paid currentness.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the four three-witness supports are simultaneously actualized or paid, does not identify their later collision descendants, and does not by itself force a spanning 5+5 cover.

## Proof
Fix one rectangle anchor c with outer vertices u,v. By R3 exactly one of (u,c,v),(v,c,u) is tight; call the resulting c-middle trimer J=(u,c,v) after renaming u,v according to its tight direction. Its first terminal dimer {u,c} and second terminal dimer {c,v} have opposite trimer-induced polarities. R117 gives opposite old polarities on the same two physical dimers, so the old pair either agrees with the trimer pair componentwise or disagrees componentwise. Suppose it disagrees. Then the old sign on {u,c} is the head-facing sign relative to J, while the old sign on {c,v} is the tail-facing sign relative to J. Since both old signs have both witnesses delta and epsilon, the exact ordered turns (delta,u,c) and (c,v,epsilon) are tight (or with delta,epsilon exchanged). Together with the tight middle turn (u,c,v), this makes delta,u,c,v,epsilon a tight five-path. By R9 its support X_c={u,c,v,delta,epsilon} is globally longest. But every possible rectangle star gives X_c forbidden overlap: for the private stars, X_alpha={x,y,alpha,delta,epsilon} meets B only in x (and C only in y), X_beta={x,z,beta,delta,epsilon} meets A only in x, and X_gamma={y,z,gamma,delta,epsilon} meets A only in y; for the second-anchor stars, X_z={beta,z,gamma,delta,epsilon} is disjoint from A, X_y={alpha,y,gamma,delta,epsilon} is disjoint from B, and X_x={alpha,x,beta,delta,epsilon} is disjoint from C. Each contradicts the global minimum overlap three encoded by the R100 triangle (and in the disjoint cases already R13). Hence anti-alignment is impossible. Therefore the old polarities align with the trimer terminal polarities. The opposite outer vertex of J is then a third witness with the same polarity as delta and epsilon on each terminal dimer. Apply the same argument at the second rectangle anchor. The two middle trimers are vertex-disjoint by the R117 rectangle list, so R41 supplies the synchronized two-anchor interaction on these four three-witness supports.
