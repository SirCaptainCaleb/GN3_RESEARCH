# R456 — Universal fixed-turn mate-or-exit reduction

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample beyond order ten. Fix any proper tight turn J=(a,x,c) together with a lawful pair-bearing fixed-turn lineage in the scope of R172. Then, unless a spanning two-cover or an earlier accepted fixed-turn contact exit occurs, there is a chosen continuation to one of two explicit physical currencies: (1) an R173-admissible two-hole mate clause; or (2) a same-polarity two-witness R38 collision on a named reverse terminal dimer of one residual rail in an R359 all-internal currentization of J, with one witness the retained source middle x and the other a named endpoint of the opposite residual rail. Indeed, steer the lineage to the target floor {a,c} as before and apply R359. Endpoint return gives (1) by R358. In the all-internal branch, apply R455: the literal U-to-V or V-to-U residual-rail splice proposal forces the concrete partner-only terminal collision (2). Thus the all-internal residual no longer needs the replay-prone R160 fixed-trimer remint as its generic exit. The fixed turn J, middle x, source currentization, collided terminal dimer, second witness, and literal splice proposal remain attached to outcome (2).

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample beyond order ten; J is a proper tight turn with a lawful pair-bearing R172 lineage; accepted R358,R359 and provisional R455.

## Scope
Order-free strengthening of the fixed-turn reduction. It replaces the generic all-internal historical pair/contact exit by a concrete residual-terminal same-support collision that consumes partner-rail geometry and avoids the source-trimer replay caveat of R448. Earlier unrelated fixed-turn contact exits remain accepted outputs.

## Applicability
Use after any mechanism produces a pair-bearing fixed-turn lineage. The preferred all-internal output is now the R455 residual-terminal R38 collision, because its support and two witnesses are explicit and role-linked; use older R387/R389 routes only when their source-terminal payment ancestry is specifically needed.

## Persistence
The fixed turn J and lineage persist to target-floor entry. Endpoint-return mate clauses retain their literal proposal. In the all-internal branch the chosen R359 currentization J,U,V, source middle x, ordered residual-rail splice proposal, exact seam holes, collided reverse terminal dimer, and opposite-rail endpoint witness are retained as historical graph-intrinsic certificate data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert a single mate clause or a single R38 collision closes H. Does not claim the two currencies are mutually exclusive across alternative continuations. Does not infer a mate cycle from isolated clauses. Does not erase richer R389 four-birth/payment ancestry when separately useful.

## Proof
Follow the accepted R387 fixed-turn lineage until either closure/an earlier explicit contact exit occurs or the target-aligned floor {a,c} is reached. Apply R359 to the retained turn J=(a,x,c). If some exact H-{a,c} return exposes x as a rail endpoint, R358 gives the literal two-hole mate clause, proving outcome (1). Otherwise R359 is in its all-internal regime and provides a currentization H=J sqcup U sqcup V with the four x-signed reverse terminal dimers. Apply R455 to that currentization. Its literal residual-rail splice proposal U*V with J untouched, or symmetrically V*U, has exactly two seam holes; reversing whichever hole is bad gives a second same-polarity witness on a reverse terminal dimer already signed by x. R38 therefore yields outcome (2), with all support/witness/proposal data explicit. This exhausts the R359 return dichotomy.
