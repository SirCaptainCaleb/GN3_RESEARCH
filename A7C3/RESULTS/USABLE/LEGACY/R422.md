# R422 — Fresh-coordinate paid-floor steering from any ancestry-bearing mass-two pair

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample with |V(H)|>=6 and let F be any ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced opposite-sign pair whose singleton supports are (p),(t), with named sign witnesses alpha,beta. Then for every prescribed physical vertex z in V(H) there is a chosen ancestry-retaining continuation which either gives a spanning two-cover or reaches an ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced-pair floor in which (z) is literally one singleton support. If z is p or t, use F itself. If z lies outside B={p,t,alpha,beta}, apply R420. If z is a sign witness not already equal to p or t, choose two auxiliary vertices u,v outside B. For z=alpha, use R3 on the z-middle triple {u,z,v}; whichever orientation is tight makes either (u,z) or (v,z) a tail-signed dimer by R41, opposite to the retained head-signed singleton (p). For z=beta use the exact dual to obtain a head-signed dimer containing z opposite to the retained tail-signed singleton (t). In either witness case the dimer sign comes from the displayed named tight turn, so R224 preserves z literally to a descendant mass-two floor. The complete ancestry ledger of F remains historical data. Different z-lineages are alternative. Thus single-coordinate floor selectability is universal once a mass-two ancestry-bearing pair is available; what remains nontrivial is simultaneous control of the second floor coordinate or consumption of the retained ancestry.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample with |V(H)|>=6; F is an ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced pair with singleton supports (p),(t) and named witnesses alpha,beta; accepted R3,R41,R224 and provisional R420.

## Scope
Order-free one-coordinate floor-steering parent. In O4 the order hypothesis is automatic. This theorem reconstructs a precise ancestry-preserving quantifier that was only compressedly remembered in historical Complete Floor Steering summaries.

## Applicability
Use after any ancestry-bearing mass-two checkpoint, including R415/R416/R417. Any desired graph vertex may be chosen as one literal floor coordinate without sacrificing the prior ancestry ledger. Therefore downstream work should not count one-coordinate floor access itself as progress; the meaningful targets are prescribed-pair synchronization, protected-role transfer, or an ancestry-sensitive interaction.

## Persistence
Retain the complete incoming floor ancestry. In nontrivial steering cases also retain the prescribed vertex z, chosen auxiliary vertices, the named tight turn certifying the dimer containing z, the R224 refund orientation, and literal singleton (z) at the descendant floor. The old second singleton and old active representative need not remain current.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prescribe or control the second descendant floor singleton. Does not claim floors for different z coexist. Does not preserve an independently chosen capture/protection role unless already guaranteed by another theorem. Does not imply an arbitrary prescribed floor pair can be reached. Does not make repeated floor reminting progress or close the conjecture.

## Proof
Write the incoming floor, after taking the exact head/tail dual if necessary, as (alpha;(p)) together with ((t);beta). Let B={p,t,alpha,beta}; |B|<=4. Fix z in V(H). If z=p or z=t, the incoming floor already has the required coordinate. Suppose z lies outside B. Since |V(H)|>=6, there exists u outside B union {z}; apply provisional R420 to obtain closure or a mass-two floor with literal singleton (z), retaining the incoming ancestry. It remains to treat a sign witness not already equal to a support. Suppose z=alpha and alpha not in {p,t}. Because |V(H)|>=6 choose distinct u,v outside B. Apply boundary antisymmetry R3 to the triple {u,alpha,v} with alpha in the middle. Exactly one of (u,alpha,v) and (v,alpha,u) is tight. In the first case D=(u,alpha) is tail-signed by witness v; in the second D=(v,alpha) is tail-signed by witness u. By R41 this is a signed physical dimer containing alpha, disjoint from (p), with polarity opposite the retained head-signed singleton (p). Hence they form an ancestry-bearing balanced pair, and the sign on D is certified by the displayed named tight turn. Apply R224 and choose endpoint alpha; it yields closure or a mass-two floor containing literal singleton (alpha). The incoming floor ancestry remains historical data in the chosen ledger. The case z=beta is the exact dual: place beta in the middle of a triple on two fresh auxiliaries; whichever orientation is tight gives a head-signed dimer containing beta, opposite to the retained tail-signed singleton (t), and R224 preserves beta. Coincident witnesses cause no problem: if alpha=beta is not already a support coordinate, either witness construction applies. This exhausts all z.
