# R139 — Endpoint-preserving P4-plus-trimer amplification is not locally valid

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Boundary antisymmetry together with a tight four-path Q=(a,b,c,d) and a disjoint tight trimer K={x,y,z} does not force the endpoint five-set {a,d,x,y,z} to support a tight five-path. There is an explicit boundary-antisymmetric turn assignment with abc, bcd, and xyz tight but with {a,d,x,y,z} non-Hamiltonian.

## Hypotheses
Only the local Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry relation R3 and the displayed tight-path/trimer hypotheses.

## Scope
Finite counterexample to the returned R132 endpoint-preserving strengthening. It does not contradict accepted R126, which only guarantees some P5 somewhere on the seven vertices.

## Applicability
Use to prevent future arguments from extracting K∪{a,d} as a guaranteed P5 support from R126/P125. Support control must retain the full alternative family, as in corrected C130/R138.

## Persistence
The counterexample is an explicit graph-intrinsic finite turn assignment; all unspecified reversal pairs can be extended arbitrarily subject to R3.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not refute the controlled-support replacement R138 and does not rule out endpoint completion under additional hypotheses not present in the local R132 statement.

- **Curation note:** Explicit finite counterexample to the false endpoint-preserving P4-plus-trimer amplification.
- **Review status:** accepted
- **Review note:** Explicit local counterexample independently verified. On E={a,d,x,y,z} the stated R3 assignment has exactly 12 tight P4 orderings and no tight P5; adjoining b,c with abc and bcd tight preserves the induced non-Hamiltonicity of E. Thus the rejected endpoint-preserving R132 statement is genuinely false under only its local hypotheses.

## Proof
On E={a,d,x,y,z}, for each unordered triple declare exactly the following three ordered triples tight, and declare their complete reversals bad:\n{a,d,x}: adx, axd, dax.\n{a,d,y}: ady, ayd, day.\n{a,d,z}: daz, dza, zda.\n{a,x,y}: xay, xya, yxa.\n{a,x,z}: axz, azx, xaz.\n{a,y,z}: yza, zay, zya.\n{d,x,y}: xdy, xyd, yxd.\n{d,x,z}: dxz, dzx, zdx.\n{d,y,z}: dzy, zdy, zyd.\n{x,y,z}: xyz, yxz, yzx.\nThis satisfies R3 and in particular xyz is tight. Direct inspection gives exactly the following tight four-paths on E: adxz, axdy, daxz, dazx, dzay, dzya, xayd, xyza, yxaz, zayd, zdax, zday. Their respective missing vertices and blocked right-extension turns are: y with xzy bad; z with dyz bad; y with xzy bad; y with zxy bad; x with ayx bad; x with yax bad; z with ydz bad; d with zad bad; d with azd bad; x with ydx bad; y with axy bad; x with ayx bad. Hence none of the twelve tight four-paths extends to a tight five-path, and every tight five-path would have a tight first four-path, so E supports no tight P5. Now adjoin b,c. Declare abc and bcd tight, making Q=(a,b,c,d) a tight P4; retain xyz tight; orient every other new reversal pair arbitrarily subject to R3. The endpoint set E is unchanged and remains non-Hamiltonian. This is the required counterexample.
