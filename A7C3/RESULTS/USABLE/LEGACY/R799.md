# R799 — Every R789 single-wrap branch is a same-root inward cover switch or one central two-witness dimer packet

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R789 frame with m>=6 and suppose one of its two single-wrap branches occurs. Put rho=q_{m-3}. Then one of the following holds. (INWARD-COVER) For some root r in X, H-r has an exact two-cover K_r sqcup P on which K_r is a Hamilton P5 on {L,R} union (X-{r}) and P is a Hamilton path on all of Q[1,m-1] whose endpoint pair is contained strictly inside Q. Thus the original {L,R}-ended long rail in the R594 H-r cover is replaced, for the same root, by a long rail with both cuts moved inward. (CENTRAL-TAIL) In SW1, the tested cross-depth dimer S=(v,d)=(q_2,q_{m-2}) is tail-signed by the two distinct witnesses s=q_{m-1} and rho=q_{m-3}: both (v,d,s) and (v,d,rho) are tight. (CENTRAL-HEAD) In SW2, the same tested dimer S=(v,d) is head-signed by the two distinct witnesses u=q_1 and q_3: both (u,v,d) and (q_3,v,d) are tight. More explicitly, the only route to CENTRAL-TAIL/HEAD is that both wrap seams alpha=(s,u,v) and beta=(d,s,u) of Q[1,m-1] are bad. In SW1 their reversals make (d,v,u,s) a tight P4; if additionally (rho,d,v) is tight then Q[1,m-1] has the Hamilton order (q_3,...,rho,d,v,u,s), while if it is bad R3 gives (v,d,rho), yielding CENTRAL-TAIL. In SW2 both bad seams make (u,s,d,v) tight; if (d,v,q_3) is tight then Q[1,m-1] has Hamilton order (u,s,d,v,q_3,...,rho), while if it is bad R3 gives (q_3,v,d), yielding CENTRAL-HEAD.

## Hypotheses
Accepted crossed R621/R594/R789 frame, m>=6, in one of R789 single-wrap branches. Use accepted R3 and the pure outer-six gate theorem R582.

## Scope
Noncircular accepted-only refinement of the R789 single-wrap residue. No R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,R350,generic R358,R508,capture,payment,R542,R696,R436,or computation.

## Applicability
Use instead of generic P4 or R542 handling after a single-wrap R789 output. Test the two interior wrap seams of Q[1,m-1]. Any tight seam already gives a full interior Hamilton rotation and hence INWARD-COVER via R582. If both fail, test the one remaining cross-depth seam (q_{m-3},d,v) in SW1 or (d,v,q_3) in SW2. A tight test again gives INWARD-COVER; a bad test leaves the unique central packet on (q_2,q_{m-2}) with the displayed witness pair. The next consumer should act on this central physical support directly, not spend it generically through R542/R696.

## Persistence
In INWARD-COVER retain root r, the outer R582 P5 K_r, the exact Hamilton order P on Q[1,m-1], and both old/new H-r exact covers. In CENTRAL retain the single-wrap orientation, the two failed interior wrap seams, the explicit four-Q P4, tested dimer (v,d), its polarity, and its two named Q witnesses.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the CENTRAL packet closes H, is scarce merely because it is two-witness, or should be paid by R542. Does not assert both CENTRAL polarities coexist. Does not consume the explicit four-Q P4 separately; it is retained only as part of the double-fail central packet. In INWARD-COVER the outer root r is supplied existentially by R582.

## Proof
Put Q^circ=Q[1,m-1]=(u,v,q_3,...,rho,d,s), with rho=q_{m-3}, and let alpha=(s,u,v), beta=(d,s,u) be its two wrap seams. First record the common cover conclusion. Whenever we exhibit any tight Hamilton path P on support V(Q^circ), accepted R582 supplies some r in X and a Hamilton P5 K_r on {L,R} union (X-{r}). K_r and P are disjoint and cover H-r, so K_r sqcup P is a two-cover of H-r; it is exact because a Hamilton H-r together with singleton r would two-cover H. Accepted R594 gives the original exact H-r cover T_r sqcup Q. Since every P below has both endpoints in Q-{L,R}, this is INWARD-COVER.\n\nSW1. R789 gives (d,v,u) and (v,d,s) tight. If alpha is tight then P=(s,u,v,q_3,...,rho,d) is a Hamilton path on Q^circ, so INWARD-COVER holds. If beta is tight then P=(v,q_3,...,rho,d,s,u) is such a Hamilton path, again giving INWARD-COVER. Hence suppose alpha and beta are both bad. R3 gives their reversals (v,u,s) and (u,s,d) tight. Together with (d,v,u), the first gives the explicit tight P4 (d,v,u,s). Test gamma=(rho,d,v). If gamma is tight, then P=(q_3,...,rho,d,v,u,s) is a Hamilton path on Q^circ: all initial turns are inherited from Q, and the final seam turns are gamma,(d,v,u),(v,u,s). Thus INWARD-COVER holds. If gamma is bad, R3 gives (v,d,rho) tight. Since R789 already gives (v,d,s), the same tested dimer (v,d) is tail-signed by the two distinct witnesses rho and s, proving CENTRAL-TAIL.\n\nSW2. R789 gives (u,v,d) and (s,d,v) tight. As above, alpha tight gives the rot1 Hamilton path and beta tight gives rot2, hence INWARD-COVER. If both are bad, R3 again gives (v,u,s) and (u,s,d). Together with (s,d,v), the second yields the explicit P4 (u,s,d,v). Test delta=(d,v,q_3). If delta is tight, then P=(u,s,d,v,q_3,...,rho) is a Hamilton path on Q^circ, using (u,s,d),(s,d,v),delta and then inherited Q turns. Hence INWARD-COVER. If delta is bad, R3 gives (q_3,v,d) tight. Together with the existing (u,v,d), the tested dimer (v,d) is head-signed by the two distinct witnesses q_3 and u, proving CENTRAL-HEAD. These cases exhaust both single-wrap orientations.
