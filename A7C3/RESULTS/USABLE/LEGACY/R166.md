# R166 — Two-Sided Middle-Incidence Seam Test

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let a,b,c,r,s be distinct vertices with abc tight. Then either rab and bcs are both tight, in which case (r,a,b,c,s) is a tight five-path, or bar is tight, or scb is tight. Equivalently, failure of the left seam rab produces the reverse trimer (b,a,r), and failure of the right seam bcs produces the reverse trimer (s,c,b).

## Hypotheses
The five vertices are distinct and abc is tight.

## Scope
Universal Strong Level-(1) local turn lemma. No deletion frame, pair payment, smallest-counterexample hypothesis, or current representative is required.

## Applicability
Use whenever a retained/fixed turn abc sits between proposed predecessor r and successor s. It reduces the two-seam test to either literal five-path assembly or one of two exact reverse-trimer certificates. The archived Two-Sided Middle-Incidence Discharge is the fixed pair-deletion/payment specialization.

## Persistence
All conclusions are graph-intrinsic tight-path/turn facts and persist historically. Any current roles that supplied r,s in an application are separate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The reverse trimers are certificates, not contradictions or spanning covers. No refund/payment conclusion is included unless the application separately satisfies the signed-support payment hypotheses. The theorem does not say which seam fails.

## Proof
If rab and bcs are tight, then the three consecutive turns of (r,a,b,c,s) are rab, abc, bcs, all tight, so the five-path is tight. If rab is bad, Boundary Antisymmetry R3 gives bar tight. If bcs is bad, R3 gives scb tight. These alternatives exhaust the two tests.
