# R379 — Johnson paired-design equality induces a regular linear clique geometry

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume the t=2 equality setting of R375 and write r=kq. Let G be the induced Johnson graph J(r,k)[B] on the selected k-sets. For every physical (k-1)-core S, the selected blocks containing S form a clique L_S of order q. Every vertex F in B lies on exactly k such core-cliques L_{F\\{f}}, f in F. Every edge of G lies in exactly one core-clique, namely the clique indexed by the intersection of its endpoints. Consequently G is k(q-1)-regular and the open neighborhood of every vertex is the disjoint union of k cliques K_{q-1}. Equivalently, the incidence structure with points B and lines {L_S: |S|=k-1} is a regular partial linear space with line size q and point degree k, and G is its point graph.

## Hypotheses
Finite k-uniform family in the t=2 equality case of R375; r=kq.

## Scope
Pure finite-set/graph structural theorem.

## Applicability
Use to study equality families via local graph geometry, clique incidence, spectral methods, or partial-linear-space classification rather than only block-design counts.

## Persistence
The Johnson graph, core-cliques, and incidence structure are intrinsic finite combinatorial data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the core-cliques are maximal in every degenerate q=1 case and does not classify all such partial linear spaces globally.

## Proof
R375 gives exactly q selected blocks through every (k-1)-core S, so they form a clique L_S of order q in J(r,k). A selected block F contains exactly k different (k-1)-cores F\\{f}, hence lies on exactly k such cliques. If two selected blocks are adjacent in J(r,k), their intersection has size k-1 and is their unique common core, so every edge lies on exactly one L_S. By R378, for each f in F there are exactly q-1 neighbors obtained by replacing f, and neighbors arising from distinct f differ from F in different deleted elements and therefore intersect each other in only k-2 elements; they are not adjacent. Thus N_G(F) is the disjoint union of k cliques K_{q-1}, giving degree k(q-1). The line-intersection property follows because two distinct (k-1)-cores can lie together in at most one k-set.
