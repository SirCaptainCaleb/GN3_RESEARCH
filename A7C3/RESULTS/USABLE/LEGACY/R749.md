## Proof
Write X-{e}={alpha,beta,gamma}, where gamma is the M_R-partner of e and {alpha,beta} is the other M_R edge. By the R537 classification of a transitive matching-height four-cell, for each M_R edge {x,y} and each w outside it both (x,y,w) and (y,x,w) are tight. Applying this to {alpha,beta} with w=gamma gives the two tight trimers T_1=(beta,alpha,gamma) and T_2=(alpha,beta,gamma). Only these two trimers are used, so no assumption about the relative height of the two cross matchings is needed.

(1) The consecutive triples of J=(u,L,e,R,s) are (u,L,e), tight by the R594 endpoint reverse star; (L,e,R), tight by hypothesis; and (e,R,s), tight by the other R594 endpoint star. So J is a tight P5. Since |X-{e}|=3, W is a nonempty proper induced subsystem, so pc(W)<=2 by R4 and W has an exact two-cover. If W had a tight Hamilton path P, then J and P would be vertex-disjoint tight paths covering V(H), giving a spanning two-cover and contradicting pc(H)=3 (R4). Hence W has no tight Hamilton path.

(2) Let m>=5. For i=1,2 write T_i=(p_0,p_1,p_2). The sequence (p_0,p_1,p_2,q_2,q_3,...,q_{m-2}) enumerates V(W), and its consecutive triples are (p_0,p_1,p_2), (p_1,p_2,q_2), (p_2,q_2,q_3), and triples internal to Q. The first is tight by the R537 classification and the last kind are tight because Q is a tight path. By (1) the sequence is not a tight path, so (p_1,p_2,q_2) or (p_2,q_2,q_3) is bad, and boundary antisymmetry R3 makes (q_2,p_2,p_1) or (q_3,q_2,p_2) tight. Both T_1 and T_2 have p_2=gamma, with p_1=alpha and p_1=beta respectively. Hence if (q_3,q_2,gamma) is not tight, then (q_2,gamma,alpha) and (q_2,gamma,beta) are both tight. R537 with A=q_2, B=gamma, c=alpha, d=beta then gives either a tight Hamilton P4 on Z={q_2,alpha,beta,gamma} or the transitive matching-height no-P4 cell structure on Z with top matching {{q_2,gamma},{alpha,beta}}.

(3) Dually, for m>=5 the sequence (q_2,...,q_{m-2},p_0,p_1,p_2) enumerates V(W) with consecutive triples internal to Q together with (q_{m-3},q_{m-2},p_0), (q_{m-2},p_0,p_1) and (p_0,p_1,p_2). By (1) it is not tight, so one of the two seam triples is bad and R3 gives (p_0,q_{m-2},q_{m-3}) or (p_1,p_0,q_{m-2}) tight. Taking T_1 gives the ordered pair (x,y)=(beta,alpha) and T_2 gives (x,y)=(alpha,beta), which is the stated alternative. In the second alternative (y,x,q_{m-2}) is tight, and (y,x,gamma) is tight by the R537 classification, so the ordered dimer (y,x) has the two distinct witnesses gamma and q_{m-2}, both outside {x,y}; note q_{m-2} is outside X. R537 with A=y, B=x, c=gamma, d=q_{m-2} gives either a tight Hamilton P4 on {alpha,beta,gamma,q_{m-2}} or the cell structure with top matching {{alpha,beta},{gamma,q_{m-2}}}.

(4) For m=4 we have Q[2,m-2]=(q_2) and W={alpha,beta,gamma,q_2}, so a tight Hamilton path of W is exactly a tight Hamilton P4 on this four-set, and by (1) there is none. For i=1,2 with T_i=(p_0,p_1,p_2), the sequences (p_0,p_1,p_2,q_2) and (q_2,p_0,p_1,p_2) are therefore not tight; since (p_0,p_1,p_2) is tight, R3 gives (q_2,p_2,p_1) tight and (p_1,p_0,q_2) tight. Ranging over i=1,2 yields (q_2,gamma,alpha), (q_2,gamma,beta), (alpha,beta,q_2), (beta,alpha,q_2) all tight. R537 now applies to Z={q_2,alpha,beta,gamma} with A=q_2,B=gamma,c=alpha,d=beta, and its no-P4 hypothesis is literally verified by (1); hence Z is a transitive matching-height no-P4 four-cell with top matching {{q_2,gamma},{alpha,beta}}.

All conclusions are statements about which ordered triples of H are tight, together with the non-Hamiltonicity of one fixed induced subsystem. Nothing is selected, captured, paid, or actualized, so the left and right outputs hold simultaneously.
