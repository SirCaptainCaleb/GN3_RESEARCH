# R138 — Endpoint-preserving P4-plus-trimer amplification

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let Q=(a,b,c,d) be a tight four-vertex path and let K={x,y,z} be disjoint from Q and support a tight trimer. Then some tight five-path has support in the six-support family {Q∪{x}, Q∪{y}, Q∪{z}, K∪{a,b}, K∪{c,d}, K∪{a,d}}.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; Q is a tight four-path; K is a disjoint three-set supporting a tight trimer.

## Scope
Local support-controlled repair of the failed endpoint-preserving R132 formulation. No order-ten minimality or support-family assumptions are used.

## Applicability
Use when R126 alone gives too little support control. In a constrained support family, eliminate impossible members of the displayed six-support list to force one of the survivors.

## Persistence
The conclusion is graph-intrinsic on the fixed seven vertices and names only physical supports.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that K∪{a,d} is always Hamiltonian. In particular it does not recover the rejected endpoint-preserving R132 statement; the successful support may instead be Q∪{w}, K∪{a,b}, or K∪{c,d}.

## Proof
Orient K as (x,y,z), so xyz is tight, and suppose for contradiction that none of Q∪{x}, Q∪{y}, Q∪{z}, K∪{a,b}, K∪{c,d}, K∪{a,d} supports a tight five-path. For every w∈K, Q∪{w} is non-Hamiltonian. Since Q=(a,b,c,d) is tight, the failed candidates (w,a,b,c,d) and (a,b,c,d,w) force wab and cdw bad, hence baw and wdc tight by R3. The failed candidate (b,a,w,d,c), whose first and last turns are now tight, forces awd bad and hence dwa tight. Thus for every w∈K the packet baw, dwa, wdc is tight.\n\nThe three reversal pairs on K give eight local patterns, with two types up to relabeling. In the cyclic type take xyz, yzx, zxy tight. Since K∪{a,b} is non-Hamiltonian, (b,a,z,x,y) forces azx bad, hence xza tight. Since K∪{c,d} is non-Hamiltonian, (y,z,x,d,c) forces zxd bad, hence dxz tight. Since K∪{a,d} is non-Hamiltonian, (d,x,z,a,y) forces zay bad, hence yaz tight; then (x,d,y,a,z) forces xdy bad, hence ydx tight. But (y,d,x,z,a) is now a tight five-path on K∪{a,d}, contradiction.\n\nIn the transitive type take xyz, xzy, yxz tight. Non-Hamiltonicity of K∪{a,b} makes (b,a,x,z,y) force axz bad, hence zxa tight. Non-Hamiltonicity of K∪{c,d} makes (y,x,z,d,c) force xzd bad, hence dzx tight. Non-Hamiltonicity of K∪{a,d} makes (d,z,x,a,y) force xay bad, hence yax tight; then (y,d,z,x,a) forces ydz bad, hence zdy tight. But (z,d,y,a,x) is now a tight five-path on K∪{a,d}, contradiction. Therefore at least one of the six named supports is Hamiltonian.
