# R234 — Five-exterior replacement family may have a universal failed hole locally

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
There exists an eleven-vertex Level-(1) boundary-antisymmetric turn system with a tight carrier P6 K={0,1,2,3,4,5} and exterior E={6,7,8,9,10} such that E is non-Hamiltonian, and for every carrier vertex k the four replacement supports (E\\{z}) union {k} are Hamiltonian exactly for z in {7,8,9,10}, while the replacement support (E\\{6}) union {k} is non-Hamiltonian for every k. Thus one exterior vertex can be a universal failed hole across the entire carrier even while every R192 row has exactly four successes. The same model can simultaneously have every complementary carrier six-set (K\\{k}) union {z} non-Hamiltonian. Therefore neither Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry nor the row-wise four-of-five replacement pattern forces global coverage of all five exterior holes.

## Hypotheses
Only Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry is assumed for the explicit local turn model. This is not asserted to be a genuine smallest-counterexample equality frame.

## Scope
Local sharpness fence for coverage conjectures about the five-exterior replacement family.

## Applicability
Use to reject arguments that infer every exterior hole is successful for at least one carrier vertex merely from R192-type row density or local turn constraints. Any theorem forcing the fifth hole to appear must use additional global-smallest-counterexample, longest-carrier, ancestry, or exact-cover structure.

## Persistence
The countermodel is one fixed graph-intrinsic turn table; all Hamiltonicity statements are finitely reconstructible from the displayed signature.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not refute the possibility that full smallest-counterexample hypotheses force all five holes globally. Does not assert a universal failed hole exists in every R192 frame. Does not improve or weaken the accepted R232 dense-column lower bound.

## Proof
Let K={0,1,2,3,4,5}, E={6,7,8,9,10}. Encode the turn table by the same convention used in R233: for each unordered triple i<j<k, the three canonical reversal-pair representatives are A=(i,j,k), B=(i,k,j), C=(j,i,k); list bits in lexicographic unordered-triple order and then A,B,C, with 1 meaning the canonical order is tight and 0 meaning its complete reversal is tight. The 495-bit table, interpreted as an unsigned binary integer and left-padded to 124 hexadecimal digits, is 5fbdb6d400000c0924fb6da49264137278a6ffffc0000000000001200e016c0924fb6da49240235278c92480000100406c00001042436600390582037395. This defines exactly one tight order in every reversal pair. Direct finite enumeration verifies K=(0,1,2,3,4,5) is tight and E has no tight P5. For each k=0,...,5, exhaustive enumeration of the five replacement supports shows success exactly for holes z=7,8,9,10 and failure for z=6. Hence the hole-column counts are 0,6,6,6,6. The same exhaustive check of all 720 orders on each six-set (K\\{k}) union {z}, for all thirty pairs (k,z), finds no tight P6. Thus one exterior hole can be absent from the entire local replacement family despite exact four-of-five row saturation, as claimed.
