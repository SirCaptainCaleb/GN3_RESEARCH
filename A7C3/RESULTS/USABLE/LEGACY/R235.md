# R235 — Reverse-terminal restoration cannot be silently spliced into the forward carrier

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let K=(k0,k1,...,km) be a proper globally longest path and apply the R215 collision web on its left terminal physical dimer in the reverse orientation D=(k1,k0). For two exterior witnesses e,f, R164 may be applied to the tight extensions (D,e) and (D,f), yielding either the local restored P4 (k1,k0,e,f) up to witness order, or the corresponding bounded reverse-contact trimer. Neither R164 alternative is a forward-carrier extension merely from these hypotheses: the retained dimer is oriented k1,k0, whereas the forward carrier seam is k0,k1. Strong Level-(1) tightness does not license reversing the carrier or cyclically rereading turns. Therefore any R215+R164 composition that grafts the restoration output back onto K requires an additional orientation/bridge theorem; physical equality of the terminal dimer is insufficient. The right-end statement is dual.

## Hypotheses
Accepted R164 and R215; K is read in its displayed forward orientation. No additional carrier-reversal or seam-bridge theorem is assumed.

## Scope
Applicability/orientation fence for proposed carrier-restoration compositions in the arbitrary-order program.

## Applicability
Use whenever R164 is invoked on an R215 reverse terminal dimer. The local restoration/contact output is valid, but downstream arguments must separately prove compatibility with the forward carrier before calling it a carrier extension, carrier replacement, or exterior descent.

## Persistence
The physical terminal dimer and both orientations are graph-intrinsic labels. The distinction between the reverse signed orientation and the forward carrier order must be retained across historical use.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say the R164 output is useless, does not forbid a separate bridge from converting it into carrier progress, and does not invalidate R164 or R215. It only forbids an unproved direct splice based on physical-support identity.

## Proof
At the left end of K=(k0,k1,k2,...), R215 fixes the reverse-oriented physical terminal dimer D=(k1,k0). For any exterior witnesses e,f the corresponding same-polarity signed turns are of the tail-extension form (k1,k0,e) and (k1,k0,f), after naming the polarity consistently with R215. R164 applied to the retained path E=D therefore tests the turn e f k0 (equivalently after the theorem naming, one gets either a four-path beginning with the ordered dimer k1,k0 and then the two witnesses, or the exact antisymmetric reverse-contact trimer). In the P4 branch the first two vertices remain k1,k0. The displayed forward carrier K begins k0,k1 and its next certified turn is k0 k1 k2; there is no accepted rule allowing the ordered pair k1,k0 to be substituted for k0,k1, nor does boundary antisymmetry permit cyclic rereading. In the contact branch the output is only the bounded three-vertex contact recorded by R164. Hence neither branch alone produces a literal forward extension or replacement of K. The right endpoint is identical after reversing left/right notation.
