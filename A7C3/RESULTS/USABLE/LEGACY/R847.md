# R847 — The crossed m=6 head-to-tail exterior weave has only two monotone Q-contact partitions

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the crossed m=6 frame Q=(L,u,v,w,d,s,R) and X={a,b,c,z}. Let T=T_1 sqcup T_2 be an exact two-cover of H-{w,v} with the following WEAVE roles: the two rail starts are exactly a,c, the two rail ends exactly b,z, T selects no X-X state, and hence each rail has the form h -> E_h -> t where h in {a,c}, t in {b,z}, and E_h is a nonempty path block consisting only of vertices from E={L,u,d,s,R}; the two E-blocks partition E. Then either comparing a T rail with the old path Q by accepted R435 yields an explicit old-state reversal, reverse trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle, or the Q-contact sets of the two rails are, up to interchanging the rails, exactly one of the two partitions {L,d,R}|{u,s} or {u,d,R}|{L,s}, and within each rail its E vertices occur in the displayed increasing Q order.

## Hypotheses
Accepted crossed R621/R723/R594 frame at m=6. T is one exact H-{w,v} two-cover satisfying the displayed WEAVE endpoint/no-X-X conditions.

## Scope
Finite m=6 source-cover normalization. No payment,capture,R508,R542,generic path growth,or computation. R435 outputs are retained constructively, not called contradictions.

## Applicability
Use on the WEAVE branch of the 2x2 signed-dimer rectangle at D=(w,v). It reduces the seam-quiet low-block rectangle residue to two physical Q-contact partition types before any further cap/Mate-Fork analysis.

## Persistence
Retain T, its two actual rail orientations, start/end X labels, assignment of E vertices to rails, and either the exact R435 output or the one of two Q-contact partitions.

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
Write the two WEAVE rails as K_1,K_2. Compare each with the original tight path Q using accepted R435. If either comparison yields an old-state reversal, reverse trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle, retain that output and stop. Hence assume no such output. R435 then forces the Q vertices encountered along each K_i to occur in increasing Q-index order. Because WEAVE has no internal X vertex other than its initial head witness h in {a,c} and terminal tail witness t in {b,z}, every internal vertex of a rail belongs to E={L,u,d,s,R}; therefore its E-block is exactly its assigned subset of E listed in increasing order. We now record three forbidden same-rail patterns. For every h in {a,c}, accepted R594 gives (u,L,h) tight, so R3 makes its complete reversal (h,L,u) bad. Thus L and u cannot lie on the same weave rail: if they did, increasing Q order makes them the first two E vertices on that rail and the path turn from its start h would be (h,L,u). For every t in {b,z}, accepted R594 gives (t,R,s) tight, so (s,R,t) is bad. Hence s and R cannot lie on the same weave rail, because if they did they would be the last two E vertices there and would be followed by its end t. Likewise accepted R723 gives (t,s,d) tight for every t in X, hence (d,s,t) is bad. Therefore d and s cannot lie together on one rail: if R is on the other rail, d,s would be the last two E vertices and create (d,s,t); if R is also on the same rail, the already-forbidden s,R pair occurs. Each WEAVE rail has a nonempty E-block and the two blocks partition the five-element set E. A 1+4 split is impossible. Indeed if the singleton is L or u, the four-vertex block contains d,s,R and hence the forbidden s,R pair; if the singleton is d, the four-block contains both L,u and s,R; if the singleton is s or R, the four-block contains L,u. Thus the split sizes are 2 and 3. Since d,s,R are distributed between two rails, some two of them must share a rail. The pairs d,s and s,R are forbidden, so the shared pair is exactly {d,R}, while s lies on the other rail. Finally L and u must be split between the two rails. If L joins {d,R}, the partition is {L,d,R}|{u,s}; if u joins {d,R}, it is {u,d,R}|{L,s}. In each case the order inside a block is the displayed increasing Q order by the no-R435 assumption. This exhausts the quiet WEAVE branch.
