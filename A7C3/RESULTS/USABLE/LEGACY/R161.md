# R161 — Root Boundary-Anchored Collision Normal Form

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and F=P0 sqcup P1 sqcup P2 any actual spanning three-cover. After finitely many actual rank-preserving owner switches there is an actual spanning three-cover F*=P0* sqcup P1* sqcup P2* in one of two dual forms. Tail form: P0* ends (...,u,v), x,y are the current heads of P1*,P2*, uv is the current last boundary state, uvx and uvy are bad, hence xvu and yvu are tight; thus reverse support (v,u) has two distinct same-polarity witnesses in current endpoint roles. Head form: P0* begins (u,v,...), x,y are the current tails of P1*,P2*, uv is the current first boundary state, xuv and yuv are bad, hence vux and vuy are tight. All boundary and witness roles occur in the same displayed final representative.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; F is any actual spanning three-cover.

## Scope
Strong-safe owner-descent / three-cover fan scope. No nontriviality assumption is needed on the two witness components beyond being distinct nonempty cover components.

## Applicability
Transforms a specified ancestry-bearing spanning three-cover into a live boundary-anchored same-polarity collision frame. Its value is transformation of a chosen representative, not anonymous root collision existence.

## Persistence
Only the final F* representative and graph-intrinsic collision turns are guaranteed. Pre-switch selected states, roles, reserve labels, envelope data, or other ancestry do not survive unless separately preserved.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Same-polarity collision is not itself a contradiction or spanning two-cover. The theorem does not preserve arbitrary pre-descent history and does not assert a final assembly coordinate.

## Proof
Start from the given actual spanning three-cover F=P_0 sqcup P_1 sqcup P_2. Because H has no spanning two-cover, not all three components can be singletons: if they were, any two vertices form a tight dimer and together with the third singleton would give a spanning two-cover. Relabel so P_0 is nontrivial. Maintain P_0 as the owner component.

Suppose the current owner ends (...,u,v), and let x be the current head of one of the other components, say P_1=(x,x_1,...). Test the literal seam turn (u,v,x). If it is bad, do nothing for this donor. If it is tight and P_1 has order at least two, replace the current three-cover by
P_0'=(P_0,x),  P_1'=(x_1,...),  P_2'=P_2.
Every inherited turn remains tight and the only new owner turn is exactly (u,v,x), so this is an actual spanning three-cover with the same number of nonempty components. This is a literal rank-three owner switch. If instead P_1 is the singleton (x), then a tight (u,v,x) would make P_0'=(P_0,x) together with P_2 a spanning two-cover, impossible in H. Hence for a singleton donor the seam is necessarily bad.

Repeat the same test for the current heads of both non-owner components, performing an absorption whenever a seam is tight and the donor remains nonempty. Every performed switch increases |P_0| by one while the two donor components remain nonempty. Therefore at most |V(H)|-3 switches can occur, so the process terminates at an actual spanning three-cover F*=P_0* sqcup P_1* sqcup P_2*. Write the final owner tail as (...,u,v) and the two other current heads as x,y. By termination, neither head can be absorbed. If a donor has order at least two, this means the corresponding seam uvx or uvy is bad; if it is a singleton, the same seam is bad because otherwise H would be two-covered. Thus both uvx and uvy are bad in this one displayed final representative.

Boundary antisymmetry R3 now gives the complete reversals xvu and yvu tight. Hence the reverse tested support (v,u) carries two distinct same-polarity witnesses x,y, while uv is literally the current last boundary state of P_0*. All boundary and witness roles are simultaneous in F*. This is exactly the R161 tail form. Applying the identical argument after reversing the choice of owner end gives the stated head-dual form if desired, but the theorem only requires one of the two dual forms. No historical currentness or representative is transported through the switches; only the final actual representative and graph-intrinsic tight turns are retained, matching the R161 persistence clause.
