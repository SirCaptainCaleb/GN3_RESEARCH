# R429 — Pair-Deletion Rigidity

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let p,t be any two distinct vertices. Then pc(H-{p,t})=2. Moreover every exact two-cover H-{p,t}=U sqcup V has both rails nontrivial: |U|,|V|>=2.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest counterexample to the Strong Level-(1) Two-Cover Conjecture; p,t are distinct vertices.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order and arbitrary deleted physical pair. No sign, witness, orientation, or order-ten hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use whenever a proof deletes a prescribed physical pair and needs an exact two-cover with two selected nontrivial rails, especially for marker actualization and fixed-turn floor steering.

## Persistence
The conclusion is universal over every exact two-cover of the same pair-deleted graph. It supplies nontriviality of both current rails but no identity or orientation persistence across different exact covers.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prescribe heads/tails or rail membership of other vertices; does not identify a canonical two-cover; does not preserve an earlier representative.

## Proof
By smallest-counterexample minimality, pc(H-{p,t})<=2. Suppose first that H-{p,t} has a Hamilton tight path W. If W=(w) has one vertex, boundary antisymmetry on p,w,t gives one of (p,w,t),(t,w,p) tight, yielding a spanning tight path on H, impossible. Otherwise write W=(w_0,w_1,...). Boundary antisymmetry on p,w_0,t gives a tight trimer K on {p,w_0,t}. The suffix (w_1,...) is tight and disjoint from K, so K together with that suffix gives a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Hence pc(H-{p,t})=2. Now let U sqcup V be any exact two-cover. If U=(u) is singleton, antisymmetry on p,u,t gives a tight trimer on {p,u,t}; together with V this is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. The same applies to V. Thus both rails are nontrivial. This is the historical Pair-Deletion Rigidity proof, later repeatedly audited and used.
