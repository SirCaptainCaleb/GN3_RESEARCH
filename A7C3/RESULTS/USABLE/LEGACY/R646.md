## Proof
Assume branch (B) of accepted R633. Its packets C_bc and C_ba are opposite-polarity signed dimers meeting only at b, so accepted R547 gives an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor F_b on physical pair {a,c}. The matching-height rule makes J_b=(a,b,c) tight. Accepted R621 gives H-a=Q[0,m-2] sqcup (c,z,b,R,s). Delete c from the displayed second rail. The surviving paths Q[0,m-2] and (z,b,R,s) form a literal two-cover of H-{a,c}; by Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429, pc(H-{a,c})=2, hence this literal two-cover is exact. The middle b of J_b is internal on (z,b,R,s). Apply R434 in this aligned frame. Its predecessor channel z->b yields a nontrivial signed dimer with signed endpoint a, and its successor channel b->R yields one with signed endpoint c. Either endpoint is already a historical signed singleton of F_b. Choosing either channel and applying R436 to that old singleton and the later nontrivial dimer gives an explicit at-anchor growth/cycle/reverse-contact event; stationary singleton replay is impossible. For the second hinge, C_za and C_zc meet only at z with opposite polarity, so R547 gives a distinct floor F_z on {a,c}. The matching-height turn J_z=(c,z,a) is tight. R621 gives H-c=Q[0,m-2] sqcup (a,b,z,R,s); delete a to obtain the literal two-cover Q[0,m-2] sqcup (b,z,R,s) of H-{a,c}, exact by R429, with z internal. R434 on predecessor b->z or successor z->R anchors a later nontrivial dimer at c or a, respectively, and R436 fires against the corresponding F_z singleton. Both hinge ancestries are retained separately. No R5 is used.
