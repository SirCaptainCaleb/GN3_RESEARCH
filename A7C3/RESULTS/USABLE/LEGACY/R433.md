# R433 — Prescribed Opposite-Singleton Replacement

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample at an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor with active supports (p),(t). For every prescribed vertex x not in {p,t}, there is a finite chosen certificate-retaining continuation yielding a spanning two-cover or another ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor (p),(x). The singleton p is retained as the fixed singleton throughout the replacement macro. The exact head/tail dual replaces p by an arbitrary prescribed x while retaining t. No freshness, capture-history, protected-anchor, or order-ten hypothesis is required.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; (p),(t) is an active ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced floor; x is distinct from p,t; Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429, reconstructed Marker Endpoint Anchor Capture R430 / Orientation-Selective Dimer Capture R431, and the one-rail unsigned-end refund R175 are available.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order. This is the exact historical Universal Both-Singleton Singleton-Replacement / prescribed opposite-singleton macro audited before Complete Floor Pair Steering.

## Applicability
Use to change exactly one coordinate of an active ancestry-bearing floor to any prescribed vertex while retaining the other singleton coordinate. It is the atomic steering macro consumed by Complete Floor Pair Steering.

## Persistence
Retain the fixed singleton p; the incoming floor ancestry; the exact pair-deletion rails of H-{p,t}; the selected rail dimer containing x; the marker orientation chosen to make x the signed endpoint; the actualization outcome; the nontrivial opposite rail used for the one-rail refund; and the final literal singleton x. Earlier protected/capture roles remain historical only unless their own persistence theorem applies.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not preserve the old floor representative state-for-state; does not preserve an additional protected/current role on p beyond keeping p as the floor singleton; does not claim different target-x continuations coexist; marker orientation does not reverse an old selected dimer orientation; does not close the both-singleton layer.

## Proof
Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429 gives an exact two-cover H-{p,t}=U sqcup V with both rails nontrivial. The prescribed vertex x lies on one rail, say U, and therefore lies on at least one selected adjacency dimer D of U. Regard the current singleton (p) as a formal head-signed singleton with witness t. Because D has two possible marker orders, R431 permits choosing its orientation so x is the marker endpoint identified by R430 in head-to-tail actualization. Run the explicit R430 terminal-turn test. In the tight branch the oriented dimer D itself is an opposite tail-signed support whose signed endpoint is x; in the bad branch antisymmetry produces the short opposite support (t,x), again with signed endpoint x. In either branch the fixed singleton (p) is untouched, so we have a singleton/nontrivial balanced pair with opposite support of order two and signed endpoint x. The other old deletion rail, say V, is nontrivial, selected in the literal pair-deletion frame, and disjoint from p and from the new order-two support (including t in the bad branch). Use its selected boundary as the outside boundary in the one-rail unsigned-end refund R175. R175 clips exactly the unsigned endpoint of the order-two support and, by Signed-Interval Rebirth in its reconstructed proof, leaves its signed endpoint x while leaving (p) untouched. Thus the nonclosing descendant floor is exactly (p),(x). The head/tail dual gives replacement of p while retaining t. This is the historical R17 prescribed singleton-replacement macro, independently audited PASS by the subsequent R18 handoff.
