# R305 — Every six vertices carry a capture-free fixed-trimer nonquiet interaction packet

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be any Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let b,d_1,d_2,y,r,s be six distinct vertices. Then there exist signed spokes S_1={b,d_1}, S_2={b,d_2}, both with common witness y, and one fixed tight trimer T on {r,y,s}, such that each S_i forms a direct mass-four balanced pair with a terminal dimer of T of opposite polarity. After declaring one pair birth first, the second birth through the same fixed T is nonquiet by R160 and forces the accepted historical signed-support/anchor interaction or cross-generation pair interaction before unrelated reset. Consequently every Strong Level-(1) system of order at least six contains a fixed-trimer nonquiet packet. The bare existence of such a packet is therefore not smallest-counterexample progress and is not evidence of puncture/capture synchronization.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; six distinct vertices; accepted R3,R39,R41,R160.

## Scope
Pure six-vertex local theorem. No path, cover, smallest-counterexample, capture, exterior-floor, payment, or puncture hypothesis is required.

## Applicability
Use to simplify R291-style constructions and to prevent counting an unlocalized fixed-trimer nonquiet packet as structural progress. A useful consumer must exploit special identities/ancestry of b,d_i,y,T, force a specific interaction output, or connect the packet to current cover geometry.

## Persistence
The two spoke signs, fixed trimer, direct pair births, and R160 historical interaction are graph-intrinsic/historical as in their parent theorems.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say R160 is useless. A packet on prescribed supports or witnesses, tied to capture/payment/current geometry, may carry genuine information. Does not assert the R160 interaction is a contradiction or a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
For i=1,2, boundary antisymmetry R3 gives exactly one tight orientation of the triple {b,d_i,y} with middle d_i, namely either (b,d_i,y) or (y,d_i,b). By R41 the terminal physical spoke S_i={b,d_i} therefore carries a signed certificate with witness y. Independently, R3 gives exactly one tight orientation T of {r,y,s} with middle y. Its two terminal dimers have opposite polarities by R41. For each i choose the terminal dimer M_i of T whose polarity is opposite to that of S_i. The supports S_i and M_i are disjoint because the six vertices are distinct, so R39 gives a direct mass-four balanced pair (S_i,M_i). Declare the first birth historical. The second birth also uses a signed dimer disjoint from the same fixed trimer T and one of T terminal dimers, so R160 says it cannot begin a quiet independent pair epoch before unrelated reset: the accepted historical signed-support/anchor interaction or cross-generation pair interaction is forced. No carrier or capture fact was used.
