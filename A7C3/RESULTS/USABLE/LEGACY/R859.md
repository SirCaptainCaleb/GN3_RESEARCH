## Proof
Fix i and write T_i=(p_0,p_1,p_2), where p_2=gamma. Since T_i sqcup Q[2,m-2] is an exact two-cover of W, T_i cannot be concatenated immediately before J: if both seam turns (p_1,p_2,u) and (p_2,u,L) were tight, then T_i followed by J would be one tight path and Q[2,m-2] the other, giving a spanning two-cover of H, impossible because pc(H)=3. Hence at least one seam is bad. By R3, at least one of its reverses (u,p_2,p_1) and (L,u,p_2) is tight. For T_1 this is (u,gamma,alpha) or (L,u,gamma); for T_2 it is (u,gamma,beta) or (L,u,gamma). If (L,u,gamma) is not tight, both u-gamma turns are tight. Accepted R723 gives (v,u,gamma) tight, so (v,u,gamma,alpha) and (v,u,gamma,beta) are tight P4s.

Likewise T_i cannot be concatenated immediately after J. If both (R,s,p_0) and (s,p_0,p_1) were tight, J followed by T_i together with Q[2,m-2] would two-cover H. Thus R3 gives at least one of (p_0,s,R) and (p_1,p_0,s). For T_1 this is (beta,s,R) or (alpha,beta,s); for T_2 it is (alpha,s,R) or (beta,alpha,s). If beta-s-R is not tight then (alpha,beta,s) is tight, and R723 gives (beta,s,d) tight, so (alpha,beta,s,d) is a P4. Dually, if alpha-s-R is not tight then (beta,alpha,s,d) is a P4. Hence absence of such a P4 forces both alpha-s-R and beta-s-R.

Now assume m>=5 and take the other rail A=Q[2,m-2]=(v,q_3,...,d). It cannot precede J. If both (q_{m-3},d,u) and (d,u,L) were tight, A followed by J together with T_i would two-cover H. R3 therefore gives (u,d,q_{m-3}) or (L,u,d). It also cannot follow J. If both (R,s,v) and (s,v,q_3) were tight, J followed by A together with T_i would two-cover H, so R3 gives (v,s,R) or (q_3,v,s). These conclusions do not depend on i and hence coexist with the trimer-seam packet. Q itself gives (L,u,v) and (d,s,R), so the first Q-rail alternatives form same-polarity endpoint collision packets with distinct witnesses v,d when m>=5. For m>=6, accepted R849 says (d,q_{m-3}) is already head-signed by a,c and (q_3,v) tail-signed by b,z, so the second alternatives are literal third same-polarity witnesses u and s on the opposite inward supports. This proves the claim.
