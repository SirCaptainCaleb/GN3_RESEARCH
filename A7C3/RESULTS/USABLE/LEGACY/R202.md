# R202 — Global-longest carrier endpoint balanced-pair refund

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K=(k_1,...,k_m), m>=4, be a proper globally longest tight path with nonempty exterior E. For every w in E, the left terminal physical dimer D_L={k_1,k_2}, oriented (k_2,k_1), and the right terminal physical dimer D_R={k_{m-1},k_m}, oriented (k_m,k_{m-1}), are disjoint signed supports of opposite polarity with the same exterior witness w. Hence they form a direct mass-four balanced pair. Moreover, because both supports lie in the single tight carrier K, the Multi-Support Adjacent-Color Refund may be realized using the contiguous middle interval of K from k_2 through k_{m-1}; this interval meets D_L and D_R in exactly one vertex each. Signed-Interval Rebirth therefore leaves two nonempty signed descendants supported exactly on the outer endpoint singletons {k_1} and {k_m}. These descendants retain the respective opposite polarities, so K canonically carries an endpoint-aligned mass-two balanced-pair ancestry on its two physical endpoints before any unrelated representative reset.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper globally longest tight path of order at least four; E=V(H)\\V(K) is nonempty.

## Scope
Order-free carrier-specific composition of the universal exterior endpoint shield with direct balanced-pair and adjacent-color refund machinery.

## Applicability
Use in the five- or six-exterior O3 faces to obtain a balanced-pair ancestry tied to the physical endpoints of the chosen longest carrier K, rather than an anonymous pair born in a punctured recompletion. This may be combined with later capture/currentization or fixed-turn machinery, subject to their representative hypotheses.

## Persistence
K, its terminal physical dimers, the exterior reverse-sign certificates, and the identity of the outer endpoint singleton descendants are graph-intrinsic/historical. Witness names may change under Signed-Interval Rebirth. No later current selection is asserted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the endpoint singleton pair is simultaneously current with K or with any exterior P5/singleton root; does not produce a literal R171 three-cover; does not preserve the original common witness w after rebirth; does not by itself force exterior-slack descent or a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
Fix w in E. By R199, (k_2,k_1,w) is tight and gives the left terminal dimer D_L={k_1,k_2} its tail-facing sign, while (w,k_m,k_{m-1}) is tight and gives the right terminal dimer D_R={k_{m-1},k_m} its head-facing sign. These polarities are opposite. Since m>=4, D_L and D_R are vertex-disjoint and have total support size four, so R39 makes them a direct balanced pair. Both nontrivial signed supports lie in the one tight carrier K. Apply R174 to this two-support family. Along K, the support-colored vertices occur first in D_L and later in D_R. Taking the contiguous interval C=(k_2,...,k_{m-1}) meets D_L only in k_2 and D_R only in k_{m-1}. R174/Signed-Interval Rebirth therefore deletes one support vertex from each without swallowing either support. Because each original support has order two, the surviving signed descendants are exactly the singletons {k_1} and {k_m}. Rebirth retains each support as a signed descendant (though its witness name may change), so the two descendants retain the opposite polarities inherited from D_L and D_R. Thus the resulting historical pair has total mass two and is canonically aligned with the two physical endpoints of K. No claim of simultaneous currentness with K or any exterior root is made.
