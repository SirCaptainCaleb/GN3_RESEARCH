# R275 — No-P4 double-contact four-cell has a unique Strong orientation signature

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,d} be a four-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament satisfying the double-contact packet from R274: the turns (a,b,c), (a,b,d), (d,b,c), (d,c,b), and (b,a,d) are tight. If X has no Hamilton tight P4, then every remaining reversal-pair choice is forced. Precisely, the tight ordered triples on X are exactly (a,b,c), (a,b,d), (a,c,b), (b,a,c), (b,a,d), (b,d,a), (c,a,d), (c,d,a), (c,d,b), (d,b,c), (d,c,a), and (d,c,b), together with no others except these same listed orientations. Equivalently, the local tournament orders at the four possible middle vertices are: middle a: b>c>d; middle b: a>d>c; middle c: d>a>b; middle d: c>b>a. Thus the non-P4 branch of R274 is one unique labeled antisymmetric four-cell, not a family of residual orientations.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry on four vertices; the five displayed R274 double-contact turns are tight; no Hamilton tight P4 exists on X.

## Scope
Pure finite four-vertex graph-intrinsic classification. No smallest-counterexample, cover, capture, payment, or historical hypothesis is required beyond the displayed double-contact turns.

## Applicability
Use after R274 in its non-P4 branch. It replaces an underdetermined bounded contact label by one exact 12-turn orientation signature, suitable for comparison with historical no-Hamilton K4 modules or direct fifth-vertex analysis.

## Persistence
The complete orientation signature is graph-intrinsic on the physical four-set X.

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
Assume the five displayed turns abc, abd, dbc, dcb, bad are tight and no tight P4 exists. Boundary antisymmetry fixes their reverses as bad. Force the remaining reversal pairs as follows. (1) In (a,b,d,c), abd is tight, so bdc must be bad; hence cdb is tight. (2) In (a,c,d,b), cdb is tight, so acd must be bad; hence dca is tight. (3) In (a,d,b,c), dbc is tight, so adb must be bad; hence bda is tight. (4) In (a,d,c,b), dcb is tight, so adc must be bad; hence cda is tight. (5) In (b,d,a,c), bda is tight, so dac must be bad; hence cad is tight. (6) In (b,c,a,d), cad is tight, so bca must be bad; hence acb is tight. (7) In (c,a,b,d), abd is tight, so cab must be bad; hence bac is tight. These seven forced choices, together with the five initial turns, determine all twelve reversal pairs. The tight orientations are exactly abc, abd, acb, bac, bad, bda, cad, cda, cdb, dbc, dca, dcb. Grouped by middle vertex, the local orders are: middle a: b>c>d; middle b: a>d>c; middle c: d>a>b; middle d: c>b>a. Thus the no-P4 double-contact cell has the claimed unique orientation signature.
