# R408 — Endpoint-status disagreement between two exact covers forces a balanced pair

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let W be a proper vertex subset. Suppose H[W] admits two literal exact two-covers F=P sqcup Q and Fprime=Pprime sqcup Qprime. If some physical vertex x in W is an endpoint of its path component in F and is internal on its path component in Fprime, then H contains a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair. Indeed delete x. Trimming the endpoint occurrence of x from F leaves a literal cover of W-{x} with at most two components, while deleting the internal occurrence of x from Fprime splits its path component into two nonempty paths and leaves the other component unchanged, giving a literal three-cover of W-{x}. Since W-{x} is proper, R159 applies to this component drop. Thus any family of exact two-covers of one proper residue that contains no balanced-pair-producing endpoint/internal disagreement must have the same endpoint set in every member.

## Hypotheses
H is a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament; W is a proper vertex subset; F and Fprime are literal exact two-covers of H[W]; x is an endpoint in F and internal in Fprime; accepted R159.

## Scope
Order-free cover-comparison theorem. No smallest-counterexample, capture, payment, or path-extremality hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use when several exact two-cover representatives of the same deletion residue are available. Any change in endpoint status across representatives immediately yields a balanced pair; therefore a genuinely quiet multiplicity branch is confined to a fixed endpoint-set fiber.

## Persistence
The two source covers are representative-relative. The balanced pair produced by R159 is graph-intrinsic historical information. No source rail or endpoint role is asserted to persist after the component-drop comparison.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say distinct covers with the same endpoint set are equal or productive. Does not make the balanced pair current or immediately closing. If x is an endpoint in both covers or internal in both, the theorem gives no conclusion.

## Proof
Let F=P sqcup Q and assume x is an endpoint of P. Removing x from P leaves either a nonempty tight path or nothing; together with Q this is a literal cover T of W-{x} with one or two components. In Fprime, x lies internally on one path, say Pprime=(...,l,x,r,...). Removing x splits Pprime into two nonempty tight subpaths Pprime_L,Pprime_R, while Qprime remains a tight path, so R=Pprime_L sqcup Pprime_R sqcup Qprime is a literal three-cover of exactly W-{x}. Because W is proper in H, so is W-{x}. Hence R159 applies with c=3 and r=|T|<=2, producing a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair in H. The final endpoint-set statement is the contrapositive: if no such balanced-pair-producing disagreement occurs among a family of exact two-covers, every vertex has the same endpoint/internal status in every cover, so all covers have the same endpoint set.
