# R591 — The unique two-bad R24 cell forces a cross-end anchor trimer

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the exact-two-bad transitive R24 cell of accepted R556 with Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=2, X={a,b,c,z}, and matching labels M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}. Put L=q_0 and R=q_m. Then the cross-end trimer (L,b,R)=(q_0,b,q_m) is tight. Indeed R556 supplies the extreme left gate Laz tight, and provisional R589 supplies cRb and bLa tight. If RbL were tight, the six-vertex order (c,R,b,L,a,z) would be a tight Hamilton P6 on X union {L,R}. Its complementary path Q^o=(q_1,...,q_{m-1}) is nonempty and tight, giving a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Therefore RbL is bad, and R3 forces its exact reversal LbR tight. Thus the signed anchor b singled out by the unique two-bad seam pattern is not merely common to the two endpoint collision packets: it lies in a graph-intrinsic tight trimer joining the two physical ends of Q.

## Hypotheses
The accepted exact-two-bad R24 frame R556; m>=2; provisional R589 cross-end reverse fan; R3.

## Scope
Specialized R24 restoration lemma for the unique minimum two-bad seam cell. The P6 obstruction is order-independent in the length of the interior rail.

## Applicability
Use in the surviving m>=6 branch of R574/R576. The long rail endpoints q_0,q_m now have a literal common bridge through the distinguished seam anchor b. This is a new cross-end coordinate unavailable in the earlier endpoint-local gate packet and is a candidate input for wrap/recompletion or common-deletion absorber constructions.

## Persistence
Retain X,Q,L,R, the distinguished anchor b, the exact one-hole P6 candidate (c,R,b,L,a,z), its bad hole RbL, and the forced tight cross-end trimer LbR.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the cross-end trimer itself is a spanning component of a minimum cover, does not make Q wrap, and does not prove the complementary middle Hamiltonian. The conclusion depends on the exact-two-bad gate packet, not on an arbitrary transitive four-cell.

## Proof
Write L=q_0,R=q_m. Accepted R556 gives Laz tight. Provisional R589 gives cRb and bLa tight. Consider K=(c,R,b,L,a,z). Its four consecutive turns are cRb, RbL, bLa, Laz. If the only uncertified turn RbL were tight, K would be a tight Hamilton P6 on X union {L,R}. Since m>=2, Q^o=(q_1,...,q_{m-1}) is a nonempty tight path, disjoint from K, and K sqcup Q^o spans H, contradicting pc(H)>2. Hence RbL is bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 gives LbR tight.
