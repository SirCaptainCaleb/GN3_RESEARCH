# R207 — Five-exterior replacement grid forces an exterior-dimer two-witness interaction

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume the non-Hamiltonian five-exterior equality face of R192 and suppose the carrier K has at least six vertices. Then some physical dimer D contained in E carries signed certificates with two distinct carrier witnesses k,kprime in V(K), arising from two of the R192 replacement P5s. Consequently R38 forces a genuine same-support two-witness interaction on D: either the two certificates have the same polarity and enter the accepted two-extension collision package, or they have opposite polarities and enter same-support exclusivity, yielding the accepted strict two-ended signed-path interaction. In particular, after R191 excludes order ten, every surviving O3 five-exterior equality face has such an interaction.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper tight path with five-vertex exterior E; E is non-Hamiltonian; accepted R192; |K|>=6. Signed terminal dimers of tight trimers are interpreted as in accepted R41/R38.

## Scope
Arbitrary-order cross-side coupling theorem for the five-exterior replacement grid. It uses one unit of ordering information from each successful P5, beyond bare support incidence.

## Applicability
Use as the canonical next consumer of R192 in the equality face. It replaces the unsupported hope of complementary carrier replacement by a forced graph-intrinsic exterior-dimer interaction with two distinct carrier witnesses. Downstream work may feed that interaction into capture, restoration, or fixed-turn machinery.

## Persistence
The physical exterior dimer D, the two distinct carrier witnesses, and their tested signed orders are graph-intrinsic historical certificates. The several replacement P5 representatives need not be simultaneously current.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify D or the two witnesses in advance; does not prescribe whether their polarities agree; does not itself produce K-k+z, exterior-size descent, or a spanning two-cover; does not assert simultaneous currentness of the two replacement P5s.

## Proof
Fix k in K. By R192 there are at least four distinct holes z in E for which S_{k,z}=(E\\{z}) union {k} supports a tight P5. For each such z choose one tight ordering of S_{k,z}. In any five-vertex ordering containing exactly one special vertex k and four vertices of E\\{z}, at least one consecutive tight trimer has k at one end and two vertices of E\\{z} in the other two positions: if k is in position 1 use turn 1-3; in position 2 use turn 2-4; in position 3 use turn 1-3 or 3-5; in position 4 use turn 2-4; in position 5 use turn 3-5. By the tight-trimer signed-terminal convention recorded in R41, the physical dimer formed by those two exterior vertices carries a signed certificate with witness k. Choose one such dimer D_{k,z} subset E\\{z}. For a fixed physical dimer D subset E, D can equal D_{k,z} only when z is not in D. Since |E|=5 and |D|=2, there are only three holes z outside D. Therefore one physical dimer can account for at most three of the at least four successful holes in a fixed k-row. Hence the set A_k={D_{k,z}: z successful} contains at least two distinct physical dimers. Suppose for contradiction that no physical exterior dimer carries certificates with two distinct carrier witnesses. Then the sets A_k are pairwise disjoint as k varies. If |K|>=6 this would require at least 2|K|>=12 distinct physical dimers inside E, but E has only C(5,2)=10. Contradiction. Thus some D lies in A_k intersect A_kprime for distinct k,kprime. The two signed certificates on the same physical support have distinct witnesses, so R38 gives exactly the stated same-polarity collision or opposite-polarity strict interaction. Finally, in the post-R191 O3 equality face H has order greater than ten and |E|=5, so |K|=|H|-5>=6.
