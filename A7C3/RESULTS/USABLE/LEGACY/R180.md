# R180 — Anchored-Capacity Reservoir Collision-or-One-Residual

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let (alpha;P),(T;beta) be an anchored-capacity realization for fixed signed anchors and witnesses, and let X=V(H)\(V(P) union V(T) union {alpha,beta}) be the ordinary reservoir. Define the head-side capacity reverse dimer D_P=(d,c) when P=(...,c,d) is nontrivial and D_P=(p,alpha) when P=(p) is singleton; define the tail-side capacity reverse dimer D_T=(b,a) when T=(a,b,...) is nontrivial and D_T=(beta,t) when T=(t) is singleton. Then exactly the following trichotomy is available: (i) if X is empty, H has a spanning two-cover; (ii) if |X|>=2, any two distinct x,y in X are same-polarity witnesses on the fixed dimer D_P (and dually D_T), so Same-Support Sign Interaction yields a strict graph-intrinsic two-witness extension or a bounded reverse trimer/contact; (iii) if |X|=1, the two capacity supports together with their two fixed witnesses cover all but one vertex. Thus the only anchored-capacity maximum avoiding closure and the accepted same-polarity collision output is a one-residual near-spanning anchored carrier.

## Hypotheses
H is a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. (alpha;P),(T;beta) form a balanced opposite-sign pair and (P,T) is an anchored-capacity maximum for the fixed witnesses and signed head/tail anchors in the sense of Anchored-Capacity Unsigned-End Reverse-Star Saturation.

## Scope
Strong-safe, graph-intrinsic collision theorem in one fixed anchored-capacity representation. No smallest-counterexample hypothesis is needed for the collision part; the empty-reservoir conclusion is the literal two-cover consequence of the signed paths themselves.

## Applicability
Use capacity statically before further payment/recompletion: a reservoir of size at least two cannot remain quiet, while the only collision-free nonclosed capacity face has one ordinary residual vertex. The theorem is the parent of the capacity-flat synchronized-floor collision.

## Persistence
The fixed reverse-dimer signs and any strict extension/reverse-contact output are graph-intrinsic. The capacity realization remains representative-relative. Original historical supports and their ancestry are retained separately and are not replaced by the capacity pair.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The one-residual case is not closed: no claim is made that its last vertex inserts into either support. No claim that capacity is monotone under later support truncation or recompletion. No claim that the collision output is a spanning two-cover. No CW-specific inference is used.

## Proof
Let (alpha;P),(T;beta) be the anchored-capacity realization and X its ordinary reservoir. Define D_P,D_T exactly as in R180. By fully reconstructible R179, every x in X gives the same tested orientation and same polarity on D_P: when P=(...,c,d), the tight turn (x,d,c) makes the fixed oriented dimer D_P=(d,c) head-signed by x; when P=(p) is singleton, (x,p,alpha) makes D_P=(p,alpha) head-signed by x. Dually every x in X gives the same tested oriented dimer D_T its tail polarity. Therefore if |X|>=2 and x,y are distinct reservoir vertices, R523 is licensed on the identical tested orientation, with distinct same-polarity witnesses x,y. Its HH or TT branch is exactly the historical same-polarity two-extension collision packet: the fixed oriented dimer has two distinct graph-intrinsic witness extensions. Hence the first collision alternative stated in R180, a strict graph-intrinsic two-witness extension, already holds. No unordered-support comparison and no additional opaque collision theorem are used.

If X is empty, every vertex of H lies in V(P) union V(T) union {alpha,beta}. We construct a spanning cover by at most two tight paths using the signed extensions themselves. The head sign means (alpha,P) is tight whenever alpha is outside P; the tail sign means (T,beta) is tight whenever beta is outside T. Because P and T are vertex-disjoint, each witness is either outside both supports or lies on the opposite support. If alpha and beta are distinct and both outside P union T, then (alpha,P) and (T,beta) are disjoint tight paths spanning H. If exactly one witness, say alpha, lies on the opposite support T, then P and T already contain alpha; append only the other outside witness beta to T when beta is outside T, so P and (T,beta) span, or if beta also lies on P then P and T already span. The head/tail dual is identical. If alpha=beta=w and w is outside both supports, then w can be absorbed into either signed path, e.g. (w,P) together with T, giving a spanning two-cover. Thus X=empty forces a spanning cover by at most two tight paths.

The only remaining cardinality is |X|=1. By definition, the anchored-capacity supports together with their two fixed witnesses then cover all vertices except that one ordinary reservoir vertex. No insertion or closure claim is made. These three cases are exhaustive and give exactly the R180 collision-or-one-residual trichotomy. The capacity realization is representative-relative, while the R179 signs and R523 collision packet are graph-intrinsic.
