# R210 — Global-longest terminal capture-to-interaction funnel

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and K a proper globally longest tight path of order at least three. Fix either terminal physical dimer D of K. Then, unless a spanning two-cover already occurs, one may choose an exact two-cover of H-D, obtain a selected surviving-carrier/exterior crossing, choose a fresh exterior sign witness avoiding that crossing, and capture either physical endpoint of the crossing in the same frame. The captured-middle mirror-turn mechanism then produces a proper tight turn J whose middle is that freshly captured vertex and enters a chosen fixed-turn lineage. Along that lineage only finitely many quiet fixed-turn episodes can occur before a graph-intrinsic historical pair/contact interaction is forced.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper globally longest tight path of order at least three; one terminal dimer is chosen.

## Scope
Order-free compression from a global-longest terminal dimer to the historical-interaction frontier. No exterior-slack, order-ten, five-exterior, six-exterior, or simultaneous-currentness hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use before specialized exterior-slack casework when it is enough to reduce the problem to an explicit historical pair/contact interaction. It shows that terminal adaptive capture is not a dead-end local resource: it has a lawful fixed-turn continuation to interaction.

## Persistence
The terminal signs and resulting proper turn are graph-intrinsic; capture and the fixed-turn continuation are lineage-relative. The eventual interaction is historical. The original exact deletion cover and crossing need not remain current.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the eventual interaction is itself a spanning two-cover, does not localize the first interaction at the captured middle, does not preserve the original crossing or complement rails, does not give exterior-size descent, and does not close O3 by itself.

## Proof
Fix the chosen terminal dimer of the globally longest path K and assume no spanning two-cover occurs. Accepted R197 supplies an exact deletion two-cover with a selected crossing between the surviving carrier K-D and the exterior, an exterior sign witness avoiding that crossing, and capture capability for either physical endpoint. Choose the carrier endpoint x of the crossing and capture x. The other crossing endpoint y lies outside K. Because x lies on the historical path K of order at least three, it has a genuine historical carrier adjacency {p,x} in K. Apply R155 to the historical carrier K, crossing {x,y}, capture event at x, and adjacency {p,x}. R155 gives a later minimum three-cover containing the unique tight mirror trimer J on {p,x,y}, with x as its middle and with the capture retained as ancestry. This J is proper because K itself is proper and leaves at least the exterior vertices. Meanwhile the nonclosing R197 payment lineage supplies an ancestry-bearing balanced pair with one singleton support. If the opposite support is nontrivial, apply R428 to reach a both-singleton floor while preserving the existing capture/refund ancestry; if it is already singleton, we are already at such a floor. Apply R432 to steer this same ancestry-bearing floor to the unordered outer pair of J, retaining the incoming capture/refund ledger. We may now enter the R172 pair-bearing lineage for this fixed graph-intrinsic turn J. R172 gives a strictly decreasing nonnegative lexicographic rank on every quiet nonclosing nonexit macro step, so only finitely many such quiet episodes can occur. Since the standing branch excludes a spanning two-cover, eventual failure of quiet continuation is one of R172s explicit historical pair/contact exits. Thus the terminal crossing, fresh capture at its carrier endpoint, captured-middle mirror turn, and finite fixed-turn continuation to historical interaction all follow with exactly the lineage-relative persistence and no same-representative transport claimed by R210.
