# R321 — Seven vertices need not contain a tight P6

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
The explicit seven-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament of R230 has maximum tight-path order exactly five and nevertheless 18 of its 21 physical five-subsets support tight P5s. Thus a P6-free Strong system can have Hamilton-five support density 18/21=6/7. In particular no theorem whose only hypothesis is a Hamilton-five density lower bound at or below 6/7 can force a P6, even locally on seven vertices. This sharply fences attempts to turn R216 or R320 density alone into path-length growth.

## Hypotheses
The explicit seven-vertex turn table and exhaustive P6-free verification of accepted R230.

## Scope
Order-free local negative result strengthening R230 with exact P5-density data. It is a regression fence, not a smallest-counterexample model.

## Applicability
Use when evaluating density-to-growth strategies. R320 gives a stronger universal lower density than R216 for large r, but density alone is not the missing carrier-growth mechanism.

## Persistence
The explicit seven-vertex turn table and the list/count of Hamiltonian five-subsets are graph-intrinsic and finitely reconstructible.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not show 6/7 is the maximum P5 density of P6-free Strong systems, does not construct arbitrarily large P6-free examples, and does not refute a density-plus-minimality, carrier, exact-cover, or ancestry theorem.

## Proof
Use the explicit local tournaments recorded in R230. Exhaustive enumeration of all 21 physical five-subsets shows that exactly three fail to support a tight P5 and the remaining eighteen do. The same accepted R230 enumeration verifies that every ordered six-tuple has a bad consecutive turn, so the maximum tight-path order remains five. Hence the P5-support density is 18/21=6/7 in a genuinely P6-free Strong Level-(1) system.
