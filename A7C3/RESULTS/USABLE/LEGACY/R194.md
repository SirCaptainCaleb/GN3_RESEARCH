# R194 — Five-exterior internal replacement forces balanced-pair birth

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K be a proper tight path with exactly five exterior vertices E. Assume E is non-Hamiltonian. Fix an internal vertex k of K. Then for at least four vertices z in E, the five-set Q=(E\\{z}) union {k} supports a tight P5, and for each such z the complementary subsystem W=V(H)\\Q=(V(K)\\{k}) union {z} admits both a literal three-cover by the two nonempty path intervals of K-k together with singleton z and an exact two-cover. Consequently R159 yields a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair in H for each such replacement frame.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper tight path with exactly five exterior vertices E; E is non-Hamiltonian; k is not an endpoint of K.

## Scope
Arbitrary order. Bridge from five-exterior replacement saturation to the generic component-drop signed-pair machinery.

## Applicability
Use in the five-exterior equality face of a higher-order smallest counterexample. Every internal carrier vertex supplies at least four punctured complement frames in which a 3-to-2 path-cover component drop produces a balanced opposite-sign pair. This is a scalable entry to R171/R172-style historical-pair descent.

## Persistence
The forced P5 supports and balanced-pair existence are graph-intrinsic. The particular exact two-cover of each punctured complement is representative-relative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify or synchronize the balanced pairs obtained from different z or k. Does not say the pair supports are the two carrier intervals or the singleton. Does not itself put the pair into a literal spanning three-cover required by R171, and does not close the five-exterior face.

## Proof
As in the five-exterior saturation proof, E cannot be Hamiltonian. Fix internal k. For any two holes x,y in E, R8 orients E\\{x,y} as a tight trimer and R146 forces one of E, E-y+k, E-x+k to support a P5; E is impossible, so at most one replacement hole fails. Thus at least four z give a tight P5 Q=(E\\{z}) union {k}. Fix such z. The complement W is (K\\{k}) union {z}. Writing K=(K_L,k,K_R), both K_L and K_R are nonempty tight path intervals, hence K_L sqcup K_R sqcup (z) is a literal 3-cover of W. By R4, the complement of the proper tight path Q has an exact 2-cover. Since W is proper, R159 applies to these 3- and 2-covers and yields a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair in H.
