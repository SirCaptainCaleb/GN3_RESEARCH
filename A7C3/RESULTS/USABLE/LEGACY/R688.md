# R688 — The R682 cross-end alternative is witness-exclusive: a second admissible witness forces endpoint capture

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the setting of pending R682: the accepted crossed R621/R649 frame with X={a,b,c,z}, endpoints L=q_0 and R=q_m, an oriented middle-gate dimer S among (a,c),(c,a),(b,z),(z,b), its two R649 short carriers K_L with V(K_L)=V(S) union {L} and K_R with V(K_R)=V(S) union {R}, and an admissible witness, meaning a vertex outside X union {L,R} that is a sign witness on the tested orientation with the polarity R682 requires. Let T be any exact two-cover of H-V(S).

(1) Exclusivity. The XET branch holds for at most one vertex. Since V(S) is deleted from H-V(S), every selected state of T incident with an endpoint e in {L,R} automatically has its other endpoint outside K_e = V(S) union {e}. Hence the R682 CAP condition for a witness v fails exactly when every selected state of T at L and every selected state of T at R has other endpoint v. So XET(v) says precisely: the only selected states at L and at R are to v. In particular XET(v) and XET(v_prime) cannot both hold for v != v_prime in the same T.

(2) Two-witness capture. Let v_1 != v_2 both be admissible R682 witnesses on the same deleted pair V(S), each for some orientation of that pair. Both orientations of a middle-gate dimer delete the same two vertices, so the two R682 dichotomies quantify over the same set of exact covers T. Then every exact two-cover T of H-V(S) lies in the CAP branch for at least one of v_1,v_2. Indeed, if T were XET for v_1, then T selects the state {L,v_1}, whose other endpoint v_1 lies outside K_L and differs from v_2; that is exactly the CAP condition for v_2 at e=L, and symmetrically {R,v_1} gives CAP for v_2 at e=R. Consequently the R527 capture and R526 payment continuation supplied by R682 apply unconditionally, retaining a named physical endpoint, a named inward witness, the tested dimer, and an actual selected crossing as source ancestry.

(3) Sharp form. The cross-end alternative is therefore not an independent geometry that must be separately consumed; it is a uniqueness condition on T. With pending R684 it is equivalent to deg_T(L)=deg_T(R)=1 with common neighbour v and T=(L,v,R) sqcup P. Any second admissible decoration of the same deleted pair, of either orientation and either polarity, refutes it. The economical way to close the XET branch is therefore to multiply witnesses on a fixed deleted pair rather than to build a consumer for the cross-end trimer.

## Scope

## Applicability
Whenever a frame supplies two distinct exterior witnesses on the same deleted middle-gate pair, stop analysing XET and take the capture. Conversely, when only one witness is available, XET is the exact obstruction, and the cheapest repair is to produce a second witness on that same deleted pair rather than to consume the cross-end trimer.

## Proof
Write W=H-V(S) and let T be an exact two-cover of W.

Step 1. Normalize the CAP condition. R682 CAP for witness v asks for an endpoint e in {L,R} and a selected state {e,y} of T with y outside K_e=V(S) union {e} and y != v. Because V(S) has been deleted from W, no vertex of V(S) occurs in T at all, so any selected state {e,y} of T automatically has y outside K_e. The CAP condition for v is therefore equivalent to: some selected state of T at L or at R has other endpoint different from v.

Step 2. Exclusivity. R682 defines XET as the failure of CAP. By Step 1, XET(v) holds if and only if every selected state of T at L and every selected state at R has other endpoint exactly v. This determines v from T whenever any selected state at L or R exists, and R682 guarantees at least one such state at each of L and R by applying R508 to the two R649 carriers K_L and K_R in the same cover T. Hence XET(v) and XET(v_prime) with v != v_prime are incompatible in one T, which is claim (1).

Step 3. Two-witness capture. Let v_1 != v_2 be admissible witnesses on the same deleted pair V(S), for orientations S_1 and S_2 of that pair. Since V(S_1)=V(S_2)=V(S), both R682 dichotomies are statements about the same exact covers T of W. Fix T. If CAP holds for v_1 we are done. Otherwise XET(v_1) holds, so by Step 2 T selects {L,v_1} and {R,v_1}. The state {L,v_1} has other endpoint v_1, which lies outside K_L by Step 1 and satisfies v_1 != v_2. By Step 1 again this is exactly the CAP condition for v_2 at e=L. Hence CAP holds for v_2, proving (2). The R527 orientation and R526 payment continuation are then those already supplied by R682 for the witness whose CAP branch was realized, with the actual selected crossing {L,v_1} as the retained source crossing.

Step 4. Sharp form. Step 2 says XET(v) forces every selected state at L and at R to go to v, so L and R have T-degree one with common neighbour v; pending R684 records the resulting normal form T=(L,v,R) sqcup P. Thus XET is a uniqueness condition on T rather than an independent configuration, and is destroyed by any second admissible witness on the same deleted pair. This is (3).

The argument uses only the statement of R682, the fact that the two orientations of a middle-gate dimer delete the same pair, and elementary bookkeeping; it introduces no new geometry, no payment theory, and no order bound.
