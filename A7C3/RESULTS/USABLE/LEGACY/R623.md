# R623 — Every crossed R24 root is a preserved singleton coordinate for arbitrary target floors

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the crossed phase and the sixteen root-labelled singleton-deletion cross states of R621. Fix a deleted root r in {a,b,c,z} and any one of the four R621 cross states belonging to H-r. The R176 birth generated from that cross state can be chosen with one signed support literally the singleton (r), and its opposite signed support of order at most two. Consequently there is a finite chosen ancestry-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover of H or a both-singleton balanced-pair floor whose one singleton is still exactly (r). Moreover, for every prescribed vertex x distinct from r, there is a further chosen continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover or an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor with supports exactly {r},{x}, with r preserved throughout the nonclosing payment and steering. Thus in the crossed R24 phase each of the four deletion roots is a universal preserved floor coordinate, already at singleton-deletion level; the four cross states for a fixed root provide alternative birth ancestries for the same root-pinned capability.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the crossed phase of pending R621; Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4. Fix one R621 cross state in the proper residue W=H-r. Use the fully reconstructible accepted proof of R176, accepted R428, and accepted R432.

## Scope
Noncircular R24 restoration. Crossed phase. Strengthens the strategic content of R621 by exploiting the fact that its R176 spare vertex is uniquely the deleted root.

## Applicability
Use before generic pair payment or R620. When a downstream argument wants a floor containing a specific crossed-phase root r and any chosen second coordinate x, start from any R621 cross state for H-r, preserve r through the short payment, then steer only the opposite singleton to x. This keeps the deletion root physically pinned while retaining the original deep/shallow weave ancestry.

## Persistence
Retain r as the literal singleton signed support from the R176 birth; the selected R621 cross state and compared exact covers; the common R176 witness and local opposite support; all R428 refund certificates; the original deep/shallow Q-cut ancestry; and, during target steering, the fixed singleton r and the replacement certificate installing x.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim target floors for different x coexist. Does not claim the four cross-state ancestries for one root can be paid simultaneously. Does not preserve the original R621 exact covers as current through payment. Does not close an arbitrary root-pinned floor. Does not invalidate the R620 staircase route.

## Proof
Fix a root r and one R621 selected cross state xy in the residue W=H-r. In the fully reconstructible proof P540 of accepted R176, the spare vertex d may be chosen arbitrarily from V(H)-W. Here V(H)-W={r}, so d=r is forced. Let R_x be the component, in the crossed source cover, containing the selected cross endpoint x.

If R_x is nontrivial, P540 chooses a literal old neighbor p of x in R_x and, by R3, produces either a head-signed oriented dimer on {x,p} with common witness y or the tail-signed reversed dimer on the same two vertices with witness y. The automatic two-vertex path through d=r and y gives the opposite singleton sign on (r). Hence the R176 balanced pair has support profile 2+1 and one support is literally (r). If R_x is a singleton component, P540 instead uses the selected state xy itself to sign singleton (x), while the same automatic path through r and y gives the opposite singleton sign on (r). Hence the birth is already a both-singleton floor {r,x}. These are the only cases.

In the 2+1 case apply accepted R428 with p=r as the fixed singleton coordinate and the signed dimer as the opposite nontrivial support. Along its chosen nonclosing continuation, R428 preserves singleton (r) unchanged and strictly pays the opposite support until it is singleton. Thus either H acquires a spanning two-cover or an ancestry-bearing floor {r,v} is reached for some v, with the entire R621/R176 birth ancestry retained. In the 1+1 case take v=x and no payment is needed.

Now fix any prescribed vertex x_0 distinct from r. Apply accepted R432 to the active floor with target pair {r,x_0}. The proof of R432 requires only one replacement macro when the current floor already shares one target coordinate; choose the shared coordinate to be r. Therefore r is retained while the other singleton is replaced by x_0. The continuation either closes H or reaches exactly the floor {r,x_0}, with the incoming ancestry ledger retained. This proves both the root-pinned floor and arbitrary-target claims.
