# R507 — Current-trimer recompletion crossings compile to boundary collision, P4, or reverse contact

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and H=J sqcup U sqcup V a literal spanning three-cover with J=(p,x,z) a tight trimer and U,V nonempty. Retain the dual forced recompletion crossings of R506. (P-deletion.) Put D=(x,z). In any exact two-cover of H-p choose a selected state crossing the physical cut {x,z} | (U union V), and let y outside {x,z} be its non-D endpoint. If the D-endpoint of the crossing is x, then either (y,x,z) is tight, in which case D carries a same-polarity two-witness interaction with the natural J-witness p and the recompletion witness y, or the exact reverse-contact trimer (z,x,y) is tight. If the D-endpoint is z, then either (x,z,y) is tight, in which case K=(p,x,z,y) is a literal tight P4 extending J through the recompletion vertex y, or the reverse-contact trimer (y,z,x) is tight. (Z-deletion.) Put E=(p,x). In any exact two-cover of H-z choose a selected crossing from {p,x} to y outside. If the E-endpoint is p, then either (y,p,x) is tight, in which case K=(y,p,x,z) is a literal tight P4 extending J at its head, or the reverse-contact trimer (x,p,y) is tight. If the E-endpoint is x, then either (p,x,y) is tight, in which case E carries a same-polarity two-witness interaction with natural J-witness z and recompletion witness y, or the reverse-contact trimer (y,x,p) is tight. Thus each compulsory singleton-deletion crossing of a current trimer has an explicit boundary-dimer consumer pinned to which trimer endpoint was touched.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; H=J sqcup U sqcup V with J=(p,x,z) tight and U,V nonempty; accepted R506; accepted R3,R38.

## Scope
Order-free graph-intrinsic local compiler for the forced recompletion crossings of a current trimer. The outputs remain pinned to the exact surviving boundary dimer and touched endpoint.

## Applicability
Use immediately after R506 or historical R333. Inspect which vertex of the surviving boundary dimer participates in the selected recompletion crossing, then apply the corresponding R3 reversal pair. Do not stop at an anonymous cross state.

## Persistence
Retain J, the chosen outer deletion, surviving oriented boundary dimer D or E, the exact recompletion cover, selected physical crossing, touched dimer endpoint, outside vertex y, and the resulting collision/P4/reverse-trimer certificate. Collision witnesses are the named deleted outer J-vertex and y.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert any one P4, collision, or reverse trimer closes H. Does not assert the two outer-deletion recompletion covers coexist. The selected crossing direction in the exact cover is irrelevant to the local R3 classification; only the physical endpoint of the surviving dimer that it touches is used.

## Proof
For H-p the surviving boundary dimer is D=(x,z), and J=(p,x,z) gives the natural tight head-sign turn (p,x,z) on D with witness p. Let y be the outside endpoint of a selected recompletion crossing from the physical set {x,z}. If the crossing touches x, apply R3 with middle x to {y,x,z}: exactly one of (y,x,z) and (z,x,y) is tight. In the first case (y,x,z) is another head-sign certificate on the identical oriented support D=(x,z), with witness y distinct from p, so R38 gives the same-polarity two-witness collision package on D. In the second case retain the explicit reverse-contact trimer (z,x,y). If the crossing touches z, apply R3 with middle z: exactly one of (x,z,y) and (y,z,x) is tight. In the first case it concatenates literally with J to the tight P4 (p,x,z,y); in the second retain (y,z,x). For H-z the surviving oriented dimer is E=(p,x), and J gives its natural tail-sign turn (p,x,z) with witness z. If a forced crossing touches p, R3 at middle p gives either (y,p,x), which concatenates with J to (y,p,x,z), or (x,p,y), the reverse contact. If it touches x, R3 at middle x gives either (p,x,y), a second tail-sign on E with witness y and hence an R38 same-polarity collision with natural witness z, or (y,x,p), the reverse contact. These are exactly the four asserted cases.
