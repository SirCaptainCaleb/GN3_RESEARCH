# R206 — Four-row common-hole synchronization does not force the local carrier replacement

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
There exists a nine-vertex Level-(1) boundary-antisymmetric turn system with a tight four-path Q=(q0,q1,q2,q3), a five-set E=F union {z} with |F|=4, such that E is non-Hamiltonian, every five-set F union {q_i} supports a tight P5 for i=0,1,2,3, but Q union {z} is non-Hamiltonian. Hence even perfect four-row synchronization on one common exterior four-set, together with a literal tight carrier P4, does not by itself force the most local omitted-vertex carrier-side P5.

## Hypotheses
Only Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry is used. This is a local turn-system countermodel, not a smallest-counterexample model and not a model of the full five-exterior equality face.

## Scope
Local negative result for the five-exterior common-hole strategy.

## Applicability
Use as a proof-design fence after four-row common-hole synchronization: a closure must exploit additional information such as the actual orderings/ancestry of the exterior-side P5s, exact-cover incidence, signs, or longer-carrier context. Bare support Hamiltonicity of the four completions is insufficient.

## Persistence
The countermodel is a fixed graph-intrinsic turn table on nine labeled vertices.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not show that the full R192 equality-face hypotheses are realizable without a complementary replacement. Does not refute a theorem using the entire carrier K, smallest-counterexample minimality, or synchronized P5 orderings.

## Proof
Use vertices Q={0,1,2,3}, F={4,5,6,7}, z=8. For each unordered triple i<j<k, use the three reversal pairs A: (i,j,k)/(k,j,i), B: (i,k,j)/(j,k,i), C: (j,i,k)/(k,i,j). By default declare the canonical A/B/C order bad and its complete reversal tight. Reverse that choice, declaring the canonical order tight, exactly for the following 31 canonical orders: 012,021,031,108,023,032,203,028,208,038,308,123,132,182,138,183,283,275,527,546,457,547,458,485,486,567,657,568,658,758,768. This specifies exactly one tight member of every reversal pair and therefore satisfies boundary antisymmetry. In this table Q=(0,1,2,3) is tight. The four supports F+q_i have respectively the following explicit tight P5 orderings: for q0, (5,7,4,6,0); for q1, (6,5,7,1,4); for q2, (6,7,5,2,4); for q3, (6,5,7,4,3). A direct finite check of all 120 vertex orders on E={4,5,6,7,8} finds no tight P5, and the same check on Q+z={0,1,2,3,8} finds no tight P5. For a compact independent encoding, listing the A,B,C truth bits in lexicographic order of unordered triples gives the 252-bit hexadecimal signature c8000f000a00140000000c0008003000000000008000300000000000dc10b49, with 1 meaning the canonical order is tight. Thus all asserted positive and negative Hamiltonicity claims are exactly reconstructible from the displayed turn table.
