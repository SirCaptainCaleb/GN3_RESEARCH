## Proof
Write L=q_0,u=q_1,R=q_m,s=q_{m-1}. Accepted R536 in the crossed R621 frame gives q_3 q_2 b tight, so D_L=(q_3,q_2) is tail-signed by b. Dually a q_{m-2} q_{m-3} is tight, so D_R=(q_{m-2},q_{m-3}) is head-signed by a.

LEFT PRESERVED ANCHOR. If m=6, D_L=(q_3,q_2) and D_R=(q_4,q_3) meet only at q_3 with opposite polarities. R547 gives the singleton floor {q_2,q_4}; in its R425 hinge cut, q_2 is the old tail endpoint of D_L, hence its old witness b remains a graph-intrinsic tail-sign certificate. If m>=7, D_L and D_R are disjoint opposite-polarity signed dimers, hence form a direct balanced pair (R39). Apply R425 to D_L with deletion set {q_3}. The surviving last interval is singleton (q_2), and because q_2 is the old tail endpoint its witness remains b. D_R is disjoint from q_3 and is untouched, so (q_2) together with D_R is still a balanced opposite-sign pair. Apply R428 with singleton q_2 fixed. Unless H is already two-covered, this reaches a both-singleton floor containing q_2. Thus in every m>=6 case we have an ancestry-bearing floor containing q_2 and the historical tail-sign certificate q_2 witnessed by b. Apply R432 with target {q_2,b}; along the nonclosing one-coordinate replacement q_2 is preserved and the full incoming ancestry ledger, including the old q_2-b sign certificate, remains historical.

Put W=H-{q_2,b}. The accepted R621 shallow cover H-b=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (R,c,a,z), punctured at internal q_2, gives three nonempty tight paths A=Q[0,1], B=Q[3,m-1], C=(R,c,a,z) covering W. By R429 choose an exact two-cover T of W. Since T has two components and A,B,C are three nonempty source components, some selected state xy of T joins two different source components. Apply the fully reconstructed R176 proof to this crossing with spare d=q_2. In P540 the spare d receives a singleton sign using one selected endpoint y as witness, through the automatic ordered two-vertex path joining d and y. Denote that actual tight extension dimer by E_new; it has support {q_2,y}, contains the anchor q_2, and is nontrivial. Since y lies in W while b is deleted, y!=b. The old historical signed support is singleton (q_2) with witness b. Apply R436 to that old singleton support and the later proper tight path E_new. Because E_new has order two, the exact-singleton stationary replay exception is impossible. Hence R436 gives strict path growth, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a reverse-contact/reversal cell at q_2.

RIGHT DUAL. If m=6, R547 leaves q_4=q_{m-2} from D_R and its old head witness a survives the hinge cut. If m>=7, apply R425 to D_R deleting q_{m-3}; singleton q_{m-2} remains head-signed by a while D_L is untouched, and R428 preserves q_{m-2} to a both-singleton floor. R432 steers the other coordinate to a. In W_R=H-{q_{m-2},a}, puncturing q_{m-2} from the shallow cover H-a=(c,z,b,L) sqcup Q[1,m] gives the three nonempty tight paths (c,z,b,L), Q[1,m-3], and Q[m-1,m]. Any R429 exact two-cover crosses two source components. Apply R176 with spare d=q_{m-2}; its automatic sign-extension dimer E_new^R has support {q_{m-2},y} with y in W_R and hence y!=a. R436 applied to the retained old singleton q_{m-2} and this nontrivial later dimer gives the dual non-replay event. This proves R661 without R224 or any R24-circular dependency.
