# R199 — Global-longest universal exterior endpoint shield

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let K=(k_1,...,k_m) be a globally longest tight path in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. For every vertex w outside K, the direct endpoint attachment turns (w,k_1,k_2) and (k_{m-1},k_m,w) are bad. Hence by boundary antisymmetry the reverse endpoint turns (k_2,k_1,w) and (w,k_m,k_{m-1}) are tight. Thus every exterior vertex simultaneously witnesses both terminal reverse systems of K as graph-intrinsic facts.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; K is a globally longest tight path; w is exterior to K.

## Scope
Order-free graph-intrinsic endpoint shielding for globally longest paths.

## Applicability
Use whenever several exact roots or exterior cells retain one globally longest path K. All exterior vertices can be treated as common witnesses to both terminal reverse systems without any representative synchronization.

## Persistence
The bad direct attachments and tight reverse turns are graph-intrinsic relative to the fixed ordering of K and survive representative changes.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert any exterior vertex attaches internally, does not join two exterior witnesses, and does not by itself produce a longer path, exact cover, sign currentization, or payment descendant.

## Proof
Fix w outside K=(k_1,...,k_m). If (w,k_1,k_2) were tight, then (w,k_1,...,k_m) would be a tight path of order m+1, contradicting global longestness of K. Therefore (w,k_1,k_2) is bad, and R3 makes its complete reversal (k_2,k_1,w) tight. Likewise, if (k_{m-1},k_m,w) were tight then (k_1,...,k_m,w) would extend K, so this direct turn is bad and R3 makes (w,k_m,k_{m-1}) tight. The argument is independent for every exterior w, so all asserted reverse turns hold simultaneously as graph-intrinsic facts.
