# R501 — Anti-aligned two-cut bridge side compiler

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the hypotheses and notation of R495. Then at least one of the following occurs.

(DR) The R495 double ancestral-end reversal K_D=(a_1,a_0,b_q,b_{q-1}) is tight.

(HP4) In the head-fork branch HF, the four-set X_H={a_1,z_1,a_0,b_q} supports a tight Hamilton P4.

(HR) In the head-fork branch HF, both tested orientations of the bridge chord {a_0,b_q} are head-signed by both named witnesses a_1 and z_1, and both tested orientations of the cross-arm head dimer {a_1,z_1} are head-signed by the common witness a_0. Explicitly, in addition to the retained R495 turns (a_1,a_0,b_q) and (z_1,a_0,b_q), the turns (a_1,b_q,a_0), (z_1,b_q,a_0), (a_0,a_1,z_1), and (a_0,z_1,a_1) are tight. Hence accepted R407 applies to the bidirectional dimer {a_1,z_1} with common witness a_0 and yields a fixed-trimer historical signed-support/anchor or cross-generation pair interaction localized at the exact deleted bridge endpoint a_0.

(TP4) In the tail-fork branch TF, the four-set X_T={b_{q-1},z_{m-1},a_0,b_q} supports a tight Hamilton P4.

(TR) In the tail-fork branch TF, both tested orientations of the bridge chord {a_0,b_q} are tail-signed by both named witnesses b_{q-1} and z_{m-1}, and both tested orientations of the cross-arm tail dimer {b_{q-1},z_{m-1}} are tail-signed by the common witness b_q. Explicitly, in addition to the retained R495 turns (a_0,b_q,b_{q-1}) and (a_0,b_q,z_{m-1}), the turns (b_q,a_0,b_{q-1}), (b_q,a_0,z_{m-1}), (z_{m-1},b_{q-1},b_q), and (b_{q-1},z_{m-1},b_q) are tight. Hence R407 applies to {b_{q-1},z_{m-1}} with common witness b_q and yields the corresponding historical interaction localized at the exact deleted bridge endpoint b_q.

Thus an R495 candidate-aware fork never stops at a bare same-support collision: it either creates a role-labelled four-vertex Hamilton path joining the two fork witnesses to the bridge chord, or creates a bidirectional cross-arm signed dimer whose common witness is one of the original deleted bridge endpoints.

## Proof
Assume first the head fork HF of R495 and abbreviate x=a_1, y=z_1, a=a_0, b=b_q. The retained fork gives the two tight trimers C_x=(x,a,b) and C_y=(y,a,b). Apply accepted R518 to C_x with fourth vertex y. If the four-set {x,y,a,b} has a tight Hamilton P4, output HP4. Otherwise the corrected no-P4 branch forces the reverse contacts (a,x,y) and (y,b,a) tight. Now apply R518 to C_y with fourth vertex x. Again a P4 gives HP4; otherwise the no-P4 branch forces (a,y,x) and (x,b,a) tight. In the joint no-P4 branch we therefore have (x,a,b),(y,a,b),(x,b,a),(y,b,a),(a,x,y),(a,y,x) all tight. The first four say that both tested orientations (a,b) and (b,a) of the bridge chord are head-signed by both witnesses x,y. The last two say that both tested orientations (x,y) and (y,x) of the physical cross-arm dimer are head-signed by the common witness a. Accepted R407 therefore applies to D={x,y} with common witness a and produces the stated historical fixed-trimer/anchor or cross-generation pair interaction localized at a=a_0. This proves HR. The tail fork is dual, but write it explicitly to preserve tested order. Put x=b_{q-1}, y=z_{m-1}, a=a_0, b=b_q. R495 gives C_x=(a,b,x) and C_y=(a,b,y) tight. Apply R518 to C_x with fourth vertex y. Outside TP4, the no-P4 branch forces (b,a,y) and (y,x,b) tight. Apply R518 to C_y with fourth vertex x. Outside TP4, it forces (b,a,x) and (x,y,b) tight. Hence both orientations (a,b),(b,a) of the bridge chord are tail-signed by x,y, while both orientations (x,y),(y,x) of the cross-arm tail dimer are tail-signed by the common witness b=b_q. R407 applies at b_q and gives TR. Together with the DR alternative already present in R495, these exhaust the fork.
