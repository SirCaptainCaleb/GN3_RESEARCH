# R532 — Historical-Support Terminal-Reversal Payment

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Let S be a graph-intrinsic signed tight support and let T=(t_0,...,t_m), m>=1, be a tight path vertex-disjoint from S. Let D be either terminal physical dimer of T and suppose a signed incarnation of D has polarity opposite to S, so (S,D) is an ancestry-bearing balanced opposite-sign pair of initial mass M=|S|+2. Then there is a chosen certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover or a strict descendant balanced pair of mass M'<M. In addition, the named terminal position of D in T supplies an explicit terminal extension/reversal certificate before the descent: for any chosen vertex nu in S, testing the literal T-side attachment of nu at D gives either a tight one-vertex terminal extension of T at that end or, by boundary antisymmetry, the exact reverse terminal trimer. This terminal certificate, the original signed-pair birth, and the physical terminal dimer D remain retained as historical ancestry of the chosen descent. The strict width decrease may be chosen so that either prescribed physical endpoint of D is the singleton surviving the first refund step. The head/tail and left/right terminal cases are exact duals.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong counterexample; S and T are vertex-disjoint tight paths; D is a terminal physical dimer of T carrying a graph-intrinsic sign opposite in polarity to S.

## Scope
Modern explicit replacement for the legacy terminal-reversal payment packet; order-free and certificate-retaining.

## Applicability
Use when a historical signed support is paired with an oppositely signed terminal dimer of a named tight path and a strict paid descendant is needed while retaining the terminal position/reversal certificate.

## Persistence
Retains the original pair birth, terminal path T and physical terminal dimer D as historical coordinates, the displayed terminal extension/reversal turn, the chosen endpoint-survival refund marker, and the resulting descendant pair. No old cover is asserted current after the refund.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not preserve the whole path T as current after payment; does not assert both endpoint-selective descendants simultaneously; does not by itself close the both-singleton floor.

## Proof
Let D be the stated signed terminal dimer of T and name the inherited tight turn witnessing its sign. First record the terminal-position certificate independently of payment. If D is the right terminal dimer (t_{m-1},t_m), choose any nu in V(S) and test (t_{m-1},t_m,nu). If tight, (t_0,...,t_m,nu) is a one-vertex tight terminal extension of T. If bad, R3 gives the exact reversal (nu,t_m,t_{m-1}) tight. For the left terminal dimer, test (nu,t_0,t_1); if tight then (nu,t_0,...,t_m) extends T, and if bad R3 gives (t_1,t_0,nu) tight. Thus in all four left/right polarity cases an explicit terminal extension-or-reversal turn is retained. Now ignore currentness of T and use the graph-intrinsic balanced pair (S,D). Its initial mass is M=|S|+2, with |S|>=1. Accepted R224 applies to the signed physical dimer D and the opposite-polarity support S. Prescribe either physical endpoint z of D. R224 gives a chosen ancestry-retaining continuation that either produces a spanning two-cover or first refunds D to the signed singleton (z) and then reaches a mass-two both-singleton floor while preserving z. In the nonclosing branch the resulting mass Mprime=2 is strictly smaller than M. Because the terminal extension/reversal turn, T, D, the original pair birth, and the chosen endpoint are graph-intrinsic historical certificate data, they remain retained while the R224 continuation changes representatives. This proves the full R532 contract, including endpoint selectivity and all left/right head/tail duals.
