# R276 — Rigid double-contact cell has transitive perfect-matching quotient

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
In the unique no-P4 double-contact four-cell of R275 on X={a,b,c,d}, partition the six physical dimers into the three opposite-edge perfect matchings B_1={{a,b},{c,d}}, B_2={{a,c},{b,d}}, and B_3={{a,d},{b,c}}. At every physical vertex, the three incident dimers inherit the same strict local order B_1>B_2>B_3 from the tight-turn signature of R275. Explicitly: at a, ab>ac>ad; at b, ab>bd>bc; at c, cd>ac>bc; at d, cd>bd>ad. Hence the four-cell has a uniform transitive three-block quotient. In particular it is not a cyclic-quotient matching-block cell.

## Proof
From R275, the local neighbor orders are: middle a: b>c>d; middle b: a>d>c; middle c: d>a>b; middle d: c>b>a. Define B1={{a,b},{c,d}}, B2={{a,c},{b,d}}, B3={{a,d},{b,c}}. At vertex a the incident edges are ab in B1, ac in B2, ad in B3, so the local order is B1>B2>B3. At b the incident edges are ba=ab in B1, bd in B2, bc in B3; the R275 order a>d>c again gives B1>B2>B3. At c the incident edges cd in B1, ca=ac in B2, cb=bc in B3; d>a>b gives B1>B2>B3. At d the incident edges dc=cd in B1, db=bd in B2, da=ad in B3; c>b>a gives B1>B2>B3. Thus orientation between matching blocks is uniform and the quotient is the transitive order B1>B2>B3. A cyclic quotient would require a directed 3-cycle among the three block labels, so this cell is not cyclic.
