# R506 — Every current trimer component forces dual recompletion cut crossings

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let H=J sqcup U sqcup V be any literal spanning three-cover in which J=(p,x,z) is a tight trimer and U,V are nonempty tight paths. Put D={x,z} and E={p,x}. Then every exact two-cover of H-p contains a selected state crossing the physical cut D | (U union V), and every exact two-cover of H-z contains a selected state crossing the cut E | (U union V). Equivalently, after deleting either outer endpoint of a current trimer component, the surviving boundary dimer cannot remain isolated from the other two source components in any exact recompletion. This conclusion depends only on the literal trimer three-cover and smallest-counterexample deletion floors; capture, first-contact, mirror ancestry, and endpoint-universality are unnecessary.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; H=J sqcup U sqcup V is a literal spanning three-cover; J=(p,x,z) is tight; U,V are nonempty. Accepted R5 and R24.

## Scope
Order-free generic parent of historical R333. It isolates the exact recompletion-crossing content of a current trimer component.

## Applicability
Use whenever a proper tight trimer is current as one component of a minimum three-cover and downstream work needs a selected crossing tied to one of its two boundary dimers after deleting the opposite outer endpoint.

## Persistence
Retain the source current trimer J, its physical boundary dimers D,E, and source residual rails U,V. The forced crossing statement quantifies over every exact two-cover of H-p or H-z; individual crossings remain representative-relative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the two singleton-deletion covers coexist, share a crossing, or preserve J as current. Does not itself capture or protect the forced crossing and does not imply a spanning two-cover. The theorem requires both residual source components U,V to be nonempty.

## Proof
Retain the hypotheses of R506. Thus H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and

  H = J sqcup U sqcup V,
  J=(p,x,z)

is a literal spanning three-cover with U,V nonempty tight paths.

DELETE p. The subsystem H-p is nonempty and proper, so accepted smallest-counterexample minimality R4 gives pc(H-p)<=2. It cannot be Hamiltonian: if Q_p were a Hamilton tight path on H-p, then Q_p together with the singleton path (p) would be a spanning two-path cover of H, contradicting pc(H)=3. Therefore

  pc(H-p)=2.                                               (1)

Let T be any exact two-cover of H-p. In the notation of accepted R508 take deletion set D={p}, absorbable block S={x,z}, and certified recompletion path Q=J=(p,x,z). Then Q spans exactly D union S. Moreover S is nonempty and proper inside V(H-p), because U and V are both nonempty. R508 therefore forces T to select a directed state crossing

  {x,z} | (V(U) union V(V)).                               (2)

Since T was arbitrary, every exact two-cover of H-p has the required crossing.

DELETE z. The same argument gives pc(H-z)=2: R4 gives at most two, and Hamiltonicity of H-z together with singleton z would two-cover H. Let T' be any exact two-cover of H-z. Apply R508 with deletion D'={z}, absorbable block S'={p,x}, and the same certified trimer Q=J. Again S' is nonempty and proper because U,V are nonempty. Hence T' selects a state crossing

  {p,x} | (V(U) union V(V)).                               (3)

Equations (2) and (3) are exactly the two conclusions of R506. No R5, R24, rail-size floor, capture, or endpoint-universality input is used.
