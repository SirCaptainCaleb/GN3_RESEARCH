# R612 — Opposite boundary carriers with three spare vertices force short-carrier payment

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Let A,B,p,q be vertices with tight trimers K_+=(p,A,B) or (A,B,p), so {A,B} occurs as one boundary state in orientation A->B, and K_-=(q,B,A) or (B,A,q), so the same physical boundary occurs in orientation B->A. Suppose there are three distinct vertices x_1,x_2,x_3 outside V(K_+) union V(K_-). Then one of the two tested orientations (A,B) or (B,A) carries a same-polarity two-witness packet whose witnesses lie outside the opposite carrier, and hence R542 forces a certificate-retaining capture/payment continuation. More explicitly, for each x_i, R3 makes exactly one of x_i A B and B A x_i tight. If two indices choose x_iAB, the tested dimer (A,B) is head-signed by those two witnesses and is the reverse boundary dimer of K_-. If two choose BAx_i, the tested dimer (B,A) is tail-signed by those two witnesses and is the reverse boundary dimer of K_+. By pigeonhole one case occurs, so R542 applies. The exact head/tail/reflected variants are included by orienting the two carriers so their displayed common boundary states are A,B and B,A.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; two tight trimers carry opposite orientations of one common physical boundary dimer; at least three distinct spare vertices lie outside both trimer supports; R3 and R542.

## Scope
Order-free Strong Level-(1) payment parent. No R24, global-longestness, deletion-root synchronization, R5, R168, R169, or computation.

## Applicability
Whenever a path comparison produces an exact reversal of a boundary state of a retained tight trimer, do not stop at a generic reversal/contact output: with three spare vertices the reversed state itself forces an R542 payment packet.

## Persistence
Retain the two opposite boundary carriers, the physical boundary dimer, the three spare vertices, the two witnesses selected by pigeonhole, the tested dimer orientation and polarity, and the R542 carrier/payment ancestry.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert which orientation wins the pigeonhole and does not assert the R542 paid descendant closes H.

## Proof
For i=1,2,3, boundary antisymmetry on the distinct triple {x_i,A,B} says exactly one of x_iAB and BAx_i is tight. If x_iAB is tight for at least two indices i,j, then the identical tested oriented dimer D=(A,B) is head-signed by the two distinct witnesses x_i,x_j. Because K_- contains the boundary state B,A, D=(A,B) is exactly the reverse boundary dimer of K_-. Both witnesses are outside K_- by hypothesis, so R542 applies. Otherwise at most one x_iAB is tight, hence BAx_i is tight for at least two indices. Then D'=(B,A) is tail-signed by two distinct spare witnesses. Since K_+ contains the boundary state A,B, D' is exactly its reverse boundary dimer, and the witnesses lie outside K_+; R542 applies again. This exhausts the two pigeonhole classes.
