# R775 — The universal first-inward stars force a depth-two P5 or a cross-depth P4 on every X-pair

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R621/R723 frame with X={a,b,c,z}, Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4, and write u=q_1, v=q_2, d=q_{m-2}, s=q_{m-1}. Accepted R723 gives, for every x in X, the two tight first-inward trimers (v,u,x) and (x,s,d). Then exactly one of the following two regimes holds. (P5) For some x in X the middle turn (u,x,s) is tight. Then the three consecutive turns (v,u,x), (u,x,s), (x,s,d) certify the explicit tight Hamilton P5 J_x=(v,u,x,s,d) on {q_2,q_1,x,q_{m-1},q_{m-2}}. (CROSS-P4) For every x in X the reverse middle turn (s,x,u) is tight. In this regime, for every two distinct x,y in X, boundary antisymmetry on the triple {x,u,y} makes exactly one of (x,u,y) and (y,u,x) tight. If (x,u,y) is tight, then (s,x,u,y) is a tight P4; if (y,u,x) is tight, then (s,y,u,x) is a tight P4. Hence in the no-P5 regime every unordered X-pair supports a role-labelled cross-depth P4 containing both first-inward hinges q_{m-1} and q_1. Thus the universal first-inward stars cannot remain as four unrelated one-sided trimers: they assemble either into a depth-two two-ended P5 through one X vertex or into a complete pair-indexed family of cross-depth P4s through q_{m-1},q_1.

## Hypotheses

## Proof
For every x in X, accepted R723 gives (v,u,x) and (x,s,d) tight. Apply R3 to the triple {u,x,s}. Exactly one of (u,x,s) and (s,x,u) is tight. If the first occurs for some x, the vertex-simple order (v,u,x,s,d) has consecutive tight turns (v,u,x), (u,x,s), (x,s,d), proving P5. Suppose no x has (u,x,s) tight. Then (s,x,u) is tight for every x in X. Fix distinct x,y. Apply R3 to {x,u,y}; exactly one of (x,u,y),(y,u,x) is tight. In the first case, (s,x,u) and (x,u,y) certify P4 (s,x,u,y). In the second, (s,y,u) and (y,u,x) certify P4 (s,y,u,x). Since x,y were arbitrary, every unordered X-pair has such a cross-depth P4. This proves the dichotomy.
