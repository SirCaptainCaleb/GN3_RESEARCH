# R610 — Minimum R24 wrap amplification: reverse P4 or universal bidirectional wrap saturation

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the exact-two-bad transitive R24 cell with globally longest rail Q=(L=q_0,q_1,...,q_{m-1},R=q_m), m>=3. By R609 Q is not double-wrap. If Q is in the double-fail branch, R579 gives the tight reverse P4 (q_1,L,R,q_{m-1}). If Q is in either single-wrap branch, then the physical wrap dimer {L,R} is saturated in both tested orientations by all four vertices of X with one common polarity. In SW1 (alpha=RLq_1 tight, beta=q_{m-1}RL bad), both LRx and RLx are tight for every x in X, so both (L,R) and (R,L) are tail-signed by every x. In SW2 (alpha bad, beta tight), both xLR and xRL are tight for every x in X, so both wrap orientations are head-signed by every x. Consequently, in either single-wrap branch, for every prescribed t in X each of the two tested wrap orientations is directly R542-payable using a tight trimer on {L,R,t} and any two witnesses from X-{t}; thus the same deleted physical pair {L,R} supports an eight-channel short-carrier payment fan indexed by t in X and the two wrap orientations.

## Proof
Let alpha=RLq_1 and beta=q_{m-1}RL. R609 excludes double wrap. In DF, R579 gives q_1LR and LRq_{m-1} tight, hence the P4 (q_1,L,R,q_{m-1}). Now assume SW1: alpha is tight and beta bad, so rot1=(R,L,q_1,...,q_{m-1}) is a tight Hamilton order of supp(Q). Since Q is globally longest, rot1 is globally longest. For every x in X, xRL is therefore bad, else x could be prepended; R3 gives LRx tight for all x. We force RLx for all x using P6-freeness of X union {L,R}. First, in (z,c,a,L,R,b), zca,caL,LRb are tight, so aLR is bad and RLa tight. Second, in (b,L,R,c,a,z), LRc,Rca,caz are tight, so bLR is bad and RLb tight. Third, in (a,b,c,L,R,z), abc,bcL,LRz are tight, so cLR is bad and RLc tight. Finally, in (z,L,R,a,b,c), LRa,Rab,abc are tight, so zLR is bad and RLz tight. Hence both LRx and RLx are tight for every x: both wrap orientations are tail-signed by all X. In SW2, rot2=(q_1,...,q_{m-1},R,L) is globally longest. Appending x is impossible, so RLx is bad and xLR tight for every x. Reflect again. Candidate (z,c,a,L,R,b) with zca,caL,aLR tight forces LRb bad and bRL tight. Candidate (b,L,R,c,a,z) with bLR,Rca,caz tight forces LRc bad and cRL tight. Candidate (a,b,c,L,R,z) with abc,bcL,cLR tight forces LRz bad and zRL tight. Candidate (b,c,L,R,a,z) has bcL,cLR,Raz tight (Raz is R607), so LRa is bad and aRL tight. Thus xLR and xRL are tight for every x: both wrap orientations are head-signed by all X. For the payment fan, fix t in X. In SW1, (R,L,t) and (L,R,t) are tight carriers; (L,R) and (R,L) are their respective reverse left-boundary dimers and have at least three exterior tail witnesses X-{t}. In SW2, (t,R,L) and (t,L,R) are tight carriers; the two wrap orientations are the respective reverse right-boundary dimers and have at least three exterior head witnesses. Choose any two of those witnesses and invoke R542. This works independently for each t and each tested wrap orientation.
