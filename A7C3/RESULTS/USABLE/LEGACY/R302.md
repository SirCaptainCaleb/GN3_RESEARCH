# R302 — Bare R38 interaction existence is universal and is not a progress alternative

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be any Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let D={u,v} be any physical dimer with two distinct witnesses x,y outside D. Then D carries one signed certificate with witness x and one signed certificate with witness y, so R38 forces a same-support two-witness interaction on D. Consequently every Strong Level-(1) system of order at least four already contains an R38 interaction. Therefore a theorem alternative stated only as either an R38 same-support interaction exists or some residue holds is logically vacuous in its first branch unless the interaction is localized by specified physical support, specified witnesses, ancestry, currentness, or another consumer-relevant coordinate.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; accepted R3, R38, and the terminal-dimer sign convention R41.

## Scope
Order-free proof-design correction and local theorem. It concerns bare existential R38-productivity, not coordinate-localized R38 certificates.

## Applicability
Use when evaluating claimed progress whose output is merely existence of some R38 same-support two-witness interaction. Such existence is free once four vertices are present. Useful descendants must retain nontrivial support/witness identity, ancestry, polarity branch, current geometry, or an explicit path/contact output.

## Persistence
The chosen dimer D, witnesses x,y, and their R38 interaction are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say R38 is useless. Coordinate-localized R38 interactions, forced polarity, explicit strict P4 outputs, collision ancestry, or interactions on strategically constrained supports may be highly productive. Does not assert closure of the Strong conjecture.

## Proof
Fix D={u,v} and distinct x,y outside D. By R3, exactly one of (u,v,x) and (x,v,u) is tight. In either case the terminal physical dimer {u,v}, with the tested order inherited from that tight trimer, is signed by witness x in the sense used by R41/R38. Likewise exactly one of (u,v,y) and (y,v,u) is tight, giving a second signed certificate on the same physical support D with distinct witness y. R38 therefore applies: if the two certificates have the same polarity, the same-polarity two-extension collision package occurs; if they have opposite polarities, the strict two-ended signed-path interaction occurs. Hence bare existence of an R38 interaction is automatic on every physical dimer having two outside vertices. In any system of order at least four such a choice exists.
