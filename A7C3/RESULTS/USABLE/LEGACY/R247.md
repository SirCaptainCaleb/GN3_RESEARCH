# R247 — Aligned common-substitute swap yields a bidirectional signed-dimer floor fork

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume the five-exterior equality face. Let E_a=S union {a} and E_b=S union {b} be two global-longest exteriors sharing the four-core S, let K=(...,u,b,v,...) be a tight ordering of the E_a-complement with b internal, and suppose the order-preserving swap K[b:a] obtained by replacing b with a is tight. Then the physical swap dimer {a,b} is signed in both orientations by named inherited turns with witnesses among u,v: global longestness of K forces one of (b,a,u) or (v,b,a) tight, and independently one of (a,b,u) or (v,a,b) tight. Consequently, choosing any tight trimer T inside S, one may pair either signed orientation of {a,b} with an opposite-polarity terminal signed dimer of T to obtain a direct mass-four balanced pair. Applying the accepted inherited-turn endpoint-selective paid-floor continuation gives two separate lawful alternatives: either a spanning two-cover occurs or an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor is reached with singleton (a) as one literal floor coordinate; and, in a separate lineage, either a spanning two-cover occurs or such a floor is reached with singleton (b) as one literal floor coordinate.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the five-exterior equality face; E_a and E_b share a four-core S; K is a globally-longest carrier complementary to E_a; b is internal on the chosen K order; the order-preserving swap b->a is tight; accepted R3, R8, R39, and R224.

## Scope
Carrier-order/signed-payment consequence of an orientation-aligned common-substitute swap. The two endpoint-selective paid floors are alternative lineages, not simultaneous current states.

## Applicability
Use after an aligned outcome of the common-substitute swap obstruction. It converts a purely carrier-order synchronization into a named bidirectional signed dimer and controlled floor ancestry preserving either the substitute vertex or the replaced carrier vertex.

## Persistence
The physical vertices a,b,u,v, the two inherited sign turns on {a,b}, and the chosen endpoint identity in each R224 continuation persist as historical ancestry. The direct balanced-pair realization and resulting floors belong to separate chosen descendants; simultaneous currentness is not asserted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not synchronize the two endpoint-selective floors, does not prescribe the second floor singleton, does not feed the mass-two floor directly into R171 contrary to R214, and does not by itself close the equality face.

## Proof
Write the local carrier segment as (...,u,b,v,...), with the evident endpoint truncations outside u,v. The aligned swap hypothesis says (...,u,a,v,...) is a tight path on the neighboring global-longest support. Consider first the path obtained from K by inserting a immediately before b, so its local segment is (...,u,a,b,v,...). Every turn away from this local segment is inherited from K; the outer seam on the u side is inherited from the aligned swap and the outer seam on the v side is inherited from K. Thus the only uncertified local turns are uab and abv. If both were tight, the resulting path would contain all vertices of K plus a, contradicting global longestness. Hence uab is bad or abv is bad. By R3, respectively bau or vba is tight. Either turn signs the physical orientation (b,a), with named witness u or v. Now insert a immediately after b, giving (...,u,b,a,v,...). The only uncertified turns are uba and bav, because the u-side outer seam is inherited from K and the v-side outer seam from the aligned swap. Global longestness forces one bad; R3 gives respectively abu or vab tight, signing the opposite physical orientation (a,b). Thus {a,b} is bidirectionally signed by named inherited turns. Choose any three vertices of S and orient them as a tight trimer by R8. Its two terminal dimers have opposite polarities. For either chosen sign on {a,b}, select the disjoint terminal dimer of opposite polarity; R39 gives a direct mass-four balanced pair. Since the {a,b} sign comes from a named inherited turn, R224 applies. Using the endpoint-selective channel preserving a gives either a two-cover or a mass-two floor with (a) literal; choosing the separate endpoint-selective channel preserving b gives the analogous alternative with (b) literal. The two continuations are not combined.
