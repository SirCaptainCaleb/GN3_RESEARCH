# R240 — Five-exterior common-four-core cross-replacement saturation

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the five-exterior equality face. Let S be a four-vertex set and let a,b be distinct vertices outside S such that E_a=S∪{a} and E_b=S∪{b} are both exteriors of global-longest tight paths. Then for every s∈S the five-set Q_s=(S\\{s})∪{a,b} supports a tight P5. Equivalently, in the R192 replacement row for carrier vertex b relative to exterior E_a, the unique failed hole is a; symmetrically, in the row for carrier vertex a relative to E_b, the unique failed hole is b. The complementary sets V(H)\\Q_s=(V(H)\\E_a\\{b})∪{s} are therefore non-Hamiltonian as single tight paths, since otherwise Q_s together with its complement would span H by two tight paths. More generally, if x_S=r longest-carrier exteriors contain S, then every unordered pair of their completion vertices produces four distinct cross-replacement P5 supports, for a total of 4*binom(r,2) distinct P5 supports localized on the same physical four-core.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the five-exterior equality face; accepted five-exterior four-of-five replacement saturation R192; S is a physical four-set; E_a=S∪{a} and E_b=S∪{b} are exteriors of global-longest tight paths.

## Scope
Arbitrary-order structural localization of high four-core occupancy in the five-exterior equality face. It uses actual longest-carrier exteriors, not merely local Hamiltonian five-sets.

## Applicability
Use when a physical four-core has occupancy at least two in the longest-carrier exterior family. It converts each pair of completions into four explicit P5 supports and identifies the exact failed-hole relation in both neighboring R192 rows. This is compatible with R231 occupancy counting and supplies concrete support packets for R239-style exact-frame capture or further carrier-coupling arguments.

## Persistence
The four-core S, completion vertices, longest-carrier exterior memberships, failed-hole identities, P5 supports Q_s, and complementary non-Hamiltonicity are graph-intrinsic. No simultaneous currentness of the corresponding global-longest carrier representatives is asserted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not synchronize P5 orderings, does not assert a P6, does not force the complementary carrier replacements to be tight, does not currentize captures across different roots, and does not by itself close the five-exterior equality face.

## Proof
Fix S and distinct completion vertices a,b with E_a=S∪{a} and E_b=S∪{b} both exteriors of global-longest tight paths. Since H is a counterexample, neither E_a nor E_b can itself support a tight P5: if E_b did, then by definition its complement K_b=V(H)\\E_b is a global-longest tight path, and K_b together with the P5 on E_b would be a spanning two-cover. Let K_a=V(H)\\E_a. Because b∉E_a, the vertex b lies on K_a. Apply R192 to the five-exterior frame (K_a,E_a) and the carrier vertex b. Among the five replacement supports (E_a\\{z})∪{b}, z∈E_a, at least four support tight P5s. For z=a this replacement is exactly S∪{b}=E_b, which is non-Hamiltonian by the preceding paragraph. Therefore z=a is the unique failed hole in this row, and for every s∈S the replacement (E_a\\{s})∪{b}=(S\\{s})∪{a,b} supports a tight P5. Interchanging a and b gives the symmetric failed-hole statement. For any s∈S, if the complementary set V(H)\\Q_s supported one tight path, that path together with the tight P5 on Q_s would be a spanning two-cover, impossible; hence the complement is non-Hamiltonian as one path. Finally, if A_S={x:S∪{x} is a longest-carrier exterior} has size r, the supports (S\\{s})∪{a,b} are distinct for distinct choices of s∈S and unordered {a,b}⊂A_S, yielding exactly 4*binom(r,2) distinct localized P5 supports.
