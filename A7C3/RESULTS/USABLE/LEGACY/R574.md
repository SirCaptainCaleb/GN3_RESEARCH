# R574 — Minimum two-bad R24 cell peels to a two-witness middle and closes through order ten

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume the unique exactly-two-bad transitive R24 seam cell of R555/R556, with Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=3. Then S=(q_1,q_0,b,z,q_m,q_{m-1}) is a tight P6. Its complement is exactly M={a,c} union V(Q[2,m-2]). Moreover the R536 left and right inward-propagation clauses apply to the complementary middle-gate edge {a,c}: q_3q_2a and q_3q_2c are tight whenever m>=3, and aq_{m-2}q_{m-3} and cq_{m-2}q_{m-3} are tight whenever m>=3. Consequently the exactly-two-bad cell is impossible for m<=5. For m=3 the complement M is the dimer {a,c}; for m=4 it is a three-set and hence has a tight Hamilton P3 by R3; for m=5 the two inward stars meet and (a,q_3,q_2,c) is a tight Hamilton P4 on M. In each case S together with that Hamilton path on M is a spanning two-cover of H. Thus any surviving exactly-two-bad order-three rail residue must have m>=6 (equivalently |V(H)|=m+5>=11).

## Hypotheses
Full accepted R543 hard frame plus the unique exactly-two-bad seam hypotheses of R555/R556; R536 inward propagation; R3.

## Scope
R24 restoration, minimum seam residue. This isolates exactly where the order-ten closure stops and the genuinely longer inward-propagation problem begins.

## Applicability
Use after R555/R556 when the six P-side seams have exactly two bad turns. For m<=5 it closes the residue immediately; for m>=6 retain the shell P6 and the two inward witness stars as the next coordinates.

## Persistence
Retain the shell P6, its complementary middle support M, and both inward-star pairs on a,c. In the surviving m>=6 branch these are simultaneous graph-intrinsic certificates.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close the m>=6 middle. Does not assert the middle support is Hamiltonian for longer Q. Does not use the unrelated exact-2+2 n=8 problem.

## Proof
By R556, at q_0 the M_S edge bz is fully outgoing, so q_0bz is tight; at q_m the same M_S edge bz is fully incoming, so bzq_m is tight. The standing R543 endpoint reverse stars give q_1q_0b and zq_mq_{m-1} tight. Hence S=(q_1,q_0,b,z,q_m,q_{m-1}) is a tight P6. Its support uses exactly b,z and the two Q endpoints with their immediate interior neighbors, so its complementary vertex set is M={a,c} union V(Q[2,m-2]).

At the left endpoint, the fully outgoing M_S gate is bz and its complementary M_S edge is ac. The R536 inward-propagation clause therefore gives q_3q_2a and q_3q_2c tight. At the right endpoint, the fully incoming M_S gate is again bz and the complementary edge is ac, so the exact right dual of R536 gives aq_{m-2}q_{m-3} and cq_{m-2}q_{m-3} tight.

Now close the short cases. If m=3, M={a,c}, which is a tight path of order two vacuously. If m=4, M={a,c,q_2}; by R3, for any ordering of these three vertices exactly one of it and its complete reverse is tight, so the three-set has a Hamilton tight P3. If m=5, M={a,c,q_2,q_3}. The left inward star gives q_3q_2c tight and the right inward star, since q_{m-2}=q_3 and q_{m-3}=q_2, gives aq_3q_2 tight. Thus (a,q_3,q_2,c) is a tight Hamilton P4 on M. In all three cases this Hamilton path on M is disjoint from S and together the two supports partition V(H), contradicting pc(H)>2. Therefore a surviving exactly-two-bad cell must have m>=6, equivalently |V(H)|=4+(m+1)=m+5>=11.
