## Proof
Let L=q_0,R=q_m. R556 and the endpoint stars give two tight P6s S_1=(q_1,L,b,z,R,q_{m-1}) and S_2=(q_1,L,z,b,R,q_{m-1}); their common complement Q^o_2=(q_2,...,q_{m-2}) is tight and nonempty for m>=6. Hence each is a literal two-cover of H-{a,c}, and R429 makes it exact. The two undecided fixed-turn incidence tests are forced locally. If Lab were tight, then (z,L,a,b,c) would be a P5 in X+L because zLa is tight (already forced in the aligned endpoint packet) and abc is tight; contradiction. Thus Lab is bad and baL is tight. Dually, if bcR were tight then (a,b,c,R,z) would be a P5 in X+R because abc and cRz are tight; hence bcR is bad and Rcb is tight. Now apply the elementary R434 incidence extraction directly, or simply read the signs. In S_1 the predecessor L->b together with baL makes (b,a) tail-signed by L, while the successor b->z together with zcb makes (c,b) head-signed by z. In S_2, z->b together with baz makes (b,a) tail-signed by z, while b->R together with Rcb makes (c,b) head-signed by R. Both witnesses in each packet lie outside J=(a,b,c), so R542 applies to both reverse terminal dimers. For explicit births use the common witness z. Pair-delete {a,b}; R557 already supplies the literal exact cover (c,z) sqcup Q. The marker Q is disjoint from (b,a) and z. Since q_1Lz is an endpoint-star turn, its reverse test zLq_1 is bad. The tail-signed branch of R526 therefore actualizes Q to the short head-signed support (L,z) with witness q_1. This gives Pi_A=((L,z)_head,(b,a)_tail). Pair-delete {b,c}; R557 supplies (z,a) sqcup Q. Since zRq_{m-1} is tight, the head-signed R526 marker test q_{m-1}Rz is bad, so the actualized opposite support is the tail-signed dimer (z,R) with witness q_{m-1}. This gives Pi_C=((c,b)_head,(z,R)_tail). The stated intersections are immediate.
