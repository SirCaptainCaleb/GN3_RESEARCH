# R450 — Universal tight-trimer mate-or-interaction front door

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample beyond order ten and let J=(a,x,c) be any proper graph-intrinsic tight trimer. Then, unless H has a spanning two-cover, there is a finite chosen continuation yielding either (1) an explicit R173-admissible two-hole mate clause anchored on J, or (2) an ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced opposite-sign floor having the source middle (x) literally as one singleton coordinate. In fact, in the all-internal branch there are four alternative such middle-containing paid-floor lineages, one from each R389 source-trimer pair birth. Apply R359. Endpoint return gives (1) by R358. Otherwise R389 supplies four direct mass-four balanced-pair births, each consisting of one role-bearing residual terminal dimer and the unique opposite-polarity terminal signed dimer M of J. The sign on M is certified by the named inherited source turn J and M contains x. Apply R224 to M in that birth and choose x as the surviving endpoint. Unless a two-cover occurs, R224 carries the same ancestry-bearing pair lineage all the way to a mass-two floor containing the literal singleton (x). Thus the all-internal R390/R389 historical-interaction output is not terminal: it canonically admits four alternative actual paid checkpoints preserving the source middle as a floor coordinate.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample beyond order ten; J is a proper tight trimer; accepted R358, R359, R389, and R224.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), beyond order ten. Order-free source-trimer consumer. It replaces the all-internal historical-interaction label by endpoint-selective paid floors retaining the trimer middle.

## Applicability
Use from any proper tight trimer when downstream work needs actual source-localized descent. Endpoint return gives a mate clause; persistent internality gives four alternative ancestry-bearing mass-two floors each containing the source middle x.

## Persistence

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
Let J=(a,x,c) be the given proper tight trimer in the hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample H. Apply accepted fully reconstructible R920.

In regime (I), some exact two-cover of H-{a,c} exposes x at a rail endpoint. The endpoint-return compiler included in R920 gives an explicit one-hole or two-hole R173-admissible mate certificate anchored on J. This is conclusion (1) of R450.

Assume regime (II), so every exact outer-pair return keeps x internal. R920 supplies a source currentization H=J sqcup U sqcup V with |U|,|V|>=3 and the four graph-intrinsic reverse terminal shields. Write U=(u_0,...,u_r), V=(v_0,...,v_s). The left shields (u_1,u_0,x) and (v_1,v_0,x) make the oriented terminal dimers D_UL=(u_1,u_0) and D_VL=(v_1,v_0) tail-signed by x. The right shields (x,u_r,u_{r-1}) and (x,v_s,v_{s-1}) make D_UR=(u_r,u_{r-1}) and D_VR=(v_s,v_{s-1}) head-signed by x.

The source trimer J supplies exactly the complementary terminal signs needed for these births: M_H=(x,c) is head-signed by witness a because (a,x,c) is tight, while M_T=(a,x) is tail-signed by witness c. Thus for each left source dimer D in {D_UL,D_VL}, D and M_H are physically disjoint signed supports of opposite polarity; for each right source dimer D in {D_UR,D_VR}, D and M_T are physically disjoint signed supports of opposite polarity. By the direct-pair clause of R514, these are four graph-intrinsic balanced-pair births. They are exactly the four source-terminal/J-terminal births described historically in the R389 branch, now reconstructed directly from the R920 shields.

Fix any one of these four births and denote its J-terminal mate by M. The support M is a signed dimer containing x, and its sign is certified by the named source turn J. Apply R425 to M deleting its other endpoint. For M_H=(x,c), delete c; the surviving singleton (x) remains head-signed by the original witness a. For M_T=(a,x), delete a; the surviving singleton (x) remains tail-signed by the original witness c. Hence in both orientations the named turn J itself continues to certify the sign on the literal singleton x; no witness migration is needed. The chosen source dimer D is untouched and has the opposite polarity. By R514, (x) and D therefore form a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair whose ancestry records the chosen one of the four source births and the cut M -> (x).

Now D is nontrivial (indeed a dimer). Apply R428 to this singleton/nontrivial balanced pair with the singleton p=x fixed. The chosen certificate-retaining continuation yields either a spanning two-cover or a both-singleton balanced floor while preserving (x) literally throughout. In the nonclosing case the resulting mass-two floor therefore has x as one singleton coordinate. The second singleton is not prescribed. Repeating this argument separately for D_UL,D_VL,D_UR,D_VR gives four alternative ancestry-bearing middle-containing floor lineages, with the corresponding source endpoint/shield provenance retained. They are alternative descendants; simultaneous currentness is not asserted.

This proves R450 using only the clean R920 return machine, R425 cut memory, R514 direct pair birth, and R428 fixed-singleton descent. No R358, R359, R389, R224, R24, R5, or exterior-size floor is used.
