# R26 — Canonical splice sign trichotomy

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement

In an imbalanced maximin singleton-root frame, test the two canonical splice turns that attach the two ends of the short rail to the long rail.

## Scope
- If both are bad, the source frame retains the two reverse short-boundary states. This face is forced when the short rail has order four or five.
- If exactly one is good, the short rail has order at least six. A two-unit singleton reset and the witness-order normal form preserve the good splice, the opposite reverse boundary state, and either a current mate subpath or one of the explicit cross-end trimers.
- If both are good, the short rail has order at least eight. There are two useful descendants with different resources:
  - moving only the two outer short-rail endpoints onto the long rail gives a two-unit reset that keeps both canonical splices and the prescribed singleton current;
  - moving two boundary vertices at each end gives the deeper central residue and selects both reverse source short-boundary states as well.

The order-eight threshold follows already in the source frame: the deepest path and the middle rail form a literal exact cover after deleting the prescribed singleton, so the one-vertex deletion rail floor rules out short-rail orders four through seven. It does not require re-rooting the deeper descendant.

These three faces live in separate representatives. No selected state is imported between them.
