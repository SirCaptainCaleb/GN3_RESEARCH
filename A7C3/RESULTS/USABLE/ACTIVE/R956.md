# R956 — A three-component broad-interval augment reduces an increasing path cover by one component

## Statement
Let F be a spanning cover of an edge-ordered complete graph by c>=3 vertex-disjoint increasing paths, oriented increasingly, with incoming/outgoing boundary labels L,U. Let p->v be a selected F-edge on one component C. Let t be the terminal of a second component A and s the source of a third component B, with A,B,C pairwise distinct. If L(t)<lambda(tv)<U(v) and L(p)<lambda(ps)<U(s), then deleting p->v and adding t->v and p->s produces a spanning cover by c-1 vertex-disjoint increasing paths. The conclusion remains valid when A or B is a singleton under the usual L(source)=-infinity and U(terminal)=+infinity conventions.

## Hypotheses
Pure edge-ordered complete graph; F is a literal spanning increasing path cover; the three named components are distinct.

## Scope
Order-free cooperative augmentation lemma. It is the collision-free length-three case of the broad interval relation and does not require a common threshold vector.

## Applicability
Use as the augmentation consumer for endpoint-rotation closures or active-active residuals. In a minimum-component cover, every such triple must violate at least one of the two broad seam inequalities.

## Persistence
Retain the exact component roles A|C|B, the removed edge p->v, the two new edges t->v and p->s, and both full seam inequalities.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not justify arbitrary alternating paths or matchings in the broad interval graph. Longer alternations can change both incidences of one original vertex and fail increasingness. Does not by itself prove R888 or the ordered-1-factorization theorem.

## Proof
Write the three distinct components as A=(a_0,...,a_r=t), C=(c_0,...,c_i=p,c_{i+1}=v,...,c_m), and B=(s=b_0,b_1,...,b_q), allowing A or B to be a singleton. Delete the selected edge p->v of C. Form\n\nA'=(a_0,...,t,v,c_{i+2},...,c_m)\n\nand\n\nB'=(c_0,...,p,s,b_1,...,b_q).\n\nTogether with every untouched component of F, these sequences partition every vertex exactly once, and the number of components changes from c to c-1 because the three components A,C,B are replaced by two.\n\nAll consecutive edge comparisons internal to the inherited pieces are unchanged. At the new seam t->v, the incoming edge at t has label L(t) when A is nontrivial and is absent when A is a singleton; the outgoing edge from v has label U(v) when v is nonterminal and is absent when v was terminal. Hence the hypothesis L(t)<lambda(tv)<U(v), with the infinite endpoint conventions, is exactly what is needed for A' to remain increasing. Likewise at the seam p->s, the incoming edge at p has label L(p) when p is nonsource and is absent otherwise, while the outgoing edge from s has label U(s) when B is nontrivial and is absent when B is a singleton. Thus L(p)<lambda(ps)<U(s) is exactly the complete seam condition for B'. No original vertex receives two new incoming or two new outgoing edges: p loses p->v and gains p->s, while v loses its old incoming edge and gains t->v. Therefore the two resulting components are vertex-simple directed paths. This proves the lemma.
