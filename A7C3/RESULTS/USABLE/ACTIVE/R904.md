# R904 — Hamiltonian crossed local frames of every order can forbid all two-covers preserving the eight-core middle rail

## Statement
For every n>=11 there is a Hamiltonian boundary 3-tournament H on n vertices with V(H)=X disjoint-union V(Q), X={a,b,c,z}, Q=(L,u,v,w_1,...,w_k,d,s,R), k=n-10>=1, and N=(v,w_1,...,w_k,d), satisfying: X is the transitive P4-free matching-height four-cell {ab,cz}>{ac,bz}>{bc,az}; X+L and X+R are P5-free; the universal reverse endpoint stars and the crossed middle gates hold; G=H[X union {L,u,s,R}] has pc(G)=2 and all four literal 4+4 covers from {(u,L,a,c),(u,L,c,a)} times {(b,z,R,s),(z,b,R,s)}; nevertheless no cover of H by at most two tight paths contains N as a contiguous block in its given order. An explicit Hamilton path is (c,v,a,b,z,d,w_1,...,w_k,L,u,s,R). Moreover the fixed core has the exact support criterion: for E subset {L,u,s,R}, G[X union E] is Hamiltonian iff (u in E and L not in E) or (s in E and R not in E).

## Hypotheses
Existence theorem for finite boundary 3-tournaments; exact complete-reversal antisymmetry and vertex-simple paths. These are graph-intrinsic crossed local data, NOT the full smallest-counterexample assumptions of R594/R619.

## Scope
Order-free limitation of the intact-middle consumer of the eight-core decomposition. One fixed eight-vertex computational certificate and a symbolic all-order completion.

## Applicability
Shows that the local eight-core partition, exact crossed 4+4 representatives, endpoint five-set obstructions, and reverse stars alone cannot imply an intact-N spanning two-cover. A consumer must exploit additional hypotheses or allow splitting/rearranging N or selected deeper cuts. The fixed core support criterion exposes the precise endpoint-pair obstruction.

## Persistence
Full 168-bit core orientation, exact bit ordering, standard-library exhaustive verifier, symbolic completion for all k>=1, Hamilton path P, and proof of the intact-N exclusion are embedded in the proof text.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Not a counterexample to the two-cover conjecture or to R24: H is Hamiltonian. Does not satisfy or assert smallest-counterexample minimality or exactness of all singleton-deletion covers. Does not rule out a proof using those extra constraints, insertion into the middle, or deeper cuts. The witness itself keeps W intact after removing v,d, so it does not establish an obstruction for every fixed cut depth. Not canonically accepted or promoted by its producer.

## Proof
This is a computer-assisted finite-certificate proof for one fixed eight-vertex core, followed by a symbolic construction for every order. No solver is needed to verify the certificate.

1. THE FIXED CORE.
Use labels (a,b,c,z,L,u,s,R)=(0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7). The 168-bit certificate in the verifier below lists the tightness of (x,y,t) for x<t, in the exact loop order y=0..7, x=0..7, t=x+1..7, excluding y in {x,t}. Reverse triples have complementary tightness. Thus the certificate defines a boundary tournament G unambiguously.

Direct exhaustive checking on this fixed core establishes:
(i) X={a,b,c,z} has matching heights {ab,cz}>{ac,bz}>{bc,az}, tight turns being descending comparisons.
(ii) For EVERY E subset of {L,u,s,R}, G[X union E] is Hamiltonian if and only if
       (u in E and L not in E) OR (s in E and R not in E).
(iii) For every x in G minus {L,u}, (x,L,u) is bad; for every x in G minus {s,R}, (s,R,x) is bad.
(iv) The eight gate turns (L,a,c),(L,c,a),(b,z,L),(z,b,L),(b,z,R),(z,b,R),(R,a,c),(R,c,a) are tight. The four enlarged paths (u,L,a,c,b), (u,L,c,a,z), (a,b,z,R,s), (c,z,b,R,s) are tight. All four pairs choosing A from {(u,L,a,c),(u,L,c,a)} and B from {(b,z,R,s),(z,b,R,s)} are tight 4+4 covers.

In particular pc(G)=2 by (ii) with E all four caps and (iv); X+L and X+R are nonHamiltonian by (ii). The endpoint reverse stars follow from (iii) by antisymmetry. The verifier checks (ii) by testing all permutations of each of sixteen sets of size at most eight, not by relying on a MILP UNSAT answer.

2. PARAMETRIC COMPLETION.
For any k>=1 introduce new vertices v,d,w_1,...,w_k. Put W=(w_1,...,w_k),
N=(v,W,d), Q=(L,u,v,W,d,s,R), and P=(c,v,a,b,z,d,W,L,u,s,R).
The resulting order is n=k+10 and the rail convention Q=(q_0,...,q_m) has m=k+5>=6.

Retain every core turn and prescribe the following comparisons:
  (x,v,w_1) tight iff x=u, for x in G;
  (w_k,d,x) tight iff x=s, for x in G;
  (x,u,v) tight iff x=L, for x in G minus {u};
  (d,s,x) tight iff x=R, for x in G minus {s}.
Require every consecutive triple of Q and of P to be tight. Complete every as-yet-unset reversal pair arbitrarily (the verifier uses the increasing outer-index orientation).

These prescriptions are consistent for every k. The four comparison families have different middle vertices v,d,u,s and involve a new vertex, so neither conflict with each other nor with the core. The four exterior Q seam turns are the specified true choices x=L,u,s,R. Internal W turns are shared by Q and P in the same direction; at entry Q uses v and P uses d, while at exit Q uses d and P uses L. These comparisons do not reverse one another. For k=1, the additional P turn (z,d,w_1) is the reversal of the prescribed bad turn (w_1,d,z), so is consistent. For k>=2 that P turn is unset by the four families. All other P turns either use a new comparison pair or are core turns (a,b,z),(L,u,s),(u,s,R), verified tight in the certificate. Thus the completion exists symbolically for all k. P is visibly vertex-simple and spanning, so H is Hamiltonian.

The core and all listed local geometry remain literally unchanged; Q is tight by construction. Thus the R810-type physical partition into this exact two-cover core and middle rail, and the crossed core representatives of R817, hold as graph-intrinsic data. This statement does NOT import the smallest-counterexample hypothesis of those results.

3. NO TWO-COVER CAN KEEP N INTACT.
Suppose a cover by at most two paths keeps N as one contiguous block in its given order. Write its component containing N as A N C and its other component as B; A,B,C consist only of core vertices and B may initially be allowed empty.

If A is nonempty its last vertex must be u by the first comparison family. If |A|>=2 its penultimate vertex must be L by the third family. If |A|>=3, the third-last vertex would make a tight (x,L,u), forbidden by core property (iii). Consequently A is empty, (u), or (L,u).
Dually C is empty, (s), or (s,R).

It follows that B contains all of X, and its cap set E satisfies u in E => L in E and s in E => R in E. By (ii), B's support is nonHamiltonian. This contradicts B being the remaining path. In particular B cannot be omitted, since it contains X. This proves the claimed exclusion.

4. SCOPE.
These examples are Hamiltonian, not smallest counterexamples. They refute an intact-N normalization inferred solely from the enumerated local frame/core data, even at arbitrary rail length. They do not refute a normalization using additional smallest-counterexample/deletion-cover constraints; they do not exclude inserting core blocks into N, splitting N, or choosing deeper cuts. In fact the displayed Hamilton path retains W intact after the selected cut that removes v and d from N. Therefore no conclusion about failure of EVERY fixed-depth end modification is justified.

5. SELF-CONTAINED VERIFIER.
The following uses only Python's standard library. It checks the finite certificate exhaustively, verifies the core paths/barriers, and implements the symbolic completion with consistency assertions. The sample k values are implementation checks only; the preceding argument proves the all-k assertion.

```python
"""Self-contained finite certificate and parametric construction; stdlib only."""
from itertools import permutations
CORE_BITS='111011100000000100000111111000001111000001100000000001100000000000001010011001001011101000000001000000111010000000000000000100100001000010001001011101001010011101001011'
a,b,c,z,L,u,s,R=range(8)
keys=[(x,y,t) for y in range(8) for x in range(8) for t in range(x+1,8) if y not in (x,t)]
assert len(CORE_BITS)==len(keys)==168
core=dict(zip(keys,map(int,CORE_BITS)))
def turn(table,t):
 x,y,q=t
 return bool(table[(x,y,q)]) if x<q else not table[(q,y,x)]
def path(table,p):return all(turn(table,t) for t in zip(p,p[1:],p[2:]))
def force(table,t,value=True):
 x,y,q=t; key=(min(x,q),y,max(x,q));bit=int(value if x<q else not value)
 assert key not in table or table[key]==bit,(t,value)
 table[key]=bit
height={frozenset(e):h for h,es in [(3,[(a,b),(c,z)]),(2,[(a,c),(b,z)]),(1,[(b,c),(a,z)])] for e in es}
for t in permutations(range(4),3):
 assert turn(core,t)==(height[frozenset(t[:2])]>height[frozenset(t[1:])])
for mask in range(16):
 E=[x for i,x in enumerate((L,u,s,R)) if mask>>i&1]
 has=any(path(core,p) for p in permutations([a,b,c,z]+E))
 assert has==((u in E and L not in E) or (s in E and R not in E))
for x in range(4):assert turn(core,(u,L,x)) and turn(core,(x,R,s))
for x in range(8):
 if x not in (L,u):assert not turn(core,(x,L,u))
 if x not in (s,R):assert not turn(core,(s,R,x))
for t in [(L,a,c),(L,c,a),(b,z,L),(z,b,L),(b,z,R),(z,b,R),(R,a,c),(R,c,a)]:assert turn(core,t)
for p in [(u,L,a,c,b),(u,L,c,a,z),(a,b,z,R,s),(c,z,b,R,s)]:assert path(core,p)
for A in [(u,L,a,c),(u,L,c,a)]:
 for B in [(b,z,R,s),(z,b,R,s)]:assert path(core,A) and path(core,B)

def construct(k):
 assert k>=1
 v,d=8,9;W=list(range(10,10+k));first,last=W[0],W[-1];T=core.copy()
 for x in range(8):
  force(T,(x,v,first),x==u)
  force(T,(last,d,x),x==s)
  if x!=u:force(T,(x,u,v),x==L)
  if x!=s:force(T,(d,s,x),x==R)
 Q=[L,u,v]+W+[d,s,R]
 P=[c,v,a,b,z,d]+W+[L,u,s,R]
 for p in [Q,P]:
  for t in zip(p,p[1:],p[2:]):force(T,t)
 n=10+k
 for y in range(n):
  for x in range(n):
   for q in range(x+1,n):
    if y not in (x,q):T.setdefault((x,y,q),1)
 assert path(T,Q) and path(T,P) and set(P)==set(range(n)) and len(P)==n
 return T,Q,P
for k in [1,2,3,4,20]:construct(k)
print('PASS: core support equivalence, crossed paths, stars, and parametric construction checks.')
print('The proof for every k is symbolic; these parameter checks are consistency checks, not an exhaustive proof over k.')
```
