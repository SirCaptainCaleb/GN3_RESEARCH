# R570 — Unique-transition survivors are one-sided Hamilton-boundary rigid

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2. Partition V(H)=D disjoint-union S disjoint-union C with S,C nonempty. Assume X=H[D union S] is Hamiltonian and T is a literal exact two-cover of H-D with exactly one selected S|C transition. Then the following rigidity alternative holds. First, H[D union C] is non-Hamiltonian; otherwise R567 applied to the two Hamilton absorbers D union S and D union C forces at least two S|C transitions in T. Second, suppose the unique transition is x y with x in S and y in C. If there are two Hamilton paths of X ending at x with distinct penultimate vertices, R566 gives a same-oriented two-tail collision on the actual crossing dimer (y,x). If there are two distinct Hamilton paths of X ending at x with the same penultimate vertex, R435 applied to the two Hamilton orders on the same support gives an exact reversed old state, a tight reverse trimer, or a vertex-simple tight cycle. Hence outside those explicit interaction outputs there is AT MOST ONE Hamilton path of X ending at x. Dually, if the unique transition is y x, then outside a same-oriented two-head crossing collision or an R435 reverse-order output there is AT MOST ONE Hamilton path of X starting at x. Thus every interaction-free tau=1 recompletion cell is intrinsically one-sided: the opposite D-augmented side is non-Hamiltonian and the absorber has boundary multiplicity at most one in the literal crossing role. Endpoint accessibility is not automatic: the relevant family of Hamilton paths may be empty.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); pc(H)>2; partition D,S,C; H[D union S] Hamiltonian; T exact two-cover of H-D with exactly one S|C transition. Uses R435,R566,R567.

## Scope
Order-free. Applies to arbitrary deletion size whenever the stated exact two-cover exists; in smallest-counterexample applications D may come from any exact deletion theorem. Not tied to R24, transitive four-cells, or the eight-vertex case.

## Applicability
New low-transition target reduction. To eliminate tau=1 it suffices to produce either a Hamilton absorber on the opposite side through the same D, or two endpoint-compatible Hamilton orders of the existing absorber. A separate endpoint-accessibility theorem can strengthen the zero-or-one conclusion to exact uniqueness.

## Persistence
Retains D,S,C, the exact cover T, crossing endpoint x and exterior endpoint y, and the relevant Hamilton boundary role of X.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert existence of any Hamilton path of X ending at x in the SC orientation or starting at x in the CS orientation. Does not assert that the listed collision/reversal/cycle outputs themselves close H. Does not apply to tau>=2.

## Proof
Assume first that H[D union C] is Hamiltonian. Apply the two-class specialization of R567 to W=S sqcup C, k=2, and the two one-rail absorber covers on D union S and D union C. Since pc(H)>2, pc(H)>=3, so the number of selected S|C transitions in T is at least 2pc(H)-1-1-2>=2, contradicting tau(T)=1. Hence H[D union C] is non-Hamiltonian. Now suppose the unique transition is x y with x in S. If X has two Hamilton paths Q_1,Q_2 ending at x with distinct penultimate vertices p_1,p_2, R566 gives y x p_1 and y x p_2 tight, a same-tested-orientation two-tail collision on the actual selected crossing dimer (y,x). If instead Q_1,Q_2 are distinct but have the same penultimate vertex p, they are two different Hamilton orders on the same vertex support X. Applying R435 to one as the reference path and the other as the comparison path yields an exact reversal of an old adjacent state, a tight reverse trimer, or a vertex-simple tight cycle. Therefore, if none of these explicit outputs occurs, there cannot be two distinct Hamilton paths of X ending at x; there may be one or none. The y x orientation is the exact dual, using Hamilton paths starting at x and the head-collision half of R566.
