# R2042 — Two-vertex source-exposure four-cap cage

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** Slack workspace R2042

## Statement

In the R2025 source-anchor lift arising from a literal exact two-cover `T` of `G=H-{A,C}`, suppose the exposed source `s` lies on a two-vertex T-rail `{s,z}`. Write the other rail literally as

`Q=(q_0,...,q_k)`, `k>=1`.

Then counterexample status forces all four tight caps

`(s,A,z)`, `(z,C,s)`, `(q_1,q_0,z)`, `(z,q_k,q_{k-1})`.

Moreover R2026 forces at least one of:

1. `(q_0,C,s)` tight;
2. `(s,A,q_k)` tight;
3. `k>=2` and both `(q_1,q_0,C)` and `(A,q_k,q_{k-1})` tight.

Thus every R2034 rigid exception carries a literal complete-reversal-only endpoint cage.

## Proof

Deleting `s` from the two-vertex T-rail and adjoining the retained anchor gives, by R2025,

`N=(A,s,C) | (z) | Q`.

Because `(z)` is a singleton, the four ordered cross-rail joins `z->A`, `C->z`, `z->q_0`, and `q_k->z` each join distinct rails and have exactly one new seam. If any unique seam were tight, that join would yield a spanning exact two-cover of `H`. Therefore the seams

`(z,A,s)`, `(s,C,z)`, `(z,q_0,q_1)`, `(q_{k-1},q_k,z)`

are all bad. Complete-reversal R3 yields respectively

`(s,A,z)`, `(z,C,s)`, `(q_1,q_0,z)`, `(z,q_k,q_{k-1})`.

Now apply R2026 to the edge-bearing rail `Q`. Its three alternatives are exactly `(s,C,q_0)` bad, `(q_k,A,s)` bad, or, when `k>=2`, both `(C,q_0,q_1)` and `(q_{k-1},q_k,A)` bad. Applying complete-reversal R3 gives the displayed second packet.

For the R2034 `E_B` form, deleting the source on the short mixed rail leaves its B-vertex `b` as `z`. If the long rail starts `v,t,...`, then `q_0=v,q_1=t`, so the fixed cap `(q_1,q_0,z)` is `(t,v,b)`. If it ends `...,t,v`, then `q_k=v,q_{k-1}=t`, so `(z,q_k,q_{k-1})=(b,v,t)`. In `E_X`, `z=v`, giving both v-anchor caps plus the two endpoint reverse caps on the long rail.
