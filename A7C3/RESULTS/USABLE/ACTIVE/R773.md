# R773 — A reverse terminal contact either makes a local P4 or steps inward into the nonreplay R703 lift

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let K=(k_0,k_1,...,k_r) be a vertex-simple tight path with r>=3. Let h lie outside K. HEAD. Suppose the reverse head-contact trimer (k_1,k_0,h) is tight. Test the turn tau_H=(k_1,k_2,h). If tau_H is tight, then (k_0,k_1,k_2,h) is a literal tight P4. If tau_H is bad, boundary antisymmetry forces (h,k_2,k_1) tight. This is exactly the internal right-form reverse-star hypothesis of accepted R703 on the old path K at i=1. Hence R703 yields either a tight Hamilton P4 on {h,k_1,k_2,k_3} or explicit Reverse-Ear geometry relative to K: a reverse state/reverse trimer or a vertex-simple tight cycle. TAIL. Suppose (h,k_r,k_{r-1}) is tight. Test tau_T=(h,k_{r-2},k_{r-1}). If tau_T is tight, then (h,k_{r-2},k_{r-1},k_r) is a literal tight P4. If tau_T is bad, antisymmetry forces (k_{r-1},k_{r-2},h) tight, exactly the internal left-form R703 hypothesis on K at i=r-2. Hence R703 yields either a tight Hamilton P4 on {k_{r-3},k_{r-2},k_{r-1},h} or explicit Reverse-Ear geometry relative to K. Thus every reverse terminal contact on a path of order at least four either produces an adjacent local P4 immediately or moves one step inward to an R703 event whose reverse-ear branch cannot merely replay the original endpoint contact.

## Hypotheses
Finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament; K=(k_0,...,k_r) tight with r>=3; h outside K; one displayed reverse terminal-contact trimer is tight. Uses accepted R3 and R703.

## Scope
Pure order-free local terminal-contact consumer. No smallest-counterexample minimality, deletion exactness, crossed frame, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,generic R358,R436,R312,or computation.

## Applicability
Preferred consumer after pending R770 produces a reverse terminal contact on a retained path of order at least four. Unlike the raw R435 endpoint-ear specialization in pending R771, this theorem first tests the opposite seam and, in the bad branch, shifts the witness one dimer inward before invoking R703. Use this route when replay of the original endpoint contact would not count as progress.

## Persistence
Retain K,h,the original endpoint contact,the tested inward seam,and either the explicit adjacent P4 or the shifted internal reverse-star plus the full R703 P4/Reverse-Ear certificate. Any role-labelled subpath embedded in K remains historical geometry.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not consume the resulting P4 or R703 reverse-ear/cycle output. Does not assert a P4 extends all of K or that the resulting geometry closes H. Requires at least four vertices on K so the R703 inward probe k_3 or k_{r-3} exists. Does not invalidate pending R771; it is the preferred nonreplay refinement.

## Proof
Head case. The old path K certifies (k_0,k_1,k_2). Test tau_H=(k_1,k_2,h). If tight, the two consecutive turns (k_0,k_1,k_2) and (k_1,k_2,h) certify the tight P4 (k_0,k_1,k_2,h). If tau_H is bad, accepted boundary antisymmetry R3 gives its complete reverse (h,k_2,k_1) tight. With Q=K, x=h and i=1 this is exactly the right-form hypothesis J_R=(x,q_{i+1},q_i) of accepted R703; r>=3 supplies q_{i+2}=k_3. Therefore R703 yields either a Hamilton P4 on {h,k_1,k_2,k_3} or its explicit Reverse-Ear/reversal/cycle output. Tail case. The old path certifies (k_{r-2},k_{r-1},k_r). Test tau_T=(h,k_{r-2},k_{r-1}). If tight, together they certify (h,k_{r-2},k_{r-1},k_r). If bad, R3 gives (k_{r-1},k_{r-2},h) tight. With Q=K, x=h and i=r-2 this is the left-form hypothesis J_L=(q_{i+1},q_i,x) of R703; r>=3 supplies q_{i-1}=k_{r-3}. Apply R703. In either shifted branch, the R703 reverse-ear construction is based on a new internal reverse-star and an all-K nonmonotone trimer, so it is not merely the original terminal-contact certificate. This proves the dual alternatives.
