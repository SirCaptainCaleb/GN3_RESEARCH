# R1060 — The normalized OUT-v pre-loss state isolates v

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789410744.952669`

## Statement

Immediately before any non-loop OUT-`v` literal first loss, in the F-normalized prefix `hat M_{r-1}`, both split copies `v,out` and `v,in` are unmatched. Hence the physical vertex `v` is an isolated singleton component.

Consequently, any legal off-augmenter detour edge `v->z` creates no seam at `v` and cannot by itself create a directed cycle through `v`.

## Proof

Write the OUT-`v` first-loss pivot as

`e_r=v->b_j`.

At the normalized prefix `hat M_{r-1}`, the moving unmatched OUT copy is `a_{r-1},out=v,out`, so `v,out` is free by construction.

For `v,in`, R1037 gives `d_F(v)=1`. In the OUT-`v` branch, R1048 and R1051 identify the unique selected old-F incidence at `v` as the outgoing transition `v->b_j`. Thus the F-state has no selected incidence at `v,in`.

Now consider the split symmetric difference `F△J`. The unique F-heavy alternating component `C_*` has fixed IN endpoint `s,in`, where R1051 gives `s∈{p,q,r}` and `s≠v`; therefore `v,in` is not its terminal endpoint. Nor can `v,in` be an internal IN-copy of `C_*`, because every internal alternating copy carries an incident `F\J` edge, while the F-state has no incidence at `v,in`.

Thus any noncommon J-incidence at `v,in`, if present, belongs to a delta-zero component. D17.416 neutral normalization places every such component in its F-state, deleting that J-incidence. A common `F∩J` incidence at `v,in` is impossible because F has none. Therefore `v,in` is unmatched in `hat M_{r-1}`.

Both split copies are free, so `v` is a literal singleton component. Adding a legal edge `v->z` from this singleton creates no predecessor turn at `v`; and because it attaches an isolated vertex by one outgoing edge, that edge cannot itself complete a directed cycle through `v`.
