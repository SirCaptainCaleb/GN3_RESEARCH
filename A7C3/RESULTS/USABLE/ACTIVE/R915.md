# R915 — A signed cross-end trimer rail forces the opposite reverse seam

## Statement
Let H be a finite boundary tournament with pc(H)>2. Let S=(p,q) be a tested oriented dimer, and let x,y,v be distinct vertices outside S. Let T be an exact two-cover of H-V(S) whose two rails are nontrivial, with one rail exactly (x,v,y) (the other orientation is handled by swapping x,y). If x and y are head witnesses on S, so (x,p,q) and (y,p,q) are tight, while v is a tail witness, so (p,q,v) is tight, then the opposite-end reverse seam (y,v,q) is tight. Dually, if x and y are tail witnesses on S while v is a head witness, then (p,v,x) is tight. For a cross rail (y,v,x), the corresponding conclusions are (x,v,q) in the head-endpoint/tail-middle case and (p,v,y) in the tail-endpoint/head-middle case. Thus a three-vertex exact-cover rail whose endpoints have one sign polarity on S and whose middle vertex has the opposite polarity deterministically emits a reverse seam at the opposite physical endpoint.

## Hypotheses
H is finite with pc(H)>2; S=(p,q) is a tested oriented dimer; x,y,v are distinct outside S; T is an exact two-cover of H-V(S) with both rails nontrivial and with one rail exactly (x,v,y) or (y,v,x); the displayed head/tail sign turns hold on the same tested orientation S.

## Scope
Order-free exact-cover parent lemma. No smallest-counterexample minimality, crossed frame, selected depth, R621, R649, R682, R684, R906, payment, or computation is needed once the signed dimer and exact cross-end trimer rail are supplied.

## Applicability
Use whenever an exact deletion cover contains a three-vertex rail x-v-y and the two physical endpoints have one sign polarity on the deleted tested dimer while v has the opposite polarity. In the crossed middle-gate XET setting, R649 supplies the endpoint polarity and any producer supplying the opposite inward-witness polarity may invoke this theorem directly, yielding the R908 seam without depending on that producer's identity.

## Persistence
Retain S with tested orientation, the exact sign turns for x,y,v, the exact two-cover T and cross-rail orientation, the R523 P4 used in the proof, the exact b_T=2 calculation, and the forced R833 reverse seam. The seam is graph-intrinsic; T states and block count remain source-cover-relative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the reverse seam closes H. Does not require or preserve any particular long rail outside the exact cover T. Does not transport selected states to other representatives. If the second T rail is trivial, R833 is not licensed by this theorem.

## Proof
Assume first that x,y are head witnesses on S=(p,q) and v is a tail witness, and orient the cross rail as (x,v,y). Then (x,p,q) and (p,q,v) are tight, so R523 gives the tight P4 K=(x,p,q,v). Apply R833 to K with middle dimer {p,q} and the actual exact cover T of H-{p,q}. Put C=V(H)\V(K). Deleting the P4 endpoints x and v from the rail (x,v,y) leaves exactly the singleton C-block {y}; the other nontrivial T rail is disjoint from K and remains one C-block. Hence b_T(x,v)=2. R833 X-REVERSE is impossible because x has no selected exterior neighbor: its only T-neighbor is v, which is the other P4 endpoint. REVERSE-CHORD is impossible because it would require the selected state v x, while T selects x v. ANTI-ENDS is impossible because v has T-degree two in (x,v,y). Therefore R833 Y-REVERSE must hold. Its selected exterior neighbor of v is uniquely y, so the forced seam is (y,v,q), as claimed. If the rail is (y,v,x), use the head witness y instead and obtain (x,v,q). For the dual polarity, suppose x,y are tail witnesses and v is a head witness. With rail (x,v,y), R523 gives K=(v,p,q,y). Again deleting endpoints v,y leaves exactly {x} plus the other T rail, so b_T(v,y)=2. Y-REVERSE is impossible because y is terminal, REVERSE-CHORD would require y v while T selects v y, and ANTI-ENDS is impossible because v has degree two. Thus X-REVERSE holds, with unique exterior predecessor x of v, forcing (p,v,x). The rail (y,v,x) gives (p,v,y) identically.
