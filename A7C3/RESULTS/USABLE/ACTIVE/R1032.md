# R1032 — G37 singleton-spoke first-loss zipper is source-rooted when both adjacent closures exist

**Audit state:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Proof provenance:** Slack `#a7c3-workspace` root `1789311947.865839`, audited against D17.419 and D17.421.

## Statement
Assume the sharp G35/G37 hard-cell setup in the SINGLETON-SPOKE branch of D17.419. Let

\[
e_i=a_{i-1}\to b_i,\qquad f_i=a_i\to b_i,
\]

be the zipper coordinates, let `r` be the first index with `chi(e_r)=1`, and for every admissible shortcut closure `g_q=a_q->b_m` let `T_q` be the transition count of the resulting closed path.

Then:

1. `W_0=0`.
2. For every admissible `q`,
   \[
   T_q=1+S_q+(S_q\bmod 2).
   \]
   Hence the admissible closure totals are `1` before the first source transition, `3` on the plateau after the first source transition, and `3` after the second source transition.
3. If **both adjacent closures** `g_{r-1}` and `g_r` are defined, then the first numerical loss of strict old-source descent occurs at the switch from `g_{r-1}` to `g_r`: `T_{r-1}=1<2` and `T_r=3>=2`. The associated `K_{2,2}` rectangle is source-rooted. Its old same-side diagonal consists of exactly one `X-X` edge incident with the physical source `t` and one `B-B` edge.
4. If either `g_{r-1}` or `g_r` is undefined because it is the unique singleton-defect loop allowed by D17.421, no literal `K_{2,2}` switch is asserted at index `r`. The numerical staircase remains valid on all admissible closures, but the would-be first-loss rectangle is interrupted by the loop defect.

## Adjustment to D17.433
D17.433 correctly derives the staircase and the source geometry of the switch, but its stated proviso excludes only the possibility that `g_r` is the unique loop. D17.421 requires **both** `g_{r-1}` and `g_r` to exist before the adjacent closures form the literal `K_{2,2}` matching switch. The additional obstruction is real: the unique loop may occur at `g_{r-1}` while `g_r` remains admissible.

Thus the durable corrected statement is the one above. No claim is made that the literal source-rooted first-loss rectangle exists when either adjacent closure is the loop defect.

## Proof
By D17.419, in the SINGLETON-SPOKE branch the distinguished cycle `C_*` contains exactly the two F-selected transition edges, both incident with the physical source `t`, and no J-selected transition edge. Therefore `w(C_*)=2`; the prefix sum `S_q` is the `0,1,2` staircase whose rises occur at the two source-transition indices.

In the G37 hard cell,

\[
\tau(F)-\tau(J)=3-1=2=w(C_*)+W_0.
\]

Since `w(C_*)=2`, necessarily `W_0=0`.

D17.421 gives, for every admissible shortcut closure,

\[
T_q=1+S_q+W_0+\gamma_q,
\]

where

\[
\gamma_q=((S_q\bmod 2)\oplus(w(C_*)\bmod 2)).
\]

Because `w(C_*)=2` is even and `W_0=0`, this reduces to

\[
T_q=1+S_q+(S_q\bmod 2).
\]

The three staircase levels `S_q=0,1,2` therefore give transition totals `1,3,3` respectively, whenever the corresponding closure is admissible.

Let `r` be the first source-transition index. If both `g_{r-1}` and `g_r` exist, then `S_{r-1}=0` and `S_r=1`, so `T_{r-1}=1` and `T_r=3`. D17.421 identifies the passage between these adjacent closures as the exact `K_{2,2}` matching switch on the rectangle with old diagonal

\[
\{a_{r-1}b_m,\;a_rb_r\}
\]

and new diagonal

\[
\{a_{r-1}b_r,\;a_rb_m\}.
\]

Because the first transition edge `e_r=a_{r-1}b_r` is incident with physical source `t`, there are two copy-geometric cases. If `a_{r-1}=t\in X`, then `b_r,a_r\in B` and `b_m\in X`; hence `a_{r-1}b_m` is the unique old `X-X` edge and is incident with `t`, while `a_rb_r` is `B-B`. If `b_r=t\in X`, then `a_{r-1},b_m\in B` and `a_r\in X`; hence `a_rb_r` is the unique old `X-X` edge and is incident with `t`, while `a_{r-1}b_m` is `B-B`. Thus the literal first-loss rectangle, when defined, is source-rooted exactly as claimed.

Finally, D17.421 explicitly allows one unique shortcut index at which `a_q=b_m`, so `g_q` is a loop and the closure is undefined. Its `K_{2,2}` switch statement applies only when **both** adjacent closures exist. Therefore a loop at `g_{r-1}` (as well as a loop at `g_r`) prevents the literal switch at `r`, although it does not alter the formula on the remaining admissible closures. This is precisely the missing qualification in D17.433.

## Scope
This result certifies the SINGLETON-SPOKE numerical staircase and the source-rooted geometry of the first transition switch. It does not bridge across the unique loop defect and does not assert a physical first-loss rectangle when one of its two adjacent shortcut closures is undefined.
