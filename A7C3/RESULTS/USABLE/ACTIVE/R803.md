# R803 — A long-rail deep crossing is multiple, reverse-ear geometric, or pinned to the first/last deep vertex

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let Q=(q_0,...,q_m) be a vertex-simple tight path with m>=6. Put L=q_0,u=q_1,v=q_2, M=Q[3,m-3], rho=q_{m-3}, d=q_{m-2},s=q_{m-1},R=q_m. Test A=(u,v,d) and B=(v,d,s), and choose one bounded tight deletion path D as follows: if A is bad, boundary antisymmetry gives C_L=(d,v,u) tight and take D={d,v,u}; if A is tight and B is bad, take C_R=(s,d,v) and D={s,d,v}; if A and B are tight, take C_#=(u,v,d,s) and D={u,v,d,s}. For every exact two-cover T of H-D let tau_M(T) be the number of selected T-states with one endpoint in M and the other in C=V(H)-(D union M). Then tau_M(T)>=1. Moreover, for every such T exactly one of the following holds: (MULTI) tau_M(T)>=2; (EAR) comparing the M-subpath(s) of T with the original path Q via R435 yields an explicit reversal of an old Q-state, a tight reverse trimer, or a vertex-simple tight cycle; (PINNED-ONE) tau_M(T)=1, one T rail is a pure C-path, the other consists of the entire M-block followed by one C-block or vice versa, the M-block is literally (q_3,q_4,...,q_{m-3}), and the unique selected M|C state is therefore exactly (rho,y) or (y,q_3) for some y in C. Thus a unique quiet deep crossing can occur only at the first or last vertex of the untouched deep interval.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; Q is a vertex-simple tight path of order at least seven (m>=6). Uses accepted boundary antisymmetry R3, smallest-counterexample complement exactness R4, Reverse Ear R435, and absorber-complement unique-transition normal form R560.

## Scope
Order-free long-rail refinement. No R24 frame, four-cell classification, endpoint gates, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,R350,generic R358,R508,capture,payment,R436,or computation.

## Applicability
Apply whenever a smallest counterexample contains a tight path Q of order at least seven. First choose the bounded trimer/P4 deletion by the two cross-depth tests. In any exact complement two-cover count selected states across M=Q[3,m-3]. Two or more crossings are retained directly. If there is exactly one, use R560 and R435; outside explicit reverse-ear geometry the crossing is forced to be incident with q_3 or q_{m-3}. Downstream arguments may therefore treat the tau=1 cell as a nearest-deep boundary event rather than an anonymous interior crossing.

## Persistence
Retain Q, the chosen branch and deletion path D, the exact cover T, M and C, tau_M(T), and all selected M|C states. In EAR retain the exact R435 reversal/reverse-trimer/cycle. In PINNED-ONE retain the pure C rail, mixed rail orientation, literal M-block order, outside endpoint y, and whether the unique state is (rho,y) or (y,q_3).

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert tau_M(T)>=2. Does not treat an R435 cycle as a contradiction. Does not identify the outside vertex y in PINNED-ONE or claim the pinned crossing alone closes H. Does not assert different deletion-branch covers coexist. When m=6, q_3=rho and the two pinned physical deep vertices coincide, though the crossing orientation remains distinguished.

## Proof
Write E=V(H)-V(Q), M=Q[3,m-3], and choose D by the displayed tests. In the LEFT case A=(u,v,d) is bad, so R3 gives (d,v,u) tight. In the RIGHT case A is tight and B=(v,d,s) is bad, so R3 gives (s,d,v) tight. In the FOUR case A and B are tight and concatenate to the tight P4 (u,v,d,s). In each case D is therefore the support of a proper graph-intrinsic tight path, so accepted R4 supplies an exact two-cover T of H-D. Put C=V(H)-(D union M). Both M and C are nonempty: m>=6 makes M nonempty, while C contains at least the physical outer vertices not deleted. Suppose T selected no state crossing M|C. Then each T rail lies wholly in one side of the partition. Since T has exactly two nonempty rails and both sides are nonempty, one rail is a Hamilton tight path on all of C and the other lies on M. But the original Q supplies a tight Hamilton path on D union M: in LEFT use Q[1,m-2]=(u,v,q_3,...,rho,d); in RIGHT use Q[2,m-1]=(v,q_3,...,rho,d,s); in FOUR use Q[1,m-1]=(u,v,q_3,...,rho,d,s). This old-Q path and the Hamilton C rail are disjoint and span H, contradicting pc(H)=3. Hence tau_M(T)>=1. If tau_M(T)>=2 we are in MULTI. Assume tau_M(T)=1. Apply accepted R560 to the partition D,M,C, using the displayed old-Q Hamilton path as a one-path cover of H[D union M] and T as the two-cover of H-D. R560 says exactly one T rail is mixed and consists of one M-block and one C-block in one of the two orders, every vertex of M lies in that unique M-block, and the other T rail is wholly in C. Let K_M be the tight M-block. Compare K_M with the original tight path P_M=(q_3,q_4,...,rho) using accepted Reverse Ear R435. If K_M encounters two P_M vertices in decreasing P_M order, R435 gives an old-state reversal, tight reverse trimer, or vertex-simple tight cycle, which is EAR. Otherwise all P_M contacts on K_M occur in increasing order. Since V(K_M)=V(P_M)=M and every M vertex occurs exactly once, K_M must literally be (q_3,q_4,...,rho). If the mixed rail has order K_M followed by a C-block, its unique M|C state is (rho,y), where y is the first C vertex. If the mixed rail has a C-block followed by K_M, its unique crossing is (y,q_3), where y is the last C vertex. This is PINNED-ONE and exhausts the possibilities.
