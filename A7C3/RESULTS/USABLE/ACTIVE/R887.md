# R887 — Boundary tournaments are line-graph comparison orientations; edge-orderability is exactly acyclicity

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament on vertex set V and use boundary antisymmetry. Define the comparison orientation Gamma(H) on the vertex set E(K_V) as follows: whenever e={u,v} and f={v,w} are distinct incident edges of K_V, orient the line-graph edge e->f exactly when the ordered turn (u,v,w) is tight. Then: (1) Gamma(H) is a well-defined orientation of the full line graph L(K_V). (2) A vertex-simple sequence P=(v_0,...,v_k) is a tight path in H iff its consecutive ordinary edges e_i={v_{i-1},v_i} satisfy e_1->e_2->...->e_k in Gamma(H). Thus tight paths are precisely the directed line-graph edge chains whose underlying K_V walk is vertex-simple. (3) H is representable by a global total order < on E(K_V), with (u,v,w) tight iff {u,v}<{v,w}, iff Gamma(H) is acyclic. In the acyclic case any topological ordering of Gamma(H) is such an edge order. (4) If Gamma(H) is cyclic, let C be a shortest directed cycle. C is chordless in L(K_V). If |C|=3, its three ordinary edges are either a star {va,vb,vc}, giving a directed 3-cycle a->_{T_v}b->_{T_v}c->_{T_v}a in the local middle-v tournament, or the three edges of an ordinary triangle, giving all three cyclic turns tight. If |C|=r>=4, the edges of C are exactly the edges of a simple ordinary r-cycle (v_0v_1,v_1v_2,...,v_{r-1}v_0), and the directed cycle orientation says every cyclic consecutive turn (v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}) is tight (indices mod r). Hence every failure of edge-orderability has a minimal holonomy certificate of star-triangle type or vertex-simple cyclic-turn type.

## Hypotheses
H is finite and satisfies accepted exact complete-reversal antisymmetry R3. No smallest-counterexample, path-cover, R24, deletion, gate, capture, payment, or order assumption is used.

## Scope
Order-free representation and integrability theorem. It identifies the ternary tight-turn relation with an orientation of L(K_V), characterizes the edge-ordered subclass by acyclicity, and classifies shortest comparison cycles.

## Applicability
Use as a new front door for global arguments. The acyclic branch is exactly ordinary edge-ordered complete-graph mathematics. In the cyclic branch, choose a shortest comparison cycle and retain either a local star directed triangle or a vertex-simple cyclic tight-turn certificate. This gives a decomposition orthogonal to aligned/crossed gate analysis.

## Persistence
Retain Gamma(H), the ordinary-edge vertex corresponding to each line-graph vertex, the exact arc/turn dictionary, and for a shortest directed cycle its ordered edge list and induced star/ordinary-cycle support.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove the acyclic/edge-ordered class has path-cover number at most two. Does not claim a local star directed triangle or a tight cycle by itself closes a smallest counterexample. A directed path in Gamma(H) may correspond to an ordinary trail with repeated original vertices; tight-path equivalence requires the stated vertex-simplicity condition.

## Proof
For each unordered ordinary edge e={u,v} of K_V create one vertex of Gamma. Two such vertices are adjacent exactly when the ordinary edges meet, so the underlying graph is L(K_V). If e={u,v} and f={v,w}, the two possible directions e->f and f->e correspond respectively to (u,v,w) and (w,v,u). Accepted R3 says exactly one of this complete-reversal pair is tight, so exactly one line-graph direction is present. This proves (1).

For P=(v_0,...,v_k), put e_i={v_{i-1},v_i}. The line-graph arc e_i->e_{i+1} is, by definition, equivalent to tightness of (v_{i-1},v_i,v_{i+1}). Therefore every consecutive triple of P is tight iff every consecutive comparison arc is directed forward. Adding the stipulated vertex-simplicity of P gives (2).

If a total edge order < realizes H, every arc e->f of Gamma points from the earlier edge to the later edge, so Gamma has no directed cycle. Conversely, suppose Gamma is acyclic. Take any topological total ordering < of its vertices E(K_V). For every distinct u,v,w, the incident edges e={u,v}, f={v,w} are adjacent in Gamma, and e->f iff (u,v,w) is tight. A topological order places e<f exactly in that case. Hence < reproduces every tight turn of H. This proves (3).

Now suppose Gamma has a directed cycle and choose one C=e_0->e_1->...->e_{r-1}->e_0 of minimum length. If two nonconsecutive vertices e_i,e_j of C were adjacent in L(K_V), their line-graph edge has one orientation. If e_i->e_j, that chord together with the directed segment from e_j forward to e_i gives a shorter directed cycle; if e_j->e_i, the complementary directed segment from e_i forward to e_j plus the chord does. Thus C is chordless.

For r=3, three pairwise incident 2-subsets are either the three edges of one ordinary triangle or three edges sharing one common ordinary vertex v. In the star case, writing them va,vb,vc in cyclic line-graph order gives (a,v,b),(b,v,c),(c,v,a) tight. In the ordinary-triangle case, cyclically label the original vertices a,b,c so the comparison edges are ab->bc->ca->ab; then (a,b,c),(b,c,a),(c,a,b) are tight.

For r>=4, chordlessness means nonconsecutive ordinary edges e_i,e_j are disjoint, while consecutive ones meet. Put v_i=e_{i-1} cap e_i (indices mod r). Nonconsecutive-disjointness forces the v_i to be distinct and e_i={v_i,v_{i+1}}. Hence the e_i are exactly the edges of the simple ordinary cycle v_0v_1...v_{r-1}v_0. Finally e_i->e_{i+1} is equivalent to (v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}) tight for every i. This proves (4).
