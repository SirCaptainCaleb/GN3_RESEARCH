# R363 — Girth-five Moore edge bound

## Statement
Let G be a finite simple graph on m>=2 vertices with no triangles and no 4-cycles. Then |E(G)| <= m*sqrt(m-1)/2. Equality can occur only when m-1 is a perfect square d^2, G is d-regular, and for every vertex v the radius-two Moore count is exact: {v}, N(v), and the sets N(u)\\{v} for u in N(v) partition V(G). Equivalently every two nonadjacent vertices have exactly one common neighbor and G is a diameter-two girth-five Moore graph at the support level.

## Hypotheses
G is a finite simple graph with girth at least five.

## Scope
Pure finite graph theorem, independent of Strong Level-(1).

## Applicability
Use whenever a local obstruction graph is known triangle-free and C4-free. It converts local forbidden 3/4-cycles into an O(m^{3/2}) edge ceiling and gives rigid equality geometry.

## Persistence
Graph-intrinsic degree/neighborhood data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not classify all Moore graphs or assert equality exists for every square m-1.

## Proof
For each vertex v, triangle-freeness makes N(v) independent, and C4-freeness makes the sets N(u)\\{v}, u in N(v), pairwise disjoint. They are also disjoint from N(v). Hence 1+sum_{u in N(v)} d(u) <= m for every v. Summing over v gives sum_u d(u)^2 <= m(m-1). Cauchy gives (2e)^2=(sum d)^2 <= m sum d^2 <= m^2(m-1), so e<=m sqrt(m-1)/2. Equality forces equality in every local count and in Cauchy, hence regularity d=sqrt(m-1) and exact radius-two partitions around every vertex.
