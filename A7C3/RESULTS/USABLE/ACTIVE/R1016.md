# R1016 — Universal small-deletion exactness through three vertices

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. For every vertex set D with 1<=|D|<=3, the induced subsystem H-D has path-cover number exactly two.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; D is a vertex set of order one, two, or three.

## Scope
R24-free clean core of historical R5. The theorem stops exactly before the four-vertex deletion case, which is the quarantined R24-sensitive residue.

## Applicability
Use whenever a proof needs an exact two-cover after deleting at most three named vertices. This replaces historical invocations of R5 whose actual deletion set has order at most three.

## Persistence
Pure induced-subsystem exactness. Any chosen exact two-cover is representative-relative, but the equality pc(H-D)=2 is graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert exactness after deleting four vertices. In the four-deletion case the deleted four-set need not be Hamiltonian, and the missing argument is precisely the R24-sensitive content of historical R5.

## Proof
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let D be a vertex set with 1<=|D|<=3. By smallest-counterexample minimality R4, the proper induced subsystem H-D has path-cover number at most two. We show it is not Hamiltonian.\n\nIf |D|=1, D itself is a singleton tight path. If H-D had a Hamilton tight path, that path together with the singleton D would be a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction.\n\nIf |D|=2, the two vertices of D form a tight dimer. Again a Hamilton path of H-D together with this dimer would two-cover H, contradiction.\n\nIf |D|=3, write D={a,b,c}. By boundary antisymmetry R3, for any ordering (a,b,c), exactly one of (a,b,c) and its complete reversal (c,b,a) is tight. Thus D supports a Hamilton tight trimer. A Hamilton path of H-D together with that trimer would again be a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction.\n\nTherefore H-D is non-Hamiltonian in every case. Since R4 already gives pc(H-D)<=2 and H-D is nonempty in the smallest-counterexample setting, we conclude pc(H-D)=2.\n\nNo statement is made for |D|=4. The proof stops exactly where the deleted set ceases to be automatically Hamiltonian under R3; that missing four-vertex case is the quarantined R24-sensitive portion of historical R5.
