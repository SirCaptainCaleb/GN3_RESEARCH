# R566 — Unique-transition crossing inherits the absorber Hamilton boundary fan

## Statement
Let H be a finite exact-reversal tight-turn system with pc(H)>k. Partition V(H)=D disjoint-union S disjoint-union C with S,C nonempty, and suppose X=H[D union S] is Hamiltonian. Let T be a literal k-path cover of H-D having exactly one selected S|C transition. By R560, T has one mixed rail and k-1 pure C-rails. (SC orientation.) Suppose the unique transition is the selected state x y with x in S and y in C. For every vertex p in (D union S)-{x} for which X has a Hamilton tight path ending with the ordered state (p,x), the turn (y,x,p) is tight. Hence the tested crossing dimer (y,x) is tail-signed by every such Hamilton predecessor p. In particular, two distinct Hamilton predecessors p_1,p_2 force a same-oriented two-tail witness collision on the actual selected crossing dimer (y,x). (CS orientation.) Dually, if the unique transition is y x with y in C and x in S, then for every p for which X has a Hamilton tight path starting with (x,p), the turn (p,x,y) is tight; thus the tested crossing dimer (x,y) is head-signed by every such Hamilton successor p, and two distinct successors force a same-oriented two-head witness collision. Therefore a unique recompletion transition converts Hamilton boundary multiplicity of the absorber into a role-sensitive witness fan on the literal selected crossing.

## Hypotheses
Finite exact-reversal tight-turn system; pc(H)>k; V(H)=D sqcup S sqcup C with S,C nonempty; H[D union S] Hamiltonian; T is a k-cover of H-D with exactly one S|C transition. Accepted/pending R560 unique-transition normal form and R3 boundary antisymmetry.

## Scope
Order-free and k-rail general. Specializes to the A7C3 k=2 smallest-counterexample recompletion setting but is not tied to any small order, transitive four-cell, or R24 rail size.

## Applicability
Use when a deletion absorber D union S has multiple Hamilton boundary states at the S-endpoint of a transition-minimal cover. The theorem converts that multiplicity into an R523 same-support witness fan on the actual crossing dimer, preserving the deletion cover and transition coordinates.

## Persistence
Retains D,S,C, the literal cover T, unique crossing orientation and endpoints x,y, and each Hamilton predecessor/successor p as the exact witness of the resulting signed crossing certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the crossing collision alone closes H. Does not apply when T has more than one S|C transition without additional block bookkeeping. Does not require the different Hamilton absorber paths to coexist as current covers; only their graph-intrinsic boundary states are used.

## Proof
Assume first that the unique selected transition is x y with x in S and y in C. By R560, after relabelling T consists of one mixed rail A B, where A is the unique S-block ending at x and B is a nonempty C-block beginning at y, together with k-1 rails lying wholly in C. Let Q be any Hamilton tight path of H[D union S] ending with ordered state (p,x). Replace A in the mixed rail by Q, keep B in its literal T-order, and keep every other T-rail unchanged. The resulting k vertex-disjoint sequences cover all of H. Every turn is certified by Q, by T inside B and the other rails, and, if B has at least two vertices, by the original T turn beginning with the selected state xy, except possibly the single splice turn (p,x,y). If (p,x,y) were tight, these sequences would form a literal k-cover of H, contrary to pc(H)>k. Hence (p,x,y) is bad. Exact reversal antisymmetry R3 gives (y,x,p) tight. Since p was arbitrary among Hamilton predecessors of x in H[D union S], every such p is a tail witness on the same tested crossing dimer (y,x); two distinct predecessors therefore give the R523 two-tail collision packet. For the dual orientation y x, R560 writes the mixed rail as B A with B ending at y and A beginning at x. Replace A by a Hamilton path Q starting (x,p). The only possible missing splice is (y,x,p), which must be bad, so R3 gives (p,x,y) tight. Thus every Hamilton successor p is a head witness on the same tested crossing dimer (x,y).
