# R366 — Fixed-trimer P6-or-square-root bad-graph dichotomy

## Statement
Let P be a tight trimer in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let X be any set of m>=3 vertices disjoint from P. Form the bad-extension graph G_P on X, joining x,y exactly when V(P) union {x,y} does not support a tight P5. Then at least one of the following conclusions is guaranteed: (i) V(P) union X contains a tight P6; or (ii) G_P has girth at least five and |E(G_P)| <= m*sqrt(m-1)/2, so at least [1-1/sqrt(m-1)]*binom(m,2) pairs of X extend P to a tight P5. More sharply, if |E(G_P)| exceeds the Moore bound then conclusion (i) must occur. Equality in the edge bound forces the Moore-type radius-two geometry of R363.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); P is a tight trimer; X is disjoint from P.

## Scope
Order-free local growth-or-density theorem.

## Applicability
Use as a local dichotomy: either a P6 is already present on the trimer plus outside set, or the bad P5-extension graph is square-root sparse.

## Persistence
The bad-extension graph and path-support conclusions are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say the two branches are mutually exclusive: a P6 may coexist with a sparse bad graph. Does not claim high P5 density alone forces P6.

## Proof
By R328 G_P is triangle-free. If it contains a 4-cycle on x1,x2,x3,x4, then neither diagonal can be present, so the restriction is exactly K_{2,2}; R348 forces a tight P6 on P together with those four vertices. If no 4-cycle exists, G_P has girth at least five and R363 gives e<=m sqrt(m-1)/2, hence bad-pair density at most 1/sqrt(m-1).
