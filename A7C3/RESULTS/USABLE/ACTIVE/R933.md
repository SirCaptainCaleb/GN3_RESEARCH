# R933 — Two-ended Hamilton absorber gives a two-hole spanning splice

## Statement
Let H be a finite exact-reversal tight-turn system with pc(H)>1. Let X and Y partition V(H), and let Y have a literal two-path cover U sqcup V with both rails nonempty. Choose an endpoint x of U and an endpoint y of V. In one displayed orientation write U=(x,u_1,...,u_r), V=(v_0,...,v_{s-1},y), and suppose X union {x,y} has a Hamilton tight path Q=(y,q_1,...,q_t,x); the other three endpoint-role choices are exact duals.

Then concatenating V-y, Q, and U-x gives a vertex-simple spanning word K whose complete uncertified-turn set consists of at most the two attachment turns alpha=(v_{s-1},y,q_1) when the left residual seam exists and beta=(q_t,x,u_1) when the right residual seam exists. Every other consecutive turn of K is certified. Hence if pc(H)>1 at least one existing attachment turn is bad, and exact reversal gives reverse(alpha) OR reverse(beta), omitting nonexistent holes.

Stronger pc(H)>2 seam clause: if pc(H)>2, then BOTH residual seams must exist and BOTH alpha and beta are bad. Indeed if at most one bad attachment turn existed, cutting the vertex-simple spanning word K at one edge of that bad triple would delete the only bad consecutive turn and leave two contiguous tight paths spanning H. Therefore a one-hole configuration, or a two-hole configuration with exactly one bad hole, is impossible when pc(H)>2. By exact reversal, both reverse(alpha) and reverse(beta) are then tight simultaneously.

## Hypotheses
Finite exact-reversal tight-turn system; X,Y partition V(H); U,V are vertex-disjoint nonempty tight paths covering Y; x,y are endpoints on different rails in the required opposite roles; X+x+y has a Hamilton tight path exposing y,x as displayed. The simultaneous-two-hole conclusion additionally assumes pc(H)>2.

## Scope
Order-free and size-free absorber splice theorem. The new clause strengthens the existing complete two-hole ledger specifically in the pc>2 regime and handles all residual-seam degeneracies.

## Applicability
Use R572 as before for a mate disjunction under pc>1. In a hypothetical smallest counterexample or any pc>2 system, use the stronger clause: both attachment holes must physically exist and both are bad, so both exact reversed turns are simultaneously certified. This is a seam/window fact, not a standalone absorber hierarchy.

## Persistence
Retain X,Y, literal rails U,V, chosen endpoint roles x,y, exact Hamilton absorber order Q, the vertex-simple spanning word K, both possible attachment turns, and whether each seam exists. In the pc>2 clause retain both reversed bad turns simultaneously.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert existence of the endpoint-role Hamilton absorber. Does not apply when x,y lie on the same residue rail. The pc>2 clause does not by itself close H; it supplies simultaneous exact reversed seam turns only after the complete bad-turn ledger is verified.

## Proof
In the displayed endpoint orientation form
  K=(v_0,...,v_{s-1},y,q_1,...,q_t,x,u_1,...,u_r),
omitting an empty residual piece when U-x or V-y is empty. Because X,Y partition V(H), Q spans X union {x,y}, and Q meets U sqcup V only at x,y, K is vertex-simple and spans H.

Every consecutive triple wholly inside the surviving V segment is tight, every triple wholly inside Q is tight, and every triple wholly inside the surviving U segment is tight. At the left attachment the only possibly uncertified consecutive turn is alpha=(v_{s-1},y,q_1), when all three displayed vertices exist. At the right attachment the only possibly uncertified turn is beta=(q_t,x,u_1), when all three exist. Thus the complete uncertified-turn set of K is a subset of {alpha,beta}, with absent seams omitted.

If every existing attachment turn were tight, K itself would be a Hamilton tight path, contradicting pc(H)>1. Hence at least one existing hole is bad. Exact reversal antisymmetry gives the usual mate disjunction reverse(alpha) OR reverse(beta).

Now assume pc(H)>2. We prove that both seams exist and both holes are bad. Suppose instead that the complete bad-turn list of K has size at most one. It cannot have size zero, by the preceding paragraph, so let gamma be its unique bad consecutive turn. Since gamma is a consecutive triple of the vertex-simple spanning word K, choose either of the two word edges lying inside gamma and cut K at that edge. The two resulting contiguous subwords are vertex-disjoint, cover V(H), and may be singletons or dimers. Any consecutive triple lying wholly inside either subword is also a consecutive triple of K distinct from gamma, hence tight: the cut destroys gamma and creates no new turns. Therefore the two subwords are tight paths spanning H, contradicting pc(H)>2.

Thus K has at least two bad turns. Its complete uncertified-turn set has at most the two holes alpha,beta, while every other turn is certified tight. Hence both alpha and beta must exist and both must be bad. Exact reversal gives both reverse(alpha) and reverse(beta) tight simultaneously. In particular either residual seam being absent would leave at most one possible bad turn and is impossible under pc(H)>2.

The other three endpoint-role choices are obtained by forming the corresponding role-correct concatenation and repeating the same complete-window argument; no tight path is silently reversed. This proves the strengthened theorem.
