# R547 — Hinged opposite-sign dimers collapse to opposite-sign singleton supports, with floor status only inside a payment lineage

## Statement
Let P and T be two graph-intrinsic signed tight dimers of opposite polarity in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Suppose their physical supports meet in exactly one hinge vertex b. Write u for the other vertex of P and v for the other vertex of T. Then deleting the hinge b from the two historical support orders and applying Signed-Interval Rebirth leaves a head-signed singleton on u from the head-signed dimer and a tail-signed singleton on v from the tail-signed dimer, regardless of which endpoint b occupied in either dimer order. Hence (u) and (v) are physically disjoint opposite-sign singleton supports and therefore form a graph-intrinsic balanced pair of total support mass two. In a hypothetical smallest-counterexample signed-payment lineage this is an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor, with both ancestor dimers and the hinge-cut witnesses retained.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); two graph-intrinsic signed tight dimers of opposite polarity; their supports intersect in exactly one vertex b.

## Scope
Pure signed-path contact lemma. No smallest-counterexample assumption is needed for the singleton descendants themselves. The floor terminology is the usual smallest-counterexample lineage specialization.

## Applicability
Use whenever opposite-polarity dimer supports overlap in one physical vertex. It turns an overlapping pair, which is not itself balanced because it is not disjoint, into a disjoint mass-two balanced pair without any marker actualization, cover exchange, or payment descent.

## Persistence
Retain both original signed dimer orders/witnesses, the common hinge b, the two nonshared endpoints u,v, and the R425 cut ancestry. If b was the signed endpoint of a dimer, the singleton witness migrates to b exactly as prescribed by R425; otherwise the old witness persists.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the two original dimers were disjoint. Does not close the resulting both-singleton floor. Does not preserve old witness names when the hinge was the signed endpoint.

## Proof
Let the head-signed dimer be P=(p_0,p_1) and the tail-signed dimer be T=(t_0,t_1), with V(P)∩V(T)={b}. Let u=V(P)\{b} and v=V(T)\{b}. Apply R425 to P with deletion set {b}. The unique surviving interval is the singleton (u). If u=p_0, it remains head-signed by P's old head witness. If u=p_1, then p_0=b was deleted immediately before it, so R425 makes (u) head-signed by witness b. Thus in either order (u) is graph-intrinsically head-signed. Dually apply R425 to T. If v=t_1 its old tail witness remains; if v=t_0 then t_1=b was deleted immediately after it, so (v) is tail-signed by witness b. Hence (v) is graph-intrinsically tail-signed. Since the two original supports met only at b, u and v are distinct, so these singleton supports are physically disjoint and have opposite polarity. By the project balanced-pair convention they form a mass-two balanced pair. The original dimer orders and the two hinge-cut events are retained as ancestry; no current representative is claimed.
