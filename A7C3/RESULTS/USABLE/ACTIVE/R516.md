# R516 — Same-polarity terminal-sign no-P4 cell has exactly four alignment signatures

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,y} be four distinct vertices in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with (a,b,c) tight. Suppose the left terminal physical dimer {a,b} carries a y-sign of the same tail polarity as its natural C-sign, with arbitrary tested order, and {b,c} carries a y-sign of the same head polarity as its natural C-sign, again with arbitrary tested order. Equivalently, at least one of (a,b,y),(b,a,y) is tight and at least one of (y,b,c),(y,c,b) is tight. If X has no tight Hamilton P4, then the reverse contacts (b,a,y) and (y,c,b) are both tight. Define A=[(a,b,y) tight] and B=[(y,b,c) tight]. Then for each (A,B) there is exactly one no-P4 orientation of the twelve boundary-antisymmetry reversal pairs on X. Writing xyz for the tight ordered triple (x,y,z), complete representative sets are: 00={abc,bca,cab,yba,ayb,bay,acy,cya,yac,ycb,byc,cby}; 01={abc,acb,cab,yba,bya,bay,acy,cya,cay,ycb,byc,ybc}; 10={abc,bca,bac,aby,ayb,bay,yca,cya,yac,ycb,cyb,cby}; 11={abc,acb,bac,aby,bya,bay,yca,cya,cay,ycb,cyb,ybc}. Each displayed set contains exactly one orientation from every reversal pair and contains no tight Hamilton P4. No transitivity of the three local comparisons at a fixed middle vertex is asserted; in particular the 00 local tournaments are cyclic.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; a,b,c,y are distinct; (a,b,c) is tight; the two y-signs have the same polarities as the natural terminal signs but arbitrary tested dimer orders; X has no tight Hamilton P4.

## Scope
Correct reconstructible four-vertex orientation classification. Repairs the stale strict-order table in R280 while preserving its sound reverse-contact and four-alignment-cell content.

## Applicability
Use whenever a tight trimer and a fourth vertex enter the same-polarity/no-P4 terminal-sign branch. The exact 12-turn row is determined by the two alignment bits A,B.

## Persistence
All twelve tight-turn representatives, the two alignment bits, and the reverse contacts are graph-intrinsic on X.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that every local three-way comparison at a fixed middle is transitive. Does not classify the perfect-matching-block quotient; use the corrected C262 revision for that. Does not rule out any of the four signatures.

## Proof
First force the contacts. If (b,a,y) is already the certified left same-polarity sign there is nothing to prove. Otherwise (a,b,y) is tight. If (b,a,y) were bad, R3 would make its reversal (y,a,b) tight, and together with (a,b,c) the order (y,a,b,c) would be a tight Hamilton P4, contradiction. Hence (b,a,y) is tight. Dually, if (y,c,b) is not already the certified right sign then (y,b,c) is tight; if (y,c,b) were bad, R3 would make (b,c,y) tight and (a,b,c,y) would be a tight P4. Thus both reverse contacts are forced. Now consider the twelve independent reversal pairs determined by choosing a middle vertex and an unordered pair of endpoints on X. For fixed bits A,B, the turns (a,b,c),(b,a,y),(y,c,b), the A-choice between (a,b,y) and (y,b,a), and the B-choice between (y,b,c) and (c,b,y) fix five of the twelve pairs. There remain seven binary choices. Check the 2^7=128 assignments against the exact no-P4 predicate: for every one of the 24 vertex orders (x0,x1,x2,x3), it is forbidden that both (x0,x1,x2) and (x1,x2,x3) are tight. Exactly one assignment survives for each A,B. Reading one representative from each reversal pair gives the four displayed 12-turn rows. Conversely, direct inspection of each displayed row verifies boundary antisymmetry pair-by-pair and verifies that none of the 24 Hamilton orders has both required consecutive turns. Hence the four rows are exhaustive and exact. In the 00 row, for example, at middle a the tight comparisons (c,a,b),(b,a,y),(y,a,c) form the cycle c>b>y>c, so no strict local order is claimed.
