# R519 — Universal common-anchor spoke-fan polarity theorem

## Statement
Let e be a vertex of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let X be a set of t>=3 distinct vertices disjoint from e. For every distinct x,y in X, boundary antisymmetry determines a unique tight e-middle trimer on {x,e,y}. Reading its terminal support incident with x gives a signed certificate on the physical spoke {e,x} with witness y, retaining the tested dimer order of that occurrence. Consequently every spoke {e,x} carries t-1 witness-indexed signed certificates. If t>=4, then among the t-1>=3 certificates on one physical spoke, at least two use the same one of the two possible tested dimer orders. Those two certificates are therefore legitimately comparable by R38 and give a graph-intrinsic same-support two-witness interaction on that tested oriented dimer. For t=3 no such interaction is forced: the two certificates may use opposite tested dimer orders, and the exact R516 doubly-misaligned 00 cell realizes this obstruction. Thus the tested-order-safe universal conclusion is complete witness coverage for t>=3 and an R38 interaction on every spoke for t>=4; no quantitative strict-versus-collision count follows without additional alignment.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; e is a vertex; X is a set of at least three distinct vertices disjoint from e. For the every-spoke R38 conclusion require |X|>=4.

## Scope
Reconstructible tested-order-safe common-anchor fan theorem. It corrects both the false R252 strict-count theorem and the overstrong t=3 R38 conclusion inherited by R308.

## Applicability
Use for common-anchor witness families. At any size t>=3 retain all witness-indexed certificates with their tested orders. When t>=4, pigeonhole tested order and invoke R38 on every spoke. At t=3 do not infer an R38 interaction without an explicit tested-order match.

## Persistence
The anchor, outer set, centered tight trimers, physical spoke supports, witness identities, and tested dimer orders are graph-intrinsic. For t>=4 the chosen same-tested-order witness pair and resulting R38 interaction are retained. No simultaneous currentization or payment descendant is asserted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not force an R38 interaction on every spoke when t=3. Does not bound the number of opposite-polarity strict spokes. Does not infer a Hamilton P4 from fan size alone or transport independent witness ancestry.

## Proof
Fix x in X. For every y in X\\{x}, apply R3 to x,e,y with middle e. Exactly one of (x,e,y) and (y,e,x) is tight. Reading the terminal dimer incident with x gives a signed certificate on the physical support {e,x} witnessed by y; importantly, retain whether its tested oriented dimer is (x,e) or (e,x). Hence the spoke has t-1 distinct witness certificates. There are only two possible tested dimer orders. If t>=4, then t-1>=3, so two distinct witnesses y,z produce certificates with the same tested order. Those two are legitimate inputs to R38 on one oriented dimer; R38 gives the corresponding same-support two-witness interaction. Repeat independently for every x. For t=3 there are only two witness certificates, and no pigeonhole conclusion is available. This failure is real: in the exact R516 00 no-P4 signature, choose the common anchor b and outer set {a,c,y}. On each b-spoke the two outer-witness certificates occur in opposite tested dimer orders. If a same-tested-order R38 interaction were forced anyway, its opposite-polarity branch would contradict the exact no-P4 cell. Thus t=3 guarantees complete witness coverage but not a universal R38 interaction, while t>=4 guarantees one on every spoke. No parity comparison of polarity labels across differing tested orders is made.
