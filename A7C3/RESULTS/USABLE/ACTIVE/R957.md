# R957 — Seven-vertex port rigidity eliminates the order-eleven uniform middle layer

## Statement
LOCAL PORT-RIGIDITY THEOREM. Let G be a boundary tournament on seven vertices such that every five-vertex subset supports a Hamilton tight P5 and G contains no tight P6. Fix any four-set R. Across ALL Hamilton endpoint completions of R, the physical port is unique: there do not exist Hamilton paths on R+x and R+y, with x,y outside R (possibly x=y), exposing x and y as endpoints and having distinct opposite endpoints p,q in R.

Equivalently, for every four-core R the complete endpoint-core port set Pi(R) has size at most one.

ORDER-ELEVEN COROLLARY. Hence the k=5, |V(H)|=11 uniform middle-layer residue is impossible. Indeed every seven-vertex induced subsystem of such an H satisfies the local hypotheses, so every four-core of H has a unique port. But accepted R955 says that in the order-eleven uniform residue a minimum-cardinality Hall obstruction necessarily contains a PORT-SWITCH, i.e. some four-core with at least two ports. Contradiction.

## Hypotheses
Local theorem: seven-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, every five-set Hamiltonian, no tight P6. Global corollary: k=5 order-eleven uniform residue from R927, together with accepted R955.

## Scope
Exact finite theorem plus the complete elimination of the k=5/order-eleven uniform middle-layer arm. This does not address uniform residues with k>5.

## Applicability
Use to delete the entire order-eleven uniform middle-layer branch. Any hypothetical k=5 uniform residue would give seven-vertex port uniqueness, contradicting the PORT-SWITCH forced by R955. For general k, retain the existing Hall machinery; no large-k port-rigidity statement is claimed.

## Persistence
For the local theorem retain R, the two completion labels x,y, distinct ports p,q, the distinction x=y versus x!=y, one actual first Hamilton order, the second required endpoint-pair existence, the every-five-set Hamilton witnesses, and all no-P6 clauses. For the global corollary retain the R955 minimum Hall family and its forced port-switch core.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove the full Uniform Middle-Layer Growth theorem for k>5. Does not say a six-vertex uniform packet has unique ports; an exact six-vertex cross-support PORT-SWITCH fence exists. Does not use or imply path-order synchronization. Does not eliminate the Universal Source-Crossing arm.

## Proof
We first prove the local seven-vertex theorem. Let G have seven vertices, every five-set Hamiltonian, and no tight P6. Fix a four-core R and suppose two physical ports p!=q are realized at R. Thus there are completion labels x,y outside R and actual Hamilton paths on R+x and R+y exposing the completion labels as endpoints, with opposite endpoints p and q respectively. There are two cases.

CASE I: x=y. Then one five-support S=R+x has two Hamilton endpoint pairs {x,p} and {x,q} sharing the completion vertex x. Relabel the seven vertices so
  R={0,1,2,3}, x=4, p=0, q=1,
with the two remaining vertices 5,6. Choose one actual Hamilton order O of S with endpoint pair {4,0}. There are exactly 2*3!=12 such orders. It is enough to refute each possible choice of O separately. For each choice impose the three turn literals certifying O and require, in addition, an actual Hamilton order of S with endpoint pair {4,1}.

CASE II: x!=y. Relabel
  R={0,1,2,3}, x=4, y=5, p=0, q=1,
with the seventh vertex 6. Put S=R+4 and T=R+5. Choose one actual Hamilton order O of S with endpoint pair {4,0}; again there are exactly 12 possibilities. Fix its three turn literals and require an actual Hamilton order of T with endpoint pair {5,1}.

For either case and each of the twelve choices of O use the following exact CNF.

BOUNDARY VARIABLES. On seven vertices there are 7*6*5/2=105 complete-reversal pairs of ordered triples. Introduce one Boolean variable for each pair, with the lexicographically smaller orientation as the positive literal.

NO P6. For each ordered six-tuple of distinct vertices, forbid simultaneous tightness of its four consecutive turns. There are 7P6=5040 such four-literal clauses.

UNIFORM FIVE-SET HAMILTONICITY. There are C(7,5)=21 five-subsets. For each five-subset U introduce one witness variable for each of its 5!=120 vertex orders. A witness implies the three consecutive turn literals of that order, giving 21*120*3=7560 binary implication clauses. Add one 120-literal existence clause for each U. Thus every five-set has an actual Hamilton P5 and no endpoint restriction is imposed.

FIRST PORT REALIZATION. The chosen first Hamilton order O is imposed by its three turn literals as unit clauses. Branching over all 12 endpoint-preserving choices is exhaustive because the hypothesized first port realization supplies at least one such actual order.

SECOND PORT REALIZATION. In Case I, introduce 12 witness variables for the orders of S whose endpoint pair is {4,1}. In Case II, introduce 12 witnesses for the orders of T whose endpoint pair is {5,1}. Each witness implies its three turn literals, and one 12-literal clause requires one witness.

Therefore every normalized case has
  105 + 21*120 + 12 = 2637 variables
and
  5040 + 21*120*3 + 21 + 3 + 12*3 + 1 = 12661 clauses.
No endpoint-pair cleanliness, Hall incidence, or Hamiltonicity beyond the 21 five-sets is assumed.

EXHAUSTIVE VERIFICATION. The Integrator generated all 24 CNFs directly from this definition: twelve Case-I instances and twelve Case-II instances. A standalone watched-literal DPLL solver with exact unit propagation, static weighted occurrence choice sum_C 2^{-|C|}, variable-index tie breaking, and false-before-true branching proves every instance UNSAT. The solver was independently sanity-checked against exhaustive truth-table evaluation on 100 random CNFs with at most seven variables, with no disagreement.

For Case I the twelve searches close between 180,092 and 306,967 nodes, maximum decision depth 43. For Case II they close between 95,490 and 113,962 nodes, maximum depth 44. Hence neither same-support nor different-support two-port realization can occur. Every four-core in G has at most one physical port.

Now suppose H is in the k=5, |H|=11 uniform middle-layer residue. Every seven-vertex induced subsystem of H satisfies the local hypotheses because every five-set of H is Hamiltonian and H has no P6. If any four-core R of H had two ports, retain the involved completion supports and enlarge their union, if necessary, to any seven vertices. The local theorem would forbid those two port realizations. Thus every four-core in H has a unique port.

But accepted R955 says that in exactly this order-eleven uniform residue, a minimum-cardinality Hall-deficient family in the complete all-endpoint incidence graph necessarily contains a PORT-SWITCH, i.e. a four-core with at least two distinct physical ports. Contradiction. Therefore the k=5 order-eleven uniform middle-layer residue does not exist. This proves R957.
