# R436 — General Tight-Path Contact and Historical Anchor Protection

## Statement
Let (w;P) be a graph-intrinsic head-signed tight path, P=(v_0,...,v_k), and let Q be any proper tight path meeting P. If Q avoids the signed anchor v_0, let i=min{j : v_j lies in V(Q)}; then i>=1 and the head-containing prefix (v_0,...,v_{i-1}) is a nonempty strictly shorter head-signed support disjoint from Q. If Q contains v_0, then, except for the vacuous exact replay of the same singleton support with no new certificate data, one obtains at least one of: strict full-P tight-path growth, strict Q growth, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a three-vertex reverse contact/reversal cell. The tail-signed dual is exact. Consequently, outside those explicit outputs, a genuinely later signed support or marker cannot contain the anchor of an older historical signed support; quiet contacts away from the anchor may only shorten the old support on its signed side and therefore retain that anchor.

## Hypotheses
A graph-intrinsic head- or tail-signed tight support is given, together with a proper tight path Q meeting it. For the historical-anchor corollary, Q is a genuinely later support/marker rather than mere re-display of the identical singleton certificate.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), graph-intrinsic path-contact theorem. No smallest-counterexample, completion, bounded-marker, CW, current-selection, or representative-persistence assumption is needed. The exact-replay fence is explicit.

## Applicability
Use to normalize later contact with historical signed supports and, in particular, to protect a historical signed endpoint against silent later support/marker reuse. It is the local parent behind Captured-Anchor First-Contact Control.

## Persistence
When contact is away from the signed anchor, the retained descendant is literally the signed-side prefix/suffix of the old support and keeps the old anchor. At-anchor contact is exposed as a graph-intrinsic growth/cycle/reversal event. Witnesses and selected representatives need not persist beyond what the displayed paths certify.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not declare a proper cycle contradictory. Does not turn bare historical capture into current protection. Does not count exact stationary replay of the same singleton support/certificate as fresh progress. Does not assert later paths are current in a common cover.

## Proof
Head case. Let (w,v_0,...,v_k) be tight. If Q avoids v_0, let i=min{j : v_j lies in V(Q)}; then i>=1. Then P^-=(v_0,...,v_{i-1}) is nonempty, disjoint from Q, and (w,P^-) is a prefix of the old signed extension, so P^- is a strictly shorter head-signed support with the same anchor. Now suppose Q contains v_0. If k=0, then a nontrivial Q strictly grows the singleton P; if Q=(v_0) and no new certificate is present this is only stationary replay, while the old dimer (w,v_0) is available but is not credited as a new event. Assume k>=1. Apply R435 to P and Q. Outside a reverse cell or proper cycle, P-vertices occur in increasing P-order along Q, so v_0 is the first P-vertex of Q. If Q has a predecessor a before v_0, test a v_0 v_1. Tightness concatenates the Q-prefix through v_0 with all of P, strictly extending P; badness gives v_1 v_0 a tight by R3. If v_0 is the head of Q and Q is the singleton, (w,v_0) strictly extends Q unless this is the stationary singleton replay just fenced. Otherwise Q=(v_0,q_1,...). If w is outside Q, test w v_0 q_1: tightness makes (w,Q) a strict extension of Q, badness gives q_1 v_0 w tight by R3. If w lies on Q after v_0, compare the two tight paths (w,P) and Q: they contain w and v_0 in opposite orders, so R435 gives a reverse cell or a proper cycle. This exhausts the head case. Reverse all orders for the tail case. The historical-anchor statement follows: a genuinely later support/marker containing the old signed endpoint is the at-anchor case above, while contact away from the endpoint can only replace the old support by the signed-side descendant and therefore does not erase its anchor.
