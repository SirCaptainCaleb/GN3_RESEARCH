# R1030 — P5-free direct twin wall saturates the actual selected far dimer

**Audit state:** PASS
**Proof provenance:** Slack `#a7c3-workspace` root `1789300001.841769`

## Statement
Retain the P5-free twin-wall residue of the DIRECT source-wall branch from D17.430.

- **OUT.** The selected far boundary is `b->d`, and the two source-anchor wall paths give `(d,b,t)`, `(b,t,A)`, and `(b,t,C)` tight. Then both `(b,d,A)` and `(b,d,C)` are tight. Equivalently, the two source anchors `A,C` are distinct tail witnesses on the actual selected far dimer `(b,d)`.
- **IN.** The selected far boundary is `d->b`, and the twin wall paths give `(A,t,b)`, `(C,t,b)`, and `(t,b,d)` tight. Then both `(A,d,b)` and `(C,d,b)` are tight. Equivalently, `A,C` are distinct head witnesses on the actual selected far dimer `(d,b)`.

Thus the P5-free DIRECT twin-wall residue rotates both source anchors onto the same physical far dimer already carried by the source-gate wall packet.

## Hypotheses
The exact D17.430 DIRECT twin-wall packet in either orientation; the induced five-set on `{A,C,t,b,d}` has no tight Hamilton P5; boundary antisymmetry R3.

## Scope
This is a local packet normalization. It does not by itself give a spanning two-cover, strict transition descent, or a source-gate zipper absorption theorem. Its gain is that the two source anchors become simultaneous signed witnesses on the actual selected far dimer rather than only on the source wall through `t`.

## Applicability
Use in the P5-free DIRECT branch whenever a later consumer is sensitive to signed witnesses on the physical far dimer. In OUT the witnesses have the polarity represented by `(b,d,A)` and `(b,d,C)`; in IN the dual polarity is represented by `(A,d,b)` and `(C,d,b)`.

## Persistence
Retain the physical vertices `A,C,t,b,d`, the selected far-edge orientation, both twin wall P4s, and the two forced witness turns. The result does not anonymize or replace the far dimer.

## Proof
### OUT orientation
Retain the two tight wall P4s

`(d,b,t,A)` and `(d,b,t,C)`.

Equivalently, `(d,b,t)`, `(b,t,A)`, and `(b,t,C)` are tight, and the actual selected far boundary is `b->d`.

Test `(b,d,A)`. If it were bad, R3 would give its complete reversal `(A,d,b)` tight. Together with the already tight turns `(d,b,t)` and `(b,t,C)`, this would make

`(A,d,b,t,C)`

a literal tight Hamilton P5 on the five vertices `{A,C,t,b,d}`, contrary to the P5-free hypothesis. Hence `(b,d,A)` is tight.

The same argument with the anchors interchanged shows `(b,d,C)` is tight: if it were bad, R3 would give `(C,d,b)` and then

`(C,d,b,t,A)`

would be a Hamilton P5. Therefore both source anchors are tail witnesses on the actual selected far dimer `(b,d)`.

### IN orientation
Retain the dual twin wall P4s

`(A,t,b,d)` and `(C,t,b,d)`.

Thus `(A,t,b)`, `(C,t,b)`, and `(t,b,d)` are tight, and the actual selected far boundary is `d->b`.

Test `(A,d,b)`. If it were bad, R3 would give `(b,d,A)` tight. Then the other anchor supplies the prefix turns `(C,t,b)` and `(t,b,d)`, so

`(C,t,b,d,A)`

would be a tight Hamilton P5, contradiction. Hence `(A,d,b)` is tight. Interchanging `A,C` gives `(C,d,b)` tight, because otherwise `(A,t,b,d,C)` is a Hamilton P5.

Thus both source anchors are head witnesses on the actual selected far dimer `(d,b)`.

No R887, R902, payment, replay, R24, or R5 is needed after the D17.430 twin-wall packet is present.
