# R1010 — A minimum-edge root requirement can fail even in a two-coverable edge-ordered complete graph

## Statement
Let H be an edge-ordered complete graph on h vertices such that (i) H has a spanning cover by two increasing paths and (ii) every increasing path of H has order at most h-2. Form G from H by adding vertices x,y, placing xy below every old H-edge, preserving the old order on E(H), and placing every xH- and yH-edge above every old H-edge. Then G has a spanning cover by two increasing paths, but no spanning two-path cover of G selects the globally minimum edge xy. Consequently the proposed Extreme-Edge Root Theorem is false. A concrete ten-vertex instance follows from accepted R823: take its n=8 matching-layer construction with parts A,B of size four, choose the internal order so that each part has an increasing Hamilton path, and then apply the two-vertex extension.

## Hypotheses
Finite edge-ordered complete graph H with a spanning two-increasing-path cover and longest increasing path order at most |H|-2; G is the displayed two-vertex extreme extension. For the concrete K10 instance use R823 at n=8 with internal edge order chosen to make both four-vertex parts Hamiltonian.

## Scope
Pure edge-ordered counterexample family, with a concrete K10 instance. It is a fence against forcing the globally minimum edge into a spanning two-cover, not against R888 itself.

## Applicability
Use to reject any proof strategy whose parent theorem requires every edge-ordered complete graph to admit a two-cover selecting its global minimum edge. The minimum edge may remain a boundary condition, but successful covers must be allowed to avoid it.

## Persistence
Retain H, its longest-path bound, one explicit two-cover of H, new vertices x,y, the block order xy < E(H) < all new spokes, and the proof that any xy-containing increasing path contains at most one old H-vertex. For the concrete instance retain the R823 A|B partition with |A|=|B|=4.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not refute R888. Does not show a minimum R888 counterexample can have this extension form. Does not reopen longest-complement research.

## Proof
Generic extension. Let H have h vertices, let P|Q be a spanning two-cover of H, and assume every increasing path in H has order at most h-2. Add x,y. Order xy first, then all old edges in their H-order, then all spokes from {x,y} to H. Because every spoke is later than every old edge, append x to the terminal of P and y to the terminal of Q. This gives a spanning two-cover of G.\n\nSuppose instead that a spanning two-cover of G selects xy. Since xy is globally minimum, it is the first edge of its rail. Once that rail next enters H through a spoke, that spoke is later than every H-edge, so no further old H-edge can follow. Hence the xy-rail contains at most one old vertex. The other rail is contained in H and must cover at least h-1 old vertices, contradicting the longest-path bound h-2. Thus no spanning two-cover selects xy.\n\nConcrete K10 instance. Apply accepted R823 with n=8, m=4. Partition H into A={0,1,2,3} and B={4,5,6,7}. Put every internal edge below every cross edge; choose the internal strict order so that 01<12<23 and 45<56<67, hence A and B themselves are increasing Hamilton paths. Decompose E(A,B) into four perfect matchings and order those matching blocks as in R823. The accepted theorem gives longest increasing path order at most m+ceil(m/2)=6=8-2. Therefore H satisfies the generic hypotheses. Add x=8,y=9 with 89 below all H-edges and all sixteen new spokes above all H-edges. Then (0,1,2,3,8)|(4,5,6,7,9) is a spanning two-cover of K10, but no spanning two-cover selects 89.
