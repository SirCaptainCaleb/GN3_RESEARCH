# R856 — c_X=2 split-X shape C reduces to closure, facing-anchor clauses, or one-hole X forcing

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R862 sigma_G(T)=0 five-cell frame with m>=6 and suppose the R866 forest is c_X=2 shape C: one T rail has two Q blocks and one endpoint X block, and the other rail has the remaining Q block and the other endpoint X block. Apply R867. Outside explicit Reverse-Ear geometry, write the two Q blocks on the first rail as G_i,G_j with i<j; the endpoint X block may lie before or after them, and the second rail may have either Q-X or X-Q orientation.

For m>=7 the monotone physical configurations have the following complete normal form. If {i,j}={0,1}, every configuration closes by restoring q_2 and q_{m-2} except the inward-facing orientation G_0-G_1-X_A together with X_B-G_2. In that sole residue, after restoring q_2 on the first rail, the second-rail proposal X_B,q_{m-2},G_2 has exactly two holes and yields the clause
(q_{m-2},y_2,y_1) OR (q_{m-1},q_{m-2},y_2),
where X_B=(y_1,y_2). The second literal always gives a labelled P4 against the fixed head signs a,c on (q_{m-1},q_{m-2}); the first gives R542 if X_B=G_3={a,c} and a labelled P4 if X_B=G_4={b,z}.

If {i,j}={1,2}, every configuration closes except the inward-facing orientation X_A-G_1-G_2 together with G_0-X_B. In that sole residue, after restoring q_{m-2} on the first rail, the second-rail proposal G_0,q_2,X_B has exactly two holes and yields the clause
(y_1,q_2,q_1) OR (y_2,y_1,q_2).
The first literal always gives a labelled P4 against the fixed tail signs b,z on (q_2,q_1); the second gives a labelled P4 if X_B=G_3 and R542 if X_B=G_4.

If {i,j}={0,2}, the first rail may be left in its original T form. The single-G_1 rail absorbs both deleted anchors. If it is X_B-G_1, then K=(q_2,X_B,G_1,q_{m-2}) is, for m>=7, a spanning proposal with exactly one hole (q_2,y_1,y_2); hence that hole is bad and (y_2,y_1,q_2) is forced tight, giving a labelled P4 when X_B=G_3 and an R542-ready packet when X_B=G_4. If it is G_1-X_B, then K=(q_2,G_1,X_B,q_{m-2}) has exactly one hole (y_1,y_2,q_{m-2}); hence (q_{m-2},y_2,y_1) is forced tight, giving R542 when X_B=G_3 and a labelled P4 when X_B=G_4. Thus the nonadjacent Q-pair is stronger than a mate clause: it forces a single source-decorated reverse X literal.

At m=6, G_1={q_3}. Every preceding closure/clause/one-hole construction remains valid unless it exposes one additional singleton-middle seam. There are only four such seam types: (q_2,q_3,x) when a restored left anchor precedes a terminal X block; (x,q_3,q_4) when an initial X block precedes a restored right anchor; and the same two forms on the single-G_1 rail in the nonadjacent case. If the extra seam is tight, the corresponding m>=7 conclusion goes through unchanged. If it is bad, R3 reverses it to (x,q_3,q_2) or (q_4,q_3,x), and R849 plus R523 gives a labelled Hamilton P4 using respectively a distinct witness from {b,z} or {a,c}. Hence shape C has no quiet monotone residue for any m>=6: it either closes, gives an inward-facing two-hole anchor clause whose realized literal is P4- or R542-ready, forces a one-hole reverse-X literal with the same consumer-ready typing, or at order eleven yields a labelled inward P4.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in accepted crossed R862 sigma=0 frame; m>=6; R866 shape C; accepted R867,R616,R621,R649,R849,R3,R523,R542.

## Scope
Noncircular R24 restoration. Complete monotone consumer of c_X=2 Split-X shape C. Uses literal anchor restoration, physical one/two-hole proposals, and fixed-frame sign packets; no R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,generic R358, or generic R436.

## Applicability
After R866/R867 identifies shape C, first record which physical Q pair lies on the two-Q rail and whether its endpoint X block faces inward or outward. Adjacent Q pairs close except the unique inward-facing configuration, which gives the displayed anchor clause. A nonadjacent G_0-G_2 pair should be treated by the stronger one-hole insertion on the single-G_1 rail. At m=6 test the unique singleton-middle seam first; badness is already a labelled inward P4.

## Persistence
Retain the physical Q-pair, directions and identities of X_A and X_B, the exact restored rails, the one/two-hole proposal and complete hole set, and the fixed source sign used to type the realized reverse X or inward-Q literal. For R542 outputs retain its short carrier; for P4 outputs retain the literal order.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim a labelled P4 or R542 continuation closes H. Does not consume R867 Reverse-Ear outputs. Does not address Internal-X shape B or sigma>0. Alternative clause/payment branches are not asserted to coexist.

## Proof
Put v=q_2, w=q_3, d=q_{m-2}, r=q_{m-3}, s=q_{m-1}. Outside R867 Reverse-Ear geometry, the two Q blocks on the three-block rail occur in increasing Q order. Call its endpoint X block X_A and the X block on the other rail X_B=(y_1,y_2) in actual T order when needed.

We first record two boundary sign resources exactly as in the fixed crossed frame. R616 gives the enlarged roots H-z=(q_1,q_0,a,c,b) sqcup Q[2,m] and H-b=(q_1,q_0,c,a,z) sqcup Q[2,m]. If z q_1 v were tight, then (z,q_1,v,...,q_m) together with (q_0,a,c,b) would two-cover H, so R3 gives v q_1 z; similarly v q_1 b is tight. Thus D_0=(v,q_1) is tail-signed by b,z. On the right, R621 gives H-a=Q[0,d] sqcup (c,z,b,q_m,s) and H-c=Q[0,d] sqcup (a,b,z,q_m,s). If d s a were tight, Q[0,d],s,a together with (c,z,b,q_m) would two-cover H, hence a s d is tight; similarly c s d is tight. Thus D_2=(s,d) is head-signed by a,c. R649 supplies R542-ready fixed head polarity on every orientation of G_3={a,c} and fixed tail polarity on every orientation of G_4={b,z}.

CASE {G_i,G_j}={G_0,G_1}. If the first rail is X_A-G_0-G_1, restore v between G_0,G_1 and append d after G_1. For m>=7 this gives a tight rail X_A,G_0,v,G_1,d; the second source rail is untouched, so the two rails span H. If instead the first rail is G_0-G_1-X_A, restore v there. When the second rail is G_2-X_B, prepend d to it, giving d,G_2-X_B; both rails are tight and span H. Thus only G_0-G_1-X_A together with X_B-G_2 remains. The first restored rail is tight for m>=7. On the second rail consider X_B,d,G_2. Its only holes are (y_1,y_2,d) and (y_2,d,s), so R3 gives A=(d,y_2,y_1) OR B=(s,d,y_2). B is a tail sign on D_2 opposite the fixed head witnesses a,c, hence choose one distinct from y_2 and use R523 to obtain a labelled P4. A is a head sign on the reverse X_B dimer: if X_B=G_3 it is same-head on an R649 reverse-boundary packet, so R542 applies; if X_B=G_4 it is opposite the fixed tail sign and R523 gives a P4.

CASE {G_i,G_j}={G_1,G_2}. If the first rail is G_1-G_2-X_A, prepend v and restore d between G_1,G_2; this gives a tight rail v,G_1,d,G_2-X_A for m>=7, so closure follows with the untouched second rail. If instead it is X_A-G_1-G_2, restore d. When the second rail is X_B-G_0, append v to it, giving X_B-G_0,v and closure. Thus only X_A-G_1-G_2 together with G_0-X_B remains. Consider G_0,v,X_B on the second rail. Its holes are (q_1,v,y_1) and (v,y_1,y_2), giving clause A=(y_1,v,q_1) OR B=(y_2,y_1,v). A is a head sign on D_0 opposite its tail witnesses b,z, hence R523 gives a labelled P4. B is a tail sign on the reverse X_B dimer: P4 for X_B=G_3, R542 for X_B=G_4 by R649.

CASE {G_i,G_j}={G_0,G_2}. Leave the entire three-block source rail, including its selected G_0-G_2 transition and endpoint X_A, untouched. All missing vertices v,d must be absorbed by the single-G_1 rail. If that rail is X_B-G_1, use K=(v,y_1,y_2,G_1,d). For m>=7 the source X_B-G_1 splice and the old Q extension G_1,d are unaffected, so the unique hole is h=(v,y_1,y_2). If h were tight, K with the untouched first rail would two-cover H. Thus h is bad and R3 forces (y_2,y_1,v), a tail sign on the reverse X_B dimer. R649/R523 gives a P4 for X_B=G_3, while for X_B=G_4 the same-tail sign with new witness v makes an R542-ready two-witness packet. If the single-G_1 rail is G_1-X_B, use K=(v,G_1,y_1,y_2,d). Its unique hole for m>=7 is (y_1,y_2,d), so R3 forces (d,y_2,y_1), a head sign on the reverse X_B dimer. R649 gives R542 for X_B=G_3 and a P4 for X_B=G_4.

Finally m=6, so G_1=(q_3), v=q_2,d=q_4. Review the preceding constructions. Whenever a restored anchor meets G_1 on the side opposite an inherited X splice, the lost second G_1 vertex leaves exactly one extra seam. In the {0,1} cells with first rail G_0-G_1-X_A this seam is (v,q_3,x); in the {1,2} cells with X_A-G_1-G_2 it is (x,q_3,d). In the nonadjacent case X_B-G_1 the extra seam is (y_2,q_3,d), and in G_1-X_B it is (v,q_3,y_1). No other new seams occur. If the relevant extra seam is tight, the m>=7 closure, two-hole clause, or one-hole forcing proceeds unchanged. If (v,q_3,x) is bad, R3 gives (x,q_3,v), a head sign on D_L=(q_3,v), while R849 tail-signs D_L by b,z; choose a distinct witness and R523 gives a P4. If (x,q_3,d) is bad, R3 gives (d,q_3,x), a tail sign on D_R=(d,q_3), while R849 head-signs D_R by a,c; again R523 gives a P4. The two nonadjacent extra seams are the same two forms. Hence every m=6 exception is already a labelled inward P4. This exhausts shape C.
