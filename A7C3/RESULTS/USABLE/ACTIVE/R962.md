# R962 — A trimer deletion rail in a minimum R888 counterexample forces a four-vertex compatibility star with four blocked Hamilton extensions

## Statement
Let G be a minimum-order counterexample to R888. Suppose for some z there is an exact two-path cover G-z=A|B with |A|=3. Put X=A union {z}. Then X is non-Hamiltonian. For every x in X, the three-set X-{x} has an increasing Hamilton path and G-x has an exact two-cover (X-{x})|B. Hence the singleton-deletion cover family may be chosen so that the four physical vertices in X form one compatibility clique in the sense of R926; in the canonical rail-incidence root they are four edges incident with one root port P and Omega_P=X. Moreover B union {x} is non-Hamiltonian for every x in X.

## Hypotheses
G is a minimum-order edge-ordered complete-graph counterexample to R888; G-z has an exact two-cover with one rail of order three.

## Scope
Pure edge-order singleton-fiber reduction plus accepted R926 support-root interpretation.

## Applicability
Use to attack the first possible short deletion rail. It reduces the case to four simultaneous blocked one-vertex Hamilton extensions of one fixed Hamilton rail B, with a four-vertex non-Hamiltonian port universe X.

## Persistence
Retain z, the literal trimer support A, fixed opposite Hamilton rail B, X=A+z, the four exact covers (X-x)|B, and the common R926 root port universe Omega_P=X.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove that trimer rails are impossible, does not synchronize the Hamilton orders on X-x, and does not classify X beyond non-Hamiltonicity.

## Proof
Let G be a minimum-order counterexample to R888 and suppose G-z=A|B is an exact two-path cover with |A|=3. Put X=A∪{z}. By R959 both A and B are nonempty, indeed of order at least three. First X is non-Hamiltonian: if X had an increasing Hamilton path, that path together with the literal increasing Hamilton path on B from the displayed cover would be a spanning two-path cover of G, contradiction.\n\nFix x∈X. The induced graph on X-{x} has three vertices. Every edge-ordered triangle has an increasing Hamilton path: among its three edge labels, the minimum and maximum edges share one vertex, and traversing the minimum edge into that common vertex and then the maximum edge gives a two-edge increasing path. Hence X-{x} is Hamiltonian. Since B is disjoint from X and is Hamiltonian in the original cover, G-x has the exact two-cover (X-{x})|B. We may therefore choose these four covers for the four singleton deletions x∈X.\n\nFor distinct x,y∈X, the chosen support partitions for G-x and G-y restrict on V(G)-{x,y} to the same unordered partition (X-{x,y})|B. Thus x and y are compatible in the sense of R926. Hence the four physical vertices of X form a K4 in the compatibility graph, which R926 identifies with the line graph L(R) of a simple triangle-free root R. In a simple triangle-free graph, four pairwise incident edges must have a common endpoint: choose two meeting at P; any third edge incident with both either also contains P or completes a triangle, so it contains P; the same applies to the fourth. Let P be this common root port.\n\nUsing the cover C_z, the P-side rail support is X-{z}; therefore the R926 port universe is Omega_P=(X-{z})∪{z}=X. There can be no further physical edge incident with P, because every edge incident with P belongs to Omega_P by the definition of a port universe, while Omega_P=X. Thus the four vertices of X are exactly the four physical root edges incident with P.\n\nFinally fix x∈X. If B∪{x} had an increasing Hamilton path, then together with the already established Hamilton path on X-{x} it would form a spanning two-path cover of G, contradiction. Hence B∪{x} is non-Hamiltonian for every x∈X. This proves all assertions.
