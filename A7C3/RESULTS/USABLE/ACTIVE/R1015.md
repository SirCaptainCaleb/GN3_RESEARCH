# R1015 — Global-longest one-third order floor and triple-longest equality

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample of order n and let K be a proper global-longest tight path of order L with exterior E=V(H)\\V(K). Then 4<=|E|. Moreover E has an exact two-cover P sqcup Q, and for every such exact two-cover |P|,|Q|<=L. Consequently |E|<=2L and L>=ceil(n/3). Equality n=3L holds if and only if some exact exterior two-cover has |P|=|Q|=L; equivalently, under equality every exact exterior two-cover has both rails of order L. In that case H is literally the disjoint union of three global-longest tight paths K,P,Q of the same order L.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper graph-intrinsic globally longest tight path.

## Scope
R24-free size geometry of a global-longest carrier and its exact exterior two-cover. This is the clean successor to R219 with only the quarantined one-unit exterior-floor strengthening removed.

## Applicability
Use to bound exterior slack relative to a global-longest carrier, obtain L>=ceil(n/3), and isolate the extremal n=3L face as a literal three-global-longest-path frame. The only content intentionally not retained from R219 is the stronger |E|>=5 floor.

## Persistence
K and the inequalities are graph-intrinsic. Any chosen exact exterior two-cover P,Q is representative-relative, but equality forces every such cover to have both rails at the global-longest order L.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert |E|>=5. The missing upgrade 4->5 is exactly the R169/R5 four-deletion residue and remains quarantined with the R24 order-three singleton-rail core. Does not close the equality face and does not assert uniqueness of the exterior two-cover.

## Proof
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample of order n, and let K be a proper globally longest tight path of order L. Put E=V(H)\\V(K).\n\nFirst we prove the clean lower floor |E|>=4. Since K is proper, E is nonempty. If |E|=1, the unique exterior vertex is a singleton tight path, so it together with K is a spanning two-path cover of H, contradiction. If |E|=2, the two exterior vertices form a tight dimer, again giving a spanning two-cover with K. If |E|=3, choose any ordering (a,b,c) of E. By boundary antisymmetry R3 exactly one of (a,b,c) and (c,b,a) is tight, so E supports a tight Hamilton trimer. That trimer together with K is again a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Hence |E|>=4.\n\nNow apply smallest-counterexample minimality R4 to the proper graph-intrinsic tight path K. It says that H[E] has path-cover number exactly two, so choose an exact two-cover E=P sqcup Q. Since K is globally longest in H and P,Q are tight paths of H, |P|<=L and |Q|<=L. Therefore |E|=|P|+|Q|<=2L. Since n=L+|E|, we obtain n<=3L, equivalently L>=ceil(n/3). The same inequalities apply to every exact exterior two-cover.\n\nSuppose n=3L. Then |E|=n-L=2L. For any exact exterior two-cover P sqcup Q we have |P|+|Q|=2L and separately |P|,|Q|<=L, forcing |P|=|Q|=L. Thus K,P,Q are three pairwise disjoint globally longest tight paths of order L whose union is V(H). Conversely, if some exact exterior two-cover has |P|=|Q|=L, then n=|K|+|P|+|Q|=3L. This proves the equality characterization.\n\nNo five-exterior floor is used. The historical R219 clause |E|>=5 is intentionally not proved here; its extra one unit is precisely the R169/R5 four-deletion residue blocked by quarantined R24. Thus this route isolates all of the one-third scale and triple-longest equality mechanism from R24 while retaining the clean universal floor |E|>=4.
