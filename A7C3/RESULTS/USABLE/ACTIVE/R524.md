# R524 — Two-slot insertion theorem for reversal-symmetric prepend/append ternary systems

## Statement
Let V be a finite set and E a relation on ordered triples of distinct vertices. Assume (PA) for every distinct u,v,w, at least one of (u,v,w) and (w,u,v) lies in E, and (RS) reversal symmetry: (u,v,w) is in E if and only if (w,v,u) is in E. Call a vertex sequence tight when every consecutive ordered triple lies in E. Then for every tight path P=(v_0,...,v_{k-1}) and every x outside P, at least two of the k+1 literal insertion positions of x in P produce a tight path. Consequently every n-vertex system satisfying (PA)+(RS) has at least 2^(n-1) spanning tight paths. This bound is sharp: fix a total order on V and declare (a,b,c) tight exactly when b is not the maximum of {a,b,c}; then the spanning tight paths are exactly the permutations decreasing to the minimum and then increasing, hence there are exactly 2^(n-1).

## Hypotheses
Finite ordered-triple relation; distinct vertices in each tested triple; prepend/append condition (PA); reversal symmetry (RS).

## Scope
Order-free theorem for general ternary path systems. It is deliberately outside the antisymmetric Strong Level-(1) regime and serves as the exact insertion-model comparison theorem.

## Applicability
Use whenever a quotient, residual, or auxiliary ternary relation satisfies both prepend/append completeness and reversal symmetry. It gives literal insertion, not merely rerouting, and an exponential Hamilton-path count.

## Persistence
The two insertion slots are literal positions in the retained ordered path P. The Hamilton-path count is graph/relation intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Without reversal symmetry, (PA) alone does not guarantee literal insertion into a fixed tight path; first/last-position never conditions can block two adjacent insertion slots. This theorem does not apply to boundary-antisymmetric Strong Level-(1) turns.

## Proof
Let P=(v_0,...,v_{k-1}) and x notin V(P). For each adjacent pair e_i=(v_i,v_{i+1}), consider the three possible middle vertices on the 3-set {x,v_i,v_{i+1}}. By (RS), for a fixed middle the two reverse orders have the same status. Put q_y=1 when the two orders with middle y are tight. Applying (PA) to the three cyclic choices gives q_x OR q_{v_i}, q_{v_i} OR q_{v_{i+1}}, and q_{v_{i+1}} OR q_x. Hence at most one q is zero. Thus e_i can forbid at most one insertion position: if v_i is the unique forbidden middle it blocks insertion immediately before v_i; if x is forbidden it blocks insertion between v_i and v_{i+1}; if v_{i+1} is forbidden it blocks insertion immediately after v_{i+1}; if no middle is forbidden it blocks none. Every new triple created by inserting x at a slot is associated with exactly one adjacent pair of P in this way, while all old triples not involving x remain unchanged. Therefore a bad insertion slot must be blocked by at least one adjacent pair. There are only k-1 adjacent pairs, each blocking at most one slot, but there are k+1 insertion slots, so at least two are good. For the count, fix x. Every spanning tight path of V minus {x} has at least two distinct insertions of x. Distinct parent paths and slots give distinct children because deleting x recovers both the parent order and the slot. Therefore N(V)>=2N(V minus {x}); starting from N of a one-vertex system equal to 1 gives N(V)>=2^(n-1). For sharpness, linearly order V and declare (a,b,c) tight iff b is not the largest of the three. This satisfies (PA)+(RS). A spanning permutation is tight iff it has no interior local maximum. Such a permutation is uniquely obtained by choosing which vertices other than the minimum lie to its left, placing the chosen set in decreasing order and the complement in increasing order. Thus exactly 2^(n-1) spanning tight paths occur.
