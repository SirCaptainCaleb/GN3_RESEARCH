# R432 — Complete Floor Pair Steering

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let S={p,t} be the two singleton supports of any ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced opposite-sign floor. Fix any prescribed target pair T={a,c} of distinct vertices. Then there is a finite chosen ancestry-retaining continuation, using at most two singleton-replacement macros, which yields either a spanning two-cover or an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor whose singleton supports are exactly {a,c}. Equivalently, with delta_T(S)=2-|S intersect T|, every nonclosing replacement macro strictly lowers delta_T by one until delta_T=0. If one current singleton already belongs to T, it may be preserved while the other coordinate is replaced by the missing target vertex; if S is disjoint from T, first install one target vertex and then preserve it while installing the other.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong counterexample; an ancestry-bearing both-singleton balanced-pair floor S={p,t} is active; T={a,c} is any prescribed pair of distinct physical vertices. The historical singleton signs and pair ancestry are available to the replacement macro.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order and arbitrary prescribed target pair. The target pair may be fixed independently of the incoming floor. No compatibility, capture-history, order-ten, or representative-overlap hypothesis is required.

## Applicability
Use whenever an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor exists and a proof needs to place its two singleton supports on an independently prescribed physical pair. This is the target-coordinate engine behind the historical fixed-turn reentry theorem R423.

## Persistence
The steering continuation retains the full physical ancestry ledger of the incoming paid floor: original pair birth and earlier refund/contact certificates, the actualizing dimer/orientation used for each replacement, its pair-deletion rails and marker-actualization certificate, the fixed singleton during each one-coordinate descent, successive floor coordinates, and final target-aligned pair-deletion frame. Witness names may change under certified rebirth. The source representative and old selected rails need not remain current.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not imply the target floor is in the same representative as the incoming floor; does not preserve arbitrary witness names, old rails, crossings, or seams as current; does not close the target floor; does not make target distance a monotone under arbitrary unrelated proof operations; does not permit a bare formal relabeling without the physical replacement certificates.

## Proof
Let the active ancestry-bearing floor be S={p,t} and the prescribed target pair be T={a,c}. If S=T, stop. If |S intersect T|=1, relabel the common coordinate as p and the missing target as x. Apply R433 to obtain a two-cover or the exact floor (p),(x)=T. If S and T are disjoint, first apply R433 while retaining p and prescribing x=a, obtaining a two-cover or floor (p),(a). Now retain a and use the exact head/tail dual of R433 to replace p by c, obtaining a two-cover or floor (a),(c). Thus at most two replacement macros suffice. At every nonclosing macro the intersection with the target pair grows by exactly one, so delta_T(S)=2-|S intersect T| decreases exactly by one. The retained ledger is the concatenation of the incoming floor ancestry and the one or two R433 replacement certificates. No claim is made that an additional protected/current role on a fixed singleton survives unless a separate protection theorem supplies it.
