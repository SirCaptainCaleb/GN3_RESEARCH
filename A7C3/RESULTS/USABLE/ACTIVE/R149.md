# R149 — Distinguished-trimer current-or-cross

## Statement
Let W admit an exact two-cover T, and let J=(j_0,j_1,j_2) be a distinguished tight trimer on three vertices of W. Among exact two-covers of W choose T maximizing the number of the two directed states j_0j_1 and j_1j_2 that are selected. Then either J occurs literally as a consecutive current trimer of T, or T contains a selected state crossing the vertex cut V(J) | (W\\V(J)).

## Hypotheses
W is the residual vertex set in a setting where exact two-covers of W exist; J=(j_0,j_1,j_2) is a certified tight trimer on V(J) subset W; T is chosen among exact two-covers of W to maximize selected overlap with the two directed states of J.

## Scope
Generic exact-residual-two-cover lemma. The historical application was the 4+3+3 residual compiler, especially the canonical-Z mate J_Z=(q_2,z,q_0), but the proof uses only the stated exact-two-cover and tight-trimer hypotheses.

## Applicability
Use when a named trimer must either be made literally current in a chosen exact residual two-cover or else converted into a physical current crossing incident with its three-vertex support. Particularly useful before capture, endpoint-role, or assembly consumers that require current rather than merely graph-intrinsic data.

## Persistence
In the first arm, J is literal selected currentness only in the chosen overlap-maximal target T. In the second arm, the crossing is a literal selected state of that same T. No historical transport is used.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not make J current in other representatives; does not say the crossing is captured, protected, a seam, or final-assembly data; does not preserve any parent candidate or parent cover; does not imply a spanning two-cover of H. The historical source explicitly retired the stronger false scratch inference that one total old-component cross forces all old internal edges to survive.

## Proof
Historical proof recovered from A7C2_MATH_EXPLORER_000006.md, Theorem 4. Suppose J is not literally current and no selected state of overlap-maximal T crosses V(J)|(W\\V(J)). Both sides are nonempty and T has exactly two components, so one component lies entirely on V(J) and the other entirely on W\\V(J). The J-side component contains all three vertices of J and hence is a tight three-vertex path on that support. Replace only that component by the certified tight path J=(j_0,j_1,j_2). The other component is unchanged, so the result remains an exact two-cover of W, now selecting both directed states of J, contradicting maximality. Therefore J is current or a selected cross touches V(J).
