# R583 — Four-vertex complement forces a synchronized full insertion-obstruction array

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2. Let Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=1, be a vertex-simple tight path whose complement X=V(H)\\V(Q) has exactly four vertices. Then for every x in X, the induced subsystem on V(Q) union {x} is non-Hamiltonian. Consequently, for every gap i=0,...,m-1, the literal order obtained by inserting x between q_i and q_{i+1} fails. Define the present insertion turns A_i(x)=(q_{i-1},q_i,x) when i>=1, B_i(x)=(q_i,x,q_{i+1}), and C_i(x)=(x,q_{i+1},q_{i+2}) when i+2<=m. At least one present turn is bad, so by R3 at least one corresponding exact reverse turn Abar_i(x)=(x,q_i,q_{i-1}), Bbar_i(x)=(q_{i+1},x,q_i), Cbar_i(x)=(q_{i+2},q_{i+1},x) is tight. These gap clauses hold simultaneously for all four x on the same ordered Q. For every internal gap 1<=i<=m-2, at least one of the following occurs: two distinct x,y in X have both Abar_i(x),Abar_i(y) tight, giving a same-oriented two-witness packet on (q_i,q_{i-1}); two distinct x,y have both Cbar_i(x),Cbar_i(y) tight, giving such a packet on (q_{i+2},q_{i+1}); or two distinct x,y have both reverse-middle turns Bbar_i(x)=(q_{i+1},x,q_i) and Bbar_i(y)=(q_{i+1},y,q_i) tight. At the left endpoint gap i=0 with m>=2, absent a two-witness packet among the Cbar_0(x), at least three distinct x have Bbar_0(x) tight; the exact right-end dual holds. Thus an order-three singleton-deletion rail frame does not merely have endpoint reverse stars: its four-vertex complement imposes a synchronized obstruction array along every gap of the long rail.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; pc(H)>2; Q is a tight path; exactly four vertices lie outside Q.

## Scope
Order-free four-vertex-complement parent theorem. It does not require smallest-counterexample minimality, R24, global-longestness, a transitive/cyclic four-cell classification, or a deletion-root normalization.

## Applicability
In the live R24 restoration, if H-z=P sqcup Q is an exact singleton-deletion two-cover with |P|=3, then X=V(P) union {z} is exactly the four-vertex complement of Q, so the full four-witness gap array applies immediately. Use it as a scalable replacement for endpoint-only seam enumeration and as a source of carrier-position pressure for absorber/transition arguments.

## Persistence
The ordered path Q, the physical exterior set X, every gap index, each reverse-turn disjunction, and every realized two-witness or reverse-middle packet are graph-intrinsic in the fixed frame. No representative change is made.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not choose a unique reverse disjunct at a gap. A reverse-middle pair (q_{i+1},x,q_i),(q_{i+1},y,q_i) is not by itself an R38 same-support collision. A terminal two-witness packet is not claimed to close H. The theorem does not prove R24 or Hamiltonicity of any complementary middle. It does not assume or imply Q is globally longest.

## Proof
Fix x in X. The other three vertices X-{x} support a tight Hamilton trimer by R3. If H[V(Q) union {x}] had a Hamilton tight path K_x in any order, then K_x together with that trimer would be a spanning two-cover of H, contradicting pc(H)>2. Hence V(Q) union {x} is non-Hamiltonian. In particular, for each gap i, the literal order Q with x inserted between q_i and q_{i+1} is not tight. Every old Q-turn outside that insertion window remains certified, and the only new turns are the present members of A_i(x),B_i(x),C_i(x). Therefore at least one is bad. R3 makes its complete reversal tight, giving the displayed reverse clause. This works independently for every x and gap, so all clauses coexist as graph-intrinsic facts on one Q. For an internal gap, let L,M,R be the subsets of X for which Abar_i, Bbar_i, Cbar_i respectively are tight. The clause says L union M union R=X. If neither terminal support carries two distinct witnesses, then |L|<=1 and |R|<=1. Since |X|=4, necessarily |M|>=2, proving the trichotomy. At i=0 only M and R occur; if |R|<=1 then |M|>=3. The right endpoint is dual.
