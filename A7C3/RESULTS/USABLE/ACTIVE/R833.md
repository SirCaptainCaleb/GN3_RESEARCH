# R833 — Two-block middle-dimer puncture of a P4 is a reverse seam, reverse endpoint chord, or anti-aligned endpoints

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2, let K=(x,p,q,y) be a vertex-simple tight P4, put D={p,q} and C=V(H)\V(K), and let T=T_1 sqcup T_2 be a literal exact two-cover of H-D whose two rails are nontrivial. Let b_T(x,y) be the number of maximal nonempty C-blocks obtained from the two T-rails after deleting x and y. Then b_T(x,y)>=2. If b_T(x,y)=2, at least one of the following source-visible alternatives holds. (X-REVERSE) T selects a directed state c x with c in C, and the reverse seam (p,x,c) is tight. (Y-REVERSE) T selects a directed state y c with c in C, and the reverse seam (c,y,q) is tight. (REVERSE-CHORD) T selects the directed endpoint chord y x. (ANTI-ENDS) x and y are nonadjacent T-vertices of degree one, x is the first vertex of its T-rail, and y is the last vertex of its T-rail (the two endpoints may lie on the same or different rails). More precisely, writing epsilon=1 if {x,y} is a selected T-state and 0 otherwise, the exact block-count identity is b_T(x,y)=deg_T(x)+deg_T(y)-epsilon. Thus in the two-block case either epsilon=0 and both endpoints have degree one, or epsilon=1 and their degrees are {1,2}. In the latter case the only orientation avoiding X-REVERSE and Y-REVERSE is y x; in the former case the only orientation avoiding both reverse seams is ANTI-ENDS.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); pc(H)>2; K=(x,p,q,y) is a tight P4; T is a literal exact two-cover of H-{p,q} with both rails nontrivial.

## Scope
Order-free exact-cover refinement of middle-dimer P4 recompletion. No smallest-counterexample minimality is used after the nontrivial two-cover is supplied. In a smallest counterexample R429 supplies the required cover/nontriviality for every physical middle dimer.

## Applicability
Apply to any P4 K=(x,p,q,y) after deleting its middle dimer. It is especially useful when several P4s share the same deleted dimer and exact two-cover: any carrier whose endpoint-pair deletion leaves only two complementary blocks is reduced to a named reverse seam, a selected reverse endpoint chord, or a rigid anti-aligned endpoint placement.

## Persistence
Retain K,D,C,the literal cover T,the block count b_T(x,y),the T-degrees and adjacency bit of x,y,and whichever selected state/reverse seam/endpoint placement is obtained. Reverse seams are graph-intrinsic; selected states and endpoint roles are source-cover-relative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that some endpoint pair has b_T=2. Does not assert the reverse seam, reverse chord, or anti-aligned endpoint placement closes H. Does not identify different exact two-covers or transport selected states between representatives. Does not infer capture, payment, protected contact, or long-path growth.

## Proof
Put W=V(H)\D and S={x,y}. The P4 K is a tight path on exactly D union S, so accepted R508 makes (D,S,K,T) a recompletion packet, and accepted R509(1) gives b_T(x,y)>=2. For the exact count, T is a forest with two components, hence |E(T)|=|V(T)|-2. Let epsilon be 1 when xy is a T-edge and 0 otherwise. Deleting x,y removes deg_T(x)+deg_T(y)-epsilon edges. The remaining graph is exactly the disjoint union of the maximal C-blocks, so b_T(x,y)=(|V(T)|-2)-(|E(T)|-deg_T(x)-deg_T(y)+epsilon)=deg_T(x)+deg_T(y)-epsilon. Because both T-rails are nontrivial, each of x,y has degree one or two. Assume b_T=2. If epsilon=0, then deg_T(x)=deg_T(y)=1. If the unique incidence at x is c->x with c in C, use the C-to-S endpoint case j=0 of R509: its literal spanning two-path proposal has unique uncertified turn (c,x,p), so pc(H)>2 makes that turn bad and R3, as already packaged by R509, gives (p,x,c) tight. Dually, if the unique incidence at y is y->c, the S-to-C endpoint case i=m has unique hole (q,y,c), forcing (c,y,q). If neither incidence occurs, the unique edge at x is directed x->C and the unique edge at y is directed C->y; therefore x is the initial endpoint of its T-rail and y the terminal endpoint of its T-rail. Since epsilon=0 they are nonadjacent, giving ANTI-ENDS. If epsilon=1, the degree sum is three, so one of x,y has degree one and the other degree two. If the selected chord is y->x, retain REVERSE-CHORD. If it is x->y and x is the degree-two vertex, its other path incidence is necessarily c->x, so the preceding X-REVERSE argument applies. If instead y is degree two, its other incidence is y->c and Y-REVERSE applies. This exhausts the two-block case.
