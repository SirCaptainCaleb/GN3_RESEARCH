# R1022 — Every ancestry-bearing singleton floor re-enters current maximum-three-forest space through a proper trimer

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and suppose an ancestry-bearing balanced opposite-sign pair with both supports singleton is active. Then there is a finite certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover of H or a literal minimum spanning three-cover J sqcup U sqcup V in which J is a named proper tight trimer arising from the paid-floor ancestry. More precisely, choose any physical pair {x,z}; an exact cover H-{x,z}=U_0 sqcup V_0 has an internal rail vertex b because |H|>10. Orient {x,b,z} as a tight turn J_0=(a,b,c) with {a,c}={x,z}, steer the floor to {a,c}, and run the fixed-turn interaction compiler R445 in this same pair-deletion frame. A first protected return may restore the aligned floor with endpoint a completed; a second predecessor-channel attempt at the same fixed turn is nonquiet by R434. Every resulting nonclosing explicit output contains a proper tight trimer J, and R4 exact-covers H-V(J), producing the asserted current maximum three-forest.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; an ancestry-bearing both-singleton balanced floor is active. Use accepted R3,R4,R429,R432,R434,R445,R533.

## Scope
Global bridge from historical paid-floor ancestry back to current representative dynamics. It does not assert strict Morse descent after reentry and does not identify the new forest with the source representative.

## Applicability
Use whenever capture/payment, cross-state descent, or signed-support machinery reaches a genuine ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. The floor is not terminal: it closes H or re-enters literal maximum-three-forest space through a named proper trimer after at most one protected aligned return.

## Persistence
Retain the incoming floor ancestry, chosen target pair {x,z}, fixed exact pair-deletion cover U_0|V_0, internal vertex b, fixed turn J_0, floor-steering certificates, the first R445 output/return, completed endpoint ledger A_{J_0}, the second-channel nonquiet certificate when needed, the extracted trimer J, and the final R4 complement two-cover. Source covers need not remain current.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove the reentered forest has smaller largest rail or lower global holonomy rank. Does not assert different output branches coexist. Does not treat a bare formal singleton pair without ancestry as productive. The only use of the order bound is to guarantee an internal vertex in the fixed pair-deletion cover.

## Proof
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let F be an active ancestry-bearing both-singleton balanced floor. By accepted R533, |V(H)|=n>10. Choose any two distinct physical vertices x,z. Accepted Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429 gives an exact two-cover\n\n  H-{x,z}=U_0 sqcup V_0\n\nwith both rails nontrivial. If |U_0|=r and |V_0|=s, then r+s=n-2. The total number of rail-internal vertices is (r-2)+(s-2)=n-6>0, so choose an internal vertex b of one rail. By R3, exactly one of the two complete reversals on {x,b,z} is tight. Relabel the two outer vertices as a,c so that\n\n  J_0=(a,b,c)\n\nis a tight proper trimer and {a,c}={x,z}. The displayed pair-deletion cover is therefore also the exact frame H-{a,c}=U_0 sqcup V_0, with b internal.\n\nUse accepted Complete Floor Pair Steering R432 to steer the incoming ancestry-bearing floor to the prescribed outer pair {a,c}. Unless a spanning two-cover occurs during steering, we now have an ancestry-bearing aligned both-singleton floor (a),(c), while the fixed exact frame U_0|V_0 remains available as graph-intrinsic data. Thus all hypotheses of accepted R445 hold for J_0 and this fixed frame.\n\nRun R445, taking the predecessor channel as its first birth and protecting the endpoint a exactly as in that theorem. R445 is finite. Unless H closes by a spanning two-cover, it yields either: (i) one of its explicit non-descent geometric outputs; or (ii) a completed protected return to an aligned both-singleton floor (a),(c), with a reached as the protected singleton coordinate.\n\nSuppose first that R445 yields explicit geometry. We extract a proper tight trimer J from that certificate. For a reverse-contact/reversal output, the displayed reverse turn is already such a trimer. A signed-BB reverse four-path contains a consecutive tight trimer. A strict +2 extension, strict path growth, or fusion output contains a tight path of order at least three and hence a consecutive trimer. A vertex-simple tight cycle contains a consecutive tight trimer. Finally, an explicit historical anchor/witness-contact event in this R445 frame comes from an endpoint-anchored signed dimer birth supplied by R434; the retained sign witness together with that tight dimer is exactly a tight three-vertex turn. Since n>10, every such three-vertex support is proper in H. Thus every nonclosing explicit R445 output contains a named proper tight trimer J with its physical ancestry retained.\n\nIt remains to handle the protected-return branch. At the completion of that return, accepted R434 records endpoint a in the fixed-turn completed-endpoint set A_{J_0}. Return to the same graph-intrinsic exact pair-deletion frame U_0|V_0 and inspect the predecessor channel at b again. R434 states that when the anchored endpoint e already lies in A_{J_0}, the new nontrivial dimer containing the retained historical singleton anchor e is an explicit anchor-contact/strict-growth event and the episode is nonquiet. Taking e=a, the second predecessor attempt therefore cannot begin another quiet protected return. It immediately supplies explicit geometry of the kind just treated, in particular an endpoint-anchored signed dimer with its exact sign witness or a stricter path/cycle/reversal output. Hence after at most one protected aligned return we again obtain a named proper tight trimer J, unless H has already closed.\n\nNow apply accepted smallest-counterexample minimality R4 to J. Since J is a proper graph-intrinsic tight path, R4 gives an exact two-cover P sqcup Q of H-V(J). Restoring J gives\n\n  H = J sqcup P sqcup Q.\n\nBecause pc(H)=3 by R4, this is a literal minimum spanning three-cover, equivalently an actual maximum spanning three-forest. Retain the complete incoming floor ancestry, target pair, fixed pair-deletion frame, fixed turn J_0, R445/R434 certificates, extracted trimer J, and the chosen R4 complement cover. No claim is made that the old frame remains current after this final currentization.\n\nThus every genuine ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor has a finite route to a spanning two-cover or to current maximum-three-forest space through a named proper trimer. The proof uses no R24, R5, R168, or R169.
