# R849 — Crossed high-side rectangles yield an inward Q signed pair, with certified payment only from m>=7

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R621 frame with X={a,b,c,z}, Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=6, L=q_0, u=q_1, R=q_m, s=q_{m-1}. The same fixed graph contains two synchronized signed-dimer rectangles. On the left, (L,u) and S_L=(q_3,q_2) are tail-signed by each of b,z. On the right, (s,R) and S_R=(q_{m-2},q_{m-3}) are head-signed by each of a,c. Thus S_L and S_R are opposite-polarity inward Q dimers with fully named endpoint-root witnesses. If m=6, their supports meet exactly in hinge q_3, so R547 yields the graph-intrinsic opposite-sign singleton pair {(q_2),(q_4)} together with the two ancestor dimers and hinge-cut witnesses; no payment-lineage floor is asserted. If m>=7, the two dimer supports are disjoint and form a direct mass-four balanced pair, so accepted R514 yields a finite certificate-retaining continuation to either a spanning two-cover or an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. Hence the frame always has the two-ended signed rectangle; certified payment descent follows automatically from it only in the disjoint range m>=7.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the accepted crossed R621 frame with m>=6. Use accepted R3, R536, R547, R514, and the crossed gate/endpoint-star data retained by R621.

## Scope
Corrected form of the inward-rectangle theorem. Separates raw hinged singletonization at m=6 from genuine mass-four pair descent at m>=7.

## Applicability
Use the signed rectangle itself uniformly for m>=6. Use its automatic certified pair-payment conclusion only for m>=7. At m=6 any floor-only consumer needs an additional theorem placing the R547 hinge descendants into a signed-payment lineage.

## Persistence
Retain the four inward/endpoint signed dimers, witnesses {b,z} on the left and {a,c} on the right, and the exact m=6 hinge or m>=7 disjoint-pair certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
At m=6 does not claim an ancestry-bearing floor or permission to invoke R432. At m>=7 does not claim the paid floor has prescribed singleton coordinates or preserves Q as current. Does not assert simultaneous currentness with branch-specific descendants.

## Proof
Accepted R536 gives q_3q_2b and q_3q_2z tight. To sign (L,u), suppose buL were tight. Then (b,u,L,c,a,z) is a tight path using the endpoint star uLc, the outgoing crossed gate Lca, and matching-height turn caz; together with Q[2,m] it would span H by two paths. Thus buL is bad and R3 gives Lub tight. The same argument with (z,u,L,a,c,b) gives Luz. Hence (L,u) and S_L=(q_3,q_2) are tail-signed by both b,z. Dually, if Rsa were tight then (c,z,b,R,s,a) together with Q[0,m-2] would two-cover H, so R3 gives asR; similarly csR is tight. The right dual of R536 gives a q_{m-2} q_{m-3} and c q_{m-2} q_{m-3}. Hence (s,R) and S_R=(q_{m-2},q_{m-3}) are head-signed by both a,c. If m=6 then S_L=(q_3,q_2) and S_R=(q_4,q_3) meet exactly at q_3. R547 therefore leaves opposite-sign singleton supports q_2 and q_4 with the ancestor-dimer and hinge certificates; by the exact R547/R777 contract this is only a graph-intrinsic balanced pair unless those ancestors are separately shown to lie in a payment lineage. If m>=7 the supports are disjoint. They are then a direct balanced pair of total mass four, so accepted R514 applies and yields either a spanning two-cover or an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. This proves the corrected split conclusion.
