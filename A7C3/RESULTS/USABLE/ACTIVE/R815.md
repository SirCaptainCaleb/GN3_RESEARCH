# R815 — Two repeated reverse-middle blockers on adjacent gaps force a reverse zigzag P5

## Statement
Then exactly one of the two displayed reverse zigzags is a tight P5: (q_{i+2},x,q_{i+1},y,q_i) or (q_{i+2},y,q_{i+1},x,q_i). Equivalently, if two distinct exterior vertices are reverse-middle blockers at each of two adjacent gaps of Q, those repeated blocker identities stitch into a literal tight P5 whose three Q contacts occur in descending old-Q order q_{i+2},q_{i+1},q_i. In the R583 four-complement obstruction array, if the reverse-middle blocker sets at adjacent gaps have intersection of size at least two, this P5 occurs immediately without spending either gap through R542/R588.

## Hypotheses
Let Q=(q_0,...,q_m) be a vertex-simple tight path in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, let 0<=i<=m-2, and let x,y be distinct vertices outside V(Q). Assume the four reverse-middle blocker turns (q_{i+1},x,q_i), (q_{i+1},y,q_i), (q_{i+2},x,q_{i+1}), and (q_{i+2},y,q_{i+1}) are tight.

## Scope
Pure order-free adjacent-gap lemma. No smallest-counterexample minimality, deletion cover, R24/R5, matching-height structure, payment, capture, or computation.

## Applicability
Use on simultaneous gap-obstruction arrays before consuming individual two-witness packets or P4 ears. It upgrades repeated blocker identities across neighboring cuts to one five-vertex role-labelled object. In an R24 frame, absence of this zigzag forces every pair of adjacent reverse-middle blocker sets to intersect in at most one vertex.

## Persistence
Retain the adjacent gap index i, the two exterior identities x,y, all four blocker turns, the tested central turn between x and y through q_{i+1}, and the resulting literal P5 orientation.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the P5 is selected in any deletion cover, does not by itself give a spanning two-cover, and does not claim that reverse-middle blocker sets at adjacent gaps must overlap twice.

## Proof
Test T=(x,q_{i+1},y). If T is tight, then (q_{i+2},x,q_{i+1},y,q_i) is a tight P5: its consecutive turns are (q_{i+2},x,q_{i+1}), T, and (q_{i+1},y,q_i), all tight by hypothesis. If T is bad, accepted boundary antisymmetry R3 makes its complete reversal (y,q_{i+1},x) tight. Then (q_{i+2},y,q_{i+1},x,q_i) is a tight P5 using the other two blocker turns. The two cases are exclusive by R3. The R583 application is immediate by choosing any two vertices in the intersection of the adjacent reverse-middle blocker sets.
