# R886 — A single fragmented X dimer is a double low-component marker pair

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed beyond-ten inward-anchor five-cell frame and the BOUNDED branch of accepted R885. Suppose exactly one middle-gate X cell is fragmented, f(T)=1. Let F={x,x'} be that fragmented X cell and I the other, contiguous X cell. Then BOTH fragmented markers satisfy c_x=c(T[Y union {x}])<=3 and c_{x'}<=3, where Y=G_0 union G_1 union G_2. Moreover there is an exact binary refinement. (LOW2) At least one of x,x' has c<=2; equivalently r_Y<=3. (MAX3) Otherwise c_x=c_{x'}=3; equivalently r_Y=4. In MAX3 necessarily p=e_T(X)=1, q=e_T(X,Y)=5, both fragmented markers have d_Y=2, and the two intact-cell vertices have Y-degrees 1 and 0 in some order. Hence the full degree/c-value multisets are d_Y={2,2,1,0} and c={3,3,4,5}, with the two 3s exactly on the fragmented cell. Furthermore MAX3 has only three contracted X/Y rail skeletons. Contract each of the four maximal Y-runs to a symbol Y, keep the two vertices of I as the consecutive endcap block II, and keep the two fragmented markers individually as F. Up to swapping the two rails, reversing either displayed rail, interchanging the two fragmented labels, and reversing the internal order of I, the skeleton is exactly one of: (M0) II-Y | Y-F-Y-F-Y; (M1) II-Y-F-Y | Y-F-Y; (M2) II-Y-F-Y-F-Y | Y. Equivalently, the rail containing the intact endcap contains t=0,1,2 fragmented markers, and this integer t completely determines the coarse skeleton. In particular the all-high hypothesis c_y>=4 for every y in X is impossible for f=1, so the R535 13-word high-interleaving classification never applies in the exactly-one-fragmented regime. Also, if p=2 then necessarily q<=3 and r_Y<=3, hence p=2 lies in LOW2.

## Hypotheses
Accepted R885 BOUNDED branch with f(T)=1 in the crossed R594/R862 frame; accepted R837 endcap placement for the unique contiguous middle-gate X cell; accepted R535 notation and P4-free bound p<=2.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed R24/O6 sharpening of the exactly-one-fragmented five-cell excess branch. Pure path-forest/endcap geometry after R885; no payment, capture, R436, R5, R168, R169, R224, R533, R350, generic R358, or computation.

## Applicability
Use immediately after R885 when f=1. Split only into LOW2, equivalently r_Y<=3, and MAX3, equivalently r_Y=4 with p=1,q=5 and both fragmented markers c=3. MAX3 is already completely coarse-normalized to M0/M1/M2 according to how many fragmented markers lie on the intact-endcap rail. Do not use the R535 all-high 13-word classification in f=1. A second X-X edge p=2 is automatically confined to LOW2.

## Persistence
Retain T, F={x,x'}, the intact R837 endcap I, the residual forest T-I, both c-values, and the LOW2/MAX3 label. In MAX3 retain p=1,q=5,r_Y=4, d_Y=2 on both fragmented markers, {1,0} on I, and the M0/M1/M2 contracted skeleton with actual rail assignment and marker labels before quotienting symmetries.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close LOW2 or any of M0/M1/M2, identify which source Q-cell contributes each Y-run, or assert every coarse MAX3 skeleton is realizable in the Strong frame. Does not strengthen f=2.

## Proof
Let F={x,x'} be the fragmented middle-gate cell and I the unique intact middle-gate cell. In the BOUNDED branch of accepted R885 no R837 labelled-P4 output occurs, so accepted R837 places I as an actual endcap block of its T-rail: G_4 is initial when intact, and G_3 is terminal when intact. Put T'=T-I and let k=c(T') be its number of nonempty path components. Endcap deletion cannot split a rail, so k is either 1 or 2.

Fix x in F. Since B_x=Y union {x}=W-(I union {x'}), one has T[B_x]=T'-x'. For any vertex w of a path forest, deleting w changes the number of nonempty components by deg(w)-1: an isolated singleton removes one component, an endpoint leaves the count unchanged, and an internal degree-two vertex splits one component into two. Therefore

  c_x = k + deg_{T'}(x') - 1,
  c_{x'} = k + deg_{T'}(x) - 1.

Because k<=2 and every path-forest degree is at most two, both c_x and c_{x'} are at most three. This immediately kills the all-high R535 hypothesis in f=1.

Suppose neither fragmented marker is LOW2. Then c_x=c_{x'}=3. The displayed identities force k=2 and deg_{T'}(x)=deg_{T'}(x')=2. In particular neither fragmented marker is adjacent in T to the deleted endcap I, because such an adjacency would disappear in T' and leave its T'-degree at most one. The fragmented markers are not adjacent to each other by definition of fragmentation. Hence both of their two T-neighbors lie in Y, so d_Y(x)=d_Y(x')=2. Also there is no cross-cell X-X edge. Thus the only selected X-X edge is the intact-cell edge, giving p=1. Since k=2, the intact endcap rail was nontrivial before deletion, so its unique boundary edge leaving I exists. That edge cannot go to F, as just shown, and therefore goes to Y. Hence the two intact-cell vertices have total Y-degree exactly one, with degrees {1,0}. Consequently q=sum_{y in X} d_Y(y)=2+2+1=5, and accepted R535 gives r_Y=p+q-2=1+5-2=4. The pointwise identity c_y=r_Y+1-d_Y(y)=5-d_Y(y) gives c-multiset {3,3,4,5}, with the two 3s exactly on F. This is MAX3.

Conversely suppose r_Y=4. The intact cell contributes an X-X edge, so p>=1; accepted R594/R535 gives p<=2. If p=2, the second X-X edge must be cross-cell, incident with some fragmented marker f. Then d_Y(f)<=1. But the first part gives c_f<=3, while r_Y=q in the p=2 case, so q+1-d_Y(f)=c_f<=3 and hence q<=3, contradicting r_Y=q=4. Therefore p=1, so q=5. Since both fragmented c-values are at most three, c=5-d_Y forces d_Y=2 on each fragmented marker. The intact vertices then contribute the remaining one Y-degree, giving {1,0}; hence both fragmented c-values are exactly three. Thus r_Y=4 implies MAX3.

It follows immediately that MAX3 is equivalent to r_Y=4. Since R885 gives r_Y<=4, failure of MAX3 is equivalent to r_Y<=3; and because both fragmented c-values are at most three, failure of MAX3 is also equivalent to at least one of them being at most two. This proves the LOW2 equivalence. The preceding p=2 argument also proves directly that p=2 forces q<=3 and r_Y=q<=3.

It remains to classify the coarse MAX3 skeleton. Contract each maximal Y-run to one vertex labelled Y, while keeping the four X vertices. By maximality of the Y-runs there are no Y-Y edges in this contracted forest. In MAX3 the only X-X edge is the intact dimer I, exactly one vertex of I has one Y-neighbor and the other has none, and each fragmented marker has degree two with both neighbors in Y. Therefore the component containing I begins, up to reversal, with the endcap segment II-Y. Thereafter every fragmented marker that lies on this component is an internal separator Y-F-Y. The other component contains the remaining fragmented markers, each likewise internal, and therefore begins and ends in Y (or is a single isolated Y if it contains no fragmented marker). If t is the number of fragmented markers on the I-component, then t is 0,1,or2. The I-component uses exactly t+1 of the four Y-runs, while the other component uses the remaining 3-t Y-runs and 2-t fragmented markers. Hence, up to rail swapping, rail reversal, interchange of fragmented labels, and reversal of the two vertices of I, the three and only three skeletons are

  M0: II-Y | Y-F-Y-F-Y,
  M1: II-Y-F-Y | Y-F-Y,
  M2: II-Y-F-Y-F-Y | Y.

Conversely each possible t gives exactly the displayed path-forest skeleton. This is only a coarse X/Y classification and does not assert source-Q-cell realizability.

Therefore the exactly-one-fragmented branch has the exact LOW2/MAX3 dichotomy, and the sole MAX3 face has only the three displayed contracted rail skeletons.
