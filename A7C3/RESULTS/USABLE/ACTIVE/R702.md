# R702 — Every crossed middle-gate deletion cover gives mate geometry or an inward X-Q transition

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R621/R649 frame with X={a,b,c,z}, Q=(q_0,...,q_m), L=q_0 and R=q_m. Let D be either crossed middle-gate pair {a,c} or {b,z}, let X_D=X\D be the complementary middle-gate pair, and let T be any exact two-cover of H-D. Apply R696 to the two R649 packets on the two tested orientations of D whose complementary singleton runs through the two vertices of X_D and whose same-polarity witness pair is {L,R}. Then at least one of the following holds. (MATE) One of those packets is in the R696 END branch, hence supplies its literal spanning two-path proposal with one or two uncertified turns and the corresponding forced reverse turn / physical two-hole mate clause. (INWARD) T contains a selected adjacent state between a vertex x in X_D and an interior Q vertex q_i with 1<=i<=m-1. Thus, outside the concrete END mate geometry, every exact crossed middle-gate deletion cover already moves a physical selected cut from X into the interior of Q; no balanced-pair payment or second deletion cover is needed for this transition.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the accepted crossed R621/R649 frame. D is one of {a,c},{b,z}; T is any exact two-cover of H-D. Use pending R696 on the two X-complementary packets with witness pair {L,R}. Smallest-counterexample minimality is used only through the existence/exactness already present in the frame and R696 context; the path-forest and gate-polarity step is local.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed-frame local consumer. It uses the physical middle-gate deletion cover itself and crossed endpoint gate polarity. No five-cell classification, Reverse Ear, R39 floor descent, generic payment, R436, R450, R5, R168, R169, R224, R533, or generic R358 is used.

## Applicability
Whenever a crossed middle-gate pair is deleted, inspect the two complementary-X R696 packets together. If neither complementary singleton is an END, an actual selected X-Q state reaches q_1,...,q_{m-1}. In particular a FREE state equal to the other complementary X vertex is not a residue: the resulting internal X-dimer block must have an inward Q neighbor.

## Persistence
Retain D, the exact deletion cover T, the two complementary X labels, and either the exact R696 END proposal/hole data or the literal selected inward X-Q state with its q_i coordinate and T orientation.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that every inward X-Q selected state itself closes H, that it is unique, or that its R527 descendants remain current. Does not claim a specific interior index i. Does not use anonymous balanced-pair descent as progress. Review of this result requires review/acceptance of R696 or a simultaneous review of the dependency chain.

## Proof
Fix D and T. First take D={a,c}, so X_D={b,z}; the other deletion is the exact dual. R649 supplies two R696-ready packets on the two tested orientations of D, one with complementary singleton b and one with complementary singleton z, both with witness pair {L,R}. Apply R696 to both in the same T. They cannot both be SANDWICH: that would make both b and z have T-neighbor set {L,R}, impossible in a disjoint union of paths. Therefore at least one packet is END or FREE. If one is END, MATE holds. Assume no packet is END and choose a FREE complementary singleton e in {b,z}. By R696, e has T-degree two and has a selected neighbor x outside {L,R}. The R621 exact-frame identities span H by X union V(Q), while a,c are deleted. Hence x is either an interior Q vertex q_i, 1<=i<=m-1, or the other complementary X vertex e'. In the first case INWARD holds. Suppose x=e'. Since no packet is END, e' also has T-degree two. Thus e,e' occur as a consecutive internal two-vertex block on one T-rail, with distinct outside neighbors p,q. No vertices besides b,z and Q remain in H-{a,c}, so p,q lie on Q. If either is q_i with 1<=i<=m-1, INWARD holds. Suppose instead {p,q}={L,R}. Then that T-rail contains the b,z block between L and R. But R621 says {b,z} is fully incoming to both physical endpoints: (b,z,L),(z,b,L),(b,z,R),(z,b,R) are tight. Whichever endpoint precedes the ordered b,z block on the T-rail therefore creates the exact reverse of one of these certified tight turns. For example L,b,z reverses z,b,L and L,z,b reverses b,z,L; the R-first cases are identical at R. By boundary antisymmetry that preceding endpoint turn is bad, contradicting T tightness. Hence {p,q}!={L,R}, so one is an interior Q vertex and INWARD follows. Now take D={b,z}, X_D={a,c}. The same argument applies. If the a,c block were internal with outside neighbors exactly L,R, R621 says both endpoints are fully outgoing to {a,c}: (L,a,c),(L,c,a),(R,a,c),(R,c,a) are tight. Whichever endpoint follows the ordered a,c block on the T-rail produces the reverse of one of these tight turns, again contradicting T tightness. Thus an interior Q neighbor is forced. This proves the dichotomy for both crossed middle-gate deletions.
