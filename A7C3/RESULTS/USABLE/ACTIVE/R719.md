# R719 — A cross-end trimer R-x-L feeds R435 only into the pre-existing endpoint stars

## Statement
Retain the accepted R594 synchronized transitive frame with X disjoint from Q=(L=q_0,u=q_1,...,s=q_{m-1},R=q_m), m>=4, and the universal endpoint reverse stars (u,L,x) and (x,R,s) tight for every x in X. Fix x in X and suppose the cross-end trimer K=(R,x,L) is tight. Compare K with Q by accepted Reverse Ear R435 using consecutive Q-contacts R=q_m then L=q_0. The two seam turns required to close the R435 cycle are exactly (s,R,x) and (x,L,u). Both are automatically bad: their complete reversals (x,R,s) and (u,L,x) are the already accepted R594 endpoint-star turns. Therefore the vertex-simple cycle branch of R435 is impossible for this ear. Moreover the two possible R435 reverse-trimer outputs are exactly the already-known endpoint-star turns (x,R,s) and (u,L,x). Consequently applying R435 to a cross-end trimer (R,x,L) creates no fresh turn geometry beyond the cross-end trimer itself; its seam output is certificate-stationary endpoint-star replay. Any useful consumer of (R,x,L) must use the trimer by another mechanism rather than crediting generic Reverse-Ear/cycle progress.

## Hypotheses
Accepted R594 synchronized transitive frame; x in X; cross-end trimer (R,x,L) tight; accepted R3 and R435.

## Scope
Pure R594/R435 specialization and strategy fence. No crossed phase, deletion cover, payment, capture, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,R358, or R436.

## Applicability
Whenever a route produces a tight trimer (q_m,x,q_0), retain that trimer as new cross-end geometry but do not report the subsequent R435 call as a new Reverse-Ear/cycle output. Search instead for a direct cross-end recompletion, a four/five-cell amplification using x, or a deletion-cover consumer of the physical chord through x.

## Persistence
Retain x, cross-end trimer (R,x,L), the two failed seam turns, and their identification with the accepted endpoint stars.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say the cross-end trimer itself is useless or stationary; only its generic R435 seam consumption is stationary. Does not treat the opposite orientation (L,x,R), whose seam pattern is different. Does not assert any cross-end trimer exists.

## Proof
Let P=Q=(L,u,...,s,R) and E=K=(R,x,L). The consecutive K-contacts with P are v_i=R=q_m and v_j=L=q_0, with i=m>0=j and internal ear vertex x outside Q. In the nonadjacent R435 case the cycle candidate follows the old Q segment from L through ... through s,R and then the ear R,x,L. The only two seam turns not already certified by Q or K are (s,R,x) at the R seam and (x,L,u) at the L seam. Accepted R594 gives (x,R,s) tight and (u,L,x) tight for every x in X. By boundary antisymmetry R3, (s,R,x) and (x,L,u) are therefore bad. Thus the cycle case requiring both seams tight is impossible. The exact R3 reversals of the two failed seams are (x,R,s) and (u,L,x), which are exactly the same pre-existing R594 endpoint-star turns. This exhausts the R435 alternatives for E and proves the claim.
