# R1008 — In a non-Hamiltonian edge-ordered K4 the mate of a minimum edge is locally second-smallest

## Statement
Let X={x,y,a,b} induce an edge-ordered K4 with no increasing Hamilton path. If xy is the smallest of the six edges of X, then the opposite edge ab is smaller than each of xa,xb,ya,yb. Hence xy and ab are the two locally smallest edges of X. In the standard transitive matching-height boundary orientation induced by the edge order, the opposite pair {xy,ab} is the top matching level M_R (because a turn is tight when its first edge is earlier).

## Hypotheses
Pure edge-ordered K4; X has no increasing Hamilton P4; xy is the minimum edge of X.

## Scope
Pure four-vertex edge-order lemma.

## Applicability
Use when a critical non-Hamiltonian four-cell contains a globally minimum physical edge. It identifies the opposite mate as the other locally low edge and fixes the matching-level normalization.

## Persistence
Retain X, the minimum edge xy, its opposite edge ab, and the four strict inequalities ab<xa,ab<xb,ab<ya,ab<yb.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not classify the order among the four cross edges and does not imply a fifth vertex Hamiltonizes X.

## Proof
Assume xy is the minimum edge of X. If ya<ab, then (x,y,a,b) has edge-label sequence xy<ya<ab and is an increasing Hamilton path, contradiction. If yb<ab, then (x,y,b,a) is increasing. If xa<ab, then (y,x,a,b) is increasing. If xb<ab, then (y,x,b,a) is increasing. Therefore ab is smaller than all four cross edges xa,xb,ya,yb. Since xy is already the minimum edge, ab is the second-smallest edge of the induced K4. For the matching-height interpretation, adjacent edges in a tight ordered triple satisfy first-edge<label second-edge. Thus the matching class whose edges are locally earliest is the top class in the convention M_R>M_S>M_L used by the accepted transitive-cell toolkit, so the opposite pair {xy,ab} is M_R.
