# R975 — A trimer deletion rail in a minimum R888 counterexample cannot face a Hamilton rail of order four or five

## Statement
Let G be a minimum-order counterexample to R888. Suppose for some vertex z an exact two-path cover of G-z has a three-vertex rail A and an opposite Hamilton rail B. Then |B| is not 4 or 5. Equivalently, aside from the separate |B|=3 order-seven case, every surviving trimer fiber has |B|>=6.

## Hypotheses
G is a minimum-order counterexample to R888; G-z=A|B is an exact two-path cover with |A|=3 and B Hamiltonian. Use the accepted trimer critical-cell setup R962/R969 and the normalized left middle gate from R541.

## Scope
Pure edge-ordered R888 trimer-fiber finite-base theorem. No ordered-1-factorization hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use to raise the long opposite-rail floor in the trimer branch before any barrier or one-hole seam analysis.

## Persistence
Retain X=A union {z}, the normalized transitive matching-height cell with left outgoing M_S edge {b,z}, B=(L,q1,...,R), the two explicit left P5s (q1,L,z,b,c) and (q1,L,b,z,a), and the R963 shared-triangle completion in the |B|=5 case.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not treat |B|=3; does not close trimer fibers with |B|>=6; does not prove R888.

## Proof
Retain the accepted trimer setup from R969. Thus X=A union {z} is a non-Hamiltonian transitive matching-height four-cell and B=(L,q_1,...,R) is an increasing Hamilton path. Normalize X exactly as in the R582/P660 coordinates used by D5: the left outgoing middle edge is {b,z}. Then the accepted left-gate packet gives q_1 L b and q_1 L z tight, L b z and L z b tight, and the matching-height rule on X gives z b c and b z a tight. Hence both displayed sequences\n\n  P_a=(q_1,L,z,b,c),\n  P_c=(q_1,L,b,z,a)\n\nare increasing P5s; P_a omits a from X and P_c omits c.\n\nSuppose first |B|=4, so B=(L,q_1,q_2,R). The complement of P_a in G is the three-set {q_2,R,a}. Every edge-ordered triangle has an increasing Hamilton path: among its three edges, the smallest and largest share a vertex, and traversing them in that order gives a two-edge increasing path. Therefore P_a together with a Hamilton path on {q_2,R,a} is a spanning two-cover, contradiction. Thus |B|!=4.\n\nNow suppose |B|=5, so B=(L,q_1,q_2,q_3,R). The complement of P_a is T union {a}, where T={q_2,q_3,R}; the complement of P_c is T union {c}. If either of these four-sets were Hamiltonian, together with the corresponding P5 it would give a spanning two-cover of G. Hence both T+a and T+c are non-Hamiltonian edge-ordered K4s. They share exactly the triangle T. Accepted R963 therefore supplies an increasing Hamilton path on T union {a,c}.\n\nThe remaining four vertices are {q_1,L,b,z}. They have the explicit increasing order (q_1,L,b,z): q_1 L b is the universal endpoint reverse-star turn from the R969 setup, and L b z is the normalized left-gate turn. Thus (q_1,L,b,z) is an increasing P4. This P4 and the R963 Hamilton P5 on T+a+c are disjoint and span G, contradiction. Therefore |B|!=5.\n\nThe proof does not use the right ALIGNED/CROSSED gate and does not address |B|=3 or |B|>=6.
