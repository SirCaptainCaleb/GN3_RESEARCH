# R427 — Width-Two Signed-Pair Dimension Lowering

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair have at least one support of order two. Then there is a finite chosen certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced opposite-sign pair with a singleton support. In the head-signed dimer swallowing case (alpha;(p_0,p_1)), one may choose the relevant outside sandwich head nu distinct from alpha; if p_0p_1nu is bad then nu p_1p_0 is tight, and antisymmetry on the distinct triple {nu,p_0,alpha} supplies a second tight trimer meeting the dimer in exactly p_0. Signed-Interval Rebirth therefore leaves the singleton (p_1) head-signed. The tail-side dual is exact. All other width-two sandwich failures already strictly shrink a support or give a two-cover, so finite repetition reaches a singleton.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong counterexample; a balanced opposite-sign pair is present; at least one support has order exactly two. Use the historical Pair-Complement Sandwich seam table and choose an outside cover endpoint distinct from the relevant sign witness as in the witness-separation lemma.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order. This is a chosen certificate-retaining dimension-lowering theorem, not a global scalar monotonicity statement.

## Applicability
Use whenever pair descent reaches a signed dimer. It physically pays the last dimension drop to a singleton without voluntary support truncation and without requiring a two-witness reversed-dimer collision.

## Persistence
Retains the old dimer as historical ancestry, the R12 swallowing reverse trimer, the second witness-separated refund trimer, and graph-intrinsic signs on the descendant pair. The signed endpoint/witness on the opposite support may shift if the marker also contacts that support.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not preserve every original witness or endpoint label forever; does not claim the resulting singleton states are selected in an unrelated cover; does not close the both-singleton layer; does not require or imply Same-Polarity Two-Extension Collision.

## Proof
Historical R13 proof. For head-signed P=(p_0,p_1) with alpha p_0p_1 tight, choose an at-most-two-path cover of the outside reservoir having a nonempty component with head nu != alpha. Such a choice always exists unless H is already two-covered: if a one-path cover begins at alpha, split off alpha; if the reservoir is exactly {alpha}, then (alpha,P) together with the opposite support already two-covers H. In the unique R12 dimer-swallowing case p_0p_1nu is bad, so nu p_1p_0 is tight. Since nu,p_0,alpha are distinct, boundary antisymmetry gives exactly one of nu p_0 alpha and alpha p_0 nu tight. This order-three marker meets P exactly at p_0. Signed-Interval Rebirth therefore leaves P'=(p_1) with a graph-intrinsic head sign. Other R12 bad seams either already reduce the width-two support to one vertex, shrink the opposite support, or close; the only nonterminal repetitions strictly lower total active pair mass. The tail dual is identical. Researcher 14 independently audited Witness-Separated Outside Head, Width-Two Swallow Refund, Width-Two Dimension Lowering, and the composition PASS.
