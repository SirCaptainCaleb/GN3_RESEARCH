# R2029 — Canonical triple-exposure cap packet

**Status:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Source:** Slack workspace R2029 re-audit

## Repaired statement

In the retained source frame, suppose a low-transition exact two-cover of `G=H-{A,C}` has literal rails

`(s,v)` and `Q=(t,b_1,...,b_m,r)`,

where `{s,t,r}={p,q,r}` and `B=(b_1,...,b_m)` is nontrivial.

Lift at `s` as in R2025. Then both v-anchor caps

`(s,A,v)` and `(v,C,s)`

are tight.

Applying R2026 to `Q` forces at least one of the following orientation-sensitive alternatives:

1. `(s,C,t)` is bad, hence `(t,C,s)` is tight by R3;
2. `(r,A,s)` is bad, hence `(s,A,r)` is tight by R3;
3. both B-facing endpoint seams `(C,t,b_1)` and `(b_m,r,A)` are bad, hence both reverse caps `(b_1,t,C)` and `(A,r,b_m)` are tight by R3.

The former stronger assertions that both source-facing seams are tight and that `(r,A,s,C,t)` is a forced tight P5 are withdrawn.

## Proof

Deleting `s` from the two-vertex rail `(s,v)` leaves the singleton `(v)`, so R2025 gives the literal spanning three-forest

`N=(A,s,C) | (v) | Q`.

Join the singleton to the anchor rail by `v->A`. The resulting non-`Q` path is `(v,A,s,C)`, whose only new seam is `(v,A,s)`. If that seam were tight, this path together with `Q` would be a spanning exact two-cover of `H`. Hence `(v,A,s)` is bad, and R3 gives `(s,A,v)` tight.

Dually, join the anchor rail to the singleton by `C->v`. The path `(A,s,C,v)` has only new seam `(s,C,v)`. Counterexample status makes it bad, so R3 gives `(v,C,s)` tight.

Now apply R2026 to the edge-bearing rail `Q=(t,b_1,...,b_m,r)`. Its two source-facing seams are exactly `(s,C,t)` and `(r,A,s)`. R2026 says that at least one of these is bad, or else both rail-facing endpoint seams `(C,t,b_1)` and `(b_m,r,A)` are bad. Applying complete-reversal R3 gives precisely the three displayed cap alternatives.

No cyclic rotation of any tight turn is used.

## Removed overclaims

The previous proof tried to rule out each bad source-facing seam by combining an R3 cap, a source turn, and a cyclic rotation of one v-anchor cap. Cyclic rotation is not an axiom of Strong Level-(1) boundary tournaments. Those exclusions therefore fail, and the claimed simultaneous tightness of `(s,C,t)` and `(r,A,s)` is not certified. The downstream R2031 closure was independently falsified for the same reason.

The surviving packet is still a concrete ancestry-bearing consumer: two deterministic v-anchor caps plus one exact R2026 trichotomy on the long rail.
