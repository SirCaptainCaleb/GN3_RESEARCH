# R163 — Complete Mate-Box Collapse

## Statement
Let H satisfy pc(H)>k. For j=1,...,r let A_j={alpha_{j,i}: i in I_j} be finite families of ordered turns. Assume that for every tuple (i_1,...,i_r) there is a literal spanning k-component path proposal C(i_1,...,i_r) whose only possibly bad turns are holes h_{j,i_j} with complete reversals alpha_{j,i_j}. Then at least one family A_j is entirely tight.

## Hypotheses
Boundary antisymmetry holds; pc(H)>k; every Cartesian-product tuple of the finite mate families has the stated literal spanning k-component proposal, with no undecided/bad turn outside the listed holes and with each listed hole's complete reversal exactly the corresponding chosen alpha.

## Scope
Finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournaments. This is a generic propositional mate/candidate compiler independent of order ten and of any particular endcap geometry.

## Applicability
Use after constructing a full Cartesian box of literal candidate k-covers. It converts tuplewise candidate failure into an entire tight mate family, allowing one family to be treated as simultaneously available graph-intrinsic turns.

## Persistence
The conclusion that one whole mate family is graph-intrinsically tight persists across representative changes. The literal candidate proposals used in the proof are certificates only; their selected states are not asserted current afterward.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The theorem does not manufacture the tuple family; every tuple certificate must be literal and complete. It does not apply to a partial box, proposals with additional holes, or holes whose exact antisymmetric mates are not the selected alpha turns. It does not say which family collapses.

## Proof
Fix a tuple (i_1,...,i_r). If all chosen turns alpha_{j,i_j} were bad, Boundary Antisymmetry R3 would make every reversed hole h_{j,i_j} tight. By hypothesis those are the only possible holes in C(i_1,...,i_r), so that proposal would be a literal spanning k-cover, contradicting pc(H)>k. Hence every tuple satisfies the clause alpha_{1,i_1} tight OR ... OR alpha_{r,i_r} tight. Now suppose no A_j were entirely tight. For each j choose i_j with alpha_{j,i_j} bad. The corresponding Cartesian tuple makes every literal in its clause false, contradiction. Therefore some A_j consists entirely of tight turns.
