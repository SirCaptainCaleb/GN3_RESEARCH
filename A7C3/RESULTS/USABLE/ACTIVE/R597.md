# R597 — Endpoint-local synchronized five-badness does not discharge the R24 frame

## Statement
There exists a Strong Level-(1) orientation on six vertices X union {L,R}, with X={a,b,c,z} the transitive matching-height four-cell M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}, such that both endpoint five-cells X union {L} and X union {R} are Hamilton-P5-free, the full six-cell is Hamilton-P6-free, and, for every root x in X, exactly five of the six normalized R24 seams formed from the three tight trimer orders on X-{x} are bad. Hence neither one five-bad root nor any endpoint-local synchronization of two or all four five-bad roots forces a local Hamilton P6. In particular the R576 total-badness information (even strengthened from total at least 16 to the realized total 20 with badness multiset {5,5,5,5}) cannot by itself discharge R24.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) exact reversal antisymmetry only; X is the displayed transitive matching-height four-cell. The P5-free and P6-free properties and seam counts are part of the explicit finite certificate.

## Scope
Finite endpoint-local obstruction for the transitive R24 hard frame.

## Applicability
Use as a route fence when attempting to consume R576 or any high-badness sibling-root statement. A successful R24 discharge must add information involving an interior vertex/gap of Q, or a theorem that makes paid descendants/certificates simultaneously current.

## Persistence
Graph-intrinsic finite orientation certificate; independent of descendant currentness.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not construct a global smallest counterexample and does not satisfy or refute the full gapwise R583 array on a long Q. Does not show that Q-interior strengthened discharge is impossible. Does not deny that individual bad seams trigger R542 capture/payment; it shows only that endpoint badness and endpoint Hamilton forcing alone cannot finish.

## Proof
Write uvw for the tight turn (u,v,w). Exactly one tight representative from each reversal pair is:

middle a: Laz, RaL, baL, baR, bac, baz, caL, caR, caz, zaR.
middle b: Lba, Lbc, Lbz, RbL, Rbc, Rbz, abR, abc, abz, zbc.
middle c: LcR, Lcb, Rcb, acL, acR, acb, zcL, zcR, zca, zcb.
middle z: LzR, Lza, Lzb, Rza, Rzb, bza, czL, czR, cza, czb.
middle L: aLR, bLR, bLa, bLc, bLz, cLR, cLa, zLR, zLa, zLc.
middle R: aRL, bRL, bRa, bRc, bRz, cRL, cRa, zRL, zRa, zRc.

Each middle has ten unordered endpoint pairs, so these 60 words define a Strong orientation. Its X-only representatives are precisely the matching-height orientation. Direct lookup over the 120 orders of X+L and 120 orders of X+R finds a missing consecutive turn in every order; direct lookup over all 720 six-orders does likewise. Thus the endpoint cells are P5-free and the six-cell P6-free.

The six normalized seams, with + tight and - bad, are:
root a: zbL-, bcL-, cbL-, Rcz-, Rzb+, Rzc-;
root b: azL-, zaL-, caL+, Rca-, Rcz-, Rzc-;
root c: bzL-, azL-, zaL-, Rab-, Rba-, Rbz+;
root z: bcL-, cbL-, acL+, Rab-, Rac-, Rba-.
Every root therefore has exactly five bad seams. All checks are literal finite lookups in the displayed complete orientation table, and no circular result is used.
