# R553 — A bidirectionally universal bridge dimer cannot span a transitive four-cell without P5 or P6

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,z} be a transitive matching-height no-P4 four-cell normalized by M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}. Let u,v be distinct vertices outside X. Suppose (x,u,v) and (u,v,x) are tight for every x∈X. Then at least one of the following holds: X∪{u} supports a Hamilton P5, or X∪{u,v} supports a Hamilton P6. Equivalently, if X+u is P5-free and the six-set X+u+v is P6-free, a physical dimer uv cannot carry both universal polarities X→uv and uv→X.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) complete-reversal antisymmetry R3; X is the displayed transitive matching-height cell; u,v outside X; both universal star families are tight.

## Scope
Pure six-vertex structural lemma. No smallest-counterexample, deletion, longest-path, R24, or completion assumptions.

## Applicability
Use when two independent endpoint/rotation arguments make one oriented dimer universally enterable from and universally exitable to a transitive no-P4 four-cell. It turns that bidirectional star packet into a P5/P6 alternative.

## Persistence
Retain X with its matching-height labels, u,v, the two universal star families, and the explicit candidate paths used in the proof. The conclusion is graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert which Hamilton path occurs. The proof uses the P5-free/P6-free negations to force three exact reversal turns.

## Proof
The matching-height rule gives the X-turns bac, czb, caz, bza tight. Since (c,u,v),(u,v,z) are tight, the candidate (b,a,c,u,v,z) would be a P6 if (a,c,u) were tight. Hence (a,c,u) is bad and R3 gives (u,c,a) tight. Since (b,u,v),(u,v,a) are tight, the candidate (c,z,b,u,v,a) would be a P6 if (z,b,u) were tight. Hence (z,b,u) is bad and R3 gives (u,b,z) tight. Now consider the five-set X+u. The candidate (b,u,c,a,z) has its last two turns (u,c,a) and (c,a,z) tight. P5-freeness therefore forces (b,u,c) bad, so R3 gives (c,u,b) tight. But then (c,u,b,z,a) is a Hamilton P5 on X+u: its turns are (c,u,b), (u,b,z), and the matching-height turn (b,z,a), all tight. Contradiction. Thus at least one of the claimed Hamilton paths exists.
