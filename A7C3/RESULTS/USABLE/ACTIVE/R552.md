# R552 — Equal P4 two-path covers amplify to a 5+3 cover

## Statement
Let G be an exact 2+2 ternary system (triangle-free (3,4)-tournament). Let A and B be vertex-disjoint tight paths of order 4. Then G[V(A)∪V(B)] contains a tight path of order 5, and the complementary 3-set spans a tight path of order 3. Consequently A∪B admits a spanning two-path cover whose longer component has order 5.

More generally, if two disjoint tight paths of common order t>=3 lie in an exact 2+2 system on 2t vertices, and t-1<=3 (i.e. t<=4), the same argument produces a spanning (t+1)+(t-1) two-path cover.

## Hypotheses
Exact 2+2 on every triple. The two paths are vertex-disjoint and each is tight. No maximality in a larger ambient graph is required; the intersecting-maxima lemma is applied inside the induced 8-vertex (or 2t-vertex) subsystem. No Strong Level-(1) hypothesis.

## Scope
Order-free combinatorial lemma for the isolated (3,4)-spanning / BM4 program. Outside the antisymmetric Strong Level-(1) regime.

## Applicability
Use as the d=0, t=4 two-path-cover exchange. It upgrades a balanced 4+4 cover to a 5+3 cover without SAT. Combined with a 5+3 Hamilton-merger it yields balanced 4+4 Hamilton merger.

## Persistence
The 5-path and complementary P3 are graph-intrinsic in the induced subsystem.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not by itself produce a Hamilton path. Does not amplify a 5+3 cover. The complement-is-a-path step fails for t>=5 because the leftover has order >=4 and need not be tight as a single path. Does not claim path-cover amplification for unequal lengths.

## Proof
Let H=G[V(A)∪V(B)]. Then H is itself an exact 2+2 system. By the source lemma (Devine–Milans, noDisjointMaxPaths): in any exact 2+2 system the family of maximum tight paths is pairwise intersecting. The same splice argument does not use global maximality except to reach a contradiction, and therefore any two disjoint tight paths of equal order t spawn a tight path of order at least t+1 in their union. Taking t=4, H contains a tight path P of order 5.

Let T=V(H)\\V(P); |T|=3. On T the exact 2+2 rule supplies four edges, hence at least one tight ordered triple. Thus T spans a tight P3. The pair P sqcup (that P3) is a spanning two-path cover of H whose longer rail has order 5.

The same complement arithmetic works whenever the leftover after a (t+1)-path on 2t vertices has size t-1<=3, i.e. only for t<=4.

(For completeness we recall the source splice. Align A=a1..at with B=b1..bt. An out-edge of A is a_j a_{j+1} b_j or a_j a_{j+1} b_{j+1}, and symmetrically for B. If an out-edge exists, choose one maximizing j and then j′∈{j,j+1}. The extremal choice plus exact 2+2 forces the neighboring cyclic mate needed to splice, producing a path of order at least t+1. If no out-edge exists either way, the alternating word a_t b_t a_{t-1} b_{t-1} ... a_1 b_1 is tight of order 2t, because a missing alternating turn would itself force an out-edge.)
