# R348 — Seven-vertex Mantel equality around a fixed trimer forces a P6

## Statement
Let P be a tight trimer support in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let X be four further vertices disjoint from P. Form the fixed-trimer bad-extension graph G_bad on X, joining x,y when P∪{x,y} does not support a tight P5. If G_bad attains the R328 Mantel maximum of four edges, equivalently G_bad is K_{2,2}, then the seven-set P∪X supports a tight P6. Thus at the first nontrivial four-outside-vertex level, exact Mantel equality is a genuine path-growth certificate even though the one-edge-below patterns are not, as witnessed by R347.

## Hypotheses
Finite SAT certificate. By R328 equality, relabel X={3,4,5,6} with bad cross edges 35,36,45,46 and good within-part pairs 34,56; relabel P={0,1,2} with (0,1,2) tight. Encode the 105 reversal pairs by Boolean turn variables and assume no P6. Add 5040 no-P6 clauses (one per ordered six-tuple), 480 bad-P5 clauses (four bad supports times 120 orders), and for each good support 120 auxiliary path witnesses with three implication clauses each plus one 120-literal existence clause; add the unit clause fixing (0,1,2). The resulting 345-variable, 6243-clause CNF is UNSAT under an independent DPLL SAT solver. An independently generated binary MILP encoding of the same constraints is also infeasible.

## Scope
Pure seven-vertex local amplification at exact R328 equality.

## Applicability
Use when a fixed trimer has four outside vertices and its bad-extension graph is Mantel-extremal. It distinguishes exact extremal structure from merely high Hamilton-five density.

## Persistence
The conclusion is graph-intrinsic on the seven vertices.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not generalize automatically to larger outside sets or near-equality graphs. Does not say high P5 density alone forces P6. Does not use smallest-counterexample or exact-cover structure.

## Proof
By R328, equality in the four-vertex bad-extension graph means that after relabeling X={3,4,5,6}, the bad pairs are exactly 35,36,45,46 and the good pairs are exactly 34,56. Relabel the fixed tight trimer as P={0,1,2} with (0,1,2) tight.

Encode boundary antisymmetry on the seven vertices by one Boolean variable for each complete-reversal pair {(a,b,c),(c,b,a)} of ordered triples of distinct vertices. There are 210 ordered triples and hence 105 such variables. Choose the lexicographically smaller ordered triple in each reversal pair as the positive literal; the reverse turn is the negative literal.

Impose absence of a tight P6 as follows. For each ordered six-tuple q=(q1,...,q6) of distinct vertices, add the clause saying that not all four consecutive turns q1q2q3, q2q3q4, q3q4q5, q4q5q6 are tight. There are 7P6=5040 such four-literal clauses.

For each bad pair xy in {35,36,45,46}, and for each of the 5!=120 orders of P∪{x,y}, add the clause saying that not all three consecutive turns of that order are tight. This contributes 4·120=480 three-literal clauses.

For each good pair xy in {34,56}, introduce one auxiliary Boolean w_q for each of the 120 orders q of P∪{x,y}. Add the three implications w_q => each of q's three consecutive turns, and add one 120-literal clause asserting that at least one w_q is true. This contributes 240 auxiliary variables, 720 implication clauses, and two existence clauses. Finally add the unit clause asserting (0,1,2) tight. The resulting formula therefore has 105+240=345 variables and 5040+480+720+2+1=6243 clauses, exactly as recorded in R348.

This CNF is UNSAT by a complete deterministic DPLL search. A reconstructible search rule is: repeatedly apply unit propagation; if no unit remains, choose an unassigned variable maximizing its occurrence count with weight 4 on literals occurring in currently shortest clauses and weight 1 on all other occurrences, breaking ties by the smaller variable index; branch False before True. Number the 105 turn variables by lexicographic order of their canonical reversal-pair representatives, then the 240 witness variables first for support P∪{3,4} and then P∪{5,6}, with the 120 permutations in lexicographic order. This exhaustive search closes every branch with contradiction; an independent rerun visited 18,393 DPLL nodes to maximum branch depth 32 and found no satisfying leaf. As a separate check, translating every clause l1∨...∨lk into the equivalent binary linear inequality sum_{positive xi} xi + sum_{negative xi}(1-xi) >= 1 gives an infeasible 345-variable binary MILP.

Hence there is no Strong Level-(1) assignment on the seven vertices having the K2,2 bad-extension pattern and no tight P6. Therefore exact Mantel equality around the fixed trimer forces a tight P6. The only mathematical dependency is R328 for the equality-to-K2,2 relabeling; all remaining work is the explicit finite exhaustive verification above.
