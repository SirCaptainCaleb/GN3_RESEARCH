# R518 — Capture-coupling trimer amplifies to P4 or an explicit double-contact cell

## Statement
Let C=(a,b,c) be any tight trimer in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let y be any fourth vertex. Then either X={a,b,c,y} supports a tight Hamilton P4, or X is one of the exact four no-P4 alignment signatures of R516. More precisely, R3 supplies a y-sign on each terminal physical dimer {a,b} and {b,c}. Compare each with the corresponding natural terminal sign of C. If either y-sign has opposite polarity to the natural sign on that physical support, R263 yields a tight Hamilton P4 on X. Hence in the no-P4 branch both y-signs have the same terminal polarities as the natural C-signs; R516 then forces both reverse contacts (b,a,y) and (y,c,b) and determines one exact twelve-turn signature indexed by the two tested-order alignment bits. By R517, signatures 11,10,01 have vertex-uniform transitive opposite-edge matching-block quotients (with the stated block permutations), while signature 00 has the vertex-uniform cyclic quotient B_1>B_3>B_2>B_1. Thus the universal four-cell residue consists of three transitive quotient cells and one uniform cyclic cell; there is no quotient-inhomogeneous fourth cell.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; C=(a,b,c) is a graph-intrinsic tight trimer; y is distinct from a,b,c; accepted/provisional R263,R515,R516,R517.

## Scope
Corrected reconstructible universal four-cell theorem. Replaces R297's false R282 quotient sentence while retaining the sound P4-or-reverse-contact/signature interface.

## Applicability
Use whenever a tight trimer acquires any fourth vertex. A P4 is the opposite-polarity branch; otherwise retain the exact R516 signature and, if useful, its R517 matching-block quotient.

## Persistence
C,y,the natural terminal signs, the two y-signs and tested orders, reverse contacts, exact R516 signature, and R517 quotient type are graph-intrinsic on X.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the four-set is current as one path-cover component. Does not attach capture ancestry. Does not rule out the cyclic 00 cell or claim any quotient type closes the conjecture.

## Proof
The tight trimer C has its two natural terminal signed dimers by R515. For the left terminal physical dimer {a,b}, boundary antisymmetry R3 on {a,b,y} with middle b supplies exactly one tight y-incidence orientation, hence one y-sign on that physical support; similarly R3 supplies a y-sign on {b,c}. If either new sign has polarity opposite to the corresponding natural C-sign, accepted R263 concatenates the two distinct sign witnesses on that same support and yields a tight Hamilton P4 on all four vertices. Therefore, if X has no tight P4, both y-signs necessarily have the same polarities as the natural terminal signs, though their tested physical-dimer orders may be aligned or reversed. These are exactly the hypotheses of R516. Hence both reverse contacts are tight and the complete Strong orientation is one of its four exact twelve-turn signatures 00,01,10,11. Applying R517 to that signature gives the corrected opposite-edge matching-block classification: 11,10,01 are vertex-uniform transitive after the stated block permutations, while 00 is vertex-uniform cyclic. This proves the complete alternative without using the false local-transitivity or quotient-inhomogeneity assertions of R280/R282/R297.
