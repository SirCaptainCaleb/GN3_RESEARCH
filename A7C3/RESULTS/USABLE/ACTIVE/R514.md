# R514 — Direct balanced pairs and mass-two floors

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Any two physically disjoint signed supports of opposite polarity form a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair in the project signed-pair sense. Suppose such a pair has total support size four. Then there is a finite chosen certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover of H or an ancestry-bearing balanced pair whose two supports are both singletons. If the initial support sizes are 1+3 or 3+1, apply R428 directly while preserving the singleton support. If they are 2+2, apply R427 to obtain either a spanning two-cover or a descendant pair with a singleton support; if the opposite support is already singleton we are at the floor, and otherwise apply R428. Any separately retained graph-intrinsic static configuration present at the pair birth remains valid historical/certificate data throughout the chosen continuation, but no old cover, sign witness, marker, or support is asserted to remain simultaneously current unless a cited descendant theorem says so.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. A graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair is present with physically disjoint nonempty supports of total size four. Any additional static configuration mentioned for ancestry is already separately certified graph-intrinsically.

## Scope
Modern reconstructible replacement for the legacy Static-Core Transparent Pair Excursion wording. It isolates the exact mass-four descent actually used downstream and expresses persistence as certificate ancestry rather than as a legacy payment macro.

## Applicability
Use whenever a direct balanced pair of total support mass four is born. The only support-size profiles are 1+3, 2+2, and 3+1, and the displayed R427/R428 case split pays them to the both-singleton floor unless H is already two-covered.

## Persistence
Retain the original pair-birth certificate and the descendant certificates supplied by R427/R428. Separately certified graph-intrinsic static facts remain historical truth. The chosen singleton in R428 is preserved exactly as stated there; unrelated old current representatives and witnesses need not persist.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the old Static-Core Transparent Pair Excursion proof has been historically recovered. Does not preserve simultaneous currentness of the birth frame. Does not make pair mass globally monotone across unrelated resets and does not close the both-singleton floor.

## Proof
Let the two nonempty disjoint supports have orders s,t with s+t=4. Opposite signed polarity is exactly the balanced-pair relation, so the given signed supports form a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair. If {s,t}={1,3}, apply accepted R428 to the singleton/nontrivial pair. Its chosen certificate-retaining continuation either gives a spanning two-cover or reaches a balanced pair with both supports singleton, preserving the chosen singleton. If s=t=2, apply accepted R427 to either width-two support. Unless a spanning two-cover occurs, R427 returns a descendant balanced pair with a singleton support. If its other support is also singleton, stop. Otherwise the other support is nontrivial, so R428 applies and gives a spanning two-cover or the both-singleton floor. This exhausts all positive integer profiles with s+t=4. The continuation is certificate-retaining by R427/R428. Any separately certified graph-intrinsic static configuration remains true as historical data while representatives change; nothing here claims that its old selected states remain current. Thus the mass-four pair has exactly the stated modern floor continuation.
