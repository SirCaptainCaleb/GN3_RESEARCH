# R2017 — The zipper loop coincidence exposes four one-seam endpoint closures

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789414880.350229`

## Statement

In the physical R2015 loop case `a_q=s=b_m`, the source spoke `s` is a singleton rail. If the other two rails are

`P=(p_0,...,p_k)` and `Q=(q_0,...,q_l)`,

then each of

`s->p_0`, `p_k->s`, `s->q_0`, `q_l->s`

is an acyclic exact-two-cover-cardinality closure candidate of transition count at most two, with at most one new seam.

Hence if no strict-descent closure succeeds, both rails are nontrivial and R3 forces the four tight reverse trimers

`(p_1,p_0,s)`, `(s,p_k,p_{k-1})`, `(q_1,q_0,s)`, `(s,q_l,q_{l-1})`.

## Proof

Let `N` be the physical tight three-forest supplied by R2015 in the loop case `a_q=s=b_m`. By zipper construction, `a_q,out` and `b_m,in` are the two distinguished free split copies. Therefore both `s,out` and `s,in` are unmatched in `N`, so `s` is an isolated singleton rail.

Attach `s` to the source or sink of either other rail. For example, `N+{s->p_0}` joins the singleton rail to the distinct rail `P`, so it cannot create a directed cycle and raises matching cardinality by one to exact two-cover cardinality. There is no new turn at `s`; the only possible new turn is `(s,p_0,p_1)` when `k>=1`. Since `tau(N)<=1` and the added edge contributes at most one transition, the candidate has transition count at most two. The same argument applies to `p_k->s`, `s->q_0`, and `q_l->s`.

If `P` were a singleton, either attachment between `s` and `p_0` would be seam-free and would immediately give a literal tight two-forest. Hence under failure of every strict-descent candidate, `k>=1`; similarly `l>=1`.

If none of the four candidates succeeds physically, cardinality and cycle failure are impossible, so each unique new seam is bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 gives the complete reversed trimer tight in each case:

- bad `(s,p_0,p_1)` gives tight `(p_1,p_0,s)`;
- bad `(p_{k-1},p_k,s)` gives tight `(s,p_k,p_{k-1})`;
- bad `(s,q_0,q_1)` gives tight `(q_1,q_0,s)`;
- bad `(q_{l-1},q_l,s)` gives tight `(s,q_l,q_{l-1})`.
