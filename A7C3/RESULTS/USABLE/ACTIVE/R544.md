# R544 — Aligned two-ended transitive trap forces a Hamilton P6

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,z} be a transitive matching-height no-P4 four-cell with M(ab)=M_R, M(ac)=M_S, M(bc)=M_L. Let L,R be two exterior vertices. Suppose the following aligned gate packet is tight: caL, cbL, Lbz, Lzb, Laz, bzR, zbR, zcR, Rca, Rba, together with the intrinsic X-turn abc. Then X union {L,R} supports a Hamilton tight P6. More precisely, assuming no P6, the five candidates (c,a,L,b,z,R), (c,a,L,z,b,R), (z,b,R,c,a,L), (z,c,R,b,a,L), (z,L,a,b,c,R) successively force bLa, zLa, cRb, Lab, Rcb tight; then (R,c,b,L,a,z) is a tight P6, contradiction.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) exact reversal antisymmetry; X transitive matching-height with displayed labels; the displayed aligned gate turns are tight.

## Scope
Pure six-vertex local closure of the aligned R24 trap.

## Applicability
Use whenever two exterior vertices carry the displayed opposite middle-gate alignment around a transitive four-cell. It removes the aligned hard six-cell without any Q-interior or deletion-cover data.

## Persistence
The conclusion is graph-intrinsic. Retain the six vertices and, if used as a forcing proof, the five exact bad seams/reverse mates in order.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not address other six-cell gate polarities.

## Proof
Assume for contradiction that no Hamilton P6 exists. Candidate C1=(c,a,L,b,z,R) has turns caL, aLb, Lbz, bzR. All except aLb are tight by hypothesis, so aLb is bad and R3 gives bLa tight. Candidate C2=(c,a,L,z,b,R) similarly has sole unknown turn aLz, hence aLz is bad and zLa is tight. Candidate C3=(z,b,R,c,a,L) has turns zbR, bRc, Rca, caL, so bRc is bad and cRb tight. Candidate C4=(z,c,R,b,a,L) now has zcR, cRb, Rba tight and sole unknown baL; therefore baL is bad and Lab tight. Candidate C5=(z,L,a,b,c,R) now has zLa, Lab, abc tight and sole unknown bcR; therefore bcR is bad and Rcb tight. Finally C6=(R,c,b,L,a,z) has turns Rcb, cbL, bLa, Laz, all tight. Hence C6 is a Hamilton P6 on the six vertices, contradiction.
