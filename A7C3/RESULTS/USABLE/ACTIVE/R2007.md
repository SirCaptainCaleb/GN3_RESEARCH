# R2007 — The two no-P4 first-open residues force earlier spectator-support escape

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789413290.258349`

## Statement

In the two exact no-P4 occupied-successor residues of R2006, acyclicity of the physical normalized pre-loss three-path forest forces loss of an earlier canonical spectator support.

- In the star-`00` residue, with `u=b_{t-1}`, `b=b_t`, `c=b_{t+1}` and selected replacement successor `c->u`, at least one of `u->b` and `b->c` is absent.
- In the hook-`11` residue, with `t=1` and selected replacement successor `b_2->b_1`, the canonical spectator support `b_1->b_2` is absent.

By R1049 and F-normalization, each such absent spectator support is explained by endpoint truncation, passage behind the `C_*` frontier, or membership in an off-`C_*` component normalized to its F-state. Thus neither R516 residue is a new local first-open blocker class.

## Proof

In the star-`00` residue, suppose both canonical spectator edges

`u->b`, `b->c`

were selected together with the replacement successor

`c->u`.

Then the physical selected graph would contain the directed cycle

`u->b->c->u`,

contradicting the hypothesis that the normalized pre-loss state is a three-path forest. Hence at least one spectator support is absent.

The star branch has `t>=2`, and the later-loss hypothesis `j>t+1` gives `t<=m-2`, so `b->c` is an internal spectator adjacency rather than the right endpoint-truncated edge. The edge `u->b` is likewise a canonical J-rail edge unless `t=2` and the unique omitted endpoint adjacency is the left edge. R1049 describes the endpoint-truncated J[B] rail, while F-normalization says every noncommon J-edge disappears only after its C_* copy passes behind the toggled frontier or when its off-C_* neutral component is placed in F-state. A common F∩J edge cannot disappear. This exhausts the support-loss mechanisms.

In the hook-`11` residue, the replacement successor is `b_2->b_1`. If `b_1->b_2` were also selected, the physical selected graph would contain the directed 2-cycle

`b_1->b_2->b_1`,

again impossible in a path forest. Hence `b_1->b_2` is absent. R1049 gives the same exhaustive alternatives: it is either the explicitly omitted endpoint adjacency of J[B], or, if it belongs to J[B], its split support is behind C_* or off C_* under normalization.

Thus the two R516 no-P4 cells immediately export to the already-classified endpoint/frontier/component topology.
