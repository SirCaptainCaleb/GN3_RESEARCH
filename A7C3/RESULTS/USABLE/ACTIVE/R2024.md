# R2024 — low-transition top-fiber covers expose a source spoke

## Statement

In the retained G13 source frame, let `G=H-{A,C}=X union B` with `X={v,p,q,r}`, and let `F` be the retained source exact two-cover in which `p,q,r` are internal. If `T` is any literal physical exact two-cover of `G` with `tau_{X|B}(T)<=2`, then at least one source spoke `s in {p,q,r}` is a physical rail endpoint of `T`.

Consequently, for `W=G-{s}`, deleting `s` from `T` leaves a literal two-cover while deleting the internal occurrence of `s` from `F` leaves a literal three-cover. Active R159 therefore gives a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair. More concretely, after choosing a selected edge of `T-s` crossing two distinct components of `F-s`, active R176 permits its opposite singleton support to be instantiated at any chosen spare vertex `d in {A,C,s}`.

This is a source-exposure/component-drop export. It is not by itself a strict source-fiber descent or global closure theorem.

## Proof

Let `b_X,b_B` be the total numbers of maximal `X`- and `B`-blocks on the two rails of `T`. Since both rails are nonempty,

`tau(T)=b_X+b_B-2`.

The retained fact that `X` has no tight Hamilton four-vertex path gives `b_X>=2`: if `b_X=1`, the unique `X`-block contains all four vertices of `X` and is such a path. Also `b_B>=1`. Hence `tau(T)<=2` leaves exactly

- `(b_X,b_B)=(2,1)`;
- `(3,1)`;
- `(2,2)`.

Accepted R429 says every exact two-cover of this pair-deleted residue has both rails nontrivial.

For `(2,1)`, the only block shape is one mixed `X-B` rail (up to direction) and one isolated `X` rail. The isolated rail is nontrivial, so at least one of its endpoints is in `{p,q,r}`.

For `(3,1)`, the shape is `X-B-X` plus an isolated `X` rail. Again the isolated nontrivial `X` rail has a source-spoke endpoint.

For `(2,2)`, the possibilities are: two mixed `X-B` rails; `X-B-X` plus an isolated `B` rail; or `B-X-B` plus an isolated `X` rail. In the first two cases there are two distinct outer `X` endpoints, at least one of which is a source spoke because only one vertex of `X` is `v`. In the third case the nontrivial isolated `X` rail has a source-spoke endpoint.

Choose such an endpoint `s`. Its `T`-rail is nontrivial, so deleting `s` leaves exactly two nonempty `T` components. Thus `T-s` is a literal two-cover of `W=G-{s}`. Since `s` is internal in `F`, deleting it splits its `F`-rail into two nonempty paths while the other `F`-rail survives, giving a literal three-cover of `W`. Active R159 applies.

For the more explicit provenance statement, some selected edge of `T-s` crosses two distinct `F-s` components, otherwise two `T-s` components could not cover all three `F-s` components. Active R176 applies to such a cross-state and explicitly allows any spare vertex outside `W`; each of `A,C,s` is spare.

## Dependencies and scope

Dependencies: retained G13 source frame and non-Hamiltonicity of `X`, accepted R429, active R159, active R176.

The balanced pair is graph-intrinsic historical information only. R444's degeneracy warning remains in force; no payment, current selection, strict descent, or closure follows from the balanced pair alone.
