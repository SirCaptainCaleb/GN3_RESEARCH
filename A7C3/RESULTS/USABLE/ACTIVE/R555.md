# R555 — Minimal six-seam pattern is uniquely the inward-facing double collision

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,z} be a transitive matching-height no-P4 four-cell with M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}. Let d be a fifth vertex with X+d P5-free. Then (I) the two turns cbd and acd cannot both be tight; and (II) the two turns dba and dac cannot both be tight. Consequently, in the hard R24 order-three frame, if among the six P-side seams bcq_0, cbq_0, acq_0, q_mab, q_mac, q_mba exactly two are bad, then the bad seams are uniquely cbq_0 and q_mba. Equivalently the unique two-bad status word is (good,bad,good | good,good,bad). In that minimum cell the two forced collision dimers are the natural tested orientations (b,c) on the left and (a,b) on the right, so both collision dimers have signed anchor b.

## Hypotheses
Transitive matching-height X; the relevant fifth-vertex extension is P5-free. The R24 consequence also uses the extreme-seam forcing R546.

## Scope
Local five-cell seam classification and its R24 six-seam corollary.

## Applicability
Use to distinguish the unique exactly-two-bad residue from all higher-badness seam patterns.

## Persistence
Pure graph-intrinsic turn information.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the exactly-two-bad pattern is realizable globally, only that it is the unique minimum status pattern if exactly two seams are bad. Does not use computation in the proof.

## Proof
Write d for the fifth vertex. We use only the matching-height turns inside X and R3.

(I) Suppose cbd and acd are both tight. Since X+d is P5-free, each displayed Hamilton candidate must fail at its only not-yet-certified turn.
1. (b,a,c,d,z): bac and acd are tight, so cdz is bad; hence zdc is tight.
2. (a,b,z,d,c): abz and zdc are tight, so bzd is bad; hence dzb is tight.
3. (a,d,z,b,c): dzb and zbc are tight, so adz is bad; hence zda is tight.
4. (z,d,a,c,b): zda and acb are tight, so dac is bad; hence cad is tight.
5. (z,c,a,d,b): zca and cad are tight, so adb is bad; hence bda is tight.
6. (z,c,b,d,a): zcb and the assumed cbd are tight, so bda must be bad, contradicting the preceding line. Thus cbd and acd cannot both be tight.

(II) Suppose dba and dac are both tight.
1. (c,d,b,a,z): dba and baz are tight, so cdb is bad; hence bdc is tight.
2. (b,d,c,a,z): bdc and caz are tight, so dca is bad; hence acd is tight.
3. (b,a,c,d,z): bac and acd are tight, so cdz is bad; hence zdc is tight.
4. (a,b,z,d,c): abz and zdc are tight, so bzd is bad; hence dzb is tight.
5. (a,d,z,b,c): dzb and zbc are tight, so adz is bad; hence zda is tight.
6. But now (z,d,a,c,b) is a Hamilton P5, using zda, the assumed dac, and acb, contradiction.

For the R24 corollary, R546 says bcq_0 and cbq_0 cannot both be good, and q_mab and q_mba cannot both be good. If exactly two of all six seams are bad, then there is exactly one bad seam in each extreme pair and the middle seams acq_0,q_mac are good. On the left, if cbq_0 were good then (I), with d=q_0, would contradict acq_0 good. Hence cbq_0 is bad and bcq_0 good. On the right, if q_mba were good then (II), with d=q_m, would contradict q_mac good. Hence q_mba is bad and q_mab good. This is the unique two-bad word. R3 then turns the two bad seams into q_0bc and abq_m, so the corresponding R543 collision orientations are (b,c) head-signed and (a,b) tail-signed; both signed anchors are b.
