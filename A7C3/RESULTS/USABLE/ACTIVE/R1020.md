# R1020 — A short ancestral arm in the anti-aligned DR cell forces a balanced-pair continuation without four-deletion exactness

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and retain the anti-aligned bridge setup of R495 in its DR branch. Thus A=(a_0,...,a_p), B=(b_0,...,b_q), P=(a_0,z_1,...,z_{m-1},b_q) with m>=3, the source cover is P sqcup A^+ sqcup B^-, and K_D=(a_1,a_0,b_q,b_{q-1}) is a tight P4. Suppose either (L) p=1 and q>=3, or (R) q=1 and p>=3. Then there is a finite certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover of H or a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair. More precisely, in (L) put y=b_{q-2}. Apply R518 to the tight core (a_1,a_0,b_q) and y. If the four-set {a_1,a_0,b_q,y} is Hamiltonian, deleting a Hamilton P4 on it leaves the literal three-path reference cover B_L sqcup (b_{q-1}) sqcup P^o, where B_L=(b_0,...,b_{q-3}); R4 supplies an exact two-cover of the same residue, R7 forces a cross-class selected state, and R176 gives the balanced pair. If the four-set is P4-free, inspect its exact R516 signature through R518. When the right alignment bit is 1, (y,a_0,b_q) is tight and combines with the DR turn (a_0,b_q,b_{q-1}) to give the tight P4 (y,a_0,b_q,b_{q-1}); deleting it leaves the three classes (a_1), B_L, P^o and the same R4/R7/R176 argument applies. When the right alignment bit is 0, the signature gives both (y,b_q,a_0) and (a_0,b_q,a_1) tight. Hence for the tight trimer (y,b_q,a_0), its reverse terminal dimer (a_0,b_q) has two same-polarity tail witnesses a_1 and b_{q-1}, the latter from the DR turn (a_0,b_q,b_{q-1}); R542 yields the stated certificate-retaining continuation. In (R) the exact mirror is explicit: put y=a_2 and apply R518 to the tight core (a_0,b_q,b_0). If its four-set is Hamiltonian, delete that P4 and use the three classes (a_1), A_R=(a_3,...,a_p), P^o. In the P4-free branch, when the left alignment bit is 1, (a_0,b_q,y) together with the DR turn (a_1,a_0,b_q) gives the P4 (a_1,a_0,b_q,y), leaving (b_0),A_R,P^o; when the left alignment bit is 0, the signature gives (y,b_q,a_0) and (b_0,a_0,b_q), so for the tight trimer (y,b_q,a_0) the reverse terminal dimer (a_0,b_q) has two same-polarity head witnesses a_1 and b_0, and R542 applies. Thus neither short-arm DR cell needs arbitrary four-deletion exactness.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; retain the exact R495 anti-aligned bridge coordinates and its DR output; either p=1,q>=3 or q=1,p>=3.

## Scope
R24-free consumer of the two short-arm cells excluded from historical R499. It replaces the historical R500 four-deletion use by an R518 P4-or-signature split and R542 in the no-P4 residue.

## Applicability
Use immediately when R495 lands in DR and exactly one ancestral arm has parameter one while the opposite arm has parameter at least three. Together with a clean R499 route for p,q>=2, this consumes every DR cell once the relevant size condition is met.

## Persistence
Retain A,B,P,P^o, the DR P4, the short-arm side, the probe vertex y, the exact R518 signature if P4-free, any deletion P4 and its three named residual source classes, or in the R542 branch the tested dimer (a_0,b_q) with its two exact witness turns. R4/R7/R176 cross-state provenance is retained literally in the P4 branches; R542 ancestry is retained in the collision branch.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not repair R478 or assert the R478 P5 currentization exists. Does not prove arbitrary four-deletion exactness, R5, or R24. In the R542 branch the conclusion is a finite paid continuation, not an immediate spanning two-cover. The theorem requires the opposite arm to have at least three steps so that the named residual long-arm class is nonempty.

## Proof
Retain the exact R495 coordinates and suppose its DR branch occurs, so

  K_D=(a_1,a_0,b_q,b_{q-1})

is a literal tight P4. Write P^o=(z_1,...,z_{m-1}). We treat the two short-arm cells separately.

LEFT-SHORT CELL: p=1 and q>=3.
Put

  a=a_1,  b=a_0,  c=b_q,  w=b_{q-1},  y=b_{q-2}.

The first three vertices of K_D give the tight trimer C=(a,b,c), and the second DR turn is

  (b,c,w) tight.                                           (L1)

Apply accepted R518 to C and the fourth vertex y.

L-HAMILTON branch. Suppose the four-set D_0={a,b,c,y} supports a Hamilton tight P4 J. Since P^o is nonempty, J is proper. By R4 the complement W=H-D_0 has an exact two-cover T. Restricting the original source cover P sqcup A^+ sqcup B^- to W leaves the three nonempty literal paths

  B_L=(b_0,...,b_{q-3}),   (w),   P^o.                     (L2)

Here B_L is nonempty because q>=3. Apply R7 to this three-class partition and T. Each class has intrinsic path-cover number one while T has two rails, so T selects at least one state crossing two distinct classes. Since W is proper, R176 upgrades that actual cross-state to a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair.

L-NO-P4 branch. Suppose D_0 is P4-free. R518 therefore places (C,y) in one of the exact R516 no-P4 signatures. Let B be the right alignment bit, namely B=1 exactly when (y,b,c) is tight.

If B=1, then

  (y,b,c) tight.                                           (L3)

Together with (L1) this makes

  J'=(y,b,c,w)

a literal tight P4. Delete V(J'). R4 gives an exact two-cover T' of its complement. The restricted source cover there is the three-path cover

  (a),   B_L,   P^o.                                      (L4)

R7 again forces a selected cross-class state of T', and R176 gives a graph-intrinsic balanced pair.

If B=0, read the exact B=0 rows of R516. They contain the two tight turns

  (y,c,b) tight,   (b,c,a) tight.                          (L5)

Thus K=(y,c,b) is a tight trimer. Its right reverse boundary dimer is the tested orientation S=(b,c). The second turn of (L5) is the tail certificate (b,c,a), while the DR turn (L1) is the tail certificate (b,c,w). The witnesses a and w are distinct and lie outside K. Hence S carries two distinct same-polarity witnesses on one reverse boundary dimer of K. Accepted R542 applies and yields a finite certificate-retaining continuation to either a spanning two-cover of H or a descendant graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair with singleton support. This proves the left-short cell.

RIGHT-SHORT CELL: q=1 and p>=3.
Now put

  a=a_0,  b=b_q=b_1,  c=b_0,  w=a_1,  y=a_2.

The last three vertices of K_D are (w,a,b,c) in the displayed DR order, so in particular

  C=(a,b,c) is tight,   (w,a,b) tight.                     (R1)

Apply R518 to C and y.

R-HAMILTON branch. If D_1={a,b,c,y} supports a Hamilton P4 J, delete J. R4 gives an exact two-cover of the complement, while the source cover restricts to the three nonempty paths

  (w),   A_R=(a_3,...,a_p),   P^o.                        (R2)

The class A_R is nonempty because p>=3. R7 and then R176 give a balanced pair exactly as above.

R-NO-P4 branch. Suppose D_1 is P4-free and let A be the left R516 alignment bit, so A=1 exactly when (a,b,y) is tight.

If A=1, then (a,b,y) is tight; with (R1) this gives the literal P4

  J'=(w,a,b,y).

Deleting it by R4 leaves the three source classes

  (c),   A_R,   P^o,                                     (R3)

and R7/R176 again produce a balanced pair.

If A=0, the exact A=0 rows of R516 contain

  (y,b,a) tight,   (c,a,b) tight.                         (R4)

Let K=(y,b,a). Its right reverse boundary dimer is S=(a,b). The DR turn (w,a,b) from (R1) is a head certificate on S with witness w, and (c,a,b) from (R4) is a second head certificate on the same tested orientation with witness c. The witnesses w and c are distinct and outside K. R542 therefore yields the same certificate-retaining continuation to a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced opposite-sign pair.

Both short-arm cells are thus consumed without any invocation of arbitrary four-deletion exactness. The only four-vertex deletions used in the proof delete an already certified Hamilton P4 and therefore invoke R4 through its prescribed-path complement clause. The P4-free residues are consumed locally by the exact R518/R516 signatures and R542. No R5, R24, R168, or R169 is used.
