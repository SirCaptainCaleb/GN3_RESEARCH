# R777 — The first-inward star and Q path satisfy raw R547 hinge geometry; the resulting bare mass-two pair is free

## Statement
STRUCTURAL CONTENT (retained from revision 1). At the hinge q_1 the frame already supplies signed tight dimers of both polarities whose supports meet in exactly one vertex: the Q turn (q_1,q_2,q_3) makes D_Q=(q_1,q_2) tail-signed by q_3; accepted R594 makes D_L=(q_1,q_0) tail-signed by any x in X; accepted R723 makes D_x=(q_1,x) head-signed by q_2 for every x in X. Since X is disjoint from V(Q), any two of these meet exactly in q_1, so the hypothesis of accepted R547 is satisfied by raw accepted tight turns, with no exact cover, deletion fiber, capture, contact, actualization, payment, or minimality. R547 then returns, for each x in X, the disjoint opposite-polarity singleton supports (q_2) tail-signed and (x) head-signed, and likewise (q_0) and (x). Dually at s=q_{m-1}, with E_Q=(d,s) head-signed by rho, E_R=(R,s) head-signed by x, E_x=(x,s) tail-signed by d, R547 returns (q_{m-2}) head-signed with (x) tail-signed, and (R) with (x).

RETRACTION. Revision 1 presented the resulting balanced pairs as a mass-two floor at depth two reached without payment, and described this as making the capture/contact production line unnecessary in this frame. That framing is withdrawn as unsupported. By the canonical dictionary, a signed support is a tight support with an exact outside sign witness, a singleton (v) is head-signed by w exactly when (w,v) is tight, and a balanced opposite-sign pair is any two physically disjoint graph-intrinsic signed supports of opposite polarity. A two-vertex path is automatically tight, so in a tournament every vertex with an in-neighbour is head-signed and every vertex with an out-neighbour is tail-signed. Graph-intrinsic mass-two balanced pairs are therefore generically free, and exhibiting one is not evidence of support descent, cut movement, or any other progress against the standing R24 criterion. The correct reading of accepted R547 is that its scarce output is the RETAINED ANCESTRY - both ancestor dimers, their witnesses, and the R425 hinge cut, carried inside a signed-payment lineage - and not the balanced pair itself.

WHAT SURVIVES. The claim is now exactly this: the q_1-pencil and its dual at q_{m-1} satisfy the R547 hinge hypothesis on accepted tight turns alone. This is a statement about where R547 is applicable in the frame. It becomes mathematically valuable only if the two ancestor dimers are already carried by a payment lineage, in which case R547 supplies an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor; establishing such lineage membership is not done here and is the actual open step.

## Proof
PART A, structural (unchanged from revision 1). (1) Q is a tight path, so (q_1,q_2,q_3) is tight and D_Q=(q_1,q_2) is tail-signed with exact witness q_3. (2) Accepted R723 gives the tight trimer (q_2,q_1,x) for every x in X, so D_x=(q_1,x) is head-signed with exact witness q_2. (3) Accepted R594 gives (q_1,q_0,x) tight, so D_L=(q_1,q_0) is tail-signed with witness any x in X. (4) X is disjoint from V(Q) in the R621 frame, so V(D_Q) cap V(D_x) = V(D_L) cap V(D_x) = {q_1} exactly. (5) The polarities are opposite, so each of (D_Q,D_x) and (D_L,D_x) satisfies the hypothesis of accepted R547 with hinge b=q_1, and R547 returns the stated disjoint opposite-sign singletons on the nonhinge endpoints, with both ancestor dimers, their witnesses, and the R425 hinge-cut ancestry retained. The dual at s=q_{m-1} is verbatim with E_Q=(d,s) head-signed by rho=q_{m-3}, E_R=(R,s) head-signed by x via accepted R594, and E_x=(x,s) tail-signed by d via accepted R723; supports meet exactly in s. No exact cover, deletion fiber, R508 crossing, R527/R542 capture, R312 contact, actualization, payment descent, or minimality hypothesis is used.

PART B, the retraction. By the canonical dictionary entry for signed support, a path P=(v_0,...,v_k) is head-signed by w when (w,v_0,...,v_k) is tight and tail-signed by z when (v_0,...,v_k,z) is tight. Specialize to k=0. A singleton (v) is head-signed by w exactly when (w,v) is tight, and tail-signed by z exactly when (v,z) is tight. A two-vertex path in a tournament is automatically tight. Hence in any Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, every vertex possessing an in-neighbour is a head-signed graph-intrinsic singleton support, and every vertex possessing an out-neighbour is a tail-signed one. By the canonical dictionary entry for balanced opposite-sign pair, such a pair is precisely two physically disjoint graph-intrinsic signed supports of opposite polarity, retained together as one pair certificate. Therefore any two distinct vertices u,v such that u has an in-neighbour and v has an out-neighbour already furnish a graph-intrinsic balanced pair of total support mass two, with no hypothesis on the frame whatsoever. Consequently the mass-two balanced pairs produced in Part A are not scarce objects, and their existence cannot witness support descent, floor movement, or progress against the standing criterion of strict physical cut movement into Q. Revision 1 asserted the contrary and is corrected here. The scarce content of accepted R547 is not its pair conclusion but its ancestry conclusion: within a hypothetical smallest-counterexample signed-payment lineage it yields an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor carrying both ancestor dimers and the hinge-cut witnesses. Part A does not place D_Q,D_L,D_x,E_Q,E_R,E_x in any such lineage, so it does not deliver that conclusion. This proves the corrected statement.
