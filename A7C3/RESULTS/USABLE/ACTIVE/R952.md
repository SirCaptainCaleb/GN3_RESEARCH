# R952 — A minimum longest-complement counterexample has support-swap persistence or two parity barriers at every exterior vertex

## Statement
Retain the setting of accepted R951: G is a minimum-order counterexample to the existential longest-complement property E, P=(p_0,...,p_m) is any globally longest increasing path, and H=G-V(P) is non-Hamiltonian with increasing path-cover number exactly two.

Fix any exterior vertex x in H. Exactly one of the following two interfaces is available.

(SUPPORT-SWAP.) There exists a position i such that replacing p_i by x in the displayed P order gives an increasing path
  P'=(p_0,...,p_{i-1},x,p_{i+1},...,p_m).
Then P' has the same order as P and is therefore globally longest in G. Its complement is
  H'=(H-{x}) union {p_i},
and H' is non-Hamiltonian. By R951 applied to P', pc_inc(H')=2 and H' admits its own hierarchical two-rail complement A'|B'. Thus every valid one-position longest-support swap remains inside the minimum-counterexample state; if any such H' were Hamiltonian, G would satisfy E.

(PARITY BARRIER.) No one-position substitution p_i->x preserves the displayed increasing order. Then accepted R950 applies and x has two canonical parity replacement barriers rho_0(x),rho_1(x), with both outer substitution inequalities feasible at each barrier and the middle comparison reversed. For two exterior vertices x,y in this barrier branch, strict separation rho_r(x)<rho_r(y) on one parity supplies the actual increasing turn (x,p_{rho_r(x)+1},y).

Hence the local support-exchange problem in a minimum counterexample has no third state: a vertex either generates another globally longest support with another exact path-cover-two complement, or it carries the full two-parity replacement obstruction.

## Hypotheses
Pure finite edge-ordered complete graph. G is a minimum counterexample to property E from R951. P is any globally longest increasing path. x lies outside P. The barrier clause uses accepted R950 when all literal one-position substitutions fail.

## Scope
Independent-safe synthesis of accepted R951 and R950. It is a support-exchange interface for the existential longest-complement program, not a proof of the conjecture.

## Applicability
Use after the R951 three-rail reduction. Support swaps may be iterated only as actual globally longest displayed-order substitutions; after every swap reselect the R951 complement rails for the new support. If a chosen exterior vertex admits no swap, retain both parity barriers instead of treating nonreplacement as featureless failure. A future proof needs an extremal objective on the longest-support exchange graph or a consumer of the parity-barrier population.

## Persistence
Retain G, the actual order P, x, every successful replacement position and resulting support/complement, or both rho parity barriers and their outer/middle inequalities. After a swap retain a fresh R951 hierarchical cover of the new complement; do not transport the old A|B orders automatically.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say a support swap improves any monotone objective. Does not say every exterior vertex is replaceable. Does not synchronize the path-cover-two complements of different longest supports. Does not prove property E or R888.

## Proof
Retain G,P,H and x as in the statement.

If there exists i such that replacing p_i by x in the displayed P order is increasing, call the resulting path P'. It has exactly |P| vertices. Since P is globally longest, no increasing path has more than |P| vertices, so P' is also globally longest. Its vertex complement is exactly H'=(H-{x}) union {p_i}. Because G is a counterexample to property E, the complement of every globally longest path is non-Hamiltonian. Hence H' is non-Hamiltonian. Accepted R951 applies to the arbitrary globally longest path P': its complement H' has increasing path-cover number exactly two and admits a hierarchical decomposition A'|B' with A' globally longest in H' and B' Hamilton on the remainder. This is SUPPORT-SWAP.

If no such i exists, x satisfies exactly the hypothesis of accepted R950 with respect to the displayed increasing path P. Therefore both parity-indexed canonical barriers rho_0(x),rho_1(x) exist, are internal, have both outer substitution conditions feasible, and have the middle edge inequality reversed. For two such nonreplaceable vertices x,y, accepted R950 also gives the stated same-parity cross turn whenever rho_r(x)<rho_r(y). This is PARITY BARRIER.

The two cases are exhaustive by whether at least one displayed-order one-position substitution exists. This proves R952.
