# R413 — Endpoint-universal pair-deletion partitions glue globally unless a cross-state balanced pair is born

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample of order n>=11 and assume the endpoint-universal branch of accepted R393: for every physical pair D={a,c} and every x outside D, some exact two-cover of H-D exposes x as a rail endpoint. Then either H has a spanning two-cover, or H contains a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair arising from the R176 cross-state comparison of two exact two-covers of one common proper residue. More structurally, if no such R176 pair occurs in any comparison used below, then for every pair D all exact two-covers of H-D induce one canonical unordered rail-support partition pi_D, these pair-deletion partitions agree on every triple overlap, and they glue to one global bipartition V(H)=A sqcup B such that every exact two-cover of H-D has rail supports A\\D and B\\D. The pair-deletion rail floor then forces |A|,|B|>=3. Deleting two vertices of A gives an exact cover whose untouched B-rail is a Hamilton tight path on all of B; deleting two vertices of B similarly gives a Hamilton tight path on all of A. Those two full-block Hamilton paths form a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Hence the endpoint-universal branch cannot persist without producing an R176 common-shadow cross-state balanced pair.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample with n>=11; accepted endpoint universality R393, pair-deletion rail floor R168, cross-state upgrade R176, and partition rigidity R410.

## Scope
Global gluing theorem for the endpoint-universal branch. No capture, first-contact, density, or longest-path hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use as the preferred global consumer of endpoint universality. It replaces arbitrary endpoint-return multiplicity by either an R176 cross-state balanced pair with common-shadow provenance or an impossible global two-block decomposition whose blocks are both Hamiltonian.

## Persistence
The canonical partitions pi_D and global blocks A,B are conclusions only in the no-R176-pair branch. The R176 balanced pair is graph-intrinsic historical information. The Hamilton paths on A and B used at the end may come from different pair-deletion representatives, which is harmless because their supports are disjoint and graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the R176 balanced pair itself closes the conjecture or preserves a prescribed puncture. Does not assert canonical path orders within A or B. The gluing uses endpoint universality to obtain common triple-deletion covers and fails without that hypothesis.

## Proof
Assume the endpoint-universal property explicitly stated in R413, and assume no comparison produces the balanced pair supplied by R410 through its R176 mechanism. For a fixed pair D, R410 implies that all exact two-covers of H-D have one canonical unordered support partition pi_D; otherwise R410 already gives the desired pair. Now take distinct a,c,d. Endpoint universality for {a,c} gives an exact two-cover of H-{a,c} exposing d at a rail endpoint. The d-rail cannot be the singleton (d): if it were, the other rail would be Hamiltonian on H-{a,c,d}, while R3 supplies a tight trimer on {a,c,d}; those two disjoint paths would span H. Hence deleting endpoint d leaves two nonempty paths and therefore an exact two-cover T1 of H-{a,c,d}. Similarly endpoint universality for {a,d}, followed by trimming c, gives an exact two-cover T2 of the same residue. R410 applied to T1,T2 says either the desired balanced pair occurs or their support partitions agree. Thus pi_{a,c} and pi_{a,d}, restricted to the common triple-deletion residue, agree up to exchange. Define u~v when, for any deletion pair D disjoint from {u,v}, u and v lie in the same block of pi_D. The preceding overlap identity makes this well defined because the two-subset graph of V(H)-{u,v} is connected by single-vertex exchanges. Transitivity is checked inside one pi_D disjoint from any tested triple. There are exactly two equivalence classes A,B: at least two because every pi_D has two nonempty blocks, and at most two because any three vertices survive some pi_D, which has only two blocks. Therefore pi_D={A-D,B-D} for every pair D. Since exact two-covers have two nonempty rails, |A| and |B| are at least three: if a class had size one or two, choose a deletion pair containing that entire class, making its corresponding block empty. Choose distinct a1,a2 in A. Endpoint universality ensures an exact cover of H-{a1,a2}; its supports are A-{a1,a2} and B, so B itself has a Hamilton tight path. Likewise deleting two vertices of B gives a Hamilton tight path on all of A. The two full-block paths are disjoint and together span H, contradiction. Hence endpoint universality yields either the R410/R176 balanced pair or closure. Direct dependencies are only R3 and R410. R168, R393, R5, and R24 are not used.
