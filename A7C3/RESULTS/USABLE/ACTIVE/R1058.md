# R1058 — First-open spectator detour gives an exact two-seam interception interface

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789410528.626369`

## Statement

Work in the audited v-rooted sharp cell and the non-loop OUT-`v` literal first-loss branch. Let the actual first-loss index be `r`, with

`e_r=v->b_j`,

let `s∈{p,q,r}` be the fixed far source-spoke endpoint from R1051, and let `t+1` be the first R1033-open spectator position. Put

`c=b_{t+1}`

and assume the putative first loss occurs later, `j>t+1`.

If both spectator incidences

`b_t->c`, `c->b_{t+2}`

survive in the normalized pre-loss prefix `hat M_{r-1}`, then

`K=(hat M_{r-1}-{b_t->c})+{v->c,b_t->s}`

has exact two-cover matching cardinality and

`tau(K)=tau(F)`.

There is no new seam at `v`, while the new seam at `c` is exactly the R1033 first-open turn `(v,c,b_{t+2})` and is tight. The only remaining physical obligations are the endpoint seam at `b_t`, the endpoint seam at `s` when defined, and the possibility that the reconnection closes a selected rail into a directed cycle.

If either spectator incidence at `c` fails to survive, the failure is exhausted by the endpoint-truncated / behind-frontier / off-`C_*` split-copy alternatives. If specifically `c,out` is already behind the frontier, then at its unique earlier defect prefix the direct source-gated closure `c->s` has exact two-cover matching cardinality and transition count at most two.

Thus first-open availability is reduced to an explicit physical two-seam interception candidate, an earlier matching-level strict-descent closure, or an exact split-copy support residue.

## Proof

R1037 gives `d_F(v)=1`. In the OUT-`v` branch the unique old-F incidence at `v` is the outgoing edge `v->b_j`, so F has no selected edge entering `v`. The split copy `v,in` cannot be an internal IN-copy of the alternating component `C_*`, because every such IN-copy carries its paired old-F edge; it is also not the far IN endpoint, which is `s,in` by R1051. Every off-`C_*` neutral component is placed in its F-state by the normalized-prefix convention. Hence `v,in` remains unmatched in every normalized prefix.

Immediately before the first loss, the moving free copy is `v,out` and the fixed far free copy is `s,in`. R1051 identifies the preceding closure as

`g_{r-1}=v->s`,

which is X-X and therefore same-side. D17.421 says the preceding normalized closure lies exactly two transitions below the old floor. Since `g_{r-1}` contributes zero transitions,

`tau(hat M_{r-1})=tau(F)-2`.

Assume `b_t->c` survives. Delete this B-B edge and add the disjoint edges `v->c` and `b_t->s`. Net matching cardinality increases by one, from the spanning three-forest cardinality to the spanning two-cover cardinality. The deleted edge is same-side and both added edges cross X|B, so

`tau(K)=tau(hat M_{r-1})+2=tau(F)`.

Because `v,in` is free, adding `v->c` creates no turn at `v`. If the successor edge `c->b_{t+2}` also survives, the only new turn at `c` is

`(v,c,b_{t+2})`.

By R1050 and the definition of the R1033 first-open position, its comparison is exactly

`F_{t+1}->E_{t+1}`,

so this seam is tight. No stronger physical conclusion is automatic: the seams at `b_t` and `s`, and same-rail cycle incidence, remain genuine obligations.

For the failure classification, R1049 says `J[B]` is the displayed spectator path with one endpoint adjacency omitted. Each relevant spectator incidence is therefore either absent for that endpoint-truncation reason, common in `F∩J`, a `J\F` edge on `C_*`, or a `J\F` edge on an off-`C_*` component. Common incidences survive every normalized prefix; on-`C_*` incidences survive exactly while they remain ahead of the toggled frontier; off-`C_*` `J\F` incidences disappear under F-normalization. Hence endpoint truncation, behind-frontier status, and off-component normalization exhaust support loss.

If `c,out` is behind the frontier, let `q<r` be its unique earlier alternating-component position. At `hat M_q`, the two free copies are `c,out` and `s,in`; adding `c->s` restores exact two-cover matching cardinality. Because `q` is before the first-positive frontier, the normalized prefix has transition count at most one, and the cross edge `c->s` raises it by one. Thus the closure has transition count at most two. As in R1052, this is a matching-level descent attempt until its endpoint seams and acyclicity are checked.
