# R573 — Unique-crossing Hamilton ending forces a reverse tail on the selected dimer

## Statement

Let H be finite with pc(H)>2. Let D be proper, write W=V(H)\\D=S disjoint-union C with S,C nonempty, and suppose X=H[D union S] admits a tight Hamilton path. Let T be a literal exact two-cover of H-D with exactly one selected S|C transition.

(CS) Suppose the unique transition is the selected state x y with x in S and y in C. Then T has exactly one mixed rail, whose C-block starts at y, and the other rail is monochromatic. In particular C is covered by either one path starting at y or two paths, one of which starts at y.
If X has a tight Hamilton path ending with the ordered state (p,x), the turn (p,x,y) is bad. Hence (y,x,p) is tight by R3: the tested crossing dimer (y,x) is tail-signed by every such Hamilton predecessor p. If two such Hamilton paths exist with distinct penultimate vertices p_1,p_2, then R523 supplies a same-oriented two-tail witness collision on the actual selected crossing dimer (y,x).

(SC) Dually, if the unique transition is y x with y in C and x in S, and X has a tight Hamilton path starting with (x,p), then (p,x,y) is tight and the tested dimer (x,y) is head-signed by every such Hamilton successor p. Distinct successors force a same-oriented two-head R523 collision on (x,y).

No Hamilton path of X need end (resp. start) at x. The family may be empty.


## Hypotheses

H finite, pc(H)>2. D proper, S and C nonempty, X=H[D union S] Hamiltonian, T an exact two-cover of H-D with tau(T)=1. Tight/bad complementary by R3. The two-witness clause uses accepted R523.


## Scope

Order-free exact-two-cover unique-transition theorem. It does not treat k>2 covers, does not force endpoint accessibility of x on X, and does not require D union C to be Hamiltonian.


## Applicability

In any tau=1 exact two-cover cell with a certified Hamilton absorber D union S, test whether X has a Hamilton path ending (CS) or starting (SC) at the S-endpoint of the unique crossing. Each such path contributes a named reverse turn on the selected dimer. Two distinct neighbors trigger R523.


## Persistence

Retain D,S,C,T, the unique crossing state, the absorber X, every Hamilton path of X used, the displayed reverse turns, and any R523 collision. Selected-state roles remain representative-relative to T.


## Exclusions / nonclaims

Does not assert that a Hamilton path of X ends or starts at x. Does not force a second Hamilton order of X. Does not make D union C Hamiltonian. Does not treat k>2. Does not depend on pending R560 or R566-R570.


## Proof

Work first in CS: unique selected state xy, x in S, y in C. Since T has two rails and exactly one S|C adjacency, exactly one rail is mixed and the other is monochromatic. The mixed rail traverses an S-block, then y, then a (possibly trivial) C-block. Thus one C-block of T starts at y. The other rail is either pure S, in which case b_C=1 and C is a single path starting at y, or pure C, in which case b_C=2.

Let Q be a tight Hamilton path of X ending (p,x). Suppose for contradiction that (p,x,y) is tight. Concatenate Q with the C-block of T that starts at y to obtain a tight path Q+ covering D union S union that C-block.
If b_C=1 then Q+ spans V(H), contradicting pc(H)>2.
If b_C=2 then the remaining rail of T is a pure C-path disjoint from Q+, and {Q+, that rail} is a literal two-cover of H, contradicting pc(H)>2.
Hence (p,x,y) is bad, so (y,x,p) is tight by R3.
If p_1 and p_2 are distinct such predecessors, the two tight turns (y,x,p_1) and (y,x,p_2) are same-oriented two-tail certificates on the physical dimer (y,x). R523 yields the stated collision.

The SC case is symmetric: the mixed rail ends with an S-block starting at x, the relevant join is at the front of Q, and the forced tight turn is (p,x,y).
Existence of Q ending or starting at x is an extra hypothesis on each application, not a conclusion.
