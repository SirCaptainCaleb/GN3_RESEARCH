# R1043 — Cross-distance localization for two outside insertions

**Status:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Source:** G39 workspace localization root

## Statement

Let `B=(b_1,...,b_m)` be tight, and let `I_B(x)` be the set of gaps in the displayed B-order where one-vertex insertion of an outside vertex `x` yields a tight Hamilton word on `B∪{x}`. If `H[B∪{x,y}]` is nonHamiltonian, then

`|p-q| ≤ 1`

for every `p∈I_B(x)` and `q∈I_B(y)`.

Consequently, in the live G39 cell, if every `H[B∪{v,s}]` is nonHamiltonian and `I_B(v)≠∅`, then every companion insertion set satisfies

`I_B(s) ⊆ W_v := ⋂_{p∈I_B(v)} ({p-1,p,p+1}∩{0,...,m}).`

If the span of `I_B(v)` is `0,1,2`, then `W_v` has at most `3,2,1` gaps respectively; if the span is at least `3`, then `W_v=∅`.

The word "at most" is necessary at endpoint gaps: span zero need not produce three available gaps.

## Proof

Index the gaps of `B` by `0,...,m`, with the natural endpoint conventions. Suppose `p<q`, `p∈I_B(x)`, `q∈I_B(y)`, and `q-p≥2`.

Insert `x` into gap `p` and `y` into gap `q` simultaneously, preserving the displayed order of all B-vertices. Because the two gaps are separated by at least one untouched B-edge, no consecutive triple of the resulting Hamilton word contains both `x` and `y`. Every consecutive triple is therefore either an old tight B-triple, a triple already certified by the tight one-vertex insertion of `x`, or a triple already certified by the tight one-vertex insertion of `y`. Hence the simultaneous word is tight, contradicting nonHamiltonicity of `H[B∪{x,y}]`.

The symmetric case gives `|p-q|≤1` for all cross-pairs of legal insertion gaps.

Now fix `v`. For any companion `s`, every `q∈I_B(s)` must lie within distance one of every `p∈I_B(v)`, giving the displayed intersection formula for `W_v`.

Let `a=min I_B(v)` and `a+d=max I_B(v)`. Then

`W_v ⊆ [a+d-1,a+1]∩{0,...,m}`.

Thus `d=0,1,2` leaves at most `3,2,1` integer gaps respectively, and `d≥3` leaves none.

## Scope

This is a displayed-B-order insertion localization theorem. Full nonHamiltonicity implies the stated obstruction, but the theorem does not claim that failure of the displayed-order insertion family is sufficient for full nonHamiltonicity. No interval structure inside an individual insertion set is assumed or concluded.
