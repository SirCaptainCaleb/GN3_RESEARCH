# R564 — Right-split order-1 dichotomy and the middle-insertion candidate

## Statement
Let G be an exact 2+2 ternary system, let P=(v0,...,v{m-1}) be a tight path with m>=3, and let x be outside P. Assume Q=(v0,...,v{m-2},x) is also tight. Then either (v0,...,v{m-1},x) is a spanning tight path, or both cyclic mates (v{m-1},x,v{m-2}) and (x,v{m-2},v{m-1}) are tight. If in the latter branch the additional turn (v{m-3},x,v{m-2}) is tight, then (v0,...,v{m-3},x,v{m-2},v{m-1}) is a spanning tight path. The only unresolved cell is the simultaneous failure of (v{m-2},v{m-1},x) and (v{m-3},x,v{m-2}).

## Hypotheses
Exact 2+2. P and Q tight as displayed. Isolated (3,4) model.

## Scope
Order-free seam lemma for BM4 split-origin caps of order 1. Outside the antisymmetric regime.

## Applicability
Use on a right split whose short rail has order 1. Does not close the residual cell.

## Persistence
The listed tight turns are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove the residual cell is Hamiltonian. Does not prove global insertion. Does not revive general endpoint replacement.

## Proof
The last pair of P is (v{m-2}, v{m-1}). Exact 2+2 implies the prepend/append alternative on this triple. If (v{m-2}, v{m-1}, x) is tight, then every consecutive triple of (v0,...,v{m-1},x) is a triple of P or this last triple, so P followed by x spans. If (v{m-2}, v{m-1}, x) is missing, then among the three cyclic orders on {v{m-2},v{m-1},x} the other two, (v{m-1},x,v{m-2}) and (x,v{m-2},v{m-1}), are tight because exactly two cyclic orders are present. If in addition (v{m-3},x,v{m-2}) is tight, then (v0,...,v{m-3},x,v{m-2},v{m-1}) has exactly the two new tight turns (v{m-3},x,v{m-2}) and (x,v{m-2},v{m-1}), hence spans. The sole unresolved cell is simultaneous failure of (v{m-2},v{m-1},x) and (v{m-3},x,v{m-2}).
