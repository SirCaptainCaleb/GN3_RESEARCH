# R982 — Maximum increasing-path covers are connected by indecomposable collision exchanges

## Statement
Let G be a finite edge-ordered graph and suppose its minimum increasing path-cover number is k. Let F and F' be two spanning increasing k-path covers, encoded by their directed bipartite matchings M,M' of size |V(G)|-k. Apply the collision-poset construction of R980 to any such pair. Because k is minimum, every feasible upper-set recombination has collision weight at most zero.

(1) ZERO-WEIGHT LATTICE. The zero-weight upper sets form a distributive lattice under union and intersection. Every such upper set gives another spanning increasing k-path cover.

(2) INDECOMPOSABLE EXCHANGE CHAIN. Call a pair R980-indecomposable when its only zero-weight upper sets are the empty set and the whole symmetric-difference component set. Then any two spanning increasing k-path covers can be joined by a finite chain F=F_0,F_1,...,F_m=F' of spanning increasing k-path covers such that each adjacent pair is R980-indecomposable. Moreover the chain may be obtained recursively so that each decomposition step strictly decreases the number of selected edges in the relevant symmetric difference.

(3) STRICT GAIN-LOSS EXPANSION. For an R980-indecomposable adjacent pair, let P be its +1 alternating components and N its -1 alternating components. Then |P|=|N|. If P is nonempty, define a bipartite reachability graph from P to N by p~n iff p is below n in the collision preorder. This graph satisfies strict Hall expansion: for every nonempty proper S subset P, |N(S)|>=|S|+1, and N(P)=N. Hence it has a perfect matching from gains to reachable losses. In particular, for k=3 one has |P|<=3; if |P|=2 then each gain reaches both losses, and if |P|=3 then each gain reaches at least two losses and every pair of gains collectively reaches all three losses.

(4) SAME-SINGLETON SPECIALIZATION. If k=3 and both covers contain the same physical singleton z, then z_out and z_in are common unmatched copies, so |P|=|N|<=2. Every zero-weight recombination also retains z as a singleton. Thus any two exact two-covers of G-z, after adjoining z, can be connected through R980-indecomposable exact two-covers of the same deletion fiber; every nonzero elementary same-fiber transition has either one gain reaching its loss or two gains each reaching both losses.

All statements are conditional on the validity of R980 and do not assert that an improving positive upper set exists.

## Hypotheses
Finite edge-ordered graph. R980 collision-poset theorem. For the same-singleton clause, both spanning k=3 covers have the same singleton z.

## Scope
Pure family-level consequence of R980. Converts pairwise nonimprovement into a connected elementary exchange theory and strict gain-loss reachability.

## Applicability
Use in a hypothetical R888 counterexample after adjoining omitted vertices to singleton-deletion two-covers. Replace arbitrary pairwise collision flows by chains of indecomposable maximum-cover moves. In one deletion fiber, elementary moves have at most two gains, with the two-gain case fully cross-linked to both losses.

## Persistence
Retain the two endpoint covers, the zero-weight upper-set lattice, each recursively produced intermediate maximum cover, and for each indecomposable adjacent pair its collision preorder, gain/loss component sets, and reachability graph. For same-fiber use retain the common singleton.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove R888, does not force any positive-weight upper set, and does not assert compatibility of gain-loss routings across different indecomposable moves. Does not promote R980; this theorem remains pending while R980 is pending.

## Proof
Let F_0,F_1 be spanning increasing k-path covers in a graph whose minimum increasing path-cover number is k. Let C be the R980 symmetric-difference component set, let delta:C->{-1,0,1} be the component weights, and let <= be the R980 collision preorder. For an upper set W put w(W)=sum_{C in W}delta(C). By R980, W compiles to a spanning increasing cover with k-w(W) components. Since k is minimum, w(W)<=0 for every upper set W. Also w(empty)=w(C)=0.

1. ZERO-WEIGHT LATTICE.
Let U,V be zero-weight upper sets. Upper sets are closed under union and intersection. Weight is modular:
  w(U union V)+w(U intersect V)=w(U)+w(V)=0.
Both terms on the left are <=0 by minimum-cover nonimprovement. Hence both are zero. Thus the zero-weight upper sets are closed under union/intersection and form a distributive lattice. R980 gives a spanning increasing k-cover for every member.

2. RECURSIVE INDECOMPOSABLE CHAIN.
If the pair F_0,F_1 has no proper nonempty zero-weight upper set, it is indecomposable and there is nothing to do. Otherwise choose such a W and let H be its R980 recombination. Then H is another k-cover. Moreover M(F_0) triangle M(H) consists exactly of the changed edges in symmetric-difference components belonging to W, while M(H) triangle M(F_1) consists exactly of those in the complementary components. Since W is nonempty and proper, both symmetric differences have strictly fewer selected edges than M(F_0) triangle M(F_1). Apply induction on symmetric-difference size to F_0,H and H,F_1 and concatenate the resulting chains. This terminates and proves the indecomposable exchange-chain statement.

3. STRICT HALL EXPANSION IN AN INDECOMPOSABLE PAIR.
Now assume F_0,F_1 is indecomposable. If a proper nonempty upper set U existed with w(U)=0 it would contradict indecomposability, while positive weight is impossible. Therefore every proper nonempty upper set has w(U)<0.

Let P be the +1 components and N the -1 components. Since total weight is zero, |P|=|N|=:g. For S subseteq P let Up(S) be its upward closure and let N(S)=N intersect Up(S). Every positive component lying in Up(S) contributes +1, every negative component there belongs to N(S), and zero components contribute nothing.

First take nonempty proper S subset P. If Up(S) is the whole component set, then N(S)=N and |N(S)|=g>|S|. If Up(S) is proper, indecomposability gives
  0>w(Up(S))=|P intersect Up(S)|-|N(S)|.
Since S subseteq P intersect Up(S),
  |N(S)|>|P intersect Up(S)|>=|S|,
so |N(S)|>=|S|+1.

For S=P, if Up(P) were proper then the same inequality would give |N(P)|>|P|=g, impossible because N has only g members. Hence Up(P) is the whole component set and N(P)=N. This proves strict Hall expansion and in particular ordinary Hall, so a perfect matching from P to reachable N exists.

For k=3, each directed matching has exactly six unmatched bipartite copies. A +1 alternating path consumes two copies unmatched in F_0 but matched in F_1, so g<=3. If g=2, strict expansion for a one-element S forces both losses into its neighborhood; hence each gain reaches both losses. If g=3, each singleton gain reaches at least two losses, and each two-gain set reaches all three.

4. SAME SINGLETON.
Suppose k=3 and both covers contain the same singleton z. Then z_out and z_in are unmatched in both matchings and are not incident with the symmetric difference. Thus among the six unmatched copies of each matching, at least two are common. Therefore at most four unmatched copies can be exclusive to either matching. Each +1 component uses two F_0-exclusive unmatched copies, so g<=2 (and similarly for losses).

Any R980 recombination changes only symmetric-difference edges. Since neither copy of z is incident with such an edge, both remain unmatched and z remains a singleton. A zero-weight recombination is therefore a spanning three-cover retaining z singleton; deleting z leaves an exact two-cover of G-z. Applying the recursive construction entirely within this pair preserves z at every intermediate step. The strict Hall conclusions then specialize to the one-gain or fully cross-linked two-gain alternatives.

This proves all clauses of R982, conditional on R980.
