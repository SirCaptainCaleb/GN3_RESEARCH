# R483 — Reverse-Ear output existence is path-triple ubiquitous

## Statement
Let H be any Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, let K be a vertex-simple tight path, and let T={a,b,c} be any three distinct vertices occurring on K. For each m in T, let J_m be the unique tight trimer on T having middle m. Then at least two of the three comparisons of K with J_m necessarily satisfy the reverse-order hypothesis of R435 and hence yield an R435 output: an exact reversal of an old state, a tight reverse trimer, or a vertex-simple proper tight cycle. Indeed K induces one linear order on the three contacts T and therefore has one unique middle vertex m_K. For the contacts of J_m to occur in increasing J_m-order along K, the literal order of J_m must equal the order induced by K, which is possible only when m=m_K. Hence at most one of J_a,J_b,J_c is order-aligned with K; for each other J_m, the sequence of its three contacts along K has an inversion, and some consecutive K-contacts form a reverse-order pair to which R435 applies. Consequently bare existence of an R435 order-change output is ubiquitous and is not by itself a progress alternative. A useful R435 descendant must retain a non-generic coordinate such as a prescribed cap edge, source adjacency, deletion-fiber role, or a downstream physical proposal that consumes the particular reversal.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); K is a vertex-simple tight path containing three distinct vertices T; accepted R3 and R435.

## Scope
Order-free proof-design fence and local theorem. It concerns bare R435-output existence, not the value of a specifically localized reversal/reverse-trimer/cycle.

## Applicability
Use when evaluating a claimed advance whose terminal output is merely some Reverse-Ear reversal, reverse trimer, or proper cycle. Require a role-specific path comparison or downstream consumer; otherwise the output is free around any tight path containing three selected vertices.

## Persistence
The chosen path K, triple T, three middle-labelled tight trimers, and each resulting R435 certificate are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say R435 is useless. Exact reversal of a named cap/source state, a reverse trimer carrying prescribed coordinates, or a cycle entering a specific consumer may be highly productive. Does not assert all three middle-labelled comparisons pay, only at least two.

## Proof
Read the three vertices T in their order along K, say p<q<r. Any three-vertex path whose contacts are increasing along K must have literal order (p,q,r), so its middle vertex is q. Among the three middle-labelled tight trimers J_a,J_b,J_c, only J_q has middle q. Therefore J_m for each m in T\\{q} cannot have all three contacts increasing along K. For each such J_m, list its three vertices in the order encountered along K. Since that permutation is not increasing in J_m-order, it has a descent between two consecutive entries of the K-contact sequence. Those two vertices are consecutive K-contacts with J_m and occur in reverse J_m-order, so R435 applies to the K-subpath between them. Thus at least the two m≠q comparisons produce an exact reversed state, reverse trimer, or proper cycle.
