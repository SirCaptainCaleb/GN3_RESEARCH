# R843 — A bidirectional same-dimer P4 pair eliminates the R833 chord and anti-end residues

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2. Let p,q,x,y be four distinct vertices such that both K=(x,p,q,y) and K^*=(y,p,q,x) are tight P4s. Put D={p,q}, C=V(H)\\{x,p,q,y}, and let T=T_1 sqcup T_2 be a literal exact two-cover of H-D with both rails nontrivial. Then either b_T(x,y)>2, or T forces a source-visible reverse seam at x or y. More explicitly, if b_T(x,y)=2, at least one of the following occurs for some c in C: T selects c x and (p,x,c) is tight; T selects y c and (c,y,q) is tight; T selects c y and (p,y,c) is tight; or T selects x c and (c,x,q) is tight. Thus the REVERSE-CHORD and ANTI-ENDS residues of R833 cannot jointly survive the two opposite endpoint orders on the same middle dimer.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1), pc(H)>2; K=(x,p,q,y) and K^*=(y,p,q,x) are both tight; T is one exact two-cover of H-{p,q} with both rails nontrivial.

## Scope
Order-free fixed-cover strengthening of accepted R833. No smallest-counterexample minimality is needed once T is supplied. No capture, payment, Reverse Ear, path growth, or computation.

## Applicability
Use whenever a signed dimer has two distinct witnesses x,y in both endpoint roles, producing both P4 orders x-D-y and y-D-x. In the crossed m=6 R583 terminal-gap analysis, any two right-terminal blockers (s,d,x),(s,d,y), together with accepted R723 head signs (x,s,d),(y,s,d), give exactly this bidirectional pair on D=(s,d); hence the entire right-terminal two-witness branch becomes HIGH-BLOCK or a physical reverse seam in one exact H-{s,d} cover. The left terminal branch has the same consequence when its two witnesses are b,z, using accepted R658 tail signs on (w,v).

## Persistence
Retain both P4 orders, the common physical middle dimer D, the single exact cover T, b_T(x,y), and the selected state plus reverse seam if the two-block case occurs.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim b_T(x,y)>2 is contradictory or that the forced reverse seam closes H. Does not identify which of the four reverse-seam forms occurs. Does not compare different exact covers.

## Proof
The quantity b_T(x,y) depends only on the physical endpoint pair {x,y} and the fixed cover T, so it is identical for K=(x,p,q,y) and K^*=(y,p,q,x). If b_T(x,y)>2 we are done. Assume b_T(x,y)=2 and apply accepted R833 first to K. If X-REVERSE or Y-REVERSE occurs, one of the first two displayed reverse seams is obtained. Hence suppose neither occurs. R833 then leaves only REVERSE-CHORD, in which T selects the directed state y x, or ANTI-ENDS, in which x,y are nonadjacent degree-one T-vertices with x first on its rail and y last on its rail. Apply R833 again to K^*. If one of its reverse-seam alternatives occurs, we obtain one of the last two displayed forms. Otherwise K^* leaves only its REVERSE-CHORD, the directed state x y, or its ANTI-ENDS, with y first and x last. These residual choices cannot coexist. T is a literal path cover with fixed rail orientations, so it cannot simultaneously select both directed states y x and x y. A chord residue from one application cannot coexist with ANTI-ENDS from the other, because ANTI-ENDS explicitly makes x and y nonadjacent. Finally, if both applications take ANTI-ENDS, the first makes x a first endpoint and y a last endpoint, while the second makes y a first endpoint and x a last endpoint. If x,y lie on the same rail this reverses the two fixed endpoint roles and is impossible. If they lie on different rails, each of x and y would have to be both the first and last vertex of its own rail; since both T rails are nontrivial by hypothesis, that is impossible as well. Therefore at least one R833 application must produce a reverse seam whenever b_T(x,y)=2. This proves the lemma.
