# R884 — A tight cross-end turn compiles directly to a two-ended deep seam packet

## Statement
Retain the accepted synchronized transitive R594 frame in the surviving long regime m>=6. Write L=q_0,u=q_1,v=q_2,d=q_{m-2},s=q_{m-1},R=q_m. Fix e in X such that (L,e,R) is tight. Let gamma be the M_R-partner of e and let {alpha,beta} be the other M_R edge, so X-{e}={alpha,beta,gamma}. Then J=(u,L,e,R,s) is a tight P5, and with T_1=(beta,alpha,gamma), T_2=(alpha,beta,gamma), each T_i together with Q[2,m-2] is a literal exact two-cover of the residue W=H-V(J). The residue is non-Hamiltonian. Moreover the following graph-intrinsic seam packet holds simultaneously. (LEFT) Either (L,u,gamma) is tight, or both (v,u,gamma,alpha) and (v,u,gamma,beta) are labelled tight P4s. (RIGHT) Either {alpha,beta,s,d} supports one of the labelled P4s (alpha,beta,s,d),(beta,alpha,s,d), or both (alpha,s,R) and (beta,s,R) are tight. (Q-LEFT) Either (L,u,d) is tight or (u,d,q_{m-3}) is tight. (Q-RIGHT) Either (v,s,R) is tight or (q_3,v,s) is tight. Thus every surviving tight cross-end orientation (L,e,R) directly yields a fixed two-ended role-sensitive packet: endpoint witness enrichment, labelled P4, or cross-depth inward witness. No intermediate residue theorem is needed as a consumer interface.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in accepted R594, with m>=6, and (L,e,R) tight for e in X. Use accepted R3,R4,R537,R594,R723,R849.

## Scope
Noncircular current-O6 parent theorem. It composes the accepted mathematics formerly split between retired R749 and active R859, but is proved directly from current active inputs and does not depend on either route theorem.

## Applicability
Whenever the synchronized transitive frame exposes a tight cross-end turn (q_0,e,q_m), use this theorem directly. Do not first route through retired R749. The output preserves the exact e,gamma,alpha,beta labels, both residue trimers, both endpoint dimers, and the inward coordinates v,d.

## Persistence
Retain e, its M_R partner gamma, the other M_R edge {alpha,beta}, the P5 J, the two literal residue covers T_1 plus Q[2,m-2] and T_2 plus Q[2,m-2], and all four seam alternatives simultaneously.

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
Write X-{e}={alpha,beta,gamma}, where gamma is the M_R-partner of e and {alpha,beta} is the other M_R edge. Since X is the accepted transitive matching-height cell of R594, accepted R537 gives both T_1=(beta,alpha,gamma) and T_2=(alpha,beta,gamma) as tight trimers. Also R594 gives (u,L,e) and (e,R,s) tight, while (L,e,R) is tight by hypothesis, so J=(u,L,e,R,s) is a tight P5. Its complementary vertex set is W=(X-{e}) union Q[2,m-2]. For each i, T_i and Q[2,m-2] are vertex-disjoint tight paths spanning W, so they form a literal exact two-cover of W. If W had a tight Hamilton path P, then P together with J would be a spanning two-cover of H, contrary to the smallest-counterexample property R4. Hence W is non-Hamiltonian.

Fix i and write T_i=(p_0,p_1,p_2), so p_2=gamma. T_i cannot be concatenated immediately before J. If both seam turns (p_1,p_2,u) and (p_2,u,L) were tight, then T_i followed by J would be one tight path and Q[2,m-2] the other, spanning H. Therefore at least one seam is bad, and R3 gives at least one reverse (u,p_2,p_1) or (L,u,p_2) tight. For T_1 this is (u,gamma,alpha) or (L,u,gamma); for T_2 it is (u,gamma,beta) or (L,u,gamma). Thus either (L,u,gamma) is tight, or both (u,gamma,alpha) and (u,gamma,beta) are tight. Accepted R723 gives (v,u,gamma) tight, so in the latter case both (v,u,gamma,alpha) and (v,u,gamma,beta) are labelled tight P4s. This proves LEFT.

Likewise T_i cannot be concatenated immediately after J. If both (R,s,p_0) and (s,p_0,p_1) were tight, then J followed by T_i together with Q[2,m-2] would two-cover H. Hence R3 gives at least one of (p_0,s,R) and (p_1,p_0,s). For T_1 this is (beta,s,R) or (alpha,beta,s); for T_2 it is (alpha,s,R) or (beta,alpha,s). If (beta,s,R) is absent, then (alpha,beta,s) is tight, and R723 gives (beta,s,d), so (alpha,beta,s,d) is a labelled P4. If (alpha,s,R) is absent, then (beta,alpha,s) is tight, and R723 gives (alpha,s,d), so (beta,alpha,s,d) is a labelled P4. Therefore either one of those P4s exists or both (alpha,s,R) and (beta,s,R) are tight. This proves RIGHT.

Finally use the other residue rail A=Q[2,m-2]=(v,q_3,...,d), which is nontrivial because m>=6. A cannot precede J. If both (q_{m-3},d,u) and (d,u,L) were tight, A followed by J together with T_i would two-cover H. Hence R3 gives (u,d,q_{m-3}) or (L,u,d). A also cannot follow J. If both (R,s,v) and (s,v,q_3) were tight, J followed by A together with T_i would two-cover H, so R3 gives (v,s,R) or (q_3,v,s). These conclusions are independent of i and coexist with LEFT and RIGHT. Accepted R849 identifies the second alternatives as cross-depth witness enrichments on the inward signed dimers. No intermediate R749 or R859 theorem has been used.
