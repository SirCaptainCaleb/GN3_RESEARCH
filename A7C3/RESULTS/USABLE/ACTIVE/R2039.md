## Statement

In the retained source frame, suppose a literal exact two-cover `T` of `G=H-{A,C}` has a rail containing two consecutive internal retained source spokes in the literal order

`(...,x,t,r,y,...)`.

Then counterexample status forces at least one of the two boundary seams

`(x,t,C)` and `(A,r,y)`

to be bad. Equivalently, by R3, at least one of the reverse caps

`(x,C,t)` and `(r,A,y)`

is tight.

## Proof

Replace the single rail edge `t->r` by the anchor detour

`t->C->A->r`.

The two new internal trimers are automatically tight: `(t,C,A)` is a cyclic presentation of the retained source turn `(A,t,C)`, while `(C,A,r)` is a cyclic presentation of `(A,r,C)`. All old trimers away from the insertion remain unchanged.

Hence the only new physical obligations are exactly the two boundary seams `(x,t,C)` and `(A,r,y)`. If both were tight, the modified rail

`(...,x,t,C,A,r,y,...)`

would be a literal tight path on the old rail support together with `{A,C}`. The untouched second rail of `T` would then form, with it, a spanning exact two-cover of `H`, contradicting the counterexample hypothesis.

Therefore at least one boundary seam is bad. R3 converts bad `(x,t,C)` to the opposite orientation, cyclically `(x,C,t)`, and bad `(A,r,y)` to the opposite orientation, cyclically `(r,A,y)`.
