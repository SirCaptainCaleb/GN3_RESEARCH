# R1031 — G37 first-loss zipper must be synchronized with certified source-boundary ancestry

**Audit state:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Proof provenance:** Slack `#a7c3-workspace` root `1789300014.096059`, updated against D17.421 and D17.433–438.

## Statement
Within the G37 source-gate route, two established facts fence the remaining zipper-absorption problem.

1. The retained D17.430 five-set data do **not** imply a Hamilton P5 with the fixed far-wall vertex as an endpoint; endpoint-controlled far-wall insertion cannot be taken for free.
2. In the sharp G35 hard cell, D17.421 localizes the first numerical loss of strict old-source transition descent to one exact `K_{2,2}` two-crossing zipper rectangle, modulo the unique singleton-defect loop interruption.

The later ancestry contractions D17.433–438 sharpen how source data meet that rectangle:

- in the SINGLETON-SPOKE class, the first-loss rectangle itself is canonically source-rooted;
- in the only v-rooted X-MATED class, the cliff rectangle is not source-rooted, but the same augmenter has a canonical later terminal X-mated source boundary with transition count at most two and retained source ancestry.

Therefore the live unproved interface in this route is not a source-anonymous exact zipper and not a free endpoint-P5 splice. It is the synchronization/absorption of the exact numerical-cliff rectangle with the certified source boundary/wall packet carried by the same ancestry. A proposed relative-orientation exchange between source-anchor choices and zipper-crossing choices remains a candidate mechanism only; it is not established by these results.

## Hypotheses
The sharp G35/G37 hard-cell setup of D17.421 and D17.433–438; the D17.430 source-wall packet where relevant.

## Scope
This is a durable integration fence, not a closure theorem. It records exactly what is proved about the first-loss location and source placement, and exactly what is not available from D17.430 alone.

## Applicability
Any zipper-absorption argument in this route must:

- use the exact D17.421 `K_{2,2}` first-loss rectangle rather than an anonymous generic P4 insertion;
- retain the source-placement information from D17.433–438;
- not assume a Hamilton P5 whose endpoint is the fixed wall vertex merely from D17.430;
- explicitly justify whatever synchronization, exchange, or seam-safe pairing is used between the source packet and the numerical cliff.

## Persistence
Retain the physical first-loss rectangle and its old/new pairings, the source-root placement class, the ordered source-spoke block and X-mates in the v-rooted class, the canonical terminal source boundary when present, and the actual fixed far-wall data. Do not collapse these to an anonymous reflected `K_{2,2}`.

## Proof
R1029 supplies an explicit five-vertex boundary tournament satisfying the retained D17.430 OUT star-cycle turns while having no Hamilton P5 with the wall vertex as an endpoint. Thus endpoint control is not a consequence of the five-set packet.

Independently, D17.421 proves that consecutive normalized closures differ by a single `K_{2,2}` matching switch, with transition count changes only in `{-2,0,2}`. At the first non-loop index where the old transition floor is reached, the previous closure has transition count `tau(F)-2`, the new one has `tau(F)`, and the switch replaces two same-side edges by two `X|B` edges. This is the canonical exact two-crossing numerical cliff.

D17.433 then shows that throughout the SINGLETON-SPOKE hard cell this first-loss rectangle is source-rooted: the unique `X-X` member of its old same-side diagonal is incident with the physical source spoke responsible for the first transition rise.

D17.434 isolates the sole possible non-source-rooted cliff as the v-rooted X-MATED cell. D17.435 identifies its old source support as an ordered three-spoke block with two opposite-polarity X-mated source gates. D17.436 proves that at least one such source gate lies strictly after the v-cliff on the same augmenter. D17.438 then takes the last old F-transition on that augmenter and obtains a canonical terminal low-transition source boundary retaining the source trimer and X-mated source-block ancestry.

Hence the original Slack wording is correct in its endpoint-control fence and exact-zipper localization, but the phrase `source-anchor/P4 packet + exact K2,2 zipper` is too coarse after D17.433–438 because it can suggest that the source packet is always literally rooted at the numerical cliff. The corrected interface distinguishes source-at-cliff from source-at-terminal-boundary and identifies the missing work as synchronizing those certified objects. No relative-orientation exchange theorem is supplied by the cited results.
