# R434 — Fixed-Turn Selected-Incidence Endpoint Budget Package

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample, let J=(a,b,c) be a fixed proper tight turn, and work at an aligned exact pair-deletion frame H-{a,c}=U sqcup V. Let A_J subseteq {a,c} record the outer endpoints for which an earlier quiet selected-incidence episode has been completed all the way back to an aligned floor with that endpoint retained as a signed singleton coordinate. Then: (i) every selected predecessor r->b yields a graph-intrinsic signed dimer whose signed endpoint is a, and every selected successor b->s yields one whose signed endpoint is c; (ii) each such dimer gives either a spanning two-cover during actualization or a balanced pair birth retaining that endpoint-anchored dimer; (iii) if the anchored endpoint e already lies in A_J, the new nontrivial dimer containing the retained historical singleton anchor e is an explicit anchor-contact/strict-growth event and the episode is nonquiet; (iv) if e is fresh, immediately designate the return-to-aligned-floor continuation as an R42 protected continuation. Along that continuation either a first event touching e is exposed, or pair descent reaches a both-singleton floor with e itself as one singleton; R433 then restores the other outer endpoint while preserving e, completing a new aligned floor and enlarging A_J by e. Consequently every completed quiet aligned episode strictly enlarges A_J, so at most two such episodes are quiet for one fixed turn.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong counterexample; J=(a,b,c) is retained; the current floor is aligned on {a,c}; H-{a,c}=U sqcup V is an exact two-cover; selected predecessor/successor incidence at b is taken from that frame. For a fresh endpoint capture, the descendant continuation is designated immediately at the actual capture event as protected in the sense of R42 and follows the stated R427/R428/R433 return protocol.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order. This packages the historical Left/Right Selected-Incidence Endpoint-Anchor Extraction, Selected-Incidence Endpoint Pair Birth, and the weaker endpoint-budget content of Fixed-Turn Common-Frame Endpoint Budget. It does not include the stronger internal-middle immediate two-pair collision conclusion.

## Applicability
Use at a target-aligned fixed-turn floor to obtain a genuine two-element quiet-episode budget without treating bare historical capture or exact certificate replay as progress.

## Persistence
Retain J, the aligned frame used for the episode, the selected incidence, exact endpoint-anchored signed dimer and witness, its pair-deletion/actualization certificate, and A_J. In a fresh quiet episode retain the R42 protected continuation until the captured endpoint e has become a signed singleton; R428/R433 then preserve that singleton through return to the aligned floor. A_J records only endpoints that have completed this protected return, not arbitrary historical captures. Source rails and unrelated selected states need not survive.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim every historical capture is protected. Does not count exact certificate-stationary replay as fresh progress. Does not assert all episode representatives coexist. A first contact is an explicit output, not automatic conjecture closure. The stronger internal-middle simultaneous two-pair collision theorem is not included.

## Proof
Let the aligned floor be {a,c}. Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429 gives two nontrivial rails, so the rail containing b supplies a selected predecessor or successor. Left predecessor r->b: R3 says exactly one of r a b and b a r is tight, producing respectively the tail-signed dimer (r,a) with witness b or (b,a) with witness r; both have signed endpoint a. The successor case is the exact dual at c. Delete the resulting dimer D. R429 gives two nontrivial rails; exactly one contains its sign witness, and the other is a nontrivial disjoint marker. Test its terminal marker seam directly with R3, obtaining either the whole rail or the standard short opposite-polarity support. Thus either a spanning two-cover has already occurred in the accepted actualization branch or a balanced pair birth retaining D is obtained. Now let e be the signed endpoint of D. If e is already in A_J, an earlier quiet episode ended at an aligned floor with e retained as a signed singleton support. The new dimer D is a nontrivial proper tight path containing that singleton anchor, so R436 gives an explicit at-anchor contact/strict-growth output; this episode is nonquiet. Suppose e is fresh. At this actual capture/birth event immediately designate the following descendant route as the R42 protected continuation, with target role: e is a literal signed singleton at the next aligned floor. First run the finite R427 width-two pair descent. If any step genuinely touches e, R42 exposes the first contact and the episode is nonquiet. Otherwise e remains the anchor of the retained descendant of D. If R427 makes that descendant singleton, it must therefore be exactly (e). If instead R427 first makes the opposite support singleton, apply R428 with that singleton fixed and the e-containing support as the nontrivial side. Again any touch of e is an R42 first-contact exit; in the quiet arm the e-containing support is shortened without losing e and eventually becomes the singleton (e). If the opposite support is still nontrivial, R428 with (e) fixed pays it to singleton width or closes. We now have a both-singleton floor containing (e), with e still the completed protected endpoint of this episode. Let f be the other member of {a,c}. Apply R433 to replace the other singleton by f while preserving (e); this returns to an aligned {a,c} floor. Record e in A_J. Therefore every completed quiet aligned episode strictly enlarges A_J. Since A_J is a subset of {a,c}, at most two completed aligned episodes are quiet. No claim is made that bare historical capture, without this protected return protocol, spends the budget.
