# R2003 — First-open predecessor transport preserves a tight three-forest

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789411207.095289`

## Statement

Retain the later non-loop OUT-`v` first-loss setting, let `c=b_{t+1}` be the first R1033-open spectator slot, and let `M=hat M_{r-1}` be a literal tight normalized pre-loss three-path forest. Suppose the canonical successor `c->b_{t+2}` is selected and some selected predecessor `y->c` is present.

Then

`M_y=M-{y->c}+{v->c}`

is again a literal tight three-path forest. Its free copies are exactly `y,out` and the same far source copy `s,in`.

If `y!=s`, the unique direct closure of those defects is `y->s`. If `y=s`, the two free copies belong to the same physical vertex, and this is the unique split-copy coincidence in the transport statement.

## Proof

By R1060, `v` is an isolated singleton component of M. Deleting the selected predecessor edge `y->c` cuts one path component into a prefix ending at y and a suffix beginning at c. Adding `v->c` joins the isolated singleton v to that suffix, so it creates no directed cycle and no turn at v.

The successor `c->b_{t+2}` remains selected. Therefore the only new turn is

`(v,c,b_{t+2})`.

Since c is the first R1033-open slot, this is exactly the first-open comparison and is tight. Every other selected turn is inherited from M. Hence `M_y` is a literal tight three-path forest.

The deletion makes `y,out` free, while the fixed far free IN-copy remains `s,in`. If `y!=s`, these are distinct physical vertices and their unique direct matching closure is `y->s`. If `y=s`, the two free split copies are the OUT and IN copies of the same source vertex, so the direct closure would be the forbidden loop `s->s`. No other coincidence is possible.
