# R823 — Matching-layer edge orders give an explicit 3/4 spanning-path fence

## Statement
Let n=2m be even. Take the complete graph K_n with an equipartition V=A disjoint-union B, |A|=|B|=m. Totally order its edges so that every edge internal to A or B precedes every A-B edge. Decompose the complete bipartite edge set E(A,B) into perfect matchings M_1,...,M_m, and order the cross edges in matching blocks M_1<...<M_m, with arbitrary order inside each block. Define the associated exact-reversal boundary tournament H by declaring an ordered triple (u,v,w) tight exactly when the graph edge {u,v} precedes {v,w} in the edge order. Then tight paths in H are exactly increasing paths in the edge-ordered K_n, and every such path has at most m+ceil(m/2)=ceil(3n/4) vertices. In particular this edge-order-induced boundary tournament has no spanning tight path for n>=4. The obstruction is a two-resource budget: once an increasing path uses its first cross edge it can never return to an internal edge; its remaining suffix alternates across A|B, uses at most one edge from each perfect-matching block, and every two cross edges consume a fresh vertex on the side containing the initial internal prefix.

## Hypotheses
n=2m is even; K_n is edge ordered with all within-part edges below all cross edges and with the cross edges grouped in the ordered perfect-matching blocks of a 1-factorization of K_{m,m}. The induced ternary relation is tight(u,v,w) iff uv<vw.

## Scope
Elementary explicit fence against spanning-path or near-spanning-path conjectures for boundary tournaments arising from edge-ordered complete graphs. The point is the transferable resource-budget mechanism, not the numerically strongest known monotone-path upper bound.

## Applicability
Use when a proposed general spanning-tight-path principle relies only on exact reversal, local abundance, or complete underlying support and therefore must survive edge-order-induced boundary tournaments. The construction suggests testing path-growth arguments against layered transition resources and vertex-consumption budgets. It may also motivate multipartite, hierarchical, or recursively layered variants.

## Persistence
Retain the partition A|B, the low internal/high cross edge split, the ordered matching blocks M_i, the first-cross position of any tested path, the number c of cross edges used, and the resulting vertex-budget inequality.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
This is not claimed to be the sharpest upper bound for increasing paths in edge-ordered complete graphs; substantially stronger asymptotic constructions are known. It does not show that every Strong Level-(1) structural hypothesis used elsewhere in A7C3 is realized by this subclass unless separately checked. It does not claim the ceil(3n/4) bound is always attained by this ordering, nor that simple recursion inside A and B improves the constant.

## Proof
Let P be an increasing vertex-simple path in the prescribed edge ordering. If P uses no cross edge, then it lies entirely in A or entirely in B and has at most m vertices. Otherwise consider its first cross edge. Before that edge, every used edge is internal, and since no cross edge has yet occurred all vertices of the initial segment lie in one part; by symmetry call it A. After the first cross edge no internal edge can occur, because every internal edge precedes every cross edge in the total order. Hence the remainder of P consists entirely of cross edges and alternates between A and B. Let c be the number of cross edges used. Because the cross edges are ordered in blocks M_1<...<M_m, their matching indices along P are nondecreasing. Two consecutive cross edges cannot lie in the same M_i, since a perfect matching has no two incident edges. All cross edges after the first are consecutive path edges, so P uses at most one edge from each matching block. Therefore c<=m. Let p be the number of vertices in the initial A-segment, including the A-endpoint of the first cross edge. The c-edge alternating cross suffix introduces floor(c/2) additional A-vertices, all distinct because P is vertex-simple. Thus p+floor(c/2)<=m. The total number of vertices of P is p+c, so |P|<=m-floor(c/2)+c=m+ceil(c/2)<=m+ceil(m/2)=ceil(3m/2)=ceil(3n/4). Finally, under the translation tight(u,v,w) iff uv<vw, an ordered vertex sequence is a tight path exactly when its consecutive graph edges are strictly increasing, so the same bound holds for tight paths in H.
