# R919 — Generic tight-trimer outer-pair endpoint return yields a two-hole mate clause

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let J=(a,x,c) be any graph-intrinsic tight trimer. Choose an exact two-cover P sqcup Q of H-{a,c} in which x is an endpoint of P. Then P is not a singleton, and endpoint exposure yields an explicit one-hole or two-hole mate certificate without any rail-floor hypothesis. If P begins (x,u), then when P has order two the spanning proposal (a,x,c,u) sqcup Q has the unique uncertified turn (x,c,u), which must be bad, so (u,c,x) is tight. If P begins (x,u,v,...) with order at least three, the proposal (a,x,c,u,v,...) sqcup Q has exactly two uncertified turns (x,c,u) and (c,u,v); at least one is bad, so (u,c,x) is tight OR (v,u,c) is tight. The right-end case is the exact dual: for P=(u,x) one forces (x,a,u), while for P=(...,v,u,x) one gets (a,u,v) tight OR (x,a,u) tight. Thus every outer-pair endpoint return of the middle vertex of a tight trimer gives an explicit R173-admissible mate clause, with the order-two case degenerating to a forced one-hole mate.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; J=(a,x,c) is a tight trimer; P sqcup Q is an exact two-cover of H-{a,c}; x is an endpoint of P.

## Scope
Order-free endpoint-return compiler. Replaces the R168-dependent formulation by an exact order-two/order-at-least-three seam split.

## Applicability
Use whenever an exact outer-pair deletion cover exposes the middle of a known tight trimer. No small-deletion rail floor is needed.

## Persistence
Retain the literal spanning proposal and its one- or two-hole seam ledger; the forced reverse mate or mate disjunction is graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert which mate wins in the two-hole case; one mate clause alone does not close H; does not claim an endpoint rail has order at least three.

## Proof
Proof of R919. Let J=(a,x,c) be tight and P sqcup Q an exact two-cover of H-{a,c} with x an endpoint of P. If P=(x) is singleton, then J sqcup Q is already a spanning two-cover of H, impossible. Thus P has order at least two. Suppose x is the first vertex. If P=(x,u), consider (a,x,c,u) sqcup Q. The turn (a,x,c) is certified by J and the unique new turn is h=(x,c,u). If h were tight, the proposal would two-cover H; hence h is bad, and R3 gives (u,c,x) tight. If P=(x,u,v,...) has order at least three, the proposal (a,x,c,u,v,...) sqcup Q has exactly the two new turns h1=(x,c,u), h2=(c,u,v). Every later turn is inherited from P and the first turn is inherited from J. They cannot both be tight, so at least one is bad; R3 gives (u,c,x) tight or (v,u,c) tight. If x is the last vertex, reverse the construction: for P=(u,x), the proposal (u,a,x,c) sqcup Q has unique hole (u,a,x), forcing (x,a,u); for P=(...,v,u,x), the proposal (...,v,u,a,x,c) sqcup Q has exactly the holes (v,u,a),(u,a,x), forcing (a,u,v) or (x,a,u). This proves R919 without a rail-floor theorem.

Proof of R920. Let J=(a,x,c) be proper. By R4, H-J has an exact two-cover U sqcup V; restoring J gives the named minimum three-cover. For the outer pair {a,c}, either some exact H-{a,c} cover exposes x or every such cover keeps x internal. In the first case apply the just-proved endpoint seam argument of R919.

Assume the all-internal case. Fix any exact return P sqcup Q of H-{a,c}; it exists by R4 applied to the pair deletion, and x is internal, say P=(...,ell,x,r,...). Deleting x splits P into two nonempty tight subpaths P_L,P_R, so P_L sqcup P_R sqcup Q is a literal three-cover of W=H-J. The source U sqcup V is a literal two-cover of the same proper W. R159 therefore gives a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair.

It remains to prove the source-rail and shield assertions without R168. Neither U nor V can be singleton. If U=(y), then (x,y) is a valid two-vertex tight path and (x,y) sqcup V is an exact two-cover of H-{a,c} exposing x, contrary to all-internality. Thus both rails have order at least two. Write U=(u0,...,ur) with r>=1. The direct attachment at its left end is bad: if r>=1 and (x,u0,u1) were tight, then (x,u0,...,ur) sqcup V would be an exact H-{a,c} cover exposing x. Hence R3 forces (u1,u0,x) tight. If r=1, this reverse trimer itself uses exactly x and all vertices of U, so (u1,u0,x) sqcup V is an exact H-{a,c} cover exposing x, again impossible. Therefore r>=2 and |U|>=3. The same argument gives |V|>=3.

With both rails of order at least three, the two direct x-attachments at each end of each rail are defined. Each is bad for the same endpoint-exposure reason. Applying R3 to their complete reversals gives the four reverse terminal shields: for U, (u1,u0,x) and (x,ur,u_{r-1}); for V the analogous two turns. Together with the R159 pair this is exactly the asserted all-internal normal form. No R168, R24, R5, capture, or first-contact theorem is used.
