# R362 — Global Hamilton-five Johnson equality forces a tight P6

## Statement
Assume equality in the global Hamilton-five bad-family ceiling R326/R331 on an r-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament W, r>=6. Write r=5q. Then W contains a tight P6. Indeed R346 and R355 imply q is even and q is congruent to 0 or 1 modulo 3, so q=2,3 are impossible and the first possible value is q=4. Fix any tight trimer support P in W and form the equality-case bad-extension graph G_P from R341. It is q-regular and triangle-free on n=5q-3 vertices. Suppose G_P has no 4-cycle. For any vertex v, triangle-freeness makes N(v) independent, and C4-freeness makes the q sets N(u)-{v}, u in N(v), pairwise disjoint and disjoint from {v} union N(v). Hence n>=1+q+q(q-1)=q^2+1. Since n=5q-3, this gives (q-1)(q-4)<=0, so q<=4. Thus only q=4 remains. In that case n=17=q^2+1, so equality in the Moore count holds around every vertex: every nonadjacent pair has exactly one common neighbor and every adjacent pair has none. Hence G_P would be strongly regular with parameters (17,4,0,1). Its adjacency matrix A would satisfy A^2=3I-A+J. On the 16-dimensional all-ones-orthogonal subspace every eigenvalue satisfies t^2+t-3=0. The two irrational conjugate roots must have equal multiplicity 8, but then trace(A)=4+8(-1)=-4, contradicting trace(A)=0. Therefore G_P contains a C4. Since G_P is triangle-free, the four cycle vertices induce exactly K_{2,2}; accepted R348 applied to P and those four outside vertices yields a tight P6 in W.

## Hypotheses
W is an r-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, r>=6; equality holds in the global R326/R331 Hamilton-five bad-family density ceiling.

## Scope
Exact equality-case path-growth theorem. It uses the global equality design and fixed-trimer regularity, not Hamilton-five density alone.

## Applicability
Use to eliminate P6-free equality cases of the Johnson density bound. In a smallest-counterexample application, exact global density equality automatically enters any existing P6/contact machinery.

## Persistence
The bad family, fixed-trimer graph, and resulting P6 support are graph-intrinsic. The spectral contradiction is only for the hypothetical q=4 C4-free equality graph.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say near-equality forces P6. Does not contradict R321/R335/R347 density-only or one-edge-below counterexamples. Does not claim a P6 itself closes O4.

## Proof
Assume global R331 equality and write r=5q. R346 and R355 force q even and q≡0 or1 mod3, so q>=4. Fix any tight trimer P. R341 gives a q-regular triangle-free bad graph G_P on m=5q-3 vertices. If G_P has a C4, R348 gives a P6. Otherwise G_P has girth at least five. R363 gives q<=sqrt(m-1)=sqrt(5q-4), hence q<=4. Therefore q=4 and equality holds in R363, so G_P has exact Moore geometry of degree 4. But R369 forbids degree 4 for a diameter-two girth-five Moore graph. Contradiction. Hence G_P contains a C4 and R348 yields a tight P6.
