# R693 — Short-carrier deletion covers reduce to a physical mate clause or an internal singleton

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2, let K=(k_0,k_1,k_2) be a tight trimer, and take one boundary decomposition D={k_1,k_2}, S={k_0}; the exact dual D={k_0,k_1}, S={k_2} also holds. Let T be any exact two-cover of H-D. Since K is a tight path on exactly D union S, accepted R508 forces at least one selected S|(V(H)\V(K)) transition. Because S is the singleton {k_0}, its transition count tau(T) is exactly its degree in its T-rail and is therefore either one or two. If tau(T)=2, k_0 is internal on its T-rail and both incident selected states are explicit carrier/exterior crossings. If tau(T)=1, k_0 is a T-rail endpoint and T already certifies a physical spanning proposal with at most two holes. More precisely, if the mixed rail has form C-S, the aligned endpoint case of R509/R511 gives a one-hole proposal. If it has form S-C, write the other pure exterior rail as C'=(c_0,...,c_s). Specializing the R511 SC normal form to Q=K, m=2 and i=0 gives the complete hole set {(c_{s-1},c_s,k_1) when s>=1} union {(c_s,k_1,k_2)}, of size one or two. Boundary antisymmetry therefore forces the reverse of the unique hole, or yields the physically certified two-hole mate clause formed by the two reversals. The terminal decomposition D={k_0,k_1}, S={k_2} is the exact reverse dual. Consequently every R542 short-carrier source cover refines before payment to exactly one of two concrete geometries: a degree-one complementary singleton carrying a physical one/two-hole proposal, or a degree-two internal singleton carrying two actual exterior crossings.

## Hypotheses
H is finite Strong Level-(1) with pc(H)>2; K=(k_0,k_1,k_2) is a tight trimer; D is one boundary dimer of K and S is the opposite singleton; T is an exact two-cover of H-D. Use accepted R508, R509, R511, and R3.

## Scope
Order-free short-carrier recompletion reduction. No smallest-counterexample payment, capture, R5, R168, R169, R224, R533, generic R358, or R436.

## Applicability
Apply immediately to the exact boundary-dimer deletion cover in any R542-ready packet. If the complementary singleton is a rail endpoint, retain the exact one/two-hole proposal instead of spending the packet anonymously. Only the internal-singleton degree-two branch still needs a new consumer or endpointization argument.

## Persistence
Retain K,D,S,T, the singleton degree and literal rail position. In the degree-one branch retain the pure exterior rail, displayed spanning proposal, complete hole set, and forced reverse literal or mate clause. In the degree-two branch retain both selected crossings incident with the internal singleton.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close a physical mate clause, does not consume the internal-singleton branch, and does not assert an exact cover can be chosen with singleton degree one. It does not use capture or payment and does not claim alternative deletion covers coexist.

## Proof
Take D={k_1,k_2}, S={k_0}; the other end is dual. R508 applies with Q=K and forces tau(T)>=1. Since k_0 is a singleton in a path cover, tau(T)=deg_T(k_0) is 1 or 2. Degree two is exactly the stated internal branch. Suppose tau=1. R511(1) gives one pure exterior rail and one mixed rail with the unique S-block at an end. In the C-S orientation, the S block begins at q_0=k_0, so R511(3), j=0, invokes the aligned one-hole case of R509. In the S-C orientation, R511(2) has i=0 for Q=(k_0,k_1,k_2). Its first possible hole is absent because i<1; its other two holes are exactly (c_{s-1},c_s,k_1) when the pure exterior rail has at least two vertices, and (c_s,k_1,k_2). Hence the complete hole set has size one or two, never three. Since pc(H)>2, not all holes can be tight; R3 forces the reverse of the unique hole or gives the two reversed literals as a certified mate clause. Reversing all orders proves the k_2 endpoint case.
