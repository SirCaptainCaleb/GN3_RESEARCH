# R718 — The endpoint-star six-cell does not propagate the middle-gate bit one step inward

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,z} be the transitive matching-height P4-free four-cell with M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}. There exist Strong Level-(1) orientations on X union {L,u} satisfying simultaneously: (i) X+L is Hamilton-P5-free; (ii) X+u is Hamilton-P5-free; (iii) X+{L,u} is Hamilton-P6-free; and (iv) (u,L,x) is tight for every x in X, in which the outgoing M_S gate at u is the SAME physical M_S edge as the outgoing M_S gate at L. There also exist such orientations in which the outgoing M_S gate at u is the COMPLEMENTARY M_S edge to the outgoing gate at L. Hence the natural local packet consisting of transitive X, the two P5-free fifth-vertex cells, P6-freeness of the six-cell, and the full endpoint reverse star uLx does not determine whether the middle-gate bit is preserved or flipped from L to u. By the automorphism of the matching-height cell interchanging the two M_S edges, all four ordered gate-bit pairs occur. Therefore no R24 route may infer a deterministic one-step middle-gate propagation law from this local packet alone.

## Hypotheses
Only Strong Level-(1) complete-reversal antisymmetry is used. The finite models are on six vertices X union {L,u}; X has the displayed matching-height orientation.

## Scope
Finite local R24 strategy fence. No smallest-counterexample minimality, deletion exactness, capture, payment, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,R358, or computation-dependent hidden assumption beyond the explicit certificate verification.

## Applicability
Use when considering propagation of the alternating middle-gate polarity from q_0 to q_1 (or dually q_m to q_{m-1}) using only the endpoint-star six-cell. A successful inward propagation or zipper invariant must consume additional information absent here, such as the opposite end, a high-side root/inward-star coordinate, or an exact deletion-cover crossing.

## Persistence
Retain the two explicit six-vertex certificate words and the distinction SAME versus OPPOSITE middle-gate relation.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert either six-vertex model extends to a smallest counterexample or to the full long-Q R24 frame. Does not refute nonlocal gate synchronization using q_{m-1},q_m or exact cover data. Does not say the first-inward gate is useless; only that its bit is not determined by the endpoint-local packet.

## Proof
Fix vertex order a<b<c<z<L<u. For each middle vertex m, list the unordered endpoint pairs from the other five vertices in lexicographic order induced by this vertex order. A 10-bit word records the complete Strong orientation at middle m: bit 1 means the canonical ordered turn (p,m,q) with p<q is tight; bit 0 means its complete reversal (q,m,p) is tight. Thus six 10-bit words define all 60 reversal pairs.

MODEL S (same M_S gate; {a,c} outgoing and {b,z} incoming at both L and u):
a: 1111100000
b: 1111000110
c: 1000000000
z: 0000011111
L: 1010000100
u: 1010010100

MODEL O (opposite M_S gate; {a,c} outgoing at L while {b,z} is outgoing at u):
a: 1111101001
b: 1111000100
c: 1001000011
z: 0000010100
L: 1010000100
u: 0000100000

For clarity, the endpoint-pair orders used by these words are: at a: bc,bz,bL,bu,cz,cL,cu,zL,zu,Lu; at b: ac,az,aL,au,cz,cL,cu,zL,zu,Lu; at c: ab,az,aL,au,bz,bL,bu,zL,zu,Lu; at z: ab,ac,aL,au,bc,bL,bu,cL,cu,Lu; at L: ab,ac,az,au,bc,bz,bu,cz,cu,zu; at u: ab,ac,az,aL,bc,bz,bL,cz,cL,zL.

Direct reconstruction gives in BOTH models: the restriction to X is exactly the matching-height P4-free cell; every uLx (x in X) is tight; no one of the 120 orders on X+L is a Hamilton P5; no one of the 120 orders on X+u is a Hamilton P5; and no one of the 720 orders on X+{L,u} is a Hamilton P6. In Model S the full M_S turns at L are Lac,Lca,bzL,zbL and at u are uac,uca,bzu,zbu, so the outgoing edge is {a,c} at both. In Model O the L gate is unchanged while at u the full M_S turns are ubz,uzb,acu,cau, so the outgoing edge is {b,z}, the complementary edge. The matching-height automorphism exchanging the two M_S edges converts (same 0,0) to (same 1,1) and (opposite 0,1) to (opposite 1,0). This proves the fence.
