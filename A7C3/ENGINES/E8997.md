# E8997 — Small-Order Qualification Engine

## Theorem

Let `H` be a smallest counterexample to the assertion that every finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament has path-cover number at most two. Then

`|V(H)| > 10`.

Equivalently, every such counterexample of order at most ten admits a spanning cover by at most two tight paths.

## Published inputs

We use the following published Spare Parts only through their stated interfaces.

1. **Fixed-trimer extension** ([S9023](../SPARE_PARTS/CORE_TOOLKIT/S9023_FIXED_TRIMER_EXTENSION_BAD_GRAPH.md)). If a tight trimer `P` is disjoint from three vertices `x,y,z`, then at least one of

   `P∪{x,y}`, `P∪{x,z}`, `P∪{y,z}`

   supports a tight Hamilton `P5`.

2. **Five-vertex edge-orderability** ([S9017](../SPARE_PARTS/CORE_TOOLKIT/S9017_FIVE_VERTEX_NONHAMILTON_EDGE_ORDERABILITY.md)). Every non-Hamiltonian five-vertex boundary tournament is edge-orderable.

3. **Three-of-five Hamilton `K4` density** ([S9039](../SPARE_PARTS/CORE_TOOLKIT/S9039_THREE_OF_FIVE_HAMILTON_K4_DENSITY.md)). Every edge-ordered `K5` has at least three vertex deletions whose remaining four vertices support an increasing Hamilton `P4`.

4. **Complement-free Johnson cut bound** ([S9040(A)](../SPARE_PARTS/SPECIALIZED_TOOLKIT/S9040_COMPLEMENT_FREE_JOHNSON_5_OF_10_BOUNDS.md)). If `F` is a complement-free family of five-subsets of a ten-set `Ω` and

   `bar F = {Ω-A : A∈F}`,

   then, in the Johnson graph `J(10,5)`,

   `e_J(F,bar F) ≤ 15|F|`.

Two consequences of S9023 will be used repeatedly. Every six-set contains a Hamilton `P5`: orient any three of its vertices as a tight trimer and apply S9023 to the other three. In fact every six-set has at least four Hamilton five-subsets. Otherwise three distinct omitted vertices `x,y,z` would all yield non-Hamiltonian five-sets; orient the complementary three-set as a tight trimer and apply S9023 to `x,y,z`.

We also need one elementary consequence of minimality.

### Pair-deletion rigidity

For distinct vertices `p,t`, the proper subsystem `H-{p,t}` has path-cover number exactly two, and neither rail of an exact two-cover is a singleton.

Indeed, minimality gives a cover by at most two paths. If `H-{p,t}` had a Hamilton path beginning at `w`, orient `{p,w,t}` as a tight trimer. That trimer together with the remaining Hamilton suffix would two-cover `H`. If an exact two-cover had singleton rail `{u}`, orient `{p,u,t}` as a tight trimer and combine it with the other rail. Both alternatives contradict the choice of `H`.

## Proof

### Orders at most eight

Every set of at most three vertices is a tight path. Hence `|H|≤6` is immediately two-covered.

If `|H|=7` or `8`, choose six vertices. They contain a Hamilton `P5`, and its complement in `H` has order two or three, hence is itself a tight path. Therefore `|H|≥9`.

### Order nine

Assume `|H|=9`. Let `F_5` be the family of Hamilton five-subsets and let `B_4` be the family of four-subsets with no Hamilton `P4`.

Count incidences between six-sets and Hamilton five-subsets. Every six-set contains at least four members of `F_5`, while every five-set lies in exactly four six-sets. Thus

`4|F_5| ≥ 4 C(9,6)`,

so

`|F_5| ≥ 84`.

Now consider a five-set `Y`. If `Y` is Hamiltonian, deleting either endpoint of one Hamilton `P5` leaves a Hamilton `P4`, so at most three of its four-subsets lie in `B_4`. If `Y` is non-Hamiltonian, S9017 makes it edge-orderable and S9039 gives at least three Hamilton four-vertex deletions, so at most two of its four-subsets lie in `B_4`. Hence every five-set contains at most three members of `B_4`.

Each four-set lies in exactly five five-sets, so counting incidences `(B,Y)` with `B∈B_4` and `B⊂Y` gives

`5|B_4| ≤ 3 C(9,5)=378`.

Therefore `|B_4|≤75`.

If `H` had no spanning two-cover, then the four-vertex complement of every member of `F_5` would be non-Hamiltonian, hence would lie in `B_4`. Distinct five-sets have distinct complements, so

`|B_4| ≥ |F_5| ≥ 84`,

contradicting `|B_4|≤75`. Thus order nine is impossible.

### Order ten: support geometry

Assume `|H|=10`, and let `F` be the family of five-sets supporting a Hamilton tight `P5`.

The four-of-six consequence of S9023 gives

`⋃F = V(H)`

and

`⋂F = ∅`.

Indeed, for any vertex `x`, choose a six-set containing `x`; at least four of its five-punctures are Hamiltonian and only one omits `x`. Conversely, to avoid `x`, choose a six-set not containing `x` and take a Hamilton five-subset inside it.

No two members of `F` are disjoint, since two complementary Hamilton `P5`s would two-cover `H`.

Let

`m = min{|A∩B| : A,B∈F, A≠B}`.

Then `1≤m≤3`. Only the upper bound needs proof. Suppose every two distinct members meet in at least four vertices. Choose distinct `A,B∈F`, write

`C=A∩B`, `A=C∪{a}`, `B=C∪{b}`,

so `|C|=4`.

Any `D∈F` either contains `C`, or misses exactly one `c∈C`; in the latter case the intersection bounds with both `A` and `B` force

`D=(C-{c})∪{a,b}`.

If such a latter member exists, then any member containing a vertex outside `A∪B` must contain all of `C`, and therefore meets that latter member in only three vertices, contradiction. Such an outside-containing member exists because `⋃F=V(H)`. If no latter member exists, every member contains `C`, contradicting `⋂F=∅`. Hence `m≤3`.

### Excluding `m=3`

Choose `A,B∈F` with `S=A∩B` and `|S|=3`. Orient `S` as a tight trimer. Minimality gives a path cover of the seven-vertex residue `H-S` by at most two paths. It cannot be Hamiltonian, since that Hamilton path together with the trimer on `S` would two-cover `H`; hence take an exact cover `P|Q`.

There is a Hamilton five-set `D` inside `H-S`. If a rail has order five, use it. If a rail has order six, use the six-set Hamilton-five consequence of S9023. The only remaining profile is `4+3`; take the three-vertex rail as a tight trimer and any three vertices of the four-vertex rail as exterior vertices in S9023. Thus some Hamilton `P5` lies entirely in `H-S`.

Hence `D∈F` and `D∩S=∅`. But `A-S` has only two vertices, so `|A∩D|≤2`, contradicting `m=3`. Therefore `m≠3`.

### Excluding `m=2`

Choose `A,B∈F` with `S=A∩B` and `|S|=2`. Pair-deletion rigidity gives an exact two-cover of the eight-vertex residue `H-S`, with both rails nontrivial.

Again there is a Hamilton five-set `D` disjoint from `S`: profiles `6+2` and `5+3` are immediate from the six-set consequence or the five-rail itself; for profile `4+4`, take a consecutive trimer from one rail and any three vertices of the other and apply S9023.

Put

`U=A-S`, `V=B-S`,

and let `R` be the remaining two vertices. Thus `|U|=|V|=3` and `|R|=2`. Minimality of `m` gives

`|D∩U|, |D∩V| ≥ 2`,

so the profile of `D` across `U,V,R` is one of

`(2,2,1)`, `(2,3,0)`, `(3,2,0)`.

Choose `L∈{A,B}` with `|L∩D|=2`, let `M` be the other, and put

`X=L∩M=S`, `Y=L∩D`, `Z=M∩D`.

Then `|X|=|Y|=2`, `Z` is disjoint from `L`, and `|Z|∈{2,3}`.

If `|Z|=2`, write

`L=X∪Y∪{p}`, `M=X∪Z∪{b}`, `D=Y∪Z∪{d}`,

and let `e` be the tenth vertex. Orient `{p,b,e}` as a tight trimer. Writing `X={x_1,x_2}` and choosing any `r∈D`, S9023 applied to exterior vertices `x_1,x_2,r` produces a Hamilton five-set meeting `D` in at most one vertex, contradicting `m=2`.

If `|Z|=3`, write

`L=X∪Y∪{p}`, `M=X∪Z`, `D=Y∪Z`,

and let `e,f` be the two remaining vertices. Orient `{p,e,f}` as a tight trimer. No pair-extension `{p,e,f,u,v}` with `u,v∈X∪Y∪Z` can be Hamiltonian: intersection at least two with both `M` and `D` would force `u,v∈Z`, but then the support meets `L` only in `p`. Choosing any three distinct exterior vertices in `X∪Y∪Z` and applying S9023 therefore yields a contradiction.

Hence `m≠2`.

### Excluding `m=1`

Fix `A∈F` and let

`E=V(H)-A`.

Then `E∉F`, since Hamilton `P5`s on `A` and `E` would two-cover `H`.

Fix `a∈A`. For each `z∈E` put

`B_z=(E-{z})∪{a}`.

At most one of the five sets `B_z` is non-Hamiltonian. Indeed, if distinct `B_x,B_y` were both non-Hamiltonian, orient the three-set

`P=E-{x,y}`

as a tight trimer. Applying S9023 to `P` and the exterior vertices `x,y,a` forces one of

`E`, `B_y`, `B_x`

to be Hamiltonian, contradiction.

Thus for each `a∈A` at least four one-vertex replacements belong to `F`. As `a` varies these replacements are distinct, so every `A∈F` has at least twenty such replacements.

Put

`bar F={V(H)-B:B∈F}`.

The families `F` and `bar F` are disjoint because `F` contains no complementary pair. For every successful replacement

`B_z=(E-{z})∪{a}`,

its complement

`(A-{a})∪{z}`

lies in `bar F` and is adjacent to `A` in `J(10,5)`, where adjacency means intersection in four vertices. Hence every `A∈F` has at least twenty neighbors in `bar F`, and therefore

`e_J(F,bar F) ≥ 20|F|`.

But S9040(A) gives

`e_J(F,bar F) ≤ 15|F|`,

a contradiction. Hence `m≠1`.

The possibilities `m=1,2,3` are exhausted, so order ten is impossible. Combined with the previous cases, every smallest counterexample satisfies

`|V(H)|>10`.

This is the sole output of E8997. The proof uses neither R24 nor R5. ∎
