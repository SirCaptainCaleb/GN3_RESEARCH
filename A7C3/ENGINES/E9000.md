# E9000: Marked spectator-frame reduction

## Theorem

Let `H` be a smallest counterexample with `|H|>10` in the transitive spectator-frame interface. Write

`G=H-{A,C}=X⊔B`, `X={v,s_1,s_2,s_3}`, and `B=(b_0,...,b_m)`,

where `B` is tight. Assume the three source turns `(A,s_i,C)` are tight, the two endpoint five-cells carry the full transitive matching-gate packets and reverse endpoint stars, and `F` is a physical exact two-cover of `G` in which all three sources are internal. Assume also the inherited source-cover ledger:

`tau(F)>=3`, `d_F(v)∈{1,2}`,

and at least two sources have selected `F`-incidences into `B`.

Then the reduction terminates in one of the following six structural interfaces:

1. maximum-three-forest support escape/two-cover, or a current reverse-wrap trimer;
2. a physical exact two-cover `T` with `tau(T)<tau(F)`;
3. a lower-`tau` exact-two-cover-cardinality matching with explicit bad-turn, backtrack, or cycle debt;
4. the unique singleton-defect loop;
5. a sharp source-rooted first-loss `K_{2,2}` rectangle when both adjacent closures exist;
6. the sharp `v`-rooted marked package exported to E9001.

Retaining `F_0=(A,C)|F`, every structural leaf also supplies a legal entrance to E9003, without collapsing the six structural ports.

## 1. The active-source gate split

Here `tau` counts selected `X|B` edges. Normalize the opposite-edge matchings of the transitive four-cell by

`M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}`,

and take the left outgoing middle gate to be `{b,z}`. A source is *B-active* if `F` uses an `X|B` edge at it. At least two sources are B-active.

Call a Hamilton `P5` on `{L,R}∪(X-{x})`, with `L=b_0` and `R=b_m`, *endpoint-favorable* if it has one of the five endpoint-position patterns

`(L,R,x,y,z)`, `(R,x,y,z,L)`, `(x,R,L,y,z)`, `(x,y,R,L,z)`, `(x,y,z,L,R)`.

Equivalently, it admits one of the one-hole low endpoint cuts used in the next section.

The two-ended gate packet forces an endpoint-favorable `P5` omitting a B-active source except in one aligned rigid cell. We give the finite reduction, including the two crossed exceptional pairs.

In the aligned gate, the only exceptional active target pair is the `M_S` edge `{a,c}` complementary to the common gate. Assuming no favorable omission of `b`, the candidates

`LRcaz, zcaLR, zcRLa, RczaL, cRLaz`

force the missing seams successively until the final word is tight, a contradiction. The dual chain gives a favorable omission of `z`. Hence every active target pair other than `{a,c}` contains a favorable-omittable active source.

In the crossed gate, first consider active target pairs meeting both `M_S` edges. The four possibilities are killed by

`RzcaL -> zcaLR -> czRLa -> LRzbc`,

`LRzbc -> bacLR -> RzbcL -> zRLcb`,

`LRbza -> RbzaL -> bRLaz -> zcaLR`,

and

`RbacL -> bacLR -> abRLc -> LRbza`.

Thus only the two `M_S` edges themselves could remain exceptional.

They do not. If the active pair is `{a,c}`, the retained endpoint cells force `zLc,zRc`; no-active-omission forces `Rcb,Lcb,RzL`. Hence `(R,z,L,c,b)` is a forbidden endpoint-favorable `P5`. If the active pair is `{b,z}`, the dual endpoint data force `zLa,zRa,czL,czR,RaL`, and `(c,z,R,a,L)` is endpoint-favorable, again a contradiction. Therefore a crossed gate always admits a favorable omission of a B-active source.

Consequently the unique no-active-omission residue is aligned. There the active sources form one `M_S` edge and the complementary `M_S` edge is `{v,s}`, where `s` is the unique inactive source. The endpoint stars and aligned gates give the literal tight `P6`s

`Q^+=(b_1,b_0,v,s,b_m,b_{m-1})`,

`Q^-=(b_1,b_0,s,v,b_m,b_{m-1})`.

The two active sources together with `A,C` have a tight Hamilton `P4`, and `|H|>10` leaves a nonempty spectator middle. Thus `Q^+,Q^-`, the same outer `P4`, and the same spectator middle give maximum three-forests with identical support partition and opposite selected orientation of `{v,s}`.

Let their active rails be `P=(p_0,...,p_r)` and `Q=(q_0,...,q_r)`, with `(p_a,p_{a+1})=(u,v)`, `(q_b,q_{b+1})=(v,u)`, and

`D=a+(r-b-1)`.

Rotate from the tail of `P` when `a>0`, or from the head of `Q` when `b<r-1`. Each successful one-turn rotation preserves the selected orientation and decreases `D`; a failed new turn gives, by boundary antisymmetry, the current reverse-wrap trimer. If `D` reaches zero, one Hamilton order begins `(u,v)` and the other ends `(v,u)`, so every exterior vertex extends one of them. Absorbing an endpoint of a frozen complementary rail gives either a spanning two-cover or a maximum three-forest with different support partition. This is port (i).

Outside port (i), fix an endpoint-favorable `P5` `K_s` omitting a B-active source `s`, together with an actual selected old edge from `s` into `B`.

## 2. Canonical low cut, rooted repair, and symmetric-difference contraction

Write `L=b_0`, `R=b_m`. The five endpoint-favorable patterns give the following two-word proposals.

1. If `K_s=(L,R,x,y,z)`, use `(L,b_1,...,b_{m-1}) | (s,R,x,y,z)`. The only uncertified turn is `(s,R,x)`. If it is bad, cut `sR`, leaving singleton `(s)` and exactly one transition.
2. If `K_s=(R,x,y,z,L)`, use `(s) | (b_1,...,b_{m-1},R,x,y,z,L)`. The unique hole is `(b_{m-1},R,x)` and the transitions are `Rx,zL`. If the hole is bad, cut `Rx`; the components are `(s) | (b_1,...,b_{m-1},R) | (x,y,z,L)`, so only `zL` remains crossing.
3. If `K_s=(x,R,L,y,z)`, use dimer `(x,s)` and `(b_1,...,b_{m-1},R,L,y,z)`. The unique hole `(b_{m-1},R,L)` is all-B and the sole transition is `Ly`; cut either hole edge if bad.
4. If `K_s=(x,y,R,L,z)`, dually use `(s,z)` with the other word; the unique all-B hole is `(R,L,b_1)` and the sole transition is `yR`; cut either hole edge if bad.
5. If `K_s=(x,y,z,L,R)`, use `(x,y,z,L,s) | (b_1,...,b_{m-1},R)`. The unique hole is `(z,L,s)`; if it is bad, cut `Ls`, leaving singleton `(s)` and exactly one transition.

If the unique hole is tight, the proposal is a physical exact two-cover `T` with `tau(T)<=2<tau(F)`, giving port (ii). Otherwise the indicated cut gives a literal tight spanning three-forest `J` with `tau(J)=1`, and the `J`-component `D_s` containing `s` is either `(s)` or an X-dimer.

We first try a direct rooted repair, but a failed repair is only a retained certificate, not another terminal port. If `D_s=(s)`, choose an old `F`-incidence at a free split copy of `s` and inspect its far split copy. If the far copy is free, install the edge and join two `J`-components. If it is occupied, boundary antisymmetry on the corresponding three physical endpoints gives a tight source-rooted blocker trimer; retain it and continue from the unchanged physical `J`.

If `D_s` is an X-dimer, let `e_0` be the old `F`-edge at the free split copy of `s`. If `e_0` leaves the dimer, use the same one-edge augmentation and the same far-copy test. If `e_0` is exactly the reverse of the `J`-dimer `d`, let `e_1` be the other old `F`-incidence at `s`; delete `d` and install `e_0,e_1`. The only possible matching collision is at the far copy of `e_1`, and any newly bad turn is likewise localized there; either defect yields the same source-rooted trimer species. If compatible, the resulting selected graph has exactly two acyclic components. All unchanged turns are inherited; the turn through `s` in the reverse-dimer case is literally an old `F`-turn, and `tau<=2` because the reversed dimer is X-X and only the leaving old incidence can add a crossing edge. Thus every successful repair gives port (ii), while every failed repair retains the source trimer and continues from `J`.

Now compare the directed matchings of `F` and `J`. For each component `C` of `F triangle J`, put

`delta(C)=|F∩C|-|J∩C| in {-1,0,1}`,

`w(C)=tau(F∩C)-tau(J∩C)`.

Because `F` is a two-cover and `J` a three-forest,

`sum delta=1`.

Set

`d=tau(F)-tau(J)=tau(F)-1>=2`;

then `sum w=d`.

For any union `S` of whole alternating components, flipping `S` from `J`-state to `F`-state changes matching cardinality by `sum_S delta` and transition count by `sum_S w`. Hence if some `S` satisfies

`sum_S delta=1`, `sum_S w<d`,

the resulting selected matching has exact two-cover cardinality and transition count below `tau(F)`. If physical, this is port (ii). If nonphysical, retain its concrete bad-turn, selected two-edge backtrack, or directed-cycle debt as port (iii).

Assume no such `S` exists. Taking complements shows that every zero-delta subfamily has total weight at most zero. Let `P,N` be the numbers of `+1,-1` components. Then `P=N+1`. Every positive component has weight at least `d`, while the total negative contribution is at least `-tau(J)=-1`, since each unit of negative transition weight spends a `J`-transition and `J` has only one. Therefore

`d >= P d-1`,

so

`(P-1)d<=1`.

As `d>=2`, we obtain `P=1,N=0`.

Thus there is a unique `F`-heavy alternating path `C*`, every other alternating component is matching-balanced, and all zero-delta subfamilies have nonpositive aggregate transition weight. A neutral component not containing the unique `J`-transition carries no transition at all; at most one neutral component carries transition data. Consequently `C*` contains at least `tau(F)-1>=2` old `F`-transitions and, since at least two source spokes are B-active, at least one actual old source-to-B incidence. All remaining nontrivial motion is concentrated on this source-bearing path `C*`.

## 3. The zipper, the singleton loop, and the sharp first-loss rectangle

Orient the heavy path

`C*=e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2,...,f_{m-1},e_m`,

with `e_i∈F-J`, `f_i∈J-F`, and split-copy coordinates

`e_i=a_{i-1}->b_i`, `f_i=a_i->b_i`.

For a selected edge `g`, let `chi(g)=1` exactly when it crosses `X|B`, and define

`S_q=sum_{i<=q}(chi(e_i)-chi(f_i))`.

Replacing the first `q` `J`-edges by the first `q` `F`-edges gives a proper-prefix matching of `J`-cardinality and transition count `1+S_q`. Since `w(C*)>=d>=2` while the terminal edge contributes at most one crossing, some proper prefix has positive `S_q`. Let `r` be the first such index. Then

`S_r=1`, `chi(e_r)=1`, `chi(f_r)=0`.

Let `b_m` be the fixed far unmatched split copy. If `a_r!=b_m`, add

`g_r=a_r->b_m`.

The result has exact two-cover matching cardinality. Let `sigma(x)` be the `X/B` shore bit. Since `chi(u->v)=sigma(u) xor sigma(v)`, telescoping modulo two gives

`S_q mod 2 = sigma(a_0) xor sigma(a_q)`,

`w(C*) mod 2 = sigma(a_0) xor sigma(b_m)`,

and therefore

`chi(g_q)=(S_q mod 2) xor (w(C*) mod 2)`.

At the first positive prefix the closure has transition count `2` when `w(C*)` is odd and `3` when it is even.

The neutral components have nonpositive aggregate weight and can spend at most the single `J`-transition, so

`d<=w(C*)<=d+1`.

Since `tau(F)=d+1>=3`, the first-positive closure has transition count strictly below `tau(F)` except in the unique numerical cell

`tau(F)=3`, `tau(J)=1`, `w(C*)=2`.

Thus every nonsharp first-positive closure is port (iii), unless it is physically tight and acyclic, in which case it is port (ii).

A closing edge fails to exist only when `a_q=b_m`, in which case it would be a physical loop. The physical labels `a_q` along one alternating path are pairwise distinct, so this happens for at most one `q`. At that index both split copies of the same physical vertex are unmatched in the prefix matching. This is port (iv), the unique singleton-defect interruption.

It remains to analyze the sharp cell. Normalize every zero-delta component completely to its `F`-state. Let

`W_0=sum_D w(D)<=0`

be their aggregate transition weight. The normalized prefix `hat M_q` has three-forest matching cardinality and

`tau(hat M_q)=1+S_q+W_0`.

Whenever `a_q!=b_m`, define

`hat N_q=hat M_q+g_q`, `g_q=a_q->b_m`, `gamma_q=chi(g_q)`.

Then

`T_q:=tau(hat N_q)=1+S_q+W_0+gamma_q`,

and consecutive defined closures differ by the literal `K_{2,2}` switch

`hat N_q=hat N_{q-1}-{f_q,g_{q-1}}+{e_q,g_q}`.

The parity identity implies

`T_q-T_{q-1} in {-2,0,2}`.

An upward `+2` step occurs exactly when both removed edges are same-side and both installed edges cross `X|B`.

In the sharp cell,

`W_0=(tau(F)-tau(J))-w(C*)=2-2=0`,

and `w(C*)` is even, so

`gamma_q=S_q mod 2`.

For `q<r`, first-positive minimality and nonnegativity of the prefix transition count give `S_q in {-1,0}`; hence every defined closure before `r` has `T_q=1`. At `r`, `S_r=1`; if `g_r` exists, then `T_r=3=tau(F)`. Thus `r` is exactly the first admissible numerical threshold index.

A literal first-loss rectangle exists only when both `g_{r-1}` and `g_r` exist. If either is the unique forbidden loop, the branch is already port (iv), and no adjacent switch is asserted. Otherwise the first-loss switch is

`{f_r,g_{r-1}} <-> {e_r,g_r}`,

from two same-side edges to two crossing edges. Its old diagonal contains one X-X edge and one B-B edge, and the X-X member is incident with the physical X-endpoint `z` of the newly installed old `F`-transition `e_r`. If `z` is a source, this sharp source-rooted marked rectangle is port (v). If `z` is not a source, then `z` is the unique nonspoke vertex `v`, and we enter the `v`-rooted sharp branch.

## 4. The v-rooted marked package and the E9001 interface

In the `v`-rooted sharp branch the installed old transition `e_r` is the distinguished `v-B` edge of `F`. Put `d_v=d_F(v)` and let `e_XX` be the number of selected X-X edges of `F`. The three sources are internal, so their selected degrees contribute six on `X`. Hence

`6+d_v=2e_XX+tau(F)=2e_XX+3`.

The spectator-frame ledger gives `d_v in {1,2}`. Parity forces

`d_v=1`, `e_XX=2`.

Therefore

`b_X=4-e_XX=2`,

and from `tau=b_X+b_B-2` we obtain

`b_B=3`.

Since the unique selected edge incident with `v` is the displayed crossing edge, `{v}` is one X-block. The other X-block contains all three sources. Each source is internal in `F`, so, after complete directional reversal if necessary, it occurs literally as

`B_L -> s_1 -> s_2 -> s_3 -> B_R`.

Thus the three `F`-transitions are exactly the unique `v-B` edge and the two opposite-polarity boundary source gates `B_L->s_1` and `s_3->B_R`; both source gates are X-mated by the adjacent internal source edges.

Let `n_F,n_J` count `F`- and `J`-transitions on `C*`. Since `w(C*)=2`, globally `F` has three transitions and `J` has one,

`n_F-n_J=2`, with `0<=n_F<=3`, `0<=n_J<=1`.

Hence

`(n_F,n_J)=(2,0)` or `(3,1)`.

The transition `e_r` is the first-positive pivot. In the `(2,0)` case there is no negative `J`-contribution on `C*`, so no `F`-transition can precede `e_r`; the other `F`-transition on `C*` is therefore a source gate strictly after it. In the `(3,1)` case, if both source gates preceded `e_r`, their two positive contributions could be offset by at most the unique `J`-transition, forcing a positive proper prefix before `r`. Thus again at least one source gate lies strictly after the `v`-transition, and any source gate before it is neutralized by the unique `J`-transition.

We have obtained port (vi):

`tau(F)=3`, `tau(J)=1`, `w(C*)=2`,

with physical `J`, a unique source-bearing `F`-heavy alternating path `C*`, `d_F(v)=1`, singleton `v`-block, ordered source block

`B_L->s_1->s_2->s_3->B_R`,

exactly the unique `v-B` transition and two source boundary gates in `F`, the `v-B` edge as the first-positive zipper pivot, on-`C*` ledger `(2F,0J)` or `(3F,1J)`, and at least one X-mated source boundary gate strictly later on `C*`. The neutral components have aggregate transition weight zero. No componentwise zero-weight claim is used or needed.

We also record the pair-deletion rigidity needed by E9001. Every exact two-cover of `G=H-{A,C}` has two nontrivial rails. If `G` were Hamiltonian, a Hamilton path of `G` together with the dimer `(A,C)` would two-cover `H`. If an exact cover of `G` had singleton rail `{x}`, boundary antisymmetry on `{A,x,C}` would give a tight trimer on those three vertices; together with the other rail this would again two-cover `H`. Both are impossible.

Consequently port (vi) supplies precisely the nonambient E9001 data: the physical `J` with `tau(J)=1`; the matching-balanced neutral components; the unique heavy path `C*`; the first-positive index `r`; the exact `(2F,0J)/(3F,1J)` transition ledger; the singleton `v`-block and literal ordered source block; a later X-mated source gate; and nontriviality of every exact pair-deletion two-cover. Initializing every neutral component in its `J`-state makes the initial prefix exactly `J` and therefore physical. No R24/R5 input and no hidden componentwise-neutral assertion occurs.

## 5. E9003 entrance at every leaf

Retain throughout the original phase-one source checkpoint

`F_0=(A,C)|F`.

All constructions above belong to the same certificate-retaining prepayment analysis, before any phase-zero transition. We verify the exact external interface needed to invoke E9003, *First-source prepayment phase descent*: a retained phase-one source packet together with an actual graph-intrinsic proper tight path or certified proper-cycle break born from that analysis. E9003 then returns a spanning two-cover or a checkpoint of phased rank strictly below the participating phase-one source checkpoint; it does not authorize replay from a later phase-zero parent.

For port (i), the aligned lane already contains the literal tight proper path

`Q^-=(b_1,b_0,s,v,b_m,b_{m-1})`.

It is born directly from the retained source packet and is therefore an admissible E9003 input.

For port (ii), let `T` be the physical exact two-cover of `G` with `tau(T)<tau(F)`. Then `T!=F`. The two nonidentical exact covers `F,T` of the same pair-deletion residue, with the same restored anchor dimer `(A,C)`, form E9003's source-labelled common-residue portal. Thus the E9003 conclusion applies.

For port (iii), let `M` be the lower-transition exact-cardinality matching. If `M` is nonphysical, its defect is concrete. A bad selected turn yields its complete-reversal tight trimer by boundary antisymmetry. A selected two-edge backtrack is a literal packet-born reversal-dimer event. If all selected turns are tight but a directed cycle occurs, a certified break of that vertex-simple cycle is a proper tight path. Each object is emitted by the present prepayment construction with its source ancestry retained, so each is a legal E9003 birth rather than an unrelated path existing elsewhere in `H`.

For port (iv), let `hat M_q` be the normalized prefix at the unique loop coincidence `a_q=b_m=x`. If `hat M_q` is nonphysical, the same bad-turn, backtrack, or cycle alternatives give an eligible birth. Suppose instead that it is physical. Both split copies of `x` are unmatched, so `x` is a singleton rail of this spanning three-forest of `G`. Since `|H|>10`, the other two rails contain

`|G|-1=|H|-3>=8`

vertices and hence at least one selected edge `y->z`. The vertices `x,y,z` are distinct, and boundary antisymmetry makes exactly one of `(x,y,z)` and `(z,y,x)` tight. This proper trimer is born from the loop packet itself and is an admissible E9003 input.

For port (v), both adjacent closures exist by definition. The closure immediately preceding the first-loss switch has exact two-cover matching cardinality and transition count `tau(F)-2`. If physical, it is a nonidentical common-residue exact cover and enters E9003 as in port (ii). If nonphysical, its explicit physicality debt enters as in port (iii). Thus the source-rooted first-loss rectangle carries an E9003 continuation without requiring a separate global absorber.

The same principle applies to port (vi). This branch is not the initial singleton-loop case, so the normalized initial lower-transition closure is defined. It has exact two-cover matching cardinality and transition count strictly below `tau(F)`, and therefore yields an E9003 entrance by the same physical/nonphysical dichotomy. Independently, the full `v`-rooted marked package remains the designated structural export to E9001. The E9001 and E9003 continuations are distinct available uses of the same structural leaf; neither erases the other.

We have exhausted the complete active-source gate split, the five canonical low cuts and rooted repair, the symmetric-difference alternatives, the one-dimensional zipper and its unique loop, and the source/`v` dichotomy at the first sharp loss. Hence one of ports (i)-(vi) is produced. Port (vi) is the designated E9001 interface, while every structural port separately carries the one-time E9003 entrance just verified. This proves the marked spectator-frame reduction theorem. ∎
