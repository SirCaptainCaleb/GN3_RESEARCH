# E8999: Singleton-star spectator qualification

## Theorem

Let `H` be a smallest Strong Level-(1) boundary-tournament counterexample with `|H|>10`. Fix distinct anchors `a,c`, let

`B=(b_0,...,b_m)`

be the tight spectator path, put `X={v,p,q,r}`, and define

`W:=V(B)∪{v}=V(H)-{a,c,p,q,r}`.

Thus the top fiber is

`G=H[W∪{p,q,r}]=H-{a,c}=X⊔V(B)`.

Assume the synchronized singleton-star packet in the following explicit fiber form:

- the rank-zero fiber `H[W]` has exact two-cover `(v)|B`;
- for each source `s∈{p,q,r}`, the singleton fiber `H[W∪{s}]` has exact two-cover `(v,s)|B`;
- for each pair `{s,t}⊂{p,q,r}`, the rank-two fiber `H[W∪{s,t}]` has exact two-cover consisting of a tight trimer on `{v,s,t}` together with `B`;
- `X` has no tight Hamilton path;
- `(a,s,c)` is tight for `s=p,q,r`; and
- the retained top-fiber exact two-cover `F` of `G` has all three sources `p,q,r` internal.

Then one of the following holds.

1. **Pair-deletion portal.** There is a second exact two-cover `T` of `G`, nonidentical with `F` up to rail exchange, in which at least one source is an endpoint. The covers `F,T`, with the restored anchor dimer `(a,c)`, form the source-labelled common-residue portal admitted by Engine E9003, *First-source prepayment phase descent*. Consequently E9003 gives either a spanning two-cover of `H` or phased rank strictly below the participating phase-one source checkpoint(s).

2. **Transitive spectator-gate frame.** The four-cell `X` is a transitive matching-height cell. Both spectator ends satisfy the universal reverse stars

   `(b_1,b_0,x)` and `(x,b_m,b_{m-1})`

   for every `x∈X`; each end carries the full three-matching gate packet described below; and the retained physical cover `F` satisfies

   `τ(F)≥3`,

   `τ=b_X+b_B-2=2b_X+d_F(v)-2`,

   `b_B=b_X+d_F(v)`,

   `d_F(v)∈{1,2}`,

   with at least two of `p,q,r` incident in `F` with an actual `B`-vertex.

The second alternative is the spectator/source frame exported downstream.

## 1. Pair-deletion rigidity and the four-cell reduction

Every exact two-cover of `H-{a,c}` has two nontrivial rails. Indeed, if one rail were the singleton `u`, boundary antisymmetry makes exactly one of `(a,u,c)` and `(c,u,a)` tight; that trimer together with the other rail would two-cover `H`, a contradiction.

We next classify `X`. For a four-set with no Hamilton path, fix one tight turn and encode one representative from each reversal pair of turns in the order

`012,013,021,023,031,032,102,103,123,132,203,213`.

Fixing the first bit to be tight, reject an assignment whenever one of the twenty-four vertex orders has both consecutive turns tight. Exactly four signatures remain:

`100110010101`,

`101100010110`,

`110010110001`,

`111000110010`.

Three are the transitive matching-height cells: the six ordinary edges split into the three perfect matchings `M_R,M_S,M_L`, and a turn `xyz` is tight exactly when

`M(xy)>M(yz)`

for the resulting strict order of the matchings. The fourth signature is cyclic.

The cyclic cell has a stronger extension property: every fifth vertex `d` lies in a Hamilton `P5` on the five vertices whose two endpoints belong to the original four-cell. To see this, normalize the cyclic four-cell as `{a,b,c,z}` and suppose no such `P5` exists. If `(d,a,b)` is tight, successive failures of the candidate Hamilton words force

`dab, adz, czd, bdz, dba, bdc, dbz, bda, cad, zda, bad`

to be tight in succession, ending with the complete reversal of the first turn. If instead `(b,a,d)` is tight, the dual chain

`bad, cda, dcz, cdb, abd, zdb, cbd, adb, daz, adc, dab`

ends with its reversal. Either case contradicts boundary antisymmetry.

Apply this extension property with `d=b_0`. In the cyclic case there is a Hamilton `P5` on `X∪{b_0}` whose endpoints lie in `X`; at least one endpoint is a source. Together with the untouched suffix `(b_1,...,b_m)` this gives an exact two-cover `T` of `G` exposing a source endpoint. Since all sources are internal in `F`, the covers `F` and `T` are nonidentical. Hence the pair-deletion alternative holds.

We may therefore assume from now on that the pair-deletion alternative does not hold. Then `X` is transitive matching-height.

## 2. Quiet endpoints and the gate packet

Neither `X∪{b_0}` nor `X∪{b_m}` has a Hamilton `P5`. Consider the left end. If a Hamilton `P5` on `X∪{b_0}` had `b_0` as an endpoint, deleting it would give a Hamilton `P4` on `X`. If `b_0` were internal, both endpoints would lie in `X`, and at least one would be a source; adjoining the untouched suffix `(b_1,...,b_m)` would again give the pair-deletion alternative. The right end is identical.

For every `x∈X`, choose a tight trimer on `X-{x}`. If `(x,b_0,b_1)` were tight, the long path

`(x,b_0,b_1,...,b_m)`

together with that trimer would be an exact two-cover of `G` exposing a source endpoint: when `x` is a source it is exposed on the long rail, while for `x=v` both ends of the complementary trimer are sources. Thus `(x,b_0,b_1)` is bad, and boundary antisymmetry gives

`(b_1,b_0,x)`

tight for every `x∈X`. Dually,

`(x,b_m,b_{m-1})`

is tight for every `x∈X`. These are the universal reverse endpoint stars.

It remains to identify the full local gate structure.

### Gate lemma

Let `X` be a transitive matching-height four-cell with perfect matchings

`M_R>M_S>M_L`,

and let `d` be a fifth vertex such that `X+d` has no Hamilton `P5`. Then:

1. some edge of `M_L` is fully outgoing from `d`, meaning both orders `(d,x,y)` and `(d,y,x)` are tight;
2. some edge of `M_R` is fully incoming to `d`, meaning both `(x,y,d)` and `(y,x,d)` are tight; and
3. the two `M_S` edges have opposite full polarities, one fully outgoing and the other fully incoming.

For the first assertion, normalize

`M_R={{t,r},{l,s}}`,

`M_S={{t,s},{l,r}}`,

`M_L={{t,l},{r,s}}`.

If neither bottom edge were fully outgoing, choose on each a reverse witness. There are four possible choices; respectively the following paired Hamilton candidates show that at least one choice of the unresolved seam is tight and hence gives a `P5`:

`(l,s,r,d,t)` / `(s,l,t,d,r)`,

`(l,r,s,d,t)` / `(r,l,t,d,s)`,

`(t,s,r,d,l)` / `(s,t,l,d,r)`,

`(t,r,s,d,l)` / `(r,t,l,d,s)`.

The turns lying wholly in `X` are fixed by matching height, and each remaining pair consists of complete reversals. Thus `P5`-freeness is contradicted. This proves the first assertion; the second is its total-reversal dual.

Choose the outgoing `M_L` edge and incoming `M_R` edge. They meet in one vertex; relabel so they are `{t,l}` and `{t,r}`. Put

`A=[dts]`, `B=[dst]`, `C=[drl]`, `D=[dlr]`,

where brackets mean that the displayed turn is tight. `P5`-freeness forces `A=B` and `C=D`: if either equality failed, one of the one-hole words

`(l,d,t,s,r)`, `(r,d,l,s,t)`, `(t,r,l,d,s)`, `(s,l,r,d,t)`

would be Hamilton, according to the unequal case. Finally `A` and `C` cannot agree. If `A=C=0`, their reversals are tight; the remaining seam is then forced so that `(s,l,r,d,t)` is Hamilton. If `A=C=1`, then also `B=D=1`, and the remaining seam is forced so that `(s,d,r,l,t)` is Hamilton. Hence `A≠C`. Equivalently, exactly one middle edge is fully outgoing from `d` and the complementary middle edge is fully incoming. This proves the lemma.

Applying the gate lemma to `d=b_0` and `d=b_m`, each spectator endpoint carries a complete alternating gate packet, in addition to the universal reverse star already proved.

## 3. Transition and degree ledger of the retained source cover

Split the two rails of `F` into maximal consecutive `X`-blocks and `B`-blocks. Let `b_X,b_B` be their numbers, and let `τ` be the number of selected `X|B` transition edges. Contract every block to one vertex. The result is still a forest of two paths, now on `b_X+b_B` vertices, and all of its edges are exactly the transitions. Hence

`τ=b_X+b_B-2`.                                                    (1)

Because `X` has no Hamilton `P4`, we have `b_X≥2`: a single `X`-block would itself be a tight path through all four vertices of `X`. Also `b_B≥1` because `B` is nonempty.

We claim that `τ≥3`. If `τ=1`, equation (1) forces `(b_X,b_B)=(2,1)`. The contracted forest has one edge and one isolated `X`-block, so one rail of `F` lies wholly in `X`. If that rail is a singleton, it must be `{v}` because all sources are internal, contradicting pair-deletion nontriviality. If it has at least two vertices, one of its two rail endpoints is a source, again contradicting source internality.

If `τ=2`, then `(b_X,b_B)` is `(3,1)` or `(2,2)`. In the first case the unique `B`-block can meet at most two `X`-blocks, so an `X`-block is an isolated rail, impossible by the preceding argument. In the second case the contracted two-path forest is, up to reversal and exchange of the rails, one of the following: two disjoint `X-B` edges; an `X-B-X` path plus an isolated `B`-block; or a `B-X-B` path plus an isolated `X`-block. The last shape again has an isolated `X`-rail. In the first two shapes every terminal `X`-block must have `v` as its outer rail endpoint, since a source endpoint is forbidden; but there are two distinct terminal `X`-blocks, so this would require the single vertex `v` to lie in two blocks. Thus `τ=2` is impossible as well. Therefore

`τ(F)≥3`.                                                         (2)

Now let `d=d_F(v)` and let `e_XX` be the number of selected edges with both ends in `X`. Every `X`-block is a path, so

`e_XX=|X|-b_X=4-b_X`.

The three sources are internal in `F` and therefore each have selected degree two. Summing selected degrees over `X` gives

`6+d=2e_XX+τ`.

Substituting the preceding expression for `e_XX` yields

`τ=2b_X+d-2`.                                                     (3)

Comparing (1) and (3) gives

`b_B=b_X+d`.                                                      (4)

Since `F` is a two-path forest, `d≤2`. If `d=0`, then `v` is an isolated component and hence a singleton rail of `F`, contradicting pair-deletion nontriviality. Therefore

`d_F(v)∈{1,2}`.                                                   (5)

Finally, at least two distinct sources are physically `B`-active. Suppose not. Then two distinct sources, say `p` and `q`, have no selected incidence with `B`. Since each is internal in `F`, both have degree two already in the selected subgraph `F[X]`. But `F[X]` is a forest of maximum degree two on four vertices. A four-vertex path forest containing two distinct degree-two vertices must be the spanning `P4`, contradicting the hypothesis that `X` has no Hamilton `P4`. Hence at least two of `p,q,r` have selected source-to-`B` incidences.

Equations (1)-(5), together with the endpoint stars and matching-gate packets, are exactly the promised static source/spectator ledger.

## 4. The E9003 side portal

For completeness, we verify that the cover discrepancy constructed in the cyclic branch is genuinely a source-labelled input to Engine E9003, rather than merely a second cover.

Let

`F=F_1|F_2`

be the retained source cover and

`T=T_1|T_2`

the source-exposing cover obtained above. Both are exact two-covers of the same residue `G=H-{a,c}`. Restore the literal anchor dimer `(a,c)` to each; this gives two spanning three-forests born in the same retained phase-one source packet.

If the unordered rail-support partitions of `F` and `T` differ, some selected edge `xy` of one cover, say `T`, joins the two `F`-supports. Both `F`-rails are nontrivial, so choose an `F`-neighbor `z` of `x` on `x`'s rail. Boundary antisymmetry on `{z,x,y}` makes exactly one of

`(z,x,y)` and `(y,x,z)`

tight. Thus the comparison itself emits a proper tight trimer using the physical crossing edge and an inherited source-cover edge. The birth is intrinsic to the retained prepayment packet.

Suppose instead that the support partitions agree. Align the two rails by support and compare one `F`-order

`P=(v_0,...,v_k)`

with the corresponding `T`-order `Q`. If `Q` is not literally `P`, read along `Q` the indices of its vertices in the `P`-order. Some consecutive `Q`-vertices are `v_i,v_j` with `i>j`. If `i=j+1`, `Q` selects the reverse of an `F`-edge, giving a literal selected-reversal birth. If `i>j+1`, test the turns

`(v_{i-1},v_i,v_j)` and `(v_i,v_j,v_{j+1})`.

A bad first turn gives the tight reverse trimer `(v_j,v_i,v_{i-1})`; a bad second turn gives `(v_{j+1},v_j,v_i)`. If both are tight, then

`v_i,v_j,v_{j+1},...,v_{i-1},v_i`

is a proper tight cycle, and deleting any certified cycle edge gives the required proper-path portal. Thus any literal-order disagreement emits a proper path or certified cycle directly from the same comparison packet. If neither rail disagrees in support or order, the covers are identical up to rail exchange, contrary to the source endpoint/internal discrepancy.

Hence the pair-deletion alternative always carries an actual certificate-preserving proper-path/cycle birth from its retained phase-one source analysis. Engine E9003 applies exactly in this situation: for a retained finite packet of phase-one maximum three-forest checkpoints, such a legally born proper path or cycle yields either a spanning two-cover of `H` or a checkpoint of phased rank strictly below every participating source. No claim is made that this descent globally extinguishes a later phase-zero return.

If the pair-deletion alternative never occurs, the preceding sections show simultaneously that `X` is transitive matching-height, both endpoint five-cells are `P5`-free, the universal reverse stars and full endpoint gate packets hold, and the old physical source cover satisfies the complete transition/block/degree ledger with at least two `B`-active source spokes. This is the transitive spectator-gate alternative.

The two cases exhaust the synchronized singleton-star packet. The argument uses only the synchronized input, smallest-counterexample minimality through pair-deletion nontriviality, and boundary antisymmetry; no quarantined small-order theorem is used. ∎
