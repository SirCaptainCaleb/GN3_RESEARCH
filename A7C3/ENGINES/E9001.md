## Composite proof: marked low-transition exchange compressor

`B_L->s1->s2->s3->B_R`, `J` the canonical three-forest, and `C*` the alternating augmenter with
`e_i=a_{i-1}->b_i`, `f_i=a_i->b_i`, first-positive index `r`, and ordinary prefixes `M_q` (`q<r`) with neutral components in J-state. The retained input guarantees: every physical pre-r prefix has `tau<=1`; at a first nonphysical exchange `M_{q+1}=M_q-{f_{q+1}}+{e_{q+1}}`, every new blocker contains `e_{q+1}`; aggregate neutral weight is zero; and for `g_q=a_q->b_m`,
`tau(M_q)=1+S_q`, `chi(g_q)≡S_q (mod 2)`, where `S_q=Σ_{i<=q}(chi(e_i)-chi(f_i))`.

*Entrance.* Since `S_q∈{-1,0}`, `chi(g_q)=1` exactly for `S_q=-1`; hence every nonloop canonical candidate `C_q=M_q+g_q` has `tau(C_q)=1`. Equal free labels give the loop state. Distinct labels on different rails make `g_q` acyclic, so only its endpoint seams can be new: either `C_q` is physical or the output is seam-only debt. Same-rail free labels give cycle/backtrack.

For loop/cycle/backtrack with `q+1<r`, inspect `M_{q+1}`. If nonphysical, output the named `e_{q+1}` blocker. If physical, the new free copies are distinct and on different rails, so `g_{q+1}` is nonloop, cross-rail, acyclic and nonbacktracking, again with `tau=1`; thus one marked step reaches a later physical/seam-only candidate. At `q=r-1`, the rank-zero topological state itself is an output.

*Physical branch.* Let `T=C_q` be physical. For a two-cover, `tau=b_X+b_B-2`, where `b_X,b_B` count maximal X- and B-blocks. Since X has no tight Hamilton P4, `b_X>=2`, and `b_B>=1`; `tau(T)=1` forces `(b_X,b_B)=(2,1)`, one mixed rail plus one nontrivial isolated-X rail, exposing at least two retained sources.

On `C*`, `C_q` has the F-state split-copy occupancy. Thus an exposed source, internal in F, has its missing F-port off `C*`, at the F-only endpoint of a neutral alternating path still in J-state. Pivot by deleting its selected J-only mate and installing the missing F-only edge. Let `mu=# selected neutral F\J edges`. Each successful pivot raises `mu` by one and no later frontier pivot removes an installed F-only edge, so some pivot must fail.

The source-incident old-F edges off `C*` are the two SS mates and, in the two-transition cell, one boundary GATE. If an exposed source has the GATE frontier, the cover is still `tau=1`; the block form supplies another exposed source whose missing edge is SS. Choose it instead. Hence the controlled chase never attempts GATE and first fails on SS.

Successful SS pivots are same-side. None can delete the unique selected transition, since that would make a physical `tau=0` two-cover, impossible from the block equation; therefore physical descendants stay `tau=1`. Both SS mates cannot become F-like simultaneously: then `s2` has both ports occupied, each outer source has its inner port occupied, and at least one outer boundary gate remains F-like on untouched `C*`, leaving at most one exposed source, contradiction. So at most one SS pivot succeeds.

With zero successes, the first failed SS attempt is the output certificate: failed SS edge, deleted mate, predecessor rails and local neighbors. If the deleted mate lies on the vacancy rail, the installed edge closes the surviving segment, giving CYCLE; otherwise it is an acyclic tail swap and only endpoint seams can fail. In an acyclic `tau=0` candidate the rails are all-X and all-B, so the installed edge is SS; two bad seams around it would reverse-concatenate to a tight Hamilton P4 on X, so exactly one is bad.

With one success, assume it installs `s1->s2`; the dual is symmetric. The remaining `s2->s3` attempt must fail and cannot be CYCLE, since that would require selected `s3->s2`, colliding at `s2,in`. The source-trimer turn is tight, so the unique bad seam is outer. Tau-one exposure forces `s1` to be the second exposed source; hence `B_L->s1` is the unique off-`C*` GATE, `s3->B_R` lies on `C*`, and its selected same-side mate is `s3->v`. Thus the bad seam is `(s2,s3,v)` and `(v,s3,s2)` is tight. The OUT presentation would force forbidden isolated X-dimer `(s1,s2)`, so the last physical predecessor is
`(s1,s2,B_T)|(s3,v)`
with nonempty B-block `B_T`; the failed candidate is
`(s1,s2,s3,v)|B_T`,
has `tau=0`, and sole bad turn `(s2,s3,v)`. Split-copy uniqueness on `C*` places the consecutive first-positive packet

## Audit repair 1/4: standalone frame + entrance lemmas

The posted composition has the right mechanism, but its phrase “the retained input guarantees” suppresses proof obligations that the terminal format requires inline. I am repairing rather than failing it.

Add explicitly to the retained input frame: `tau(J)=1`; every physical exact two-cover arising in this frame has two nontrivial rails; the only F transition edges are the unique v-B incidence and the two boundary source gates; v has no other F-incidence; the v-B transition and at least one boundary gate lie on `C*`; and on `C*` the F/J transition counts are `(3,1)` or `(2,0)`, with the v-B transition preceding the opposite gate in the `(2,0)` cell. These are frame hypotheses, not conclusions of the compressor.

*Marked-prefix lemma.* With all balanced components fixed in J-state, the only changes from `M_0=J` to `M_q` are `f_i -> e_i` for `i<=q`. Hence
`tau(M_q)=1+S_q`, `S_q=sum_{i<=q}(chi(e_i)-chi(f_i))`.
For `q<r`, first-positive minimality gives `S_q<=0`; nonnegativity of transition count gives `S_q in {-1,0}`. If `M_h=M_{h-1}-{f_h}+{e_h}` is the first nonphysical prefix, deleting `f_h` cannot create a bad turn, backtrack, or directed cycle, so every newly created blocker contains `e_h`.

*Exact closure ledger.* The weight excess of `C*` is two, hence modulo two
`sum_i chi(e_i)+sum_i chi(f_i)=0`.
Writing `sigma(x)` for the X/B side of x, cancellation of internal zipper labels gives `sigma(a_0)=sigma(b_m)`. Likewise
`S_q ≡ sum_{i<=q}(chi(e_i)+chi(f_i)) ≡ sigma(a_0)+sigma(a_q) ≡ chi(g_q) (mod 2)`.
Thus `chi(g_q)=0` when `S_q=0` and `1` when `S_q=-1`, so every distinct-label canonical matching has
`tau(M_q+g_q)=1+S_q+chi(g_q)=1`.
It has exact-two-cover cardinality because `g_q` joins the two free split copies.

*One-step topological transfer.* For same-rail debt, let P start at the fixed free-IN label `b_m` and end at `a_q`, and put `f=a_{q+1}->b_{q+1}`, `e=a_q->b_{q+1}`. If f lay on P, deleting f leaves the P-suffix `b_{q+1}...a_q`, and adding e closes that suffix into a directed cycle, contradicting physicality of `M_{q+1}`. Hence f lies on another rail; deleting it splits that rail into a prefix ending at `a_{q+1}` and suffix beginning at `b_{q+1}`, while e joins P to the suffix. Therefore `b_m` and `a_{q+1}` lie on different rails. Equality `a_{q+1}=b_m` would put f out of the initial vertex `b_m` of P, again placing f on P, impossible. So the next canonical edge is distinct-label, cross-rail, acyclic and nonbacktracking. In the loop case `a_q=b_m=s`, s is a singleton rail; f cannot start at s because `s,out` is free, so the identical tail-swap argument again leaves `s` and `a_{q+1}` distinct on different rails. If `M_{q+1}` is nonphysical, the marked-prefix lemma localizes the failure at `e_{q+1}`. This proves the entrance transfer claimed in the composition without external notebook references.

## Audit repair 2/4: physical source chase

*Tau-one source exposure.* For a physical canonical cover `T`, let `b_X,b_B` count maximal X- and B-blocks on its two rails. Then `tau(T)=b_X+b_B-2`. Since X has no tight Hamilton P4, `b_X>=2`; since B is nonempty, `b_B>=1`. As `tau(T)=1`, necessarily `(b_X,b_B)=(2,1)`: one rail is mixed X-B (or B-X), the other is entirely X. Both rails are nontrivial. The isolated-X rail therefore has a source endpoint. If the X-side endpoint of the mixed rail is a source, that is a second; if it is v, then v is absent from the isolated rail, whose two endpoints are both sources. Thus every physical tau-one state exposes at least two retained sources.

*Neutral vacancy frontier and rank.* On `C*`, the closed prefix selects `e_1,...,e_q,f_{q+1},...,f_{m-1},g_q`, occupying exactly the same split copies as the full F-state `e_1,...,e_m`. Common F∩J incidences are selected as well. An exposed source s is internal in F, so its unique vacant F-port lies neither on `C*` nor on a common edge. At that split copy there is an F-incidence but no selected J-incidence, hence the balanced component containing it is an alternating path with an F-only endpoint there and a J-only opposite endpoint. Starting from J-state, each successful elementary vacancy pivot replaces the next selected `J\F` edge by its paired previously unselected `F\J` edge. Inductively the path is an F-prefix followed by a J-suffix with the vacancy at their frontier. No later frontier move removes an installed F-edge. Therefore `mu=# selected F\J edges on balanced components` rises by exactly one at every successful pivot and is finitely bounded.

*GATE avoidance and tau-one persistence.* Suppose an exposed source presents a missing boundary gate. It cannot lie on `C*`, whose split occupancy stays F-like. Hence the `(3,1)` C* placement is impossible and we are in `(2,0)`: the v-B transition and opposite boundary gate lie on `C*`, while the missing gate is the unique off-C* F-transition and the unique off-C* J-transition remains selected unless removed by a pivot. All earlier controlled successes are SS and same-side. None can delete that unique J-transition, because doing so would produce a physical exact two-cover of transition count zero, impossible from `tau=b_X+b_B-2>=1`. Thus the current cover still has tau one and exposes at least two retained sources. Choose another exposed source t. Its missing F-incidence is off `C*` and source-incident, but cannot be the same unique gate, so it is one of the two SS mates. Hence the policy can always choose SS instead of GATE. Every successful controlled pivot is same-side and, by the same tau-zero obstruction, preserves tau one.

*Finite chase and at most one success.* The bounded rank mu forbids an all-success trajectory. After every success the state is physical tau one, so the preceding paragraph supplies another controlled SS attempt until one fails. If both old SS mates were simultaneously F-like selected, `s2` would have both ports occupied and each outer source would have its inner source-block port occupied. At least one outer boundary gate lies on untouched F-like `C*`, so at least one outer source also has its boundary port occupied. At most one retained source could then be exposed, contradicting the two-source exposure just proved. Installed F-edges never disappear, so at most one SS pivot can succeed before the forced failed SS pivot.

## Audit repair 3/4: zero-success exit

Suppose the first controlled SS attempt already fails. In the IN-vacancy orientation write the missing old-F edge and selected mate as `e=x->d`, `f=x->d'`, where d and x are consecutive retained sources and the physical predecessor rail V begins at d.

If f lies on V, deleting f leaves the directed V-prefix from d through x intact, and installing `x->d` closes that prefix into a directed cycle. This is exactly the CYCLE certificate. If f lies on the other rail, deleting it splits that rail into a prefix ending at x and a suffix beginning at d'; adding `x->d` joins the prefix to V and is an acyclic two-rail tail swap. It cannot create a two-edge backtrack, since that would require the same two physical vertices to have occupied both original rails. Every inherited turn remains tight, so the only possible new bad turns are `(p,x,d)` when the predecessor p of x exists and `(x,d,y)` when the successor y of d exists. At least one existing seam is bad at first failure; complete reversal gives `(d,x,p)` or `(y,d,x)` tight respectively. The OUT-vacancy orientation is the exact directional dual. This proves the literal CYCLE/LEFT/RIGHT zero-success certificate promised by the compressor.

There is also the stated tau-zero sharpening. If the failed acyclic candidate has `tau=0`, its two rails are side-pure, one spanning X and the other B. Hence the installed edge e is necessarily SS, not B-B. If e is internal on the all-X rail and both adjacent seams were bad, complete reversal of those two seams would concatenate through e's endpoints to a tight Hamilton P4 on X, contradicting the retained frame. Thus exactly one adjacent seam is bad. At an endpoint only one seam exists, so again the failure is a single-cap SS certificate.

For later use in the one-success audit, record the cycle normal form. In any physical tau-one predecessor, if an SS frontier `e=x->d` fails by CYCLE, then the vacancy rail V beginning at d starts with the selected reverse dimer `d->x`. Indeed its selected mate `f=x->d'` lies on V. If f is crossing, then `d' in B`; f is the unique transition, so the V-segment before f is all-X. The other nontrivial isolated-X rail uses at least two X-vertices, while V already contains d and x, forcing V to begin `d->x->d'`. If f is same-side, then `d' in X`; V cannot be the mixed rail, since it would contain three X-vertices while the isolated-X rail needs at least two more. Thus V itself is the isolated-X rail and, because the mixed rail still contains an X-vertex, V is exactly `(d,x,d')`. Again its first selected edge is `d->x`.

## Audit repair 4/4: rigid one-success kernel and completion

First, a physical tau-one cover cannot have an isolated two-vertex X-rail `(s,t)` consisting of two retained sources. Otherwise the other rail together with any tight Hamilton P4 on `{A,C,s,t}` would cover H. Such a P4 always exists: if `(A,s,t)` is tight, then `(C,A,s,t)` is tight because `(C,A,s)` is a cyclic rotation of the retained turn `(A,s,C)`; if `(A,s,t)` is bad, complete reversal gives `(t,s,A)` tight, hence cyclic rotation gives `(s,A,t)` tight, and `(s,A,t,C)` is a tight P4 using `(A,t,C)`. Contradiction.

Now suppose the unique successful controlled pivot installs `s1->s2`; reverse source-block orientation is dual. The remaining mate `s2->s3` must be the failed controlled edge. It cannot fail by CYCLE: the reverse-dimer normal form would force selected `s3->s2`, colliding at `s2,in` with permanent `s1->s2`. Hence the failure is acyclic. The seam `(s1,s2,s3)` is a literal old-F source-block turn and is tight, so the unique bad seam is the outer one.

In an IN presentation at vacant `s3,in`, s2 is internal. In an OUT presentation at vacant `s2,out`, s3 has its IN port occupied by the selected mate and its OUT port occupied because the outer bad seam exists, so s3 is internal. In either presentation tau-one source exposure requires a second exposed retained source, and the only possibility is s1. Since `s1,out` is occupied by `s1->s2`, its missing F-port is its IN boundary gate `B_L->s1`. The neutral-frontier lemma puts that missing gate off `C*`; thus the C* transition placement is `(2,0)`, with the v-B transition and opposite gate `s3->B_R` on `C*`, and the left gate the unique off-C* F-transition. The successful SS pivot is off `C*` and cannot have removed the unique off-C* J-transition, so that J-transition spends the entire tau-one budget. Therefore no F-transition on `C*` has yet been selected and the original canonical coordinate satisfies `q<r`.

At `s3,out`, before the later gate `s3->B_R` is selected, C* carries a J-edge `s3->w`. There is no J-transition on C*, so `w in X`. It is not s1 because `s1,in` is free, not s2 because `s2,in` is occupied by `s1->s2`, not s3 because loops are forbidden. Hence `w=v`. The selected edge is exactly `s3->v`, the unique bad outer seam is `(s2,s3,v)`, and complete reversal gives `(v,s3,s2)` tight.

The OUT presentation is impossible. Its selected mate entering s3 would be `d'->s3`. The tail d' cannot be in X: `s1,out` is occupied, `s2,out` is the free port, `d'=s3` loops, and `d'=v` would form a selected backtrack with `s3->v`. Thus `d' in B`, making `d'->s3` the unique transition. The other rail containing `s1->s2` is then entirely X; since s3 and v lie on the first rail, that isolated X-rail is exactly `(s1,s2)`, forbidden by the two-source-dimer argument above. So the failed attempt is IN-presented at s3.

Its selected mate sharing `s2,out` is `s2->b0` with `b0 in B`: an X-head is excluded by the already free/occupied X split copies. This is the unique transition of the tau-one predecessor. The rail beginning `s3->v` can continue neither into B, which would create a second transition, nor to another X-vertex, since s1,s2 lie on the other rail. Hence it is exactly `(s3,v)`. All B-vertices lie after s2 on the other rail, so the predecessor is exactly `(s1,s2,B_T)|(s3,v)` with nonempty tight B-path `B_T=(b0,...)`. Replacing `s2->b0` by `s2->s3` gives `(s1,s2,s3,v)|B_T`, of tau zero, whose sole bad turn is `(s2,s3,v)`.

Finally write the right gate as `e_k=s3->B_R`. The J-edge sharing `s3,out` is `f_{k-1}=s3->v`, so `b_{k-1}=v`. The F-edge sharing that same IN copy `v,in` is the unique old-F v-B transition, which is the first-positive edge `e_r`. Therefore `k-1=r` and the literal packet is
`e_r=a_{r-1}->v`, `f_r=s3->v`, `e_{r+1}=s3->B_R`.
