# R525 — Nine-never-condition normalization and literal-insertion barrier

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let E be any relation on ordered triples of distinct vertices satisfying (PA): for every distinct u,v,w, (u,v,w) is in E or (w,u,v) is in E. On each unordered 3-set T, E contains a four-order subrelation still satisfying (PA), and every minimal four-order pattern is exactly one of nine never conditions xNp: one designated vertex x in T is forbidden from one designated position p in {1,2,3}, while the other four orders are retained. Equivalently, the two omitted orders are one from each cyclic class and necessarily place the same vertex in the same position. The p=2 patterns are reversal-symmetric middle blockers and obstruct at most one literal insertion slot relative to a fixed adjacent pair. The p=1 and p=3 patterns can obstruct two adjacent insertion slots. In particular (PA) alone does not imply that an outside vertex can be literally inserted into a fixed tight path. There is a four-vertex example with tight path (a,b,c) and outside vertex w for which all four insertion positions fail: on {w,a,b} take the never condition bN3, so (w,a,b) and (a,w,b) are bad; on {b,c,w} take bN1, so (b,w,c) and (b,c,w) are bad; choose either of the remaining two triple rules so that (a,b,c) is tight and choose any lawful rule on {a,c,w}. Then wabc, awbc, abwc, and abcw each contain a displayed bad turn. Thus any proof of the unrestricted spanning-path conjecture must allow rerouting/reordering or exploit more than fixed-order insertion.

## Hypotheses
Finite ordered-triple relation satisfying (PA). For the explicit barrier, four distinct vertices a,b,c,w.

## Scope
Order-free normalization/fence for the special prepend/append ternary condition. It precisely separates the easy reversal-symmetric insertion theorem from the unrestricted nine-pattern problem.

## Applicability
Use before attempting a Redei-style fixed-order insertion proof under (PA) alone. Normalize each 3-set to a never condition and track first/last-position double blockers explicitly.

## Persistence
The nine local rule types and the explicit four-vertex noninsertable path are intrinsic finite certificates.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not give a counterexample to existence of a spanning tight path under (PA), nor to the conjectured 2^(n-1) lower bound. It rules out only literal insertion into an arbitrarily fixed tight path.

## Proof
Fix an unordered triple {a,b,c}. The orders split into cyclic classes C+=(abc,bca,cab) and C-=(acb,cba,bac). Applying (PA) cyclically within either class says no two members of that class can both be bad, so at least two members of each class are tight. Deleting surplus tight orders if necessary leaves exactly two tight orders in each class, equivalently one omitted order from C+ and one from C-. There are 3*3=9 choices of omitted pair. Direct inspection shows that each pair has a unique common vertex occurring in the same position in both omitted permutations; conversely fixing a vertex x and position p omits exactly the two orders with x in position p, one from each cyclic class. Hence the nine minimal patterns are exactly xNp. For a fixed oriented adjacent pair (a,b) and outside w, the three literal placements relevant to preserving a before b are (w,a,b),(a,w,b),(a,b,w). A middle-never rule removes at most one of these, while aN1 removes the latter two and bN3 removes the former two, producing adjacent double blockers. For the explicit barrier, make bN3 the rule on {w,a,b}; then wab and awb are bad. Make bN1 the rule on {b,c,w}; then bwc and bcw are bad. Choose a lawful rule on {a,b,c} that leaves abc tight, and any lawful rule on {a,c,w}. The path abc is tight. Prepending w fails at wab; inserting between a,b fails at awb; inserting between b,c fails at bwc; appending w fails at bcw. Every local rule is one of the nine minimal (PA) patterns, so the full relation satisfies (PA).
