# R178 — Single-Relocation Plus Segment-Reversal Hamilton Descent Is Incomplete

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
There exists an eight-vertex Level-(3) boundary tournament and a Hamilton cyclic order C with raw cyclic defect cut number kappa_0(C)=3 such that every distinct single-vertex relocation of C and every proper cyclic contiguous-segment reversal of C still has kappa_0 at least three, while a genuine two-block transposition produces a cyclic order with kappa_0=2. Therefore single-vertex relocation plus arbitrary proper segment reversal is not a complete universal Hamilton-defect descent system.

## Hypotheses
Explicit Level-(3) edge-order witness on K8: 06 < 15 < 37 < 05 < 46 < 26 < 13 < 07 < 45 < 34 < 67 < 12 < 25 < 16 < 14 < 27 < 56 < 57 < 04 < 01 < 23 < 35 < 47 < 03 < 36 < 02 < 17 < 24. For a Hamilton cyclic order C, D(C) is the set of defective turn centers and kappa_0(C)=min{|F|: F subseteq E(C), D(C) subseteq V(F)}.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1) negative result, witnessed already in Level-(3). It refutes completeness of the specified local Hamilton move set, not the existence of some other descent operation.

## Applicability
Guardrail for Hamilton-order approaches: any universal descent architecture must permit genuine two-block/internal rerouting or an equivalent nonlocal operation.

## Persistence
The explicit edge-order witness is graph-intrinsic. The negative conclusion concerns the entire enumerated move neighborhood of the displayed cyclic order.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say single relocations or segment reversals are never useful. Does not refute the Universal Linear Defect Formula or Two-Short-Run Normal Form. Does not claim the exhibited system is a counterexample to the Strong Two-Cover Conjecture.

## Proof
Use the displayed K8 total edge order 06<15<37<05<46<26<13<07<45<34<67<12<25<16<14<27<56<57<04<01<23<35<47<03<36<02<17<24 and define u v w tight iff edge uv precedes edge vw. As in every such edge-order representation, complete reversal swaps the two adjacent edges, so exactly one of u v w and w v u is tight.

For the cyclic order C=(0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7), inspect each cyclic turn (v_{i-1},v_i,v_{i+1}). The resulting defect word is G D G D D G D D, so the defective centers are {1,3,4,6,7}. The minimum number of cycle edges whose incident vertices cover those defective centers is kappa_0(C)=3.

Enumerate every distinct single-vertex relocation of the displayed linear representative C: remove one of the eight vertices and reinsert it in any of the eight positions, discard the unchanged order, and deduplicate. This gives exactly 49 distinct orders. Recompute the cyclic defect centers and kappa_0 for each; every value is 3 or 4, hence none decreases below 3.

Next enumerate every proper cyclic contiguous segment, of lengths 2 through 7 and all eight cyclic starting positions, reverse that segment in place, discard the unchanged order, and deduplicate. This gives exactly 44 distinct proper cyclic segment reversals. Again every resulting kappa_0 is 3 or 4.

Finally take C'=(0,4,3,1,2,5,6,7). Its defect word is G D D G G G D D, with defective centers {1,2,6,7}, and kappa_0(C')=2. C' is obtained by the stated genuine two-block transposition, moving block (4) across block (1,2) with the indicated internal rearrangement. Therefore single-vertex relocation plus arbitrary proper cyclic segment reversal is not a complete universal defect-descent move set. All counts and defect values are reconstructed directly from the retained edge order and the definition of kappa_0.
