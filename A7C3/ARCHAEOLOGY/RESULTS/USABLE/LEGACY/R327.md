# R327 — Dual-clique conditional Hamilton-five density hierarchy

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let W be an r-vertex subset, r>=6, of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, and fix S subseteq W of size s in {0,1,2,3}. Among the five-subsets F of W containing S, the fraction that fail to support a tight P5 is at most\n\n(r-s)/[(5-s)(r-4)].\n\nEquivalently\n\nh_5(W;S) >= [1-(r-s)/((5-s)(r-4))] binom(r-s,5-s).\n\nFor s=0 this gives global Hamilton-five density at least 4(r-5)/[5(r-4)], tending to 4/5. For fixed vertex, pair, and triple the asymptotic guaranteed densities tend respectively to 3/4, 2/3, and 1/2. For every r>10 these bounds strictly improve the older R228 fractions 2/3,3/5,1/2,1/3 and the earlier quadratic R324 bounds. In particular for r=11 at least 317 of the 462 five-subsets support tight P5s.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament; W any r-set with r>=6; S subseteq W with |S| in {0,1,2,3}; accepted R195 local four-of-six Hamilton-five theorem; Johnson local-clique degree principle R326.

## Scope
Order-free quantitative strengthening of R228 and prior revision R324. Preserves a fixed physical vertex, pair, or triple while obtaining the stronger Johnson-degree density ceiling.

## Applicability
Use when a named physical vertex/dimer/trimer must occur in many Hamiltonian P5 supports. This is the preferred conditional-density estimate, while R324 remains a distinct quadratic/defect-compatible proof route.

## Persistence
Pure graph-intrinsic support counting through the fixed subset S; no ordering synchronization or representative currentness.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prescribe P5 orderings or force P6/path growth. Density-only growth remains blocked by R321. Fixed four-sets receive no nontrivial bound from this t=2 quotient argument.

## Proof
Let B_S={F\\S:S subseteq F subseteq W, |F|=5, F non-Hamiltonian}. Then B_S is k=(5-s)-uniform on N=r-s vertices. Every (k+1)=(6-s)-set U in W\\S gives a six-set S union U, and R195 says at most two of its five-subsets are bad, so U contains at most t=2 members of B_S. R326 gives p_bad<= [k+(N-k)]/[k(N-k+1)]=N/[k(N-k+1)]. Substituting N=r-s, k=5-s, and N-k+1=r-4 yields (r-s)/[(5-s)(r-4)]. The displayed Hamiltonian density and asymptotic values follow by complementation. For r>10 direct comparison gives strict improvement over R228; at r=11,s=0 the bad count is at most floor((11/35)*462)=145, hence at least 317 good five-sets.
