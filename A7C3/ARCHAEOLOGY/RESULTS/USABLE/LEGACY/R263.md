# R263 — Opposite-polarity same-dimer witnesses concatenate to a literal tight P4

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let D=(a,b) be an oriented physical dimer carrying two signed certificates with distinct witnesses x and y of opposite polarities. Orient the polarity labels so that x is the head witness and y is the tail witness. Then the certified turns are (x,a,b) tight and (a,b,y) tight, hence (x,a,b,y) is a literal tight four-vertex path. Equivalently, every opposite-polarity distinct-witness same-support interaction in R38 has an explicit strict two-ended realization as a P4 whose middle physical dimer is the signed support and whose endpoints are exactly the two witnesses.

## Hypotheses
Accepted Strong signed-dimer head/tail convention and accepted same-support interaction theorem R38; the two witnesses are distinct and lie outside the dimer support.

## Scope
Order-free graph-intrinsic unpacking of the strict two-ended signed-path output already referenced by R38. It promotes the previously compressed geometry into an explicit canonical path statement.

## Applicability
Use whenever R38 lands in the opposite-polarity branch and a downstream argument needs the actual vertex sequence rather than an interaction label. The strict path is obtained with no currentization or payment.

## Persistence
The oriented dimer, witness identities, certified turns, and resulting P4 are graph-intrinsic. Later representative changes do not erase the path fact.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert anything in the same-polarity collision branch, does not allow cyclic rereading of the P4, does not currentize the path as a selected component in an unrelated cover, and does not by itself force a two-cover.

## Proof
A head-signed certificate on the oriented dimer (a,b) with witness x is precisely the tight turn (x,a,b). A tail-signed certificate on the same oriented dimer with witness y is precisely the tight turn (a,b,y). When the two certificates have opposite polarities and x!=y, choose the orientation of D so that x is the head witness and y is the tail witness. The two consecutive tight turns share the ordered dimer (a,b), so by the definition of a tight path their concatenation (x,a,b,y) is a tight P4. This is exactly the strict two-ended signed-path interaction named but not expanded in R38.
