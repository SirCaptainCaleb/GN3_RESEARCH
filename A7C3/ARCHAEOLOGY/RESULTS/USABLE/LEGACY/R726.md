# R726 — Every X-root in the synchronized transitive frame lies in a cross-end Hamilton P4

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the accepted synchronized transitive R594 frame with X a matching-height P4-free four-cell and Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4, endpoints L=q_0,R=q_m. Fix any x in X. Let r,s,l be its unique M_R-, M_S-, and M_L-neighbors in X. Then at least one of the two labelled four-sets {L,R,x,r} and {L,R,x,s} supports a tight Hamilton P4. In particular every x in X belongs to a Hamilton P4 that also contains both physical endpoints L and R. This conclusion holds before choosing the aligned/crossed middle-gate phase and uses no deletion-cover classification beyond the standing R594 endpoint P5-free cells.

Proof. By R3 exactly one of the complete-reversal pair (R,x,L),(L,x,R) is tight. CASE A: K=(R,x,L) is tight. Apply R518 to K with probes r and s. If either four-set is Hamiltonian, done. Assume both are P4-free. The r-probe gives (x,R,r) and (r,L,x). Matching height gives (s,l,r),(r,l,x),(l,s,x),(s,x,l). P5-freeness of X+L applied to (s,l,r,L,x) forces (L,r,l); then applied to (s,L,r,l,x) forces (r,L,s). P5-freeness of X+R applied to (l,s,x,R,r) forces (R,x,s). P4-freeness of {R,x,L,s} applied to (R,x,s,L) forces (L,s,x). Hence (r,L,s,x,l) is a forbidden Hamilton P5 of X+L.

CASE B: K=(L,x,R) is tight. Again probe by r and s and assume both four-sets P4-free. The r-probe no-P4 branch gives (x,L,r) and (r,R,x). Matching height gives the same four X turns. P5-freeness of X+R applied to (s,l,r,R,x) forces (R,r,l); then to (s,R,r,l,x) forces (r,R,s). P5-freeness of X+L applied to (l,s,x,L,r) forces (L,x,s). P4-freeness of {L,x,R,s} applied to (L,x,s,R) forces (R,s,x). Hence (r,R,s,x,l) is a forbidden Hamilton P5 of X+R. Thus one of the r- or s-probe four-sets is Hamiltonian in either orientation case.

## Proof
The matching-height identities used in both cases are (s,l,r), (r,l,x), (l,s,x), and (s,x,l), because the edge-height pairs are respectively M_R>M_S, M_S>M_L, M_R>M_S, and M_S>M_L.

Case A, (R,x,L) tight. Suppose both {R,x,L,r} and {R,x,L,s} are P4-free. R518 on probe r gives (x,R,r),(r,L,x). Candidate (s,l,r,L,x) in the P5-free cell X+L has first and last turns tight, so (l,r,L) is bad and R3 gives (L,r,l). Candidate (s,L,r,l,x) then has its last two turns tight, so (s,L,r) is bad and R3 gives (r,L,s). Candidate (l,s,x,R,r) in X+R has first and last turns tight, so (s,x,R) is bad and R3 gives (R,x,s). In the P4-free s-cell, candidate (R,x,s,L) now has first turn tight, hence (x,s,L) is bad and R3 gives (L,s,x). Finally (r,L,s,x,l) has turns (r,L,s),(L,s,x),(s,x,l) tight, contradicting P5-freeness of X+L.

Case B, (L,x,R) tight. Suppose both probe four-sets are P4-free. R518 on probe r gives (x,L,r),(r,R,x). Candidate (s,l,r,R,x) in X+R forces (R,r,l). Candidate (s,R,r,l,x) then forces (r,R,s). Candidate (l,s,x,L,r) in X+L forces (L,x,s). The P4-free s-cell candidate (L,x,s,R) forces (R,s,x). Finally (r,R,s,x,l) is a Hamilton P5 of X+R, contradiction.

R3 guarantees that exactly one of the two case orientations holds for each fixed x, completing the proof.
