# R517 — Four-signature contact family has one quotient-inhomogeneous cell

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the four exact no-P4 signatures of R516 on X={a,b,c,y}. Partition the six physical dimers into opposite-edge perfect matchings B_1={{a,b},{c,y}}, B_2={{a,c},{b,y}}, B_3={{a,y},{b,c}}. At a fixed middle vertex, compare the three matching blocks through the orientations of the three incident physical dimers. The induced block tournament is vertex-uniform in every signature. For (A,B)=(1,1) it is the transitive order B_1>B_2>B_3; for (1,0), B_1>B_3>B_2; for (0,1), B_2>B_1>B_3. For the doubly misaligned signature (0,0), the induced tournament is the same directed 3-cycle at every middle vertex: B_1>B_3>B_2>B_1. Thus tested-order ambiguity creates three transitive quotient cells up to block relabeling and one genuinely cyclic quotient cell; the 00 cell is uniform cyclic, not quotient-inhomogeneous.

## Persistence

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
Use the opposite-edge blocks B_1={{a,b},{c,y}}, B_2={{a,c},{b,y}}, B_3={{a,y},{b,c}}. At middle a the incident dimers {a,b},{a,c},{a,y} represent B_1,B_2,B_3; similarly each other middle is incident with exactly one edge from each block. Read the three local comparisons from the exact R516 row. In signature 11, at middle a the turns (b,a,c),(b,a,y),(c,a,y) give B_1>B_2>B_3, and the corresponding three comparisons at b,c,y give the same order. The same direct read gives B_1>B_3>B_2 at every middle in signature 10 and B_2>B_1>B_3 at every middle in signature 01. In signature 00, the middle-a turns (c,a,b),(b,a,y),(y,a,c) give B_2>B_1, B_1>B_3, and B_3>B_2, i.e. B_1>B_3>B_2>B_1. Reading the remaining three middles from the same 00 row gives exactly the same directed block edges. Hence the quotient is vertex-uniform cyclic in 00 and vertex-uniform transitive in the other three signatures.
