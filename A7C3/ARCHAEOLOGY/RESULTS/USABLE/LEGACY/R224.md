# R224 — Inherited-turn endpoint-selective paid-floor continuation

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and suppose an ancestry-bearing balanced-pair lineage contains a signed physical dimer D whose sign is certified by a named inherited tight turn. Choose the aligned dimer refund orientation so that one prescribed physical endpoint z of D is the singleton surviving the width-two payment. Then along the accepted inherited-turn paid continuation, either a spanning two-cover occurs or the lineage reaches an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor in which (z) is literally one singleton floor coordinate. The other floor singleton is not prescribed. The head/tail dual and the two possible endpoint choices are separate alternative lineages.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; D is an aligned signed dimer inside an ancestry-bearing balanced-pair/payment lineage; its sign comes from a named inherited tight turn; one of the two orientation-compatible endpoint-selective refund channels is chosen.

## Scope
Historical Strong signed-payment theorem recovered from the audited archive. This is a lineage/persistence theorem, not simultaneous currentization of two endpoint choices.

## Applicability
Use when a signed dimer arises from a named physical turn and later arguments need one chosen physical endpoint to survive not merely the first dimer refund but all the way to the mass-two floor.

## Persistence
The named inherited turn, chosen refund orientation, physical endpoint z, refund certificate, and literal floor coordinate (z) are retained in the chosen ancestry ledger. The old marker, source cover, opposite support, and second floor singleton need not remain current or retain their old identities.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prescribe the second floor singleton. Does not assert both endpoint-selective floors coexist or can be synchronized. Does not preserve an arbitrary old literal three-cover through payment. Does not imply a target-aligned floor for any independently chosen second vertex, and does not close the conjecture.

## Proof
Let the ancestry-bearing balanced pair contain a signed physical dimer D=(d_0,d_1) and a physically disjoint opposite-polarity signed support T. Fix the prescribed endpoint z in V(D), and let d be the other endpoint. The named inherited tight turn certifying the sign of D is retained in the ancestry ledger.

Apply R425 to the historical signed path D with deletion set {d}. The unique surviving interval is the singleton (z). R425 says that (z) is graph-intrinsically signed with the same polarity as D. If z lies at the historically signed end, the old witness remains; otherwise the cut neighbor d is the new exact witness. In either case the original inherited turn, the chosen endpoint z, and the one-vertex cut from D to (z) remain exact ancestry data. The opposite support T is untouched, so (z) and T are physically disjoint signed supports of opposite polarity.

By the first clause of R514, these two supports therefore form a new graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair, with its birth certificate explicitly descended from the original D-side certificate. If T is already a singleton, this is the required ancestry-bearing mass-two floor containing the literal coordinate (z). If T is nontrivial, apply R428 to this singleton/nontrivial balanced pair with p=z. Its chosen certificate-retaining continuation preserves the singleton support (z) exactly and yields either a spanning two-cover of H or a balanced pair whose two supports are both singletons. In the latter case (z) is literally one floor coordinate and the second coordinate is uncontrolled, exactly as R224 asserts.

The opposite choice of endpoint of D is obtained by deleting the other endpoint in the first R425 step; head/tail duality is built into R425 and R428. The two endpoint choices are alternative lineages, not simultaneous states. No old cover or unrelated witness is transported. Thus the named inherited turn and original dimer remain historical ancestry, while current pair status after clipping is supplied afresh by R514. This proves R224 without R169, R35, or any exterior-cardinality assumption.
