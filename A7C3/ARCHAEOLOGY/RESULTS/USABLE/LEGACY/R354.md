# R354 — Global Johnson equality is locally far from fixed-trimer Mantel equality

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume equality in the global Hamilton-five bad-family ceiling R331 on an r-vertex set W, and write r=5q. For any tight trimer support P⊆W, accepted R341 gives a q-regular triangle-free fixed-trimer bad-extension graph G_P on n=5q-3 outside vertices, hence |E(G_P)|=q(5q-3)/2. If q>1 then 2q<5q-3=n, so |E(G_P)|<n^2/4 and therefore G_P is strictly below the R328 Mantel extremal edge count floor(n^2/4). In particular the exact-Mantel path-growth theorem R349 cannot be applied merely from global R331 equality. Combining with the R346 congruence fence, every nontrivial arithmetically admissible global equality case has q>=3 and therefore |E(G_P)|/(n^2/4)=2q/(5q-3)<=1/2. Thus global Johnson extremality produces a regular routed local structure that is quantitatively far from local Mantel extremality; these are genuinely different structural regimes.

## Hypotheses
Equality holds in R331 on W; q=r/5; P is any tight trimer support in W.

## Scope
Equality-regime separation between the Johnson density program and fixed-trimer Mantel path-growth.

## Applicability
Use to prevent feeding R331 equality directly into R328/R349. Any path-growth consumer of the global equality design must use the q-regular routing structure of R341/R342 rather than local Mantel extremality.

## Persistence
The fixed-trimer bad graph and its degree/edge count are graph-intrinsic support-family data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say R331 equality is realizable or P6-free. Does not rule out path growth from the stronger routing/design structure. It only separates two notions of extremality.

## Proof
R341 gives n=5q-3 and degree q, so e=qn/2. For q>1, n-2q=3(q-1)>0 and in fact n^2/4-e=n(n-2q)/4=3(5q-3)(q-1)/4>1, hence e<floor(n^2/4). Thus the fixed-trimer graph is strictly below Mantel equality. Combining with R346, every nontrivial arithmetically admissible global equality case has q>=3, and then 2q/n=2q/(5q-3)<=1/2.
