# R246 — Common-substitute aligned-swap obstruction clauses

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume the five-exterior equality face. Let S be a four-core with two completions a,b, let E_a=S union {a}, and choose a tight ordering K=(k_0,...,k_{m-1}) of the complementary global-longest carrier K_a. Suppose b=k_i. The neighboring exterior E_b=S union {b} has complementary global-longest support (K_a\\{b}) union {a}. Form the order-preserving swap candidate K[i:a]=(k_0,...,k_{i-1},a,k_{i+1},...,k_{m-1}). Then either K[i:a] is tight, hence is an explicitly orientation-synchronized global-longest realization of the E_b-complement, or at least one present reverse-turn blocker is tight: (a,k_{i-1},k_{i-2}) when i>=2; (k_{i+1},a,k_{i-1}) when 1<=i<=m-2; (k_{i+2},k_{i+1},a) when i<=m-3. At endpoints the clause has the corresponding single new turn, and one step from an endpoint it has the corresponding two turns. Thus every common-substitute swap position carries either an aligned longest-carrier swap or a localized one-of-at-most-three reverse-turn certificate.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the five-exterior equality face; E_a and E_b are two global-longest exteriors sharing the physical four-core S; accepted boundary antisymmetry R3 and R240 support identity.

## Scope
Order-free carrier-order refinement of four-core occupancy. This is the one-for-one replacement analogue of R236 insertion obstruction and keeps the original carrier order explicit.

## Applicability
Use on the common-substitute carrier fiber generated by an occupied four-core. Aligned outcomes synchronize actual neighboring global-longest carriers with the retained K order; failed outcomes produce exact reverse turns at the deleted position that can be combined with R236 gap clauses, capture ancestry, or restoration tests.

## Persistence
K and its displayed order are the chosen retained carrier; S,a,b and the support identity of the neighboring carrier are graph-intrinsic. Each reverse-turn alternative is graph-intrinsic. The disjunction does not select one blocker unless further information is supplied.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the order-preserving swap is always tight, does not identify which reverse blocker occurs when it fails, and does not by itself produce a longer carrier. Tightness of some other ordering on the swapped support is already known but is not used to infer the displayed ordering.

## Proof
Write b=k_i. The path K[i:a] has the same ordered vertices as K except at position i. Every consecutive triple not meeting position i is therefore already tight. The only new triples are L_i=(k_{i-2},k_{i-1},a) when i>=2, M_i=(k_{i-1},a,k_{i+1}) when 1<=i<=m-2, and R_i=(a,k_{i+1},k_{i+2}) when i<=m-3, with the evident boundary omissions. If all present triples are tight, K[i:a] is a tight Hamilton ordering of the support (K_a-{b}) union {a}; R240 identifies this support as the complement of E_b, so it is global-longest. Otherwise some present new triple is bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 turns its complete reversal tight, giving respectively (a,k_{i-1},k_{i-2}), (k_{i+1},a,k_{i-1}), or (k_{i+2},k_{i+1},a). No other turns are needed or claimed.
