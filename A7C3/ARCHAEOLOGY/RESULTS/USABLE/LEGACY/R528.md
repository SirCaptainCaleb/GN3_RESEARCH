# R528 — Captured-crossing mirror reentry does not transport ancestry to fresh exterior spokes

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let x have a valid historical capture certificate through a physical crossing D={x,y}. Accepted R155 may be invoked with any graph-intrinsic historical tight carrier A that is certified before the R155 application, satisfies x in V(A) and y notin V(A), and has a genuine carrier adjacency E={p,x} at x. The carrier A need not be the carrier that was current, selected, or even already certified in the original capture representative; a later-certified tight path through x is admissible provided D genuinely crosses it and E is one of its physical adjacencies. What the capture contributes is historical event ancestry attached to the physical captured vertex x and crossing D. R155 then currentizes the incident supports D and E through their unique tight mirror trimer while retaining that capture event as ancestry.

The lawful fence is therefore narrower than legacy R277. Merely inventing an arbitrary fresh support incident with x does not make it an R155 carrier adjacency: one must first have a genuinely certified graph-intrinsic carrier A containing that support and crossed by D. R155 also does not transport the original selected orientation of D, the original carrier/component identity, old residual rails, endpoint roles, same-frame concentration, or capture protection. Thus later-certified carriers are allowed, but unrelated fresh supports and selected-role/currentness transport remain forbidden.

## Hypotheses
Accepted fully reconstructible R155; a valid historical capture of x through D={x,y}; a graph-intrinsic tight carrier A certified by the time of R155 application with x in A, y outside A, and a genuine adjacency E={p,x} on A.

## Scope
Correction of overstrong R277. It resolves the contradiction between R277's statement and its own validity note by reading the exact reconstructed R155 contract literally. The capture carrier need not be original-frame-identical; the required invariant is a certified carrier crossed by the same captured physical pair D.

## Applicability
When auditing capture-to-mirror arguments, ask whether the proposed second support is an actual adjacency of some certified historical carrier A that D crosses. If yes, R155 may be used even if A was certified after capture. If not, incident-support existence alone is insufficient for the ancestry-bearing R155 conclusion.

## Persistence
Retain the physical captured pair D, vertex x, historical capture event, chosen certified carrier A, genuine carrier adjacency E, and later mirror-trimer certificate. No original representative roles or protection are transported unless separately proved.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say every fresh support through x is lawful. Does not transport capture ancestry to a support that is not an adjacency on a certified carrier crossed by D. Does not preserve original selected orientations, old cover components, residual rails, endpoint exposure, same-frame concentration, or protection. Does not by itself revalidate any historically invalidated downstream proof; those require independent re-audit on their exact geometry.

## Proof
Accepted R155 states: let A be a graph-intrinsic historical carrier path, let D={x,y} be a physical crossing with x in A and y outside A, retain the historical fact that x was captured using D, and choose any carrier adjacency E={p,x}; then a later minimum three-cover currentizes D and E as the boundary supports of their unique tight mirror trimer, while the capture event remains ancestry. None of these hypotheses says A must have been current in, or identical to a carrier from, the original capture frame. Historical capture is attached to the physical event (x,D), and R155 explicitly refuses to transport selected orientations or old carrier currentness. Hence if a path A is certified later but before the R155 application, and D crosses A at x, it satisfies the theorem exactly. Conversely, an arbitrary fresh support E incident with x is not enough: unless E is an adjacency of some certified carrier A with y outside A, the R155 carrier-adjacency hypothesis has not been met. This proves the corrected fence and shows that the stronger prohibition asserted in R277 was not a consequence of R155.
