# R794 — The minimum three-cell shortcut weave is nearest-deep: Reverse Ear forces q3 or q_{m-3} and a canonical gate P4

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain accepted R789 SHORTCUT-CUT in the accepted crossed R621 frame, m>=6. Put D={u,v,d,s}, M=Q[3,m-3], W=H-D, and let T# be the exact two-cover retained by R789. Use the physical three-cell partition G_L={L,a,c}, G_M=V(M), G_R={b,z,R}, where (L,a,c) and (b,z,R) are tight. Suppose (i) T# has no selected endpoint-to-M state, (ii) relative to this partition t_G(T#)=1, and (iii) H has no spanning two-cover. Then exactly one outer gate cell is an isolated T# rail and the other is joined to M by the unique intercell transition. Apply accepted Reverse Ear R435 to the mixed rail against the original Q. Either R435 produces an explicit Q-relative reversed old state, reverse trimer, or vertex-simple tight cycle, or the mixed rail has one of the following two literal normal forms.

(LEFT-MIN) G_R is the isolated rail. The mixed rail is K=(g_0,g_1,g_2,q_3,q_4,...,q_{m-3}), where {g_0,g_1,g_2}={L,a,c}, (g_0,g_1,g_2) is tight, and g_2 is in {a,c}. In particular (g_0,g_1,g_2,q_3) is a tight Hamilton P4 on {L,a,c,q_3}, and the unique intercell selected state is g_2 q_3.

(RIGHT-MIN) G_L is the isolated rail. The mixed rail is K=(q_3,q_4,...,q_{m-3},h_0,h_1,h_2), where {h_0,h_1,h_2}={b,z,R}, (h_0,h_1,h_2) is tight, and h_0 is in {b,z}. In particular (q_{m-3},h_0,h_1,h_2) is a tight Hamilton P4 on {q_{m-3},b,z,R}, and the unique intercell selected state is q_{m-3} h_0.

Thus the transition-minimum X-ENDPOINT-CROSSING residue is not an arbitrary X-to-deep-Q weave: outside Reverse-Ear geometry it is pinned to the first surviving deep coordinate on the corresponding side and carries a four-vertex gate P4.

## Proof
The crossed R621 gate turns make (L,a,c) and (b,z,R) tight, while M=Q[3,m-3] is a tight path. Hence the three physical classes have intrinsic path-cover number one. Since t_G(T#)=1, accepted R7 gives sigma_G=0, so each class occurs as one contiguous T# block. Contracting the three blocks in the two T# rails yields one edge and one isolated block. If the edge were G_L-G_R, their two complete blocks would concatenate along the unique selected intercell state to a tight Hamilton P6 on X union {L,R}; together with the disjoint tight path Q[1,m-1] this would be a spanning two-cover of H. Hypothesis (iii) excludes this. Thus the edge is G_L-G_M or G_M-G_R.

Suppose first it is G_L-G_M. Then G_R is the isolated rail and the other T# rail K contains exactly the complete G_L block and the complete M block. Compare K with the original tight path Q by accepted R435. The Q-contacts of K are L=q_0 together with every q_i for 3<=i<=m-3. If any consecutive pair of Q-contacts occurs in decreasing Q-order, R435 gives one of its explicit outputs: an old-state reversal, reverse trimer, or vertex-simple tight cycle. Otherwise all Q-contacts occur in increasing order along K. Therefore L occurs before every M vertex, and the M contacts themselves occur in the literal order q_3,q_4,...,q_{m-3}. Because both physical classes are contiguous blocks, K has the form (g_0,g_1,g_2,q_3,...,q_{m-3}) with {g_0,g_1,g_2}={L,a,c}. The unique intercell state is g_2 q_3. By the no-endpoint-to-M hypothesis g_2 is not L, so g_2 is a or c. Since K is tight, its first four vertices form the tight P4 (g_0,g_1,g_2,q_3), whose support is exactly {L,a,c,q_3}. This proves LEFT-MIN.

The G_M-G_R case is dual. Outside an R435 output, the Q contacts q_3,...,q_{m-3},R=q_m occur in increasing Q-order, so the contiguous blocks occur as M followed by G_R and the M block is literally q_3,...,q_{m-3}. Thus K=(q_3,...,q_{m-3},h_0,h_1,h_2), with {h_0,h_1,h_2}={b,z,R}. The unique intercell state is q_{m-3}h_0; no endpoint-to-M state implies h_0 is b or z. The last four displayed vertices give the tight Hamilton P4 on {q_{m-3},b,z,R}. This proves RIGHT-MIN and exhausts the minimum branch.
