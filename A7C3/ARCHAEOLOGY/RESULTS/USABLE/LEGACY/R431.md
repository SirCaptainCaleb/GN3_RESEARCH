# R431 — Orientation-Selective Dimer Capture

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let {x,y} be a physical dimer used as a disjoint actualizing marker for a graph-intrinsic signed support. Either physical endpoint may be designated as the captured signed anchor: orient the marker as (x,y) or (y,x) before actualization, and apply Marker Endpoint Anchor Capture in the appropriate polarity direction. Thus in head-to-tail actualization one may choose x or y to be the resulting tail anchor by choosing it as marker tail; dually in tail-to-head actualization one may choose either endpoint as the resulting head anchor by choosing it as marker head.

## Hypotheses
A signed support and a disjoint physical dimer {x,y} are given. The dimer is used as a marker for Signed-Support Marker Actualization. A two-vertex path has no internal turn, so both orders are legitimate tight marker orientations.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1). Special to a two-vertex marker. This is an actualization-orientation choice, not a statement about which orientation was selected in an earlier cover.

## Applicability
Use when a proof needs to actualize a signed support while forcing a prescribed physical endpoint of a dimer marker to become the new captured/signed anchor. This is the endpoint-choice step in arbitrary target floor steering.

## Persistence
The chosen physical endpoint becomes a graph-intrinsic historical signed anchor of the produced support. The chosen marker orientation is part of the actualization certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not reverse a currently selected dimer state; does not claim both orientations are simultaneously current; does not extend freely to markers of order >=3, whose reversed order need not be tight; does not itself provide later anchor protection.

## Proof
Let D={x,y} be a physical dimer marker. Both ordered paths (x,y) and (y,x) are tight, since a two-vertex path has no turn condition. In head-to-tail actualization, R430 records the marker terminal vertex as the signed/captured endpoint. Therefore orient the marker with the desired endpoint last. In the tail-to-head dual, R430 records the marker initial vertex, so orient the desired endpoint first. This marker-orientation choice does not alter or reverse any previously selected orientation of D in another representative.
