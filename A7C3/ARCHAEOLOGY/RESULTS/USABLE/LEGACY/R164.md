# R164 — Deleted-Neighbor Restoration Collision

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let E=(e_0,...,e_m) be a graph-intrinsic tight path and let k,z be distinct vertices outside E representing an old deleted endpoint neighbor and a fresh replacement endpoint witness. Head form: if m>=1 and both (k,E) and (z,E) are tight, then either (z,k,E) is tight or (e_0,k,z) is a tight reverse-contact trimer. Tail form: if both (E,k) and (E,z) are tight, then either (E,k,z) is tight or (z,k,e_m) is a tight reverse-contact trimer. If E is a singleton, the two distinct endpoint witnesses already force one of the two three-vertex contact orders by boundary antisymmetry.

## Hypotheses
E is a retained graph-intrinsic tight path; k and z are distinct and outside E. In the nontrivial head form the turns k e_0 e_1 and z e_0 e_1 are tight; in the nontrivial tail form e_{m-1} e_m k and e_{m-1} e_m z are tight.

## Scope
Universal Strong Level-(1) graph-intrinsic restoration lemma. No smallest-counterexample, exact-cover, or current-selection hypothesis is required once the two old/fresh extensions are certified.

## Applicability
Consumes endpoint-role loss when a residual path has both an historical deleted neighbor and a fresh replacement witness. Outside bounded reverse contact, it restores the deleted neighbor into a strictly longer one-ended extension, preserving the identity of the old neighbor.

## Persistence
Both alternatives are graph-intrinsic path/turn certificates and therefore persist historically after representative changes. Any current role of k,z,E that produced them does not persist unless separately protected.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The bounded reverse trimer/contact is not itself a spanning two-cover or contradiction. The theorem does not assert the restoration alternative always occurs, and does not preserve the source comparison representative or selected role-loss state.

## Proof
Head form: because (k,E) is tight, k e_0 e_1 is tight and all later turns of E are tight. Test z k e_0. If tight, then z,k,e_0,e_1,...,e_m is a tight path, proving the strict restoration extension through the old deleted neighbor k. If z k e_0 is bad, Boundary Antisymmetry R3 gives e_0 k z tight, a bounded reverse-contact trimer. Tail form: test e_m k z. If it is tight, append k,z to E using the already tight old turn e_{m-1} e_m k; if it is bad, R3 gives z k e_m tight. If E is the singleton (e), test z e k; either it is tight or its complete reversal k e z is tight, so bounded contact is immediate.
