# R448 — Fixed-Trimer Central-Anchor No-Quiet Remint

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Fix a graph-intrinsic tight trimer T=(q0,q1,q2). Its terminal signed dimers D^-=(q0,q1), tail-signed by q2, and D^+=(q1,q2), head-signed by q0, are nontrivial and have the same signed anchor q1. Suppose one historical balanced pair has been born using one of D^-,D^+ and a partner signed support disjoint from T, and a later balanced-pair birth before unrelated reset uses either terminal dimer of the same fixed T and another partner signed support disjoint from T. Then the two fixed-trimer terminal supports admit an explicit graph-intrinsic R436 at-anchor normalization: taking the earlier terminal dimer as the older signed support P and the later terminal dimer as Q, Q contains the signed anchor q1 of P, and because P,Q are dimers the singleton exact-replay exception does not apply. Hence R436 yields strict full-P growth, strict Q growth, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a three-vertex reverse-contact/reversal cell. This replaces a completely opaque interaction label by a concrete local certificate at q1, but the growth alternative may reproduce the already-known source trimer T and therefore is not, by itself, a proof of new progress or a consumer of the R160 interaction obstruction. Any stronger use must show that the returned certificate is not merely source-trimer replay or must combine it with additional partner/provenance data.

## Hypotheses
T=(q0,q1,q2) is a graph-intrinsic tight trimer. Two historical balanced-pair births are retained before unrelated reset. Each birth uses one terminal signed dimer of this same T and a partner signed support physically disjoint from T. The terminal dimer used by each birth may be the same or different. Accepted R436 is available.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), graph-intrinsic normalization of the historical fixed-trimer interaction engine. No smallest-counterexample, payment, current-cover, or pair-mass hypothesis is needed beyond the existence of the two historical births. This is a certificate refinement, not a closure theorem.

## Applicability
Use when an R160-style interaction needs to be unpacked into literal local path geometry at the fixed trimer middle. Do not count the R436 growth branch as progress without checking whether it is only the original source trimer T.

## Persistence
Retain the fixed trimer T, its middle q1, the identities of the earlier and later terminal supports, and the exact R436 growth/cycle/reversal certificate. If the certificate is the old source trimer replay, record that explicitly rather than treating it as a fresh event. Partner supports and payment descendants need not coexist.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the R160 interaction obstruction is solved. Does not claim the R436 growth output is new relative to the source data; it may be exactly the already-known trimer T. Does not claim any one growth/cycle/reversal certificate closes the conjecture. Does not treat a proper tight cycle as contradictory. Does not assert partner supports interact or coexist. Does not invoke singleton collision.

## Proof
Let T=(q0,q1,q2) be tight. The terminal dimers D^-=(q0,q1), tail-signed by q2, and D^+=(q1,q2), head-signed by q0, are both proper tight paths of order two and both have signed anchor q1. Consider two historical balanced-pair births through T before unrelated reset. Let P be the terminal dimer used in the earlier birth, with its graph-intrinsic sign, and Q the terminal dimer used in the later birth. Whether P and Q are the same terminal dimer or the two opposite terminal dimers, Q contains q1. Thus Q meets P at the signed anchor of P. Apply accepted R436. The away-anchor truncation branch is impossible. Its only stationary-replay exclusion concerns an exact singleton support, whereas P and Q have order two. Therefore R436 supplies an at-anchor certificate: strict growth of P, strict growth of Q, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a three-vertex reverse-contact/reversal cell. This certificate is graph-intrinsic and can be retained beside the two historical birth certificates. However, when P and Q are terminal pieces of the already-known source trimer, a strict-growth certificate can be exactly the original trimer T (for example by adjoining the original sign witness). Accordingly this argument refines the opaque R160 interaction label into literal local geometry but does not prove that the geometry is fresh or that the R160 obstruction has been consumed. Any later theorem using this normalization as progress must separately exclude source-trimer replay or exploit additional partner/provenance information.
