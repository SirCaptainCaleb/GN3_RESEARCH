# R349 — Fixed-trimer Mantel equality forces local P6 growth at every outside size at least four

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let P be a tight trimer in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let X be a set of m>=4 vertices disjoint from P. Form the R328 bad-extension graph G_bad on X. If G_bad attains the Mantel extremal bound floor(m^2/4), then V(P)∪X contains a tight P6. Indeed R328 equality makes G_bad a complete bipartite graph with balanced parts, each of size at least two. Choosing two vertices from each part gives a four-vertex subset X_0 on which the restricted bad-extension graph is exactly K_{2,2}; provisional R348 then forces a tight P6 on V(P)∪X_0. Thus exact Mantel equality is a genuine path-growth condition, even though R347 shows one-edge-below equality need not be.

## Hypotheses
P is a tight trimer; X is disjoint from P with |X|>=4; the fixed-trimer bad-extension graph attains Mantel equality.

## Scope
Order-free local extremal-structure-to-path-growth corollary.

## Applicability
Use only at exact R328 equality. It converts the extremal complete-bipartite bad graph into a seven-vertex P6 witness.

## Persistence
The chosen four outside vertices, restricted K2,2 bad graph, and resulting P6 are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not apply to near-equality bad graphs. R347 shows the m=4 one-edge-below case can be P6-free. Does not imply that high Hamilton-five density alone forces growth.

## Proof
R328 equality gives G_bad=K_{floor(m/2),ceil(m/2)}. Since m>=4 both classes have at least two vertices. Select x1,x2 in one class and y1,y2 in the other. On X_0={x1,x2,y1,y2}, exactly the four cross pairs are bad and the two within-class pairs are good, so the restricted bad graph is K2,2. R348 forces a tight P6 on P∪X_0.
