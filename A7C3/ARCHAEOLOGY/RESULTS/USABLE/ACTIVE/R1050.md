# R1050 — OUT-v successor regimes at the first-positive zipper pivot

## Statement
Assume the sharpened D17.408 choice and a non-loop OUT-`v` literal first-loss switch. Write the installed pivot edge as

    e_r = v->b_j

and the deleted spectator edge as

    f_r = b_{j-1}->b_j.

At the normalized first-positive state, the OUT copy of `b_j` has two relevant regimes.

1. If `b_j,out` lies behind the zipper frontier, its current selected outgoing incidence is its old `F` incidence, if one exists. Hence the newly installed old-`F` edge `v->b_j` creates an inherited `F` turn at `b_j`, or no turn if `b_j` has no old-`F` successor.
2. If `b_j,out` remains on the unflipped suffix and `b_j` is nonterminal on the endpoint-truncated `J[B]` rail, the selected successor is the spectator adjacency `b_j->b_{j+1}`. The new local turn is therefore `(v,b_j,b_{j+1})`, requiring the R887 comparison `F_j->E_j`, where `F_j={v,b_j}` and `E_j={b_j,b_{j+1}}`. For every `j<=t`, with `t` the canonical R1033 first-flip index, R1033 gives the reverse `E_j->F_j`; hence this future-suffix spectator seam is blocked. If `j=t+1` and `t<m-1`, the defining first flip instead gives `F_j->E_j`, so this one seam is tight, with all other physical and cycle obligations still separate.

No spectator-seam conclusion is asserted when `b_j` is terminal on the truncated `J[B]` rail.

## Proof
R1049 gives the endpoint-truncated spectator rail and identifies the OUT-`v` pivot as `e_r=v->b_j`, `f_r=b_{j-1}->b_j` in the literal-switch scope.

The state rule needed here does not require importing the full provisional D17.416 package. Along the alternating augmenter, the normalized prefix state is obtained from `J` by toggling the already traversed alternating prefix: each traversed `J\\F` edge is removed and its paired `F\\J` edge is installed, while the untouched suffix remains in its `J` state. Consequently a matching copy behind the frontier has its `F` incidence selected, whereas a copy on the untouched suffix retains its `J` incidence. Common incidences are unchanged and agree in both states.

Apply this to the OUT copy of physical vertex `b_j`. If it is behind the frontier, its selected outgoing incidence is exactly the old-`F` successor incidence, when present. Since `e_r=v->b_j` is itself an old-`F` edge, the resulting turn is a turn already present in the tight old forest `F`; otherwise there is no outgoing turn to check.

If `b_j,out` is still on the suffix and `b_j` is nonterminal on the chosen `J[B]` rail, R1049 says the J-successor is the displayed spectator edge `b_j->b_{j+1}`. Thus physicality at `b_j` is exactly the comparison between the v-spoke `F_j={v,b_j}` and forward spectator edge `E_j={b_j,b_{j+1}}`, namely `F_j->E_j`.

In R1033, the canonical first-flip index `t` is chosen so that `E_j->F_j` for every `1<=j<=t`: at `j=1` this is the failed left-end insertion, and for `2<=j<=t` the opposite orientation would have produced an earlier first flip. Therefore the seam is blocked for `j<=t`. When `t<m-1`, the defining flip at `j=t+1` is `F_{t+1}->E_{t+1}`, making precisely that seam tight.

## Audit adjustment
The original workspace root omitted the case in which `b_j,out` is on the unflipped suffix but `b_j` is terminal on the endpoint-truncated `J[B]` rail. In that case there is no spectator successor and hence no next-slot spectator seam to test. The audited theorem adds the necessary nonterminal hypothesis.

## Dependencies and scope
Uses audited R1049 and R1033. The behind-frontier F-state fact is reconstructed directly from alternating-prefix toggling, so no unaudited D17.416 dependency is required for this result. This is a local physicality obstruction only; it does not prove a global cover, descent, or equality of the zipper pivot with the R1033 certificate index.
