# R2013 — The distinguished D_v residue admits transition-neutral defect transport

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789414610.662149`

## Statement

The distinguished `D_v` residue can be made transition-ledger neutral. If `w(D_v) != 0`, R1039 supplies a unique opposite-weight neutral partner, so toggling the pair transports the free IN defect from `v,in` while preserving `tau=1`; thereafter every remaining neutral toggle has weight zero.

Thus continued neutral blocker transport either reaches the flipped `C_*` prefix or yields a tight three-forest with the canonical first-open successor, `tau=1`, and free copies `v,out` and `u,in`, where the direct closure `v->u` has exact two-cover cardinality and transition count at most two.

## Proof

Let `M=hat M_{r-1}` be the physical F-normalized pre-loss state. By R1060, `v,out` and `v,in` are free. The old same-side closure at this pre-loss state has transition count one, and because that closure is X-X, `tau(M)=1`.

Use R1039 to batch neutral toggles by transition weight. In the `(3F,1J)` branch every neutral component has weight zero. In the `(2F,0J)` branch the only possible nonzero neutral weights are one unique split pair `+1,-1`; every other neutral component has weight zero.

Modify the monotone R2011 blocker transport from its first required off-component toggle onward. Whenever a forced untoggled neutral component `E` has `w(E)=0`, toggle `E`. Whenever `w(E)=+1` or `-1`, toggle simultaneously the unique untoggled opposite-weight component `E'` with `w(E')=-w(E)`. The forced blocker loses its offending F-incidence on `E`; the extra toggle may create another blocker, but that blocker is simply processed next. Each batch strictly enlarges the set of J-state neutral components, preserves matching cardinality, and has total transition weight zero. Hence `tau=1` is preserved throughout.

If the balancing partner is `D_v`, that is exactly the moment the distinguished residue is reached. By R2012, `D_v` is an alternating path with J-only endpoint `v,in` and opposite F-only endpoint, say `u,in`. The opposite endpoint lies on the IN shore because the path has equal F-only and J-only edge counts, hence even length. Toggle `D_v`, together with its unique opposite-weight partner if `w(D_v)!=0`. The batch has total weight zero. It matches `v,in` and frees `u,in`; `v,out` remains free because it lies on the fixed pre-loss state of `C_*`.

The split endpoint `u,in` stays free under all later neutral toggles. It is an F-only endpoint of `F triangle J`, so it has no J-incidence, and distinct symmetric-difference components are split-vertex disjoint. Once the unique nonzero pair has been toggled, all remaining neutral components have weight zero, so the monotone R2011 repair continues without changing `tau=1`.

Consequently either the repair reaches a blocker meeting the flipped F-prefix of `C_*`, or it terminates in a literal tight three-forest `N` with the canonical successor at the first-open spectator, `tau(N)=1`, and free copies `v,out,u,in`.

Since `u!=v`, add the ordinary nonloop edge `h=v->u`. It joins two free matching copies, so `N+h` has exact two-cover matching cardinality. Because `v in X`, the edge contributes either zero transitions when `u in X` or one when `u in B`; hence `tau(N+h)<=2`.

Physically, the only new obligations of this final closure are the predecessor seam at `v`, the successor seam at `u` when defined, and the possibility that the two endpoints lie on the same rail, in which case the edge closes a directed cycle. A failed endpoint seam exports its R3 reverse trimer.
