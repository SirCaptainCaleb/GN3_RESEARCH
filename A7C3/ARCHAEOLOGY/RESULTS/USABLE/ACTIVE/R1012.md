# R1012 — Pair-deletion Hamilton-extension Hall obstruction compresses dimer and trimer short rails

## Statement
Let G be an edge-ordered complete graph with pc_inc(G)>2, let x,y be distinct vertices, and let G-{x,y}=A|B be an exact two-cover by Hamilton rail supports A,B. Form the bipartite Hamilton-extension graph E with left side {x,y}, right side {A,B}, and z~R exactly when G[V(R) union {z}] is Hamiltonian. Then nu(E)<=1. Equivalently E has a vertex cover of size at most one, so every representative has an endpoint obstruction (one deleted vertex Hamilton-extends neither rail) or a rail obstruction (one rail is Hamilton-extendable by neither deleted vertex), possibly both. If |A|=2, then x~A and y~A automatically because every three-vertex edge-ordered complete graph is Hamiltonian; hence x not~B and y not~B, so B is rail-obstructed. If |A|=3, accepted R963 forbids x not~A and y not~A simultaneously, because then A+x+y would be Hamiltonian and together with B would two-cover G. Thus for a trimer rail at least one of x,y extends A; if both extend A then B is obstructed by both, while if exactly one extends A the other deleted vertex is an endpoint obstruction.

## Hypotheses
Finite edge-ordered complete graph G with path-cover number greater than two; x,y distinct; G-{x,y} has the displayed exact Hamilton two-cover A|B. The trimer clause uses accepted R963.

## Scope
Pure support-level Hall compression for pair-deletion covers, with exact dimer/trimer short-rail consequences. No global-minimum-edge assumption, R980, threshold matching, or representative synchronization.

## Applicability
Use after choosing any pair-deletion representative in a hypothetical R888 counterexample. A perfect extension matching is immediate closure; the short-rail residuals are forced into a large-rail obstruction or a single blocked endpoint, feeding the accepted trimer/fixed-complement machinery.

## Persistence
Retain x,y, the literal rail supports A,B, the four Hamilton-extension truth values, the size-one Hall vertex cover, and in the short cases which endpoint or opposite rail is obstructed.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prove the rank-one obstruction impossible for rails of order at least four. Does not assert the Hall obstruction type is invariant under representative change. Does not close the trimer branch by itself.

## Proof
Let E be the displayed bipartite graph. If E had a matching of size two, after possibly swapping A and B we would have Hamilton paths on A+x and B+y. Their supports are disjoint and partition V(G), so they form a spanning two-cover of G, contradicting pc_inc(G)>2. Hence nu(E)<=1. In a bipartite graph on two vertices per side, König gives a vertex cover of size at most one. If the covering vertex lies on {x,y}, that deleted endpoint is adjacent to neither rail after removing the cover vertex formulation equivalently all extension edges are incident with the other endpoint; phrased directly, one endpoint is an obstruction whenever one left vertex has degree zero. If the covering vertex lies on {A,B}, all extension edges are incident with the other rail, equivalently one rail is obstructed by both endpoints. These possibilities include the empty graph and single-edge overlaps, so the stated endpoint/rail obstruction formulation is exhaustive.\n\nIf |A|=2, then A+z has three vertices for z=x and z=y. Every edge-ordered K3 has an increasing Hamilton path: choose its two smallest incident-in-a-path edges in increasing order, or simply orient the two edges of any length-two path from smaller to larger. Thus x~A and y~A. Since nu(E)<=1, neither x~B nor y~B may hold, because either such edge together with the opposite A-edge gives a perfect matching. Hence B+x and B+y are both non-Hamiltonian.\n\nNow suppose |A|=3. If both A+x and A+y were non-Hamiltonian, accepted R963 applied to the shared triangle A would make A+x+y Hamiltonian. Together with the Hamilton rail B this would be a spanning two-cover of G, contradiction. Hence at least one deleted vertex extends A. If both extend A, then no edge to B is allowed, again because any B-extension paired with the opposite A-extension would give a perfect matching; thus B is obstructed by both x,y. If exactly one, say x, extends A, then y cannot extend B, and by assumption y does not extend A, so y is an endpoint obstruction. This proves all clauses.
