# R723 — Crossed deep/shallow covers symmetrize to four-root first-inward reverse stars

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R621 frame with X={a,b,c,z}, Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4, L=q_0, u=q_1, R=q_m, s=q_{m-1}, v=q_2 and d=q_{m-2}. Then every root x in X has two literal exact singleton-deletion covers of the symmetric shallow form H-x=A_x^L sqcup Q[1,m] and H-x=Q[0,m-1] sqcup A_x^R, where the short P4s are: x=z: A_z^L=(L,a,c,b), A_z^R=(R,a,c,b); x=b: A_b^L=(L,c,a,z), A_b^R=(R,c,a,z); x=c: A_c^L=(a,b,z,L), A_c^R=(a,b,z,R); x=a: A_a^L=(c,z,b,L), A_a^R=(c,z,b,R). Consequently the first inward reverse boundary dimers of Q are universally signed by the entire four-cell: for every x in X, (v,u,x)=(q_2,q_1,x) is tight and (x,s,d)=(x,q_{m-1},q_{m-2}) is tight. Equivalently the tested dimer (q_2,q_1) is tail-signed by all four vertices of X and the tested dimer (q_{m-1},q_{m-2}) is head-signed by all four vertices of X. These four-witness stars coexist graph-intrinsically in the original crossed frame.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the accepted crossed R621 frame; Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4. Use only the literal R621 deep/shallow singleton-deletion covers and boundary antisymmetry R3.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed-frame strengthening. It symmetrizes the R621 deletion covers by moving the already-selected q_1 or q_{m-1} vertex back onto its original Q subpath, and then uses only the impossibility of a spanning two-cover to force the first-inward reverse stars. No R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,five-cell classification,generic payment,R436,or computation is used.

## Applicability
Use whenever the crossed R621 frame is available. It upgrades the previously pair-specific inward witness information to all four X vertices on the one-step-in reverse dimers (q_2,q_1) and (q_{m-1},q_{m-2}), while retaining four explicit root-labelled exact covers at each side.

## Proof
For roots z and b, accepted R621 gives the deep exact covers H-z=(u,L,a,c,b) sqcup Q[2,m] and H-b=(u,L,c,a,z) sqcup Q[2,m]. Removing the initial vertex u from the displayed short P5 and placing it back on its original Q suffix does not require a new seam: (L,a,c,b) and (L,c,a,z) are literal tight subpaths of the displayed P5s, while Q[1,m]=(u,q_2,...,R) is a literal tight subpath of Q. Hence H-z=(L,a,c,b) sqcup Q[1,m] and H-b=(L,c,a,z) sqcup Q[1,m] are literal two-covers. For roots c and a, R621 already gives the left shallow covers H-c=(a,b,z,L) sqcup Q[1,m] and H-a=(c,z,b,L) sqcup Q[1,m]. Thus the asserted A_x^L cover exists for every x in X. Dually, R621 already gives the right shallow covers for z,b: H-z=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (R,a,c,b) and H-b=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (R,c,a,z). Its right-deep covers H-c=Q[0,m-2] sqcup (a,b,z,R,s) and H-a=Q[0,m-2] sqcup (c,z,b,R,s) may be truncated at their terminal vertex s and s returned to its original Q prefix, yielding H-c=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (a,b,z,R) and H-a=Q[0,m-1] sqcup (c,z,b,R). Therefore the asserted A_x^R cover exists for every x. Each displayed two-cover of H-x is exact: if H-x were Hamiltonian then its Hamilton path together with singleton (x) would be a spanning two-cover of H. Now fix x in X and use the left shallow cover A_x^L sqcup Q[1,m]. If (x,u,v) were tight, then (x,u,v,q_3,...,R) together with A_x^L would be a spanning two-cover of H. Hence (x,u,v) is bad, and R3 forces its exact reversal (v,u,x) tight. Since x was arbitrary, (v,u,x) is tight for all x in X. Likewise use the right shallow cover Q[0,m-1] sqcup A_x^R. If (d,s,x) were tight, appending x to Q[0,m-1] and retaining A_x^R would two-cover H. Thus (d,s,x) is bad and R3 forces (x,s,d) tight for every x. This proves both universal inward stars and the full statement.
