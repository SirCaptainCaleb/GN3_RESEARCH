# R654 — Every vertex of a smallest counterexample is a universal preserved floor coordinate

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Fix any vertex p and any prescribed target x!=p. Then there is a finite chosen certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover of H or an ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced-pair floor whose singleton supports are exactly {p,x}, with p preserved as a literal singleton coordinate during the final target-steering step. In fact choose any auxiliary vertex t distinct from p (and arbitrary relative to x). Pair-Deletion Rigidity gives an exact two-cover H-{p,t}=U sqcup V with both rails nontrivial. Split U at any selected adjacent state yz into two nonempty contiguous tight subpaths A and B. Then R=A sqcup B sqcup V is a literal three-cover of the same proper residue W=H-{p,t}, while T=U sqcup V is a literal two-cover selecting yz across the distinct R-components A and B. Apply the fully reconstructed R176 proof to this explicit cross state and choose the arbitrary spare vertex d=p. The resulting balanced opposite-sign pair has one signed support literally singleton (p) and the other support of order at most two. If the opposite support is a singleton, a p-containing floor is already present; otherwise accepted R428 pays that support to singleton width while preserving (p). Finally accepted R432 preserves p and replaces only the other singleton by the prescribed x. Thus every physical vertex is universally steerable to a floor with every chosen partner. Different choices of p,x,t and split state are alternative lineages.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Use accepted Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429, fully reconstructible cross-state upgrade R176, fixed-singleton descent R428, and complete floor steering R432.

## Scope
Order-free global Strong Level-(1) theorem. No R24 frame, transitive four-cell, endpoint, capture, middle-gate, longest-path, or beyond-order-ten hypothesis is used.

## Applicability
Whenever a downstream consumer needs a floor containing a prescribed physical vertex p and any chosen second coordinate x, there is no need for a specialized source packet merely to obtain those coordinates. Delete p with any auxiliary t, split one rail of an exact pair-deletion two-cover, use the split edge as the R176 cross state with spare p, pay while preserving p, then steer the other singleton to x. Specialized floor constructions remain valuable only for the extra ancestry they retain.

## Persistence
Retain p,t; exact pair-deletion cover U sqcup V; chosen split state yz of U; split reference cover A sqcup B sqcup V; R176 birth with literal singleton p; any R428 refund certificates preserving p; and the R432 replacement ledger to target x.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not make different target floors simultaneous or current in one representative. Does not make floor coordinates rare, canonical, or contradictory. Does not preserve arbitrary source geometry beyond the split-cover ancestry. Does not imply specialized floor constructions are redundant when their physical provenance is needed downstream. Does not by itself close H.

## Proof
Fix p and choose any auxiliary t!=p. Accepted Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429 gives an exact two-cover T=U sqcup V of W=H-{p,t}, with both U and V nontrivial. Orient U=(u_0,...,u_k), k>=1, and choose any selected state y z=(u_i,u_{i+1}). Let A=(u_0,...,u_i) and B=(u_{i+1},...,u_k). Both are nonempty contiguous tight subpaths of U. Therefore R=A sqcup B sqcup V is a literal three-component path cover of exactly W. The original T is a literal two-cover of W and selects the directed state yz whose endpoints lie in the distinct R-components A and B.\n\nApply accepted fully reconstructed R176 directly to R,T,yz. Its spare vertex d may be chosen arbitrarily outside W. Since p is outside W, choose d=p. In the nontrivial-source-component case, the P540 construction signs a dimer inside W with common witness z and gives the opposite singleton sign on (p) using the automatic p-z tight dimer; in the singleton-source-component case it signs the source singleton and again gives the opposite singleton (p). Thus in every case the R176 birth has one signed support literally (p), and its opposite support has order at most two.\n\nIf the opposite support is already singleton, retain the resulting ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor {p,v}. If it has order two, apply accepted R428 with (p) as the fixed singleton side. Along its chosen nonclosing continuation p is unchanged and the opposite support strictly descends to singleton width, so closure occurs or a floor {p,v} results.\n\nNow fix the originally prescribed x!=p. Apply accepted R432 with target pair {p,x}. The current floor already shares p with the target, so use the one-coordinate replacement macro preserving p. It yields either a spanning two-cover or the exact ancestry-bearing floor {p,x}. This proves the theorem.
