# R837 — Crossed middle-gate dimers are one-way path endcaps unless they force a labelled P4

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R621/R649 frame with X={a,b,c,z}, middle-gate dimers G_3={a,c} and G_4={b,z}. Let K be any vertex-simple tight path in H. (G3 endcap.) Suppose K contains the two vertices of G_3 consecutively in either order S=(s_0,s_1). If S is not the terminal block of K, let y be the vertex immediately following s_1. Then H contains a tight Hamilton P4 on {s_0,s_1,y,w} for some fixed-frame R649 witness w distinct from y. Consequently, in the absence of this labelled P4, every contiguous occurrence of G_3 on any tight path is terminal. (G4 endcap.) Suppose K contains the two vertices of G_4 consecutively in either order S=(s_0,s_1). If S is not the initial block of K, let y be the vertex immediately preceding s_0. Then H contains a tight Hamilton P4 on {y,s_0,s_1,w} for some fixed-frame R649 witness w distinct from y. Consequently, in the absence of this labelled P4, every contiguous occurrence of G_4 on any tight path is initial. In particular, for any exact two-cover T of W=H-{q_2,q_{m-2}} in the crossed beyond-ten inward-anchor frame, if neither X dimer is fragmented and no such labelled P4 occurs, the G_4 block is the initial block of its T-rail and the G_3 block is the terminal block of its T-rail. Hence any positive excess sigma_G(T)>0 in that no-P4, unfragmented-X branch is paid entirely by fragmentation of the three Q-cells; it cannot come from an internal or wrongly oriented contiguous X block.

## Hypotheses
Accepted crossed R621/R649 frame and accepted universal four-cell theorem R518. For the final sigma corollary, T is an exact two-cover of the inward-anchor deletion residue W=H-{q_2,q_{m-2}} and both physical X dimers each occur as one contiguous T-block.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed-frame path-level theorem, valid independently of sigma_G(T), five-cell quotient shape, monotonicity, or order beyond the existence of the named frame. The final corollary specializes to the beyond-ten inward-anchor deletion cover.

## Applicability
Use before any sigma=0/sigma>0 split. First inspect whether either crossed X dimer is contiguous on a current path. A nonterminal G_3 or noninitial G_4 immediately yields source-labelled P4 geometry. If both X dimers are contiguous and no P4 occurs, pin G_4 to a rail start and G_3 to a rail end; all excess transition complexity must then live in Q-cell fragmentation.

## Persistence
Retain the actual orientation of the contiguous X dimer, its immediate path neighbor, a distinct fixed R649 same-polarity witness, and the resulting four-vertex P4 certificate. In the no-P4 branch retain the forced rail-end role of each contiguous X dimer.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not rule out fragmentation of G_3 or G_4 into singleton blocks. Does not close sigma>0 when excess lies only in Q-cell fragmentation. Does not assert the labelled P4 itself closes H. Does not require or consume R763.

## Proof
For G_3, let S=(s_0,s_1) be its actual consecutive order on K and suppose y follows s_1. Tightness of K gives (s_0,s_1,y) tight, so y is a tail witness on S. By accepted R649, for either orientation S=(a,c) or (c,a), the same tested dimer has fixed head polarity witnessed by at least three named vertices in the crossed frame; equivalently there exists a fixed w distinct from S with (w,s_0,s_1) tight, and because there are at least three choices we may choose w != y. Apply accepted R518 to the tight trimer (w,s_0,s_1) with fourth vertex y. The y-sign on the terminal physical dimer S has polarity opposite the natural head sign from w, so R518 is in its Hamilton-P4 branch. Hence {w,s_0,s_1,y} supports a tight P4. Therefore if no such P4 is retained, a contiguous G_3 block cannot have a successor and must terminate its path. The G_4 proof is dual. Let S=(s_0,s_1) be its actual consecutive order and suppose y precedes s_0. Tightness gives (y,s_0,s_1), a head sign on S. R649 supplies at least three fixed tail witnesses w with (s_0,s_1,w) tight for either orientation of G_4; choose w != y. Applying R518 to (s_0,s_1,w) with fourth vertex y gives a Hamilton P4 because the y-sign is opposite in polarity to the natural tail sign. Thus absent P4, contiguous G_4 must be initial. For an inward-anchor exact two-cover T with both X dimers unfragmented, apply the two path-level conclusions to their T-rails. Since sigma counts extra source-cell blocks beyond the five reference cells, and neither X cell contributes an extra block in this branch, every positive sigma contribution comes from splitting one or more Q-cells.
