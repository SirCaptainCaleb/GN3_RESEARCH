# R2001 — Later OUT-v first loss reduces to three exact first-open residuals

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789410868.203179`

## Statement

Retain the non-loop OUT-`v` first-loss setting with `j>t+1`, and put `c=b_{t+1}` for the first R1033-open spectator slot.

A later first loss can survive that first-open slot only if at least one of the following occurs:

1. the normalized pre-loss prefix is not a literal tight three-path forest;
2. at least one spectator split-copy at `c` has already left its `J` state;
3. the explicit first-open detour reaches the tight three-forest supplied by R1061, but the final source-gated closure `b_t->s` has a bad endpoint seam or closes a same-rail directed cycle.

If none of these residuals occurs, there is an earlier literal tight two-path cover of the underlying `G` support.

## Proof

Let `M=hat M_{r-1}` be the normalized prefix immediately before the putative later OUT-`v` first loss, with far free source copy `s,in` as in R1051.

Assume none of the three residuals occurs. Then `M` is a literal tight three-path forest and both spectator split-copies at the first-open slot remain in their J-state. By R1061,

`M' = M-{b_t->b_{t+1}}+{v->b_{t+1}}`

is a literal tight three-path forest whose free copies are exactly `b_t,out` and `s,in`.

The unique direct closure is therefore

`k=b_t->s`.

Because the third residual is assumed absent, neither endpoint seam of `k` is bad and the two endpoints lie on different rails. Hence `k` joins the terminal of one path component to the source of another. It creates no directed cycle, and its only new turns are precisely the two endpoint seams already assumed tight. Thus `M'+k` is a literal tight two-path cover of the G-support.

Therefore any surviving later `j>t+1` first-loss cell must exhibit at least one of the three stated residuals.
