# R686 — Carrier-witness saturation: capture fails only for a single witness with at most two carriers

## Statement
Let H be a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2. Let S be a graph-intrinsic signed tight support with a fixed tested orientation, and let T be one exact two-cover of H-V(S). Let K_1,...,K_k with k>=1 be retained vertex-simple tight carriers with V(K_i)=V(S) union {c_i}, the complementary singletons c_1,...,c_k pairwise distinct. Let W be a nonempty set of exact same-polarity sign witnesses on the tested orientation of S, with W disjoint from V(S) union {c_1,...,c_k}.

Applying accepted R508 once per carrier to the same T, with deletion D=V(S), absorbable block {c_i}, and recompletion path K_i, yields for each i a selected T-state {c_i,y_i} with y_i outside V(K_i)=V(S) union {c_i}. Call these k simultaneous states the forced crossing family of T.

Then the accepted R527 capture/refund branch applies to some crossing/witness pair of this family unless W={v} is a singleton and y_i=v for every i. Since v lies on exactly one rail of the two-path cover T it has at most two selected neighbours, and c_1,...,c_k are distinct, so that exceptional configuration forces k<=2. Consequently:

(i) If |W|>=2, capture is unconditional for every k>=1.
(ii) If |W|=1 and k>=3, capture is unconditional.
(iii) If |W|=1 with W={v} and k=2, then either capture holds, or v is internal on its T rail with selected neighbours c_1 and c_2, so T contains a graph-intrinsic tight trimer of consecutive order (c_1,v,c_2) or (c_2,v,c_1).
(iv) If |W|=1 and k=1, nothing is concluded beyond the R527 dichotomy for that single carrier.

Whenever capture holds, S has order two, and H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample, accepted R526 aligned dimer payment gives a finite certificate-retaining continuation to either a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced pair with singleton support, retaining S, its tested orientation, the used witness, the exact selected crossing, the chosen marker orientation, and the captured vertex.

The escape in (iii) is the only one, and it is sharp: capture fails exactly when a single available witness is selected-adjacent to every complementary singleton. Accepted R542 is the corner k=1, |W|=2 of clause (i). The CAP/XET dichotomy of pending R682 is the corner k=2, |W|=1 of clause (iii) with c_1,c_2 the two physical endpoints. Clause (ii) is the previously unused corner: three or more carriers with distinct complementary singletons defeat any single witness outright, independently of frame, order, and witness identity.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament H with pc(H)>2. S is a graph-intrinsic signed tight support with a fixed tested orientation. An exact two-cover T of H-V(S) is supplied, not produced. K_1,...,K_k are retained vertex-simple tight carriers with V(K_i)=V(S) union {c_i} and c_1,...,c_k pairwise distinct. W is a nonempty set of exact same-polarity sign witnesses on the tested orientation, disjoint from V(S) union {c_1,...,c_k}. Uses accepted R508 and R527. The payment clause additionally requires S of order two and H a hypothetical smallest counterexample, via accepted R526.

## Scope
Order-free combinatorial parent theorem for carrier/witness counting. No crossed frame, five-cell residue, order bound, or occupancy normalization. No R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,R24, generic R358, or R436. Does not supply the exact deletion two-cover.

## Applicability
Use whenever a tested signed dimer carries several tight carriers whose supports exceed V(S) by distinct single vertices. Count carriers k and available same-polarity witnesses j: capture is unconditional if j>=2 or k>=3, and only j=1 with k<=2 can escape, the k=2 escape being exactly a c_1-v-c_2 tight trimer. When a frame supplies a third carrier, prefer enlarging the carrier list over hunting for a second witness.

## Persistence
Retain S with tested orientation and polarity, the carrier list and its complementary singletons, the witness set, the chosen exact H-V(S) cover T, the forced crossing family, and in the capture branch the avoiding crossing, the used witness, the marker orientation, the captured vertex, and R527/R526 ancestry. In the k=2 escape retain the literal rail order (c_1,v,c_2).

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not produce the exact deletion two-cover. Does not assert that captures at different complementary singletons coexist as paid descendants, nor that any paid descendant is current in T. Does not assert that the k=2 escape and a capture coexist under its own hypotheses; that requires a third carrier. Does not apply when a witness coincides with a complementary singleton or lies in V(S). Gives no conclusion for k=1 with a single witness beyond R527. Does not by itself consume a cross-end trimer or close H.

## Proof
Fix the exact two-cover T of H-V(S). By definition T is a literal spanning two-path cover T_1 sqcup T_2 of H-V(S).

Step 1 (forced crossing family). Fix i in {1,...,k}. Apply accepted R508 with deletion D=V(S), absorbable block {c_i}, recompletion path Q=K_i, and the cover T. Its hypotheses hold: D is nonempty and proper; {c_i} is a nonempty proper subset of V(H)\D, since c_i is not in V(S) and H has further vertices; K_i is a vertex-simple tight path with vertex set exactly D union {c_i}; T is a literal spanning two-path cover of H-D; and pc(H)>2. R508 therefore forces a selected T-state with exactly one endpoint in {c_i} and the other in (V(H)\V(S))\{c_i}. Write one such physical state as {c_i,y_i}. Because y_i lies outside V(S) union {c_i}=V(K_i), this state is a selected carrier/exterior crossing for the R527 partition C_i=V(K_i)\V(S)={c_i} and E_i=V(H)\V(K_i). All k such states are simultaneous current facts of the single cover T, since only T and the fixed deletion D=V(S) are shared.

Step 2 (when capture applies). Let w be an element of W. Since W is disjoint from V(S) union {c_1,...,c_k}, w is neither in V(S) nor equal to any c_i. Hence the crossing {c_i,y_i} avoids w if and only if y_i is not equal to w. Suppose some pair (i,w) satisfies y_i not equal to w. Apply accepted R527 with retained tight carrier K_i, signed support S with V(S) contained in V(K_i), exact sign witness w outside V(S), the cover T, and this selected carrier/exterior crossing. The physical dimer {c_i,y_i} is disjoint from V(S) union {w}, so the capture/refund branch of R527 applies, and either physical endpoint may be designated as the captured signed anchor by choosing the corresponding marker orientation before actualization; in particular c_i may be designated. This is the capture conclusion.

Step 3 (isolating the escape). Capture therefore fails only when y_i=w for every i and every w in W. If W contained two distinct elements w and w prime, then y_1 would have to equal both, which is impossible. Hence W={v} is a singleton and y_i=v for every i.

Step 4 (the counting step). In that case the states {c_1,v},...,{c_k,v} are all selected in T, and they are pairwise distinct because c_1,...,c_k are pairwise distinct. Each is a rail adjacency incident with v. The vertex v lies in H-V(S) because W is disjoint from V(S), so v occupies exactly one position on exactly one rail of T, and a vertex of a path has at most two adjacencies on that path. Hence k<=2. This establishes clause (i) via Step 3, clause (ii) via this bound, and the dichotomy asserted in clause (iii).

Step 5 (the k=2 escape is a trimer). Suppose k=2 and capture fails. By Steps 3 and 4 the two selected states {c_1,v} and {c_2,v} are exactly the two rail adjacencies at v, so v is internal on its rail with neighbours c_1 and c_2, and that rail contains the consecutive order (c_1,v,c_2) or (c_2,v,c_1). A T rail is a tight path, so this three-vertex segment is a graph-intrinsic tight trimer in the corresponding order.

Step 6 (payment). Suppose capture holds, S has order two, and H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample. Then R527 invokes the aligned dimer payment clause of accepted R526 on S and the oriented marker {c_i,y_i}, which is a tight dimer disjoint from V(S) union {w}, giving a finite certificate-retaining continuation to either a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced pair with singleton support, while retaining the coordinates listed in the persistence contract. No later currentness of T is claimed.

Step 7 (the two known corners). With k=1 and |W|=2 the single crossing {c_1,y_1} can equal at most one of the two witnesses, so the other witness avoids it and Step 2 gives capture; this is exactly accepted R542. With k=2, |W|=1, and c_1,c_2 the two physical endpoints of the crossed frame, Steps 3 to 5 give the CAP/XET dichotomy of pending R682. The present theorem is therefore a common parent of both, and clause (ii) is the corner neither of them uses.
