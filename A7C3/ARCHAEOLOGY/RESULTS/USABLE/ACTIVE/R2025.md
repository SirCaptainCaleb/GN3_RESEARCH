# R2025 — source-exposure anchor lift to a spanning cap packet

## Statement

In the retained smallest-counterexample source frame, let `T` be a literal exact two-cover of `G=H-{A,C}` exposing a source spoke `s in {p,q,r}` as a rail endpoint, and retain the tight source trimer `(A,s,C)`.

Delete `s` from its nontrivial `T`-rail, preserving that rail's original direction, and adjoin `(A,s,C)`. The result is a literal tight spanning three-forest `N` of `H`.

For every ordered pair of distinct rails of `N`, the terminal-to-source join is an acyclic exact-two-cover-cardinality candidate. Since `H` is a counterexample and therefore has no physical spanning exact two-cover, all six joins fail physically. Active R2021 and R3 consequently give one reverse-endpoint-cap clause for each ordered rail pair.

Thus source exposure has an ancestry-bearing physical consumer on all of `H`, without passing through an anonymous balanced-pair witness.

## Proof

Let the `T`-rail containing `s` be `P`. If `s` is initial, write `P=(s,u_0,...,u_k)`; if `s` is terminal, write `P=(u_0,...,u_k,s)`. Do not reverse the rail. Accepted R429 gives nontriviality of both `T`-rails, so `P-s=(u_0,...,u_k)` is nonempty and remains tight in its original orientation. Let the other `T`-rail be `Q`.

The three paths

`(A,s,C) | (P-s) | Q`

are pairwise vertex-disjoint and cover all vertices of `H`. Hence they form a literal tight spanning three-forest `N`.

Take distinct rails `R_i=(r_i^0,...,r_i^m)` and `R_j=(r_j^0,...,r_j^n)` of `N`, and add `g_{ij}=r_i^m -> r_j^0`. The two matching copies are free and the physical endpoints lie on distinct rails. Therefore the join cannot create a directed cycle and reduces the three rails to exact two-cover cardinality.

If it were physical, it would be a spanning exact two-cover of `H`, contrary to counterexample status. By active R2021, with cycle failure impossible, at least one newly created endpoint seam is bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 reverses any such bad seam to a tight reverse trimer. This yields six cap clauses, one for each ordered pair of distinct rails.

If one seam is absent because the relevant rail is a singleton, the other seam must be bad and its reverse cap is forced. If both seams were absent, the join would be automatically physical, impossible. In particular the two nonanchor rails cannot both be singletons.

Writing `R=P-s=(u_0,...,u_k)`, the two joins touching the anchor rail `S=(A,s,C)` give especially explicit clauses:

- failure of `C->u_0` forces `(u_0,C,s)` tight, or when `k>=1`, `(u_1,u_0,C)` tight;
- failure of `u_k->A` forces `(s,A,u_k)` tight, or when `k>=1`, `(A,u_k,u_{k-1})` tight.

The identical two clauses hold for `Q`. The remaining two clauses are the directed joins between `P-s` and `Q`.

## Dependencies and scope

Dependencies: retained tight source trimer `(A,s,C)`, accepted R429 nontriviality, smallest-counterexample no-spanning-two-cover hypothesis, active R2021, and R3.

This result converts source exposure into a spanning ancestry-bearing cap packet. It does not by itself prove that one cap-guided exchange closes the conjecture; the next consumer must use these caps to construct a legal recompletion or a genuinely monotone exchange while preserving the marked data required by G13.
