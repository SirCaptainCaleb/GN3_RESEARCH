# R471 — Two-cover partition rigidity modulo balanced-pair birth

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, W a proper vertex subset, and F=P sqcup Q and F'=P' sqcup Q' two literal two-covers of H[W] with all four components nonempty. First, the representative-rigidity conclusion of R470 holds: if the unordered rail-support partitions differ, R410 yields a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair; if the partitions agree but the literal Hamilton orders differ on some common support, R435 yields an exact reversal of an old adjacent state, a tight reverse trimer, or a vertex-simple proper tight cycle; outside these outputs the two covers are literally identical up to rail exchange. In addition, there is a sharp opposite-end refinement. Suppose Q=(q_0,...,q_n) with n>=1 and some rail K of F' exposes q_n as its head. Then either the support partitions differ and the R410 balanced-pair output occurs, or after relabelling V(K)=V(Q). Write the second vertex of K as q_j. If j=n-1, K begins with the exact reversed terminal state (q_n,q_{n-1}) of Q. If j<n-1, the consecutive K-state q_n q_j is a nonadjacent reverse-order encounter with Q, so R435 yields a tight reverse trimer or a vertex-simple proper tight cycle. Dually, if some rail of F' exposes q_0 as its tail, then either a balanced pair occurs, or that rail ends with the exact reversed initial state (q_1,q_0), or R435 yields a reverse trimer/proper cycle. Therefore forcing a terminal vertex of one exact-cover rail to the opposite end of another exact cover is sufficient to obtain either the precise terminal-state inversion or an already explicit reverse-order output; the predecessor/successor need not be steered separately.

## Hypotheses
H is a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament; W is proper; F and F' are literal two-covers of H[W] with nonempty components; accepted R410 and R435. For the opposite-end refinement, the reference rail Q has at least two vertices.

## Scope
Order-free strengthening of exact-cover representative rigidity. Adds a one-coordinate endpoint-inversion criterion tailored to deletion-fiber comparisons.

## Applicability
Use when a cross-fiber argument needs the reversal of a terminal directed state. It is enough to force the old terminal vertex to the opposite rail end in another exact cover: a wrong next vertex is not failure but immediately triggers the R435 reverse-trimer/proper-cycle output. In particular this lowers the R469 cross-fiber target from a prescribed reversed endpoint dimer to opposite-end exposure of its terminal vertex.

## Persistence
The common residue and compared covers are representative-relative. Any R410 balanced pair, exact reversed directed state, reverse trimer, or proper cycle is graph-intrinsic once exhibited. The endpoint identity q_n or q_0 is retained in the special refinement.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not force opposite-end exposure to exist. Does not say a reverse trimer or proper cycle closes the conjecture. Does not localize the R435 output beyond the displayed first opposite-end state when the exact adjacent reversal fails. Does not compare different residues without an additional cross-fiber synchronization input.

## Proof
Apply R410 to F and F'. If their unordered support partitions differ, R410 gives the balanced opposite-sign pair and all conclusions follow. Hence assume the support partitions agree and relabel F' so that each rail has the same vertex support as its counterpart in F. The general representative-rigidity statement follows exactly by the index-descent argument: if a same-support rail order K differs from P=(v_0,...,v_k), the sequence of P-indices along K is a nonidentity permutation and therefore has a descent. A consecutive descent v_i v_j with i>j is a single-state reverse-order encounter in R435; when i=j+1 it is the exact reversed adjacent state, and otherwise R435 gives a reverse trimer or proper cycle. Now assume Q=(q_0,...,q_n), n>=1, and a rail K of F' has head q_n. Under the fixed support partition K must have vertex set V(Q), so K has a second vertex q_j with 0<=j<n. The consecutive K-contacts q_n,q_j are in reverse Q-order. If j=n-1, K begins with (q_n,q_{n-1}), the exact reversal of Q's terminal state. If j<n-1, the K-subpath between those consecutive contacts is the single state q_n q_j and is not the adjacent-reversal exception of R435, so R435 yields a tight reverse trimer at a seam or a vertex-simple proper tight cycle. For the dual statement, if a same-support rail K has tail q_0, write its penultimate vertex q_j. If j=1 the final state is the exact reversal (q_1,q_0) of Q's initial state; if j>1, the consecutive state q_j q_0 is a nonadjacent reverse-order encounter and R435 again yields a reverse trimer or proper cycle. Thus opposite-end exposure alone forces the advertised terminal inversion-or-output alternative.
