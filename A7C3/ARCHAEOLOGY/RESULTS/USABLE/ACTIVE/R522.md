# R522 — Two no-P4 extensions of one tight trimer force a P5

## Statement
Let C=(a,b,c) be a tight trimer in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, and let y,z be distinct vertices outside C. Suppose neither four-set C union {y} nor C union {z} supports a tight Hamilton P4. Apply the exact R516 no-P4 classification to the two fourth-vertex cells and let sigma_y,sigma_z in {00,01,10,11} be their alignment signatures. Then sigma_y=sigma_z. Indeed the shared core orientation at middle a distinguishes alignment bit A: signatures 00,01 contain (c,a,b), while 10,11 contain (b,a,c); the shared core orientation at middle c distinguishes B: signatures 00,10 contain (b,c,a), while 01,11 contain (a,c,b). Since these core turns are graph-intrinsic, both bits agree. For each common signature, one comparison of y and z now yields a literal Hamilton P5: in signatures 00 and 10, whichever of (y,a,z),(z,a,y) is tight gives respectively (c,y,a,z,b) or (c,z,a,y,b); in signature 01, whichever of (y,c,z),(z,c,y) is tight gives (b,y,c,z,a) or (b,z,c,y,a); in signature 11, whichever of (y,b,z),(z,b,y) is tight gives (c,y,b,z,a) or (c,z,b,y,a). Therefore two distinct no-P4 extensions of one tight trimer always amplify to a graph-intrinsic tight Hamilton P5 on their five-vertex union.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; C=(a,b,c) is tight; y,z are distinct and outside C; neither C union {y} nor C union {z} has a tight Hamilton P4; accepted R516.

## Scope
Order-free five-vertex synchronization theorem reconstructed from the valid core of rejected R477/P492. It uses the corrected exact four-signature table rather than the false legacy strict-order representation.

## Applicability
Use whenever the same tight trimer is tested against two distinct fourth vertices and both tests land in the no-P4 branch. The output is an explicit P5 on the five role-labelled vertices.

## Persistence
C,y,z, the common R516 signature, the chosen y/z comparison at the specified middle, and the resulting literal P5 are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not currentize the P5 as a component of an unrelated path cover. Does not assert its endpoint chord is tight or that the P5 alone closes the conjecture. Does not allow mixed no-P4 signatures on the shared core.

## Proof
Apply R516 to C with y and with z. For a generic fourth vertex w, the exact R516 rows show that the orientation of the core triple with middle a is (c,a,b) in signatures 00 and 01 and (b,a,c) in signatures 10 and 11. Thus the graph-intrinsic orientation of the shared core {a,b,c} at middle a determines A, so A_y=A_z. Likewise the core orientation at middle c is (b,c,a) in signatures 00 and 10 and (a,c,b) in signatures 01 and 11, so B_y=B_z. Hence sigma_y=sigma_z. Now use R3 on the distinct triple {y,z,m} at the indicated middle m. If the common signature is 00, the row contains (c,y,a) and (a,y,b) after the substitution w=y, and likewise for z; if (y,a,z) is tight then the three turns (c,y,a),(y,a,z),(a,z,b) certify the path (c,y,a,z,b), while the reverse comparison gives (c,z,a,y,b). The same construction works in signature 10 because its row also contains (c,w,a) and (a,w,b). In signature 01, the row contains (b,w,c) and (c,w,a); compare y,z at middle c to obtain (b,y,c,z,a) or its y/z swap. In signature 11, the row contains (c,w,b) and (b,w,a); compare y,z at middle b to obtain (c,y,b,z,a) or its swap. Thus every possible common signature yields a literal tight P5 on exactly the five vertices.
