# R2028 — Three-vertex isolated-X endpoint cap packet

**Status:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Source:** Slack workspace R2028 re-audit

## Repaired statement

In the retained R2024/R2025 source frame, suppose a low-transition exact two-cover `T` has an isolated three-vertex X-rail `P`. Choose a retained source endpoint `s` of `P`. After deleting `s`, preserve the residual rail orientation and write

`R=(u_0,u_1)`.

Then counterexample status forces at least one of the two source-facing seams

`(s,C,u_0)` and `(u_1,A,s)`

to be bad. Equivalently, R3 forces at least one of the reverse caps

`(u_0,C,s)` and `(s,A,u_1)`

to be tight.

No stronger conclusion that `P` must contain `v`, or that a unique v-anchor cap is forced, is certified by the original argument.

## Proof

Delete `s` and apply audited R2025. The resulting literal spanning three-forest is

`N=(A,s,C) | (u_0,u_1) | Q`,

where `Q` is the other T-rail.

Cut the sole edge `u_0->u_1` and add `u_1->A` and `C->u_0`. The non-`Q` component has the literal vertex order

`u_1,A,s,C,u_0`.

The inherited middle turn `(A,s,C)` is tight. The only new seam turns are exactly

`(u_1,A,s)` and `(s,C,u_0)`.

If both were tight, this path together with `Q` would be a literal spanning exact two-cover of `H`, contradicting counterexample status. Hence at least one is bad. Complete-reversal antisymmetry R3 converts the two possible bad seams respectively to the tight caps `(s,A,u_1)` and `(u_0,C,s)`.

This is exactly the `k=1` case of R2026.

## Removed overclaims

The former proof attempted to exclude the all-source three-vertex rail and then force a unique cap at `v`. Those steps repeatedly treated cyclic rotations of a tight ordered turn as automatically tight. R3 does not provide cyclic-rotation invariance: it relates only `(x,m,y)` to its complete reversal `(y,m,x)`. Therefore those stronger conclusions are withdrawn rather than promoted.

## Dependencies and scope

Trusted dependencies: R2025, R2026 (or the direct `k=1` surgery reproduced above), R3, and counterexample status of `H`.

Provisional dependencies: none.

The surviving result is an orientation-sensitive endpoint-cap disjunction. It does not classify the three vertices of the isolated X-rail.
