# R902 — Every nonHamilton five-vertex boundary tournament is edge-orderable

## Statement
Let H be a boundary 3-tournament on exactly five vertices. If H has no directed tight Hamilton path, its line-graph comparison orientation Gamma(H) is acyclic. Equivalently there is a total order on E(K_5) realizing every tight turn as an increasing consecutive-edge comparison. In contrapositive form, any nonintegrable five-vertex boundary tournament is Hamiltonian. Consequently, in any boundary tournament with pc(H)>2, the five-vertex complement of any proper tight path is edge-orderable.

## Hypotheses
Boundary antisymmetry R3; comparison orientation and shortest-cycle classification R887. The complement consequence assumes only pc(H)>2 and a tight path whose complement has exactly five vertices.

## Scope
A finite, order-free five-vertex integrability theorem. The proof below is entirely human and uses only boundary antisymmetry, the shortest-cycle classification of R887, and the human parallel-source P5 lemma recorded in S9022.

## Applicability
Use on any nonHamilton five-cell to replace its ternary turn relation by one ordinary total edge order. In a system with pc(H)>2, the five-vertex complement of any proper tight path is therefore edge-orderable.

## Persistence
The resulting comparison orientation and any topological edge order are graph-intrinsic consequences of the five-cell. A particular topological order need not be unique.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Acyclicity is necessary, not sufficient, for an arbitrary five-set to be nonHamilton. No integrability assertion is made for larger nonHamilton induced systems.

## Proof
Assume that H has no tight Hamilton P5. We prove that Gamma(H) is acyclic.

We use one elementary forcing rule repeatedly. If W=v_0v_1v_2v_3v_4 is a five-vertex word and two of its three consecutive turns are already known tight, then the third turn cannot also be tight, since otherwise W would be a Hamilton P5. Hence the third turn is bad and its complete reversal is tight by boundary antisymmetry. In the tables below

    W => xyz

means that, at that point of the row, the other two turns of W are already tight, so the forcing rule certifies xyz. Each row is read from left to right.

Suppose Gamma(H) has a directed cycle and choose one of minimum length. By R887 it is either a star triangle, an ordinary triangle, or the comparison cycle coming from a simple ordinary cycle. An ordinary five-cycle immediately gives a Hamilton P5 in its cyclic vertex order, so only ordinary four-cycles and the two kinds of triangles remain.

### 1. Ordinary four-cycle
Relabel the five vertices o,a,b,c,d so that the shortest comparison cycle is

    oa -> ab -> bc -> co -> oa.

Equivalently

    oab, abc, bco, coa

are tight. Boundary antisymmetry makes acd/dca a complementary pair. In the acd branch, oac/cao is complementary; in the dca branch, bac/cab is complementary. Thus the following four rows are exhaustive.

| branch | successive forced turns | contradiction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| acd, oac | bcoad=>dao; boacd=>aob; daobc=>cbo; cdaob=>adc; adcbo=>bcd; oabcd=>bao | oab and bao |
| acd, cao | dbcoa=>cbd; doabc=>aod; caodb=>bdo; acbdo=>bca; bcaod=>doa; doabc=>bao | oab and bao |
| dca, bac | coabd=>dba; odbac=>bdo; abcod=>doc; bdoca=>aco; dbaco=>abd; coabd=>bao | oab and bao |
| dca, cab | dcabo=>oba; dabco=>bad; obadc=>cda; cobad=>boc; bocda=>dco; dcoab=>bao | oab and bao |

Every row contradicts boundary antisymmetry. Hence a shortest comparison cycle cannot have length four.

### 2. Star triangle
Relabel so the directed star triangle is centered at o:

    aob, boc, coa

are tight. For the fifth vertex d define

    S(d)={u in {a,b,c}: uod is tight}.

Cyclically permuting a,b,c preserves the displayed star triangle. Also, replacing the ternary relation by its complete-reversal dual preserves Hamiltonicity after reversing every path; followed by swapping b and c it restores the displayed star triangle and sends |S(d)| to 3-|S(d)|. Therefore it is enough to treat |S(d)|=0 and |S(d)|=1.

#### 2a. The case |S(d)|=0
After cyclic relabelling, the initial tight turns are

    aob, boc, coa, doa, dob, doc.

The following nested branches are exhaustive because each tested pair consists of complete reversals.

| branch | successive forced turns | contradiction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| obc | dobca=>acb; doacb=>cao; dcaob=>acd; aobcd=>dcb; daobc=>oad; oadcb=>cda; bocda=>dco; bdcoa=>cdb; acdbo=>obd; caobd=>oac | oac and cao |
| cbo, oda, oba, bdc | bdcoa=>ocd; bocda=>adc; obadc=>dab; odabc=>cba; docba=>bco; dbcoa=>cbd; aobdc=>dbo; adboc=>bda; cbdao=>oad; bcoad=>dao | oda and dao |
| cbo, oda, oba, cdb | dobac=>cab; cdoba=>odc; odcab=>acd; acdbo=>obd; caobd=>oac; oacdb=>cao | oac and cao |
| cbo, oda, abo, oac | abocd=>dco; daboc=>bad; badco=>cda; cdaob=>oad; coadb=>bda; bcoad=>ocb; ocbda=>dbc; doacb=>bca; odbca=>bdo; bdoac=>cao | oac and cao |
| cbo, oda, abo, cao | dcaob=>acd; abocd=>dco; bdcoa=>cdb; acdbo=>obd; caobd=>oac | oac and cao |
| cbo, ado, obd | caobd=>oac; badoc=>dab; daboc=>oba; dobac=>cab; cdoba=>odc; odcab=>acd; aobdc=>cdb; oacdb=>cao | oac and cao |
| cbo, ado, dbo | adboc=>bda; cadob=>dac; bdaco=>oca; dboca=>obd | obd and dbo |

Thus |S(d)|=0 is impossible under the nonHamilton assumption.

#### 2b. The case |S(d)|=1
By cyclic relabelling take S(d)={c}. Thus

    aob, boc, coa, doa, dob, cod

are tight. Test odb/bdo, then the displayed complementary pair in each branch.

| branch | successive forced turns | contradiction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| odb, adc | codba=>abd; acodb=>oca; ocabd=>bac; dobac=>abo; abocd=>dco; badco=>dab; daboc=>oba | oba and abo |
| odb, cda | cdaob=>oad; acodb=>oca; bocad=>dac; dboca=>obd; obdac=>adb; coadb=>dao | oad and dao |
| bdo, bdc | bdcoa=>ocd; abocd=>oba; aobdc=>dbo; dboca=>aco; adboc=>bda; bdaco=>cad; cadob=>oda; codab=>bad; bocda=>adc; obadc=>abo | oba and abo |
| bdo, cdb | bdoac=>cao; dcaob=>acd; acdbo=>obd; caobd=>oac | oac and cao |

Hence |S(d)|=1 is impossible, and by the duality already noted so are |S(d)|=2 and 3. A shortest comparison cycle cannot be a star triangle.

### 3. Ordinary triangle
Relabel so the comparison triangle is

    oa -> ab -> bo -> oa,

or equivalently

    oab, abo, boa

are tight. Let c,d be the remaining two vertices. For an exterior vertex w in {c,d}, define its core-match set M(w) subseteq {o,a,b} by

    o in M(w) iff bwa is tight,
    a in M(w) iff owb is tight,
    b in M(w) iff awo is tight.

If c and d shared a coordinate, say u, then the core vertex u and the two exterior vertices would give three parallel tight turns with the same ordered endpoints. The human parallel-source lemma S9022 would then give a Hamilton P5 on all five vertices, contrary to assumption. Hence M(c) and M(d) are disjoint.

Up to cyclic permutation of o,a,b and exchange of c,d, every ordered pair of disjoint subsets of a three-cycle is one of

    (empty,empty),
    (empty,{o}),
    (empty,{o,a}),
    (empty,{o,a,b}),
    ({o},{a}),
    ({o},{a,b}).

The definition of M, together with boundary antisymmetry, fixes all three c-core and all three d-core comparison turns in each row. The remaining checks are as follows.

| M(c), M(d) | extra branch | successive forced turns | contradiction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| empty, empty | none | bcoda=>doc; bdoca=>aco | oca and aco |
| empty, {o} | none | bcoda=>doc; bdoca=>aco | oca and aco |
| empty, {o,a} | none | bcoda=>doc; docab=>bac; odbac=>abd; odacb=>cad; bocad=>cob; cobda=>dbo; cdboa=>bdc; oabdc=>bao | oab and bao |
| empty, {o,a,b} | abc | abcod=>doc; badoc=>dab; dabco=>ocb | ocb and bco |
| empty, {o,a,b} | cba | cbado=>dab; cdabo=>adc; boadc=>dao; cbdao=>dbc; dbcoa=>aoc; bdaoc=>oad | oad and dao |
| {o}, {a} | ocd | bcoda=>doc; docab=>bac; odbac=>abd; oabdc=>cdb; aocdb=>coa; coabd=>bao | oab and bao |
| {o}, {a} | dco | ocadb=>dac; bodac=>dob; dobca=>cbo; dcboa=>bcd; cboad=>dao; bcdao=>adc; badco=>dab; daboc=>cob; adcob=>cda; bcdao=>oad | oad and dao |
| {o}, {a,b} | cod | codba=>abd; abcod=>cba; cbado=>dab; daboc=>cob; cdabo=>adc; adcob=>ocd; oabdc=>cdb; aocdb=>coa; coabd=>bao | oab and bao |
| {o}, {a,b} | doc | docab=>bac; odbac=>abd; badoc=>dab; daboc=>cob; cdabo=>adc; adcob=>ocd; oabdc=>cdb; aocdb=>coa; coabd=>bao | oab and bao |

In the two rows with M(c)=empty and M(d)={o,a,b}, abc/cba is a complementary pair. In the M(c)={o}, M(d)={a} rows, ocd/dco is complementary. In the last two rows, cod/doc is complementary. Hence the table is exhaustive. Every row contradicts boundary antisymmetry, so a shortest comparison cycle cannot be an ordinary triangle.

All possible shortest directed comparison cycles have now been excluded. Therefore Gamma(H) is acyclic. By R887 any topological ordering of Gamma(H) is a total order on E(K_5) realizing every tight turn as an increasing comparison of consecutive ordinary edges. This proves the main assertion.

Finally, suppose H is a larger boundary tournament with pc(H)>2 and Q is a proper tight path whose complement X has five vertices. If H[X] had a Hamilton P5, that path together with Q would be a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Hence H[X] is nonHamiltonian, so the theorem gives an edge order on X. QED.
