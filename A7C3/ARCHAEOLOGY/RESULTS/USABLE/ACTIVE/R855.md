# R855 — c_X=2 shape A restores directly, with only order-eleven inward-P4 seams

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R862 sigma_G(T)=0 five-cell frame with m>=6 and suppose the R866 forest is c_X=2 shape A: one T rail has quotient type X-G_i-G_j-X with i<j and the remaining Q cell G_k is the other rail. Apply R867. Outside its explicit Reverse-Ear outputs, the two Q blocks are literal and forward. If m>=7, every physical choice {i,j}={0,1},{0,2},{1,2} restores immediately to a spanning two-cover of H. For {0,1}, replace the selected G_0-G_1 transition by the original Q segment through q_2 and extend the isolated G_2 leftward by q_{m-2}. For {1,2}, restore q_{m-2} between G_1,G_2 and extend isolated G_0 rightward by q_2. For {0,2}, leave the X-G_0-G_2-X rail untouched and extend isolated G_1 on both sides to Q[2,m-2].

At m=6, the nonadjacent {0,2} placement still closes exactly. In the {0,1} placement, the same restoration has only one possibly uncertified turn, (q_2,q_3,x), where x is the first vertex of the terminal X block after singleton G_1={q_3}; if it is tight H is two-covered, while if bad R3 gives (x,q_3,q_2), which has head polarity opposite the R849 tail-signed dimer (q_3,q_2), hence R523 yields a labelled Hamilton P4 after choosing a distinct witness from {b,z}. Dually, in the {1,2} placement the sole possible seam is (x,q_3,q_4), where x is the last vertex of the initial X block before G_1; if tight H is two-covered, while if bad R3 gives (q_4,q_3,x), opposite the R849 head-signed dimer (q_4,q_3), and R523 yields a labelled Hamilton P4 using a distinct witness from {a,c}. Therefore shape A has no quiet monotone residue: for m>=7 it is impossible in a counterexample, and at m=6 it either closes or produces an explicit source-labelled inward P4.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in accepted crossed R862 sigma=0 frame; m>=6; R866 shape A; accepted R867,R849,R3,R523.

## Scope
Noncircular R24 restoration. c_X=2 shape A only. Pure literal Q restoration plus the order-eleven R849 seam check; no R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,generic R358, or R436.

## Applicability
After R866/R867 identifies shape A, restore the deleted anchors directly rather than analyzing its Q-X transitions. For m>=7 discard the shape by an explicit spanning two-cover. At m=6 retain only the displayed inward-P4 seam branch if the restoration seam is bad.

## Persistence
Retain the physical Q-pair {G_i,G_j}, the actual orders of the two endpoint X blocks, the explicit restored two-cover proposal, and at m=6 the unique failed seam and its R849/R523 P4 certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not consume Reverse-Ear outputs. At m=6 does not claim the resulting labelled P4 alone closes H. Does not address shapes B,C,D or sigma>0.

## Proof
Let G_0=(q_0,q_1), G_1=(q_3,...,q_{m-3}), G_2=(q_{m-1},q_m), v=q_2 and d=q_{m-2}. By R867, outside explicit Reverse-Ear geometry the shape-A rail is literally E_L-G_i-G_j-E_R with i<j, where E_L,E_R are the two X dimers, and G_k is the isolated second rail.

If {i,j}={0,1}, replace the selected transition from G_0 to G_1 by the old Q segment G_0,v,G_1. Keep both endpoint-X splices inherited from T. Make the second rail d,G_2, which is the old Q interval Q[m-2,m]. For m>=7, G_1 has at least two vertices, so inserting v at its left boundary does not alter the inherited G_1-E_R terminal splice; every turn of both restored rails is certified. They span H, contradiction.

If {i,j}={1,2}, use the exact dual: restore d between G_1 and G_2 on the mixed rail and take G_0,v as the second rail. For m>=7 the inherited E_L-G_1 splice is unaffected, and the two restored rails span H.

If {i,j}={0,2}, do not alter the mixed rail E_L-G_0-G_2-E_R at all. Replace the isolated rail G_1 by v,G_1,d=Q[2,m-2]. This is tight for every m>=6 and gives a spanning two-cover immediately.

Now m=6. Then G_1=(q_3), v=q_2 and d=q_4. The nonadjacent case is still immediate because (q_2,q_3,q_4) is an old Q trimer. In the {0,1} case, the restored first rail has the form E_L,G_0,q_2,q_3,E_R. All its turns are certified except possibly (q_2,q_3,x), where x is the first vertex of E_R. If that turn is tight, the displayed two-cover closes H. If it is bad, R3 gives (x,q_3,q_2) tight. R849 says D_L=(q_3,q_2) is tail-signed by both b,z. Choose r in {b,z} with r!=x. Then (x,q_3,q_2) is a head certificate and (q_3,q_2,r) a tail certificate on the same tested dimer with distinct witnesses, so R523 gives the tight P4 (x,q_3,q_2,r).

In the {1,2} case the restored mixed rail has the form E_L,q_3,q_4,G_2,E_R. The only possible new turn is (x,q_3,q_4), with x the last vertex of E_L. If tight, closure follows. If bad, R3 gives (q_4,q_3,x) tight. R849 says D_R=(q_4,q_3) is head-signed by both a,c. Choose r in {a,c} distinct from x. The new turn is a tail certificate opposite (r,q_4,q_3), and R523 gives the tight P4 (r,q_4,q_3,x). This exhausts shape A.
