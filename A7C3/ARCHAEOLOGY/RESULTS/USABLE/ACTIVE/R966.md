# R966 — Two-ended replacement by one exterior vertex forces Reverse-Ear geometry or a Hamilton extension

## Statement
Let Q=(q_0,q_1,...,q_r) be a vertex-simple Hamilton tight path on a support X with r>=2, and let y be a vertex outside X. Assume X union {y} is non-Hamiltonian. Suppose there is a Hamilton tight path L on (X-{q_0}) union {y} and a Hamilton tight path R on (X-{q_r}) union {y}.

Then at least one of the three path comparisons (Q,L), (Q,R), or (L,R) has an explicit R435 Reverse-Ear output: a reversed old state, a tight reverse trimer at a seam, or a vertex-simple proper tight cycle. Equivalently, outside explicit R435 geometry one exterior vertex cannot simultaneously Hamilton-replace both endpoints of one Hamilton path while the full one-vertex extension remains non-Hamiltonian.

More precisely, if (Q,L) is R435-quiet then L is exactly one of
  (y,q_1,q_2,...,q_r),
  (q_1,y,q_2,...,q_r).
If (Q,R) is R435-quiet then R is exactly one of
  (q_0,...,q_{r-1},y),
  (q_0,...,q_{r-2},y,q_{r-1}).
If (L,R) is also R435-quiet, the common-vertex orders can agree only in the boundary-overlap cases r=2 or r=3, and in each such case the two certified path segments concatenate to a Hamilton path of X union {y}, contradiction.

## Hypotheses
Finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament; Q is a tight path of order at least three; y is exterior; X+y is non-Hamiltonian; both endpoint-replacement supports admit actual Hamilton tight paths L and R.

## Scope
Pure all-order path-interaction theorem. No smallest-counterexample, cover, uniformity, critical-block, payment, or global-longest hypothesis.

## Applicability
Critical-block endpoint-exchange capacity. In Arm A it proves the working claim that one y cannot realize both left- and right-end exchange in an R435-quiet cell. In Arm B uniformity supplies both endpoint-replacement supports for every y outside a Hamilton k-rail, so every such y forces explicit R435 geometry unless the forbidden k+1 extension already exists.

## Persistence
Retain Q with its literal orientation, y, actual replacement paths L and R, and whichever exact R435 comparison fires. If all comparisons are provisionally quiet, retain the forced near-end insertion forms and the literal Hamilton-extension splice.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not consume the resulting R435 output. Does not say which of the three comparisons fires. Does not imply a spanning two-cover without an additional consumer. Does not reverse any input path.

## Proof
Let Q=(q_0,q_1,...,q_r), r>=2, be a tight Hamilton path on X, let y be outside X, and assume X union {y} is non-Hamiltonian. Let L be Hamilton on (X-{q_0}) union {y} and R Hamilton on (X-{q_r}) union {y}. Suppose, toward contradiction, that all three comparisons (Q,L), (Q,R), and (L,R) are R435-quiet.\n\nFirst compare Q with L. The Q-contacts appearing in L are exactly q_1,...,q_r. By the concluding monotonicity clause of R435, because the comparison is quiet these contacts occur in increasing Q-order. Hence L is obtained by inserting y into the literal sequence (q_1,...,q_r). If y were not in one of the first two positions of L, then L would begin (q_1,q_2). Since (q_0,q_1,q_2) is tight on Q, prepending q_0 to L would give a tight Hamilton path on X union {y}, contradiction. Therefore\n  L=(y,q_1,q_2,...,q_r)\nor\n  L=(q_1,y,q_2,...,q_r).                                             (1)\n\nDually compare Q with R. Quietness forces the Q-contacts q_0,...,q_{r-1} to occur in increasing Q-order, so R is obtained by inserting y into (q_0,...,q_{r-1}). If y were not in one of the last two positions, R would end (q_{r-2},q_{r-1}); appending q_r using the tight Q-turn (q_{r-2},q_{r-1},q_r) would Hamiltonize X union {y}. Thus\n  R=(q_0,...,q_{r-1},y)\nor\n  R=(q_0,...,q_{r-2},y,q_{r-1}).                                     (2)\n\nNow compare L with R. Their common vertices are y,q_1,...,q_{r-1}. Write L_1=(y,q_1,...,q_r), L_2=(q_1,y,q_2,...,q_r), R_1=(q_0,...,q_{r-1},y), and R_2=(q_0,...,q_{r-2},y,q_{r-1}).\n\nFor (L_1,R_1), the common order has y before q_1 in L_1 but q_1 before y in R_1, so R435 fires. For (L_1,R_2), the same reversal occurs whenever r>=3; the only quiet possibility is r=2. For (L_2,R_1), y precedes q_2 in L_2 but follows q_2 in R_1 whenever r>=3; the only quiet possibility is r=2. For (L_2,R_2), the same reversal with q_2 occurs whenever r>=4; for r=2 the common pair q_1,y is reversed, so R435 fires; the only quiet possibility is r=3. Thus all three comparisons can be quiet only in the following three boundary-overlap cases.\n\nIf r=2, L=L_1=(y,q_1,q_2) and R=R_2=(q_0,y,q_1). The consecutive tight turns from R and L concatenate to the Hamilton path (q_0,y,q_1,q_2) on X union {y}, contradiction.\n\nIf r=2, L=L_2=(q_1,y,q_2) and R=R_1=(q_0,q_1,y). They concatenate to (q_0,q_1,y,q_2), again Hamilton on X union {y}.\n\nIf r=3, L=L_2=(q_1,y,q_2,q_3) and R=R_2=(q_0,q_1,y,q_2). Their overlapping certified turns concatenate to the Hamilton path (q_0,q_1,y,q_2,q_3).\n\nEvery quiet possibility therefore contradicts non-Hamiltonicity of X union {y}. Hence at least one of (Q,L), (Q,R), or (L,R) has an explicit R435 output. No reversal of an input path is used.
