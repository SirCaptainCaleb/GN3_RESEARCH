# R700 — Two-probe trimer amplification gives a P4 or dual reverse-boundary R542 packets

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample, let K=(a,b,c) be any tight trimer, and let y_1,y_2 be two distinct vertices outside V(K). Then at least one of the following holds. (P4) For some i in {1,2}, the four-set V(K) union {y_i} supports a tight Hamilton P4. (DUAL-CP) Both reverse boundary dimers of K are simultaneously two-witness R542-ready packets: the reverse initial dimer D_L=(b,a) is tail-signed by both y_1,y_2, and the reverse terminal dimer D_R=(c,b) is head-signed by both y_1,y_2. Consequently accepted R542 applies independently to D_L with carrier K and complementary singleton c, and to D_R with carrier K and complementary singleton a. The two packet facts coexist graph-intrinsically, although their capture/payment descendants are alternative continuations. Indeed, apply accepted R518 to K with fourth vertex y_i for i=1,2. If either application yields its P4 branch, stop. Otherwise the no-P4 branch of R518 gives, for each i, both reverse contacts (b,a,y_i) and (y_i,c,b). The first pair of turns is exactly same-tail two-witness polarity on tested D_L=(b,a), while the second is same-head two-witness polarity on tested D_R=(c,b); both witnesses lie outside K, so R542 applies at both boundaries. In particular, a two-witness short-carrier SANDWICH output from pending R696 never has to be paid on its old inward-Q support: regarding the witness sandwich itself as K and choosing any two distinguished outside probes converts it either to a labelled P4 or to two new short-carrier packets supported on the cross dimers joining the sandwich middle to its two exterior witnesses.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K=(a,b,c) is tight; y_1,y_2 are distinct and outside K. Use accepted R518 and R542.

## Scope
Order-free local compiler. Pure four-cell amplification plus short-carrier typing. No crossed-frame, deletion-root, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,generic R358, R436, or R24 input.

## Applicability
Whenever a tight trimer is the current geometric residue and at least two named outside vertices matter, probe the trimer with both before invoking generic payment. A P4 is immediate progress. If neither probe gives a P4, retain both reverse boundary dimers as simultaneous two-witness R542 packets with the SAME named probe pair. For an R696 witness sandwich at q_1, choose one X vertex and the opposite Q endpoint R; this moves the packet from the inward-Q dimer to X-q_1 cross supports while retaining a nonlocal endpoint witness. Use the exact dual at q_{m-1} with L.

## Persistence
Retain K, the two probe identities y_1,y_2, both R518 four-cell tests, and in the no-P4 branch all four reverse-contact turns. Retain both tested reverse boundary dimers, their common two-witness set, carrier K, and complementary singleton identities. Later R542 descendants are alternative and need not coexist.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert either R542 descendant closes H or that the two paid descendants coexist. Does not claim repeated application is a globally monotone process. Does not by itself consume the END/FREE branches of pending R696. Does not assert a P4 is spanning.

## Proof
Fix K=(a,b,c) and y_i outside K. Accepted R518 says that either K union {y_i} has a Hamilton P4, or its no-P4 branch forces the exact reverse contacts (b,a,y_i) and (y_i,c,b). If the P4 branch occurs for i=1 or 2, outcome P4 holds. Otherwise, for i=1,2 both displayed contacts hold. Thus on the fixed tested dimer D_L=(b,a), the turns (b,a,y_1) and (b,a,y_2) are two distinct tail certificates, so D_L is same-tail two-witness signed. Since K begins with boundary state (a,b), D_L=(b,a) is exactly its reverse initial boundary dimer; y_1,y_2 are outside K. Accepted R542 applies, with complementary carrier singleton c. Likewise on D_R=(c,b), the turns (y_1,c,b) and (y_2,c,b) are two distinct head certificates. Because K ends in boundary state (b,c), D_R is exactly the reverse terminal boundary dimer of K; R542 applies with complementary singleton a. All four turns are graph-intrinsic facts of the same H, so the two packet certificates coexist even though R542 continuations are alternative. This proves the corollary.
