# R917 — BI-ANCHORS saturate across the opposite middle gate unless the inward five-set is Hamiltonian

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R914 BI-ANCHOR branch with X={a,b,c,z}, matching heights {ab,cz}>{ac,bz}>{bc,az}, v=q_2 and d=q_{m-2}. Then independently at the two inward anchors: (LEFT) either H[X union {v}] has a tight Hamilton P5, or both cross-depth turns (b,z,v) and (z,b,v) are tight; equivalently v tail-signs both tested orientations of G_4={b,z}, while R914 already head-signs both orientations of G_3={a,c} by v. (RIGHT) either H[X union {d}] has a tight Hamilton P5, or both cross-depth turns (d,a,c) and (d,c,a) are tight; equivalently d head-signs both tested orientations of G_3, while R914 already tail-signs both orientations of G_4 by d. Consequently, if neither inward five-set X+v nor X+d is Hamiltonian, the full symmetric inward sign matrix holds simultaneously: v and d head-sign both orientations of G_3 and tail-sign both orientations of G_4. The proof is finite and computer-free once accepted R902 supplies edge-orderability of a nonHamilton five-set.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest boundary-tournament counterexample in the accepted crossed R914 BI-ANCHOR branch. X is the accepted R594 transitive matching-height four-cell. Uses accepted R902: every nonHamilton five-vertex boundary tournament is edge-orderable.

## Scope
Crossed long phase, BI-ANCHOR consumer. Accepted-input, computer-free proof. No R905/R906, capture, payment, five-cell taxonomy, Reverse Ear, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533, generic R358, or computation.

## Applicability
After R914 enters BI-ANCHORS, test the two induced five-sets X+v and X+d. A nonHamilton side immediately supplies both missing opposite-gate cross-depth sign bits. If both are nonHamilton, retain the full 2-by-2 inward sign matrix. If a side is Hamiltonian, retain its literal Hamilton P5 as a role-sensitive absorber candidate rather than converting it to generic P5 currency.

## Persistence
Retain X with its matching-height labels, v,d, the four R914 anchor turns, for each nonHamilton five-set the R902 edge order and the exact two-case Hamilton-path contradiction, and the resulting cross-depth sign turns. In a Hamilton branch retain an explicit Hamilton P5 order when later chosen. All sign conclusions are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert X+v or X+d is nonHamiltonian, does not assert the two Hamilton/nonHamilton branches synchronize any exact deletion covers, and does not close H. A Hamilton P5 on X+v or X+d is only an absorber support until a cover-valued consumer is supplied. The full sign matrix is not claimed by itself to force a Hamilton P6 or a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
LEFT. Assume H[X union {v}] is nonHamiltonian. By accepted R902 it is edge-orderable; fix a total edge order < realizing tight turns. The matching-height orientation of X gives the incident-edge inequalities ab<ac<az, ab<bz<bc, cz<ac<bc, and cz<bz<az. The R914 anchors (v,a,c) and (v,c,a) give av<ac and cv<ac. We prove bz<bv. Suppose instead bv<bz. Compare bv and cv. If bv<cv, then the ordinary-edge sequence of the vertex order (b,v,c,a,z) is bv<cv<ac<az, so (b,v,c,a,z) is a tight Hamilton P5 by the edge-order dictionary. If cv<bv, then (c,v,b,z,a) has edge sequence cv<bv<bz<az and is a tight Hamilton P5. Both contradict nonHamiltonicity. Hence bz<bv, which is exactly the tight turn (z,b,v). Next prove bz<vz. Suppose instead vz<bz. Compare vz and av. If vz<av, then (z,v,a,c,b) has vz<av<ac<bc; if av<vz, then (a,v,z,b,c) has av<vz<bz<bc. Either is a tight Hamilton P5, contradiction. Therefore bz<vz, exactly (b,z,v) tight. Thus v tail-signs both orientations of G_4.

RIGHT. Assume H[X union {d}] is nonHamiltonian and fix its R902 edge order. R914 gives (b,z,d) and (z,b,d), hence bz<zd and bz<bd. We prove ad<ac, i.e. (d,a,c) tight. Suppose ac<ad. Compare bd and ad. If bd<ad, then (c,z,b,d,a) has cz<bz<bd<ad; if ad<bd, then (z,c,a,d,b) has cz<ac<ad<bd. Either is a Hamilton P5, contradiction. Hence ad<ac. Similarly prove cd<ac. Suppose ac<cd. Compare zd and cd. If zd<cd, then (a,b,z,d,c) has ab<bz<zd<cd; if cd<zd, then (b,a,c,d,z) has ab<ac<cd<zd. Again either is Hamilton, contradiction. Thus cd<ac, exactly (d,c,a) tight. Therefore d head-signs both orientations of G_3. The two side arguments use only graph-intrinsic turns in the common R914 frame, so when both five-sets are nonHamiltonian all eight inward gate-sign turns coexist. This proves the theorem.
