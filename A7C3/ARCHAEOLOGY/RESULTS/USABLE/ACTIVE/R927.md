# R927 — Global absence of universal source crossings saturates one middle layer and forces the odd uniform residue

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest counterexample to the boundary-tournament two-path-cover conjecture. Call a literal exact singleton-deletion source C_x=A|B of H-x universally crossed at z in A if every literal exact two-nonempty-path cover of H-z selects an adjacency with one endpoint in A-{z} and the other in B; define the dual notion for z in B.

Then at least one of the following holds.

(U) There exist a physical source cover C_x=A|B and a vertex z on one source rail which is universally crossed relative to that source.

(M) There is an integer k>=2 with |V(H)|=2k+1 such that every k-subset of V(H) supports a Hamilton tight path and no (k+1)-subset supports one.

More strongly, if (U) fails globally, fix ANY exact source C_x=A|B and write |A|=k, |B|=l. Then for every deletion label y and every partition V(H)-{y}=S disjoint-union T with |S|=k and |T|=l, there is a literal exact H-y two-cover whose rail supports are exactly S|T. Hence every k-set and every l-set of H is Hamiltonian. The no-two-cover condition then forces k=l, giving (M).

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample, so accepted R4 supplies pc(H)=3 and pc(H[W])<=2 for every nonempty proper W. Tight paths are vertex-simple and contiguous subpaths are tight; singletons and dimers are vacuously tight.

## Scope
Global O4 parent reduction. The proof uses smallest-counterexample minimality plus literal singleton-deletion cover manipulation. It does not use R24, payment, capture, compatibility graphs, rail-incidence roots, extremal cover families, Reverse Ear, or boundary antisymmetry beyond the ambient tight-path conventions.

## Applicability
Use before splitting into the two current D17 research arms. If a universal source crossing is found, enter Universal Source-Crossing Absorption. If none exists anywhere, the theorem immediately gives the odd uniform middle-layer residue. The stronger partition-saturation conclusion may be reused inside the uniform arm whenever actual k|k support partitions are needed.

## Persistence
In branch U retain the exact source C_x=A|B, offending vertex z, and the universal quantifier over actual exact H-z covers. In the quiet proof retain each literal singleton source used in the three-substitution exchange; no Hamilton order is transported between distinct covers. In branch M retain only support-level Hamiltonicity of every k-set and non-Hamiltonicity of every (k+1)-set; no canonical Hamilton order is asserted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not absorb the universal-crossing branch and does not prove the uniform middle-layer residue impossible. Does not synchronize Hamilton orders across different supports or deletion fibers. Does not infer Hamiltonicity of subsets smaller than k by arbitrary restriction. Does not use or imply a monotone compatibility potential.

## Proof
Let H be a smallest counterexample. By R4, pc(H)=3 and every proper induced subsystem has path-cover number at most two.

1. Exact singleton sources and the rail floor. Since any set of at most four vertices is covered by two vacuous paths of order at most two, |V(H)|>=5. For each x, H-x has path-cover number at most two. If it is Hamiltonian, split a Hamilton order into two contiguous subpaths of orders at least two; if its cover number is two, take a minimum two-cover. Thus exact two-nonempty-path singleton sources exist. Moreover no exact H-x two-cover can contain a singleton rail (y): the other rail together with the vacuous dimer (x,y) would two-cover H. Hence every source rail has at least two vertices.

2. Quiet deleted-label substitution. Fix ANY exact source
  C_x=A|B
of H-x and z in A. Put S=A-{z}; S and B are nonempty. Suppose z is not universally crossed relative to this source. Choose an exact two-cover T of H-z having no selected adjacency between S and B.

Let R be the T-rail containing x. If all vertices of R other than x lie in only one of the classes S,B, then the other T-rail cannot mix S and B either. Since both classes are nonempty and the two rails cover S union B union {x}, the rail R must contain all of one class together with x and the other rail all of the opposite class. Hence either S union {x} or B union {x} is Hamiltonian.

Otherwise R-x meets both S and B. Because no selected S-B adjacency exists, x is internal on R and separates one contiguous S-side from one contiguous B-side. The other T-rail is monochromatic. If it is an S-rail, then the B-side of R contains all of B and that side together with x is a Hamilton path on B union {x}. If the other rail is a B-rail, then the S-side of R contains all of S and together with x is a Hamilton path on S union {x}. Again one of the two augmented supports S+x or B+x is Hamiltonian.

The B+x possibility is impossible: the original source rail A is Hamiltonian, disjoint from B+x, and A union (B+x)=V(H), so those two paths would two-cover H. Therefore S+x is Hamiltonian. Pair an actual Hamilton path on S+x with the untouched original B-rail. This gives a literal exact H-z cover with support partition
  (A-{z}+{x}) | B.
The dual statement holds for z in B. No Hamilton order has been transported from one fiber to another; only a newly certified support path and the untouched opposite source rail are used.

3. Three quiet substitutions perform one support exchange. Assume now that alternative (U) fails globally, meaning no vertex is universally crossed relative to any exact singleton source. Fix an exact source C_x=A|B and choose a in A, b in B. Apply the quiet substitution three times.

First, from source C_x and deleted vertex a obtain the exact H-a cover
  (A-{a}+{x}) | B.
Second, regard that literal cover as the source at a and delete b. Since b lies on its B-rail, obtain the exact H-b cover
  (A-{a}+{x}) | (B-{b}+{a}).
Third, regard this cover as the source at b and delete x, which lies on its first rail. Obtain the exact H-x cover
  (A-{a}+{b}) | (B-{b}+{a}).
Thus any one-for-one exchange a<->b between the two support classes of a fixed H-x source is realizable by actual singleton covers. The intermediate covers are alternatives, used sequentially; no simultaneous currentness is asserted.

4. Saturation of one fiber. Write |A|=k and |B|=l. The graph of k-subsets of V(H)-{x}, joined when they differ by one exchange, is connected. Repeatedly applying the preceding three-substitution move therefore shows: for EVERY k-subset S of V(H)-{x}, there is an exact H-x two-cover with supports
  S | ((V(H)-{x})-S).
Hence every k|l support partition of H-x is realized by actual Hamilton rails.

5. The same side sizes occur at every deletion label. Let y be any vertex. If y=x use the original source. If y lies in A, one quiet substitution from C_x gives the exact H-y source
  (A-{y}+{x}) | B,
whose rail sizes are k,l. If y lies in B, use the dual source
  A | (B-{y}+{x}).
Starting from this size-k|l source at y and repeating the exchange argument of step 4 saturates EVERY k|l partition of H-y.

Consequently every k-subset of V(H) is Hamiltonian: given such a set S, choose y outside S and realize S as the k-rail of an H-y cover. Likewise every l-subset is Hamiltonian.

6. The side sizes must be equal. We have
  |V(H)|=k+l+1.
Suppose k<l. Choose any Hamilton l-set and one Hamilton order on it. Because l>=k+1, a contiguous subpath of that order on exactly k+1 vertices is tight. Its complement in H has
  k+l+1-(k+1)=l
vertices, hence is Hamiltonian by the preceding saturation. These two disjoint tight paths span H, contradiction. The case l<k is dual. Therefore k=l, so |V(H)|=2k+1.

Every k-set is Hamiltonian. No (k+1)-set can be Hamiltonian, because its k-vertex complement is Hamiltonian and the two Hamilton paths would span H. This is alternative (M).

Thus if (U) does not occur, (M) must occur, proving the dichotomy and the stronger fiberwise partition-saturation statement.
