# R423 — Fixed-Turn Singleton-Checkpoint Reentry

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Fix an arbitrarily preselected retained proper tight turn J=(a,b,c). From any ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor (p),(t), there is a finite chosen continuation which, unless a spanning two-cover or an accepted explicit historical pair/contact exit occurs first, steers the active floor to (a),(c). In the exact aligned pair-deletion frame H-{a,c}=U sqcup V, selected predecessor/successor incidence at b yields an endpoint-anchored signed dimer at a or c; each quiet aligned reentry captures a previously uncaptured endpoint of J. Hence at most two aligned reentries are quiet, and every later aligned reentry yields a spanning two-cover or an explicit historical signed-support contact / cross-generation pair interaction.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; J=(a,b,c) is any preselected proper tight turn retained as a graph-intrinsic lineage coordinate; an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor exists. The theorem uses Complete Floor Pair Steering, Pair-Deletion Rigidity, the audited selected-incidence endpoint-anchor extraction, actualization, and the Fixed-Turn Common-Frame Endpoint Budget.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order. The quantifiers are turn-first and floor-independent: J may be fixed before the floor is chosen/produced, and the incoming ancestry-bearing floor need not already use a or c. This is the historically audited fixed-turn reentry composition.

## Applicability
Use when a desired physical pair {a,c} is the outer pair of a chosen proper turn J and an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor is already available. Along a chosen nonclosing/non-exit continuation, the floor can be aligned to {a,c} without requiring compatibility between its original singleton coordinates and J.

## Persistence
The physical turn J remains graph-intrinsic and fixed. Complete Floor Pair Steering retains the full physical certificate ledger of the incoming ancestry-bearing floor through singleton replacements, including earlier pair-mass/refund/contact certificates, while the current floor coordinates change. The theorem does not require the original floor representative, original witnesses, or old selected paths to remain current. At the aligned checkpoint the exact pair-deletion frame and subsequent endpoint-capture/contact history belong to the chosen J-lineage.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert arbitrary same-representative synchronization; does not promise preservation of fixed witness names during steering; does not say every explicit pair/contact exit closes; does not make Psi_J a global rank across turn switches; does not assert that an arbitrary merely historical pair that is no longer an active retained pair can be attached to J. The exact historical sources explicitly prove the turn-first theorem at the ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. For a higher-mass pair predating the choice of J, the sources separately prove birth-frame-agnostic retained-turn taxation for later retained pairs, but this proposal does not strengthen the chronology beyond the explicit floor-level theorem.

## Proof
Fix the arbitrary preselected proper turn J=(a,b,c) and any ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. Apply R432 with target {a,c}. Unless a spanning two-cover occurs, this reaches an aligned floor on {a,c}. Now invoke corrected R434 at every aligned common-frame episode. Its selected-incidence extraction either gives an explicit contact/growth output or, for a fresh endpoint e in {a,c}, starts an R42-protected descendant and returns quietly to another aligned floor only after e has become a retained signed singleton coordinate. Thus a completed quiet episode strictly enlarges the completed-endpoint set A_J subseteq {a,c}. Reuse of an endpoint already in A_J is nonquiet by the explicit anchor-contact/strict-growth clause of R434. Hence at most two aligned episodes are quiet. This proves the historical Fixed-Turn Singleton-Checkpoint Reentry conclusion without using the compressed R172 lex-clock proof as a dependency; R432 supplies arbitrary floor-to-target alignment and R434 supplies the exact finite endpoint budget.
