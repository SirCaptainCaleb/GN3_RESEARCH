# R684 — The R682 cross-end alternative is an entire exact deletion rail

## Statement
Retain the XET branch of pending R682 for an oriented crossed middle-gate dimer S and exterior witness v. Let T be the exact two-cover of H-V(S) used there. Then, after orienting one component, T has the literal form
T = (L,v,R) sqcup P  or  T=(R,v,L) sqcup P,
where the displayed cross-end trimer is an entire T rail and P is a tight Hamilton path on V(H)\(V(S) union {L,v,R}).

Indeed XET arises precisely because no selected carrier/exterior crossing from singleton L in the R649 carrier K_L avoids v, and no such crossing from singleton R in K_R avoids v. The selected states {L,v} and {R,v} lie on one T rail. If L had any second rail-neighbor p, then p lies outside K_L (T omits S and p is distinct from L,v), so {L,p} would be a selected L-exterior crossing avoiding v, contradiction. Hence L is a rail endpoint. The identical argument makes R a rail endpoint. A path rail containing consecutive L-v-R with both L and R endpoints has exactly those three vertices. The other rail therefore spans every remaining vertex of H-V(S).

## Hypotheses
Pending R682 XET branch, with its exact H-V(S) cover T and the two R649 singleton-carrier crossing conditions.

## Scope
Source-frame sharpening of R682. No additional minimality, payment, R358, R436, or small-deletion theorem.

## Applicability
Whenever R682 falls into XET, replace the phrase “cross-end tight trimer” by the stronger exact-cover normal form: the cross-end trimer is one entire rail of H-S and the other rail is Hamiltonian on its complement. Consume this exact two-rail representative before invoking generic trimer machinery.

## Persistence
Retain S,v,T, the orientation L-v-R or R-v-L, the fact that L and R are T-rail endpoints, and the complementary Hamilton rail P.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not itself splice S back into T or produce a spanning two-cover of H. Depends on pending R682.

## Proof
In the XET proof of R682, the same exact cover T selects states on physical supports {L,v} and {R,v}, and no selected carrier/exterior crossing out of L or R avoids v. Both selected states lie on one T rail and give consecutive order L,v,R or R,v,L. Suppose L had a second neighbor p on that rail. Since T is a cover of H-V(S), p is not in S; and p is distinct from L and v. The R649 L-carrier K_L has vertex set V(S) union {L}, so p lies in V(H)\V(K_L). Therefore the selected adjacency {L,p} is a carrier/exterior crossing for K_L that avoids v, contradicting the defining XET assumption. Hence L has no second neighbor and is a rail endpoint. The same argument using K_R makes R a rail endpoint. A vertex-simple path rail containing both endpoints L,R with v as their common consecutive neighbor can contain no additional vertex, so it is exactly (L,v,R) or (R,v,L). Since T has exactly two rails and spans H-V(S), the other rail P contains every vertex outside V(S) union {L,v,R} and is tight. This proves the claim.
