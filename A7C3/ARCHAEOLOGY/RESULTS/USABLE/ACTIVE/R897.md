# R897 — A LOW2 fragmented marker exposes a five-vertex inward absorber or an inward-anchor P4

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R885 BOUNDED branch with f(T)=1 and the accepted R886 LOW2 regime. Choose a fragmented marker x with c_x=c(T[Y union {x}])<=2. Put v=q_2, d=q_{m-2}, D_x={v,d} union (X-{x}), and R_x=Y union {x}=V(H)-D_x. Choose any tight trimer C on X-{x}. Then the induced path forest T[R_x] covers R_x with exactly c_x tight path components. Applying accepted R522 to the common tight trimer C with the two probes v,d gives one of two source-visible alternatives. (P4) At least one of C union {v} or C union {d} supports a tight Hamilton P4. (ABSORBER) D_x=C union {v,d} supports a tight Hamilton P5. In the ABSORBER branch, if c_x=1 then T[R_x] is a Hamilton path and H is immediately the literal spanning two-cover D_x sqcup R_x, impossible in a counterexample. Hence every surviving ABSORBER branch has c_x=2 and yields the literal spanning three-cover H=P_x sqcup U sqcup V, where P_x is a Hamilton P5 on D_x and U,V are exactly the two components of T[R_x]. Therefore LOW2 reduces to an inward-anchor P4 or to one exact five-vertex-absorber three-cover; the only extra distinction needed before generic three-cover splice machinery is whether U or V is a singleton.

## Hypotheses
Accepted crossed R594/R862 frame; accepted R886 LOW2; accepted R522; x is a LOW2 fragmented marker with c_x<=2.

## Scope
Noncircular consumer translation for the LOW2 branch. Pure induced-path-forest bookkeeping plus the accepted two-probe P5 synchronization theorem. No payment, capture, R5, R168, R169, R224, R533, generic R358, R436, or computation.

## Applicability
Use immediately after R886 enters LOW2. The c_x=1 absorber branch closes. For c_x=2 retain the exact source-labeled P5 P_x on {q_2,q_{m-2}} union (X-{x}) together with the two literal residue rails U,V. If both U,V are nontrivial, the packet is exactly the kind of spanning three-cover targeted by the pending clean splice parent R879; if one is a singleton, isolate that singleton-residue face rather than returning to coarse fragmentation counting.

## Proof
Retain accepted R886 LOW2 and choose a fragmented marker x with c_x=c(T[Y union {x}])<=2. Let v=q_2 and d=q_{m-2}. Set D_x={v,d} union (X-{x}) and R_x=Y union {x}. Since W=Y union X and T is a two-path forest on W, deleting the three vertices X-{x} from the literal T-rails leaves the induced forest T[R_x]. By the definition of c_x in R886, this forest has exactly c_x connected path components. Each component is a contiguous subpath of a T-rail and hence is tight. It covers all of R_x.\n\nBy accepted R594, every three-subset X-{x} supports a tight trimer; choose one orientation C on those three vertices. Apply accepted R522 to the tight trimer C with the two distinct outside probes v and d. R522 says: if either C union {v} or C union {d} has a Hamilton P4, retain that P4; otherwise C union {v,d}=D_x has a Hamilton P5. This gives the stated P4/ABSORBER alternative.\n\nAssume the ABSORBER branch, and let P_x be a Hamilton P5 on D_x. If c_x=1, then T[R_x] consists of one Hamilton tight path on all of R_x. Since D_x and R_x partition V(H), P_x together with that Hamilton path is a literal spanning two-cover of H, contradicting the standing counterexample hypothesis. Therefore every surviving ABSORBER branch has c_x=2.\n\nWhen c_x=2, write U and V for the two components of T[R_x]. They are vertex-disjoint tight paths covering R_x. Thus H=P_x sqcup U sqcup V is a literal spanning three-cover with the exact source-labelled five-vertex component P_x on {q_2,q_{m-2}} union (X-{x}). No claim is made that U,V are both nontrivial; if they are, the packet has exactly the form targeted by the separately pending generic splice theorem R879, while a singleton component must be treated separately. This proves the stated reduction without using R879.
