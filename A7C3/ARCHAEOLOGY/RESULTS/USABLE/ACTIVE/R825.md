# R825 — Four cross-pair cut obligations have a middle-edge, complementary-pair, or three-star normal form

## Statement
Let T be a vertex-disjoint path cover (in particular, any exact two-cover) on a vertex set W containing four distinguished vertices X={a,b,c,z}. Consider the four cuts S_ab={a,b}, S_az={a,z}, S_cb={c,b}, S_cz={c,z}, each against W minus that two-set. Assume that for each of these four cuts T selects at least one adjacent state crossing the cut. Partition the six physical X-edges into M_R={ab,cz}, M_S={ac,bz}, M_L={az,bc}. Then T contains one of the following seven source-visible configurations. (MS) A selected M_S state ac or bz. (AC-EXT) selected X-exterior states incident with a and c. (BZ-EXT) selected X-exterior states incident with b and z. (RL) one selected M_R state and one selected M_L state. (STAR-ab) selected X-exterior states incident with a and b, together with a selected M_L state. (STAR-az) selected X-exterior states incident with a and z, together with a selected M_R state. (STAR-cb) selected X-exterior states incident with c and b, together with a selected M_R state. (STAR-cz) selected X-exterior states incident with c and z, together with a selected M_L state. Here an X-exterior state means a selected state with exactly one endpoint in X. Moreover, in (RL) the selected M_R and M_L states share exactly one X vertex, hence they are consecutive adjacencies of one T rail and form a selected three-vertex X segment. If T is a tight path cover and X is the transitive matching-height cell with M_R>M_S>M_L, that segment is oriented from its M_R edge through the common pivot to its M_L edge and is a tight X-trimer.

## Hypotheses
T is a vertex-disjoint path cover on W containing X={a,b,c,z}; each of the four displayed two-vertex cuts has at least one selected T-state crossing it. The final orientation sentence additionally assumes T is tight and X is the transitive matching-height cell M_R>M_S>M_L.

## Scope
Pure finite cut-incidence lemma. No smallest-counterexample minimality, Strong Level-(1) antisymmetry, R24, R508, payment, capture, Reverse Ear, or computation is needed for the seven-shape classification. It is designed to consume four simultaneous R508 obligations once they have been established elsewhere.

## Applicability
Use whenever four recompletion carriers on the same deleted support force crossings of the four cross-pair cuts ab,az,cb,cz in one fixed exact cover. First harvest an M_S selected state if present. Otherwise extract an inclusion-minimal family of selected states covering the four cut obligations; it is either one of three complementary pairs or one of four three-stars, with the physical translations listed in the statement.

## Persistence
Retain the fixed path cover T, the labelled X-set and matching partition M_R,M_S,M_L, the four cut obligations, the actual selected states chosen for an inclusion-minimal covering family, and its one of seven physical shape labels. In RL retain the common X pivot and the literal three-vertex rail segment.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert any of the seven shapes closes H. Does not assert the selected exterior states have distinct exterior endpoints. Does not assert a star is the entire incidence pattern of T, only that the displayed subconfiguration is selected. Does not infer capture, payment, protected contact, or a spanning path. The seven labels are alternatives for a retained inclusion-minimal obligation cover and need not be mutually exclusive in the full T.

## Proof
Number the cuts 1=S_ab, 2=S_az, 3=S_cb, 4=S_cz. A selected state with no X endpoint crosses none of them. A selected state with exactly one X endpoint has signature 12 at a, 13 at b, 34 at c, or 24 at z. For an X-X state, direct separation gives signature 23 for either M_R edge ab or cz, signature 14 for either M_L edge az or bc, and signature 1234 for either M_S edge ac or bz. Thus if a selected M_S state exists we are in (MS). Otherwise every selected state relevant to the four obligations has one of the six 2-subset signatures 12,13,14,23,24,34. Because all four obligations are met, choose an inclusion-minimal selected subfamily F whose signature union is {1,2,3,4}. If |F|=2, its two 2-subsets must be disjoint complements. The three complementary pairs are 12+34, 13+24, and 14+23, translating respectively to AC-EXT, BZ-EXT, and RL. Now suppose |F|>=3. Inclusion-minimality means every member of F contains a cut-index not contained in the union of the other members. Since there are only four indices and every signature has size two, |F| cannot exceed three: four members would each require a distinct private index, forcing four pairwise distinct private indices while each 2-subset also contains a second index belonging to another member, which would destroy privacy for at least one member; equivalently an inclusion-minimal edge cover of four labelled vertices by 2-edges has at most three edges. Hence |F|=3. Regard the three signatures as edges of K_4 on vertices {1,2,3,4}. They cover all four vertices and every edge has a private endpoint. A three-edge graph on four vertices with this property is necessarily K_1,3: a path P4 has a middle edge with no private endpoint, and any graph containing two disjoint edges already contains a complementary two-edge cover, contradicting minimality. The four possible centers give signature sets {12,13,14}, {12,24,23}, {13,34,23}, {24,34,14}, translating to STAR-ab, STAR-az, STAR-cb, STAR-cz respectively. Finally, in RL one selected edge belongs to M_R and one to M_L. Any edges from two distinct perfect matchings of K_4 meet in exactly one vertex. Since both are selected states of a path forest, at their common vertex they are the two consecutive rail adjacencies, giving a literal three-vertex X segment. If the rail is tight and X has matching heights M_R>M_S>M_L, the rail direction must traverse the M_R edge first and the M_L edge second, so the segment is a tight matching-height trimer. This proves the classification.
