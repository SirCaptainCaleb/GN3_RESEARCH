# R143 — Type-(2,2,3) private rotations force exterior-dimer sign interaction

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the accepted type-(2,2,3) motif L=X∪Y∪{p}, B=X∪Z, D=Y∪Z with exterior pair {e,f}. Suppose t is a private endpoint, meaning t∈Y is an endpoint of a tight ordering of D or symmetrically t∈X is an endpoint of a tight ordering of B. Then the physical exterior dimer {e,f} carries two signed certificates with distinct witnesses p and t, obtained from the original short trimer {p,e,f} and the endpoint-rotated short trimer {t,e,f}. Hence R38 forces a nontrivial same-support interaction on {e,f}: either the two certificates have the same polarity and enter the accepted two-extension collision package, or they have opposite polarities and yield the accepted strict two-ended signed-path interaction. Moreover, if two distinct private endpoints t1,t2 are available among the two long-root orderings, then {e,f} carries signed certificates with the three distinct witnesses p,t1,t2; therefore two of the three certificates have the same polarity, so a same-polarity two-extension collision on the exterior dimer {e,f} is unavoidable.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; minimum overlap two; accepted type-(2,2,3) normalization R130/R131; endpoint replacement rule R140; boundary antisymmetry and accepted sign-interaction machinery.

## Scope
Graph-intrinsic sign consequence of private endpoint rotation; no payment or simultaneous-currentness claim.

## Applicability
Use to split the type-(2,2,3) endpoint analysis. Two or more private endpoint occurrences immediately force an exterior-dimer same-polarity collision; a single private endpoint already forces a two-witness same-support interaction. The only collision-free endpoint regime can therefore have at most one private endpoint occurrence across the two long roots.

## Persistence
The physical dimer {e,f}, witness identities, and resulting R38 interaction certificates are graph-intrinsic. The two trimer orientations are separate certificates and need not be current in one representative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify the polarity of an individual certificate, does not assert simultaneous payment descendants, and does not by itself turn the R38 collision or strict interaction into a spanning 5+5 cover.

## Proof
Suppose first that t∈Y is an endpoint of D; the B/X case is symmetric. By R140, E=(D\\{t})∪{p} is a global-longest support. In the original exact root H−X, the short component has support P0={p,e,f}. In H−X after replacing D by E, the physical complement of E is P1={t,e,f}; by R3, for the prescribed middle e exactly one of (p,e,f),(f,e,p) is a tight trimer orientation on P0, and exactly one of (t,e,f),(f,e,t) is a tight trimer orientation on P1. By R41, each tight trimer supplies signed terminal dimers. In both cases the physical dimer {e,f} is terminal; its witness is the opposite outer vertex, namely p for P0 and t for P1. Thus the same physical support {e,f} has two signed certificates with distinct witnesses p and t. R38 applies: equal polarity gives the accepted two-extension collision package; opposite polarity gives same-support exclusivity and the strict two-ended signed path. If two distinct private endpoints t1,t2 are available, repeat the argument for each. The dimer {e,f} then has three certificates with witnesses p,t1,t2. There are only two polarities, so two certificates have the same polarity; their witnesses are distinct, and R38 forces a same-polarity two-extension collision.
