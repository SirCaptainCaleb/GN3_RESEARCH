# R168 — Small-Deletion Staircase Rail Floor

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Let D be a deletion set of size d in {1,2,3}, and let H-D=P sqcup Q be any exact two-cover. Then |P|,|Q| >= 5-d. Thus one-vertex deletion has rail floor 4, pair deletion has rail floor 3, and arbitrary three-vertex deletion has rail floor 2.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest counterexample; D subset V(H) with |D|=d in {1,2,3}; P sqcup Q is an exact two-cover of H-D.

## Scope
Smallest-counterexample small-deletion structure. Applies to arbitrary deletion sets of the stated sizes, not only tight-path deletion sets.

## Applicability
Parent rail-size theorem for deletion arguments. It strictly strengthens bare pair-deletion nontriviality and removes separate singleton-rail exclusions after one-, two-, and three-vertex deletion.

## Persistence
A size constraint on every exact two-cover of the fixed deletion. It is graph/deletion intrinsic at the quantified level; no specific current rail survives a representative change.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
No lower bound is asserted here for four-vertex deletion rails beyond exact two-cover status. No orientation, marker ancestry, selected state, endpoint role, or common-shadow information from specialized pair/trimer theorems is supplied.

## Proof
Suppose without loss of generality |P|<=4-d. Put E=D union V(P). Since d in {1,2,3}, E is nonempty and |E|<=4. But H-E=Q is Hamiltonian, so pc(H-E)=1. This contradicts R5, which gives pc(H-E)=2 for every nonempty deletion of at most four vertices. Therefore |P|>=5-d. The same argument gives |Q|>=5-d.
