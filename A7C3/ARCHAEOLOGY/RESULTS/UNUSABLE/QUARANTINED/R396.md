# R396 — Common-shadow three-cap closure-or-terminal-collision theorem

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let T={a,b,c}. Suppose one exact two-cover H-T=U sqcup V simultaneously supports endpoint-restoration caps for all three deleted vertices: for each t in T, t can be attached to one physical end of U or V by a tight endpoint turn, recovering a tight two-cover of H-(T\\{t}). Then either H has a spanning two-cover, or two distinct deleted vertices t,tprime attach at the same physical end of the same residual rail. In the latter case the terminal physical dimer at that end carries same-polarity signed certificates with the two distinct witnesses t,tprime in the same tested order, so R38 yields the accepted same-polarity two-extension collision package localized to that terminal dimer. Consequently any triple-deletion residue whose three R395 cap representatives coincide on one exact residual cover either closes or carries a literal same-end terminal collision.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; T is a three-vertex set; H-T=U sqcup V is one exact two-cover; each t in T has an endpoint cap on this same U sqcup V.

## Scope
Order-free fixed-shadow synchronization theorem for the endpoint-universal branch.

## Applicability
Use after R395 whenever two or three restoration caps can be aligned to one exact H-T representative. In particular, if H-T has a unique exact two-cover up to swapping the two rails, all three caps align and the theorem applies.

## Persistence
The exact residual cover U sqcup V, cap target ends, and any resulting terminal collision are literal same-representative data. The collision witnesses are the named deleted vertices.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the three R395 cap representatives always coincide. Does not classify the same-polarity collision further and does not close the conjecture in that branch.

## Proof
By R168 each rail of H-T has order at least two, so each of U,V has two well-defined physical ends with terminal dimers. Assign to each t in T one physical end e(t) at which its assumed cap attaches. If e(a),e(b),e(c) are pairwise distinct, attach all three deleted vertices simultaneously. Attachments on different rails are disjoint, and attachments at opposite ends of one rail commute because each uses only the original terminal dimer at its own end. The two resulting tight paths cover every vertex of H, contradiction. Therefore in a surviving counterexample two distinct vertices t,tprime share one end. If that end is the left end U=(u1,u2,...), the two cap turns are (t,u1,u2) and (tprime,u1,u2); dually at a right end they are (...,u_{r-1},u_r,t) and (...,u_{r-1},u_r,tprime). Thus the same oriented terminal dimer has two distinct witnesses with identical polarity and tested order. Accepted R38 gives the same-polarity two-extension collision package.
