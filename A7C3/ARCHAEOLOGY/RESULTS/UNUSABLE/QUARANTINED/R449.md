# R449 — Four-Support Sign-Splice Checkerboard Descent

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Retain the accepted R440 four-support theorem. In the paired specialization, suppose the four supports are grouped as two retained historical balanced opposite-sign pairs Pi_1=(H_1,T_1) and Pi_2=(H_2,T_2), with all four supports nontrivial and pairwise vertex-disjoint. Then three classes of R440 outputs are recyclable. (A) If a cell (H_i,T_j) is GG and concatenates to one tight carrier A containing both complete signed supports, R174 refunds the two touched supports. If i=j, Pi_i drops by at least two; if i!=j, Pi_i strictly descends through H_i and Pi_j strictly descends through T_j, with the untouched partners unchanged. (B) If one row i has two GB cells, choose the off-diagonal cell (H_i,T_j), j!=i, and perform its literal GB transfer; T_j loses exactly its head vertex while H_j is untouched, so Pi_j strictly descends. Dually, a column with two BG cells has an off-diagonal BG transfer strictly descending the corresponding original pair through its head support. Thus the +2-extension/reverse-trimer row/column-overcrowding subtest need not be treated as an exit. (C) If a cell is BB, write H=(...c,d), T=(a,b,...). R3 gives the tight reverse four-path (b,a,d,c). Its two turns (b,a,d) and (a,d,c) make (b,a) a tail-signed dimer with witness d and (d,c) a head-signed dimer with witness a. These two reversed terminal dimers are disjoint, oppositely signed, and have total mass four, so R39 gives a direct ancestry-bearing balanced pair and hence either a spanning two-cover or a mass-two floor. In the beyond-order-ten smallest-counterexample setting, any such BB floor enters R249 and then R387, yielding a spanning two-cover, an R173-admissible mate clause, or an accepted historical contact/pair exit. Consequently, in the paired R441/R442 use case, GG fusion, row/column overcrowding, and BB are all downstream-consumable. The only R440 residues not reduced by this theorem are the R436 witness/contact growth-cycle-reversal branch and the finite quiet checkerboard boundary; in the R442 dimer-sided specialization the quiet boundary is already consumed by R445.

## Proof
Let Pi_1=(H_1,T_1) and Pi_2=(H_2,T_2) be the two retained historical balanced opposite-sign pairs, with all four supports nontrivial and pairwise disjoint, and use exactly the cell notation of the accepted R440 proof P454. For a cell H=(...c,d), T=(a,b,...), the tested seam turns are cda and dab.

(A) GG fusion. If both seams are tight, the literal concatenation A=(H,T) is one tight carrier containing the complete supports H_i and T_j. Apply R174 to those two disjoint nontrivial signed supports inside A. It deletes one support vertex from each while leaving nonempty signed descendants. If i=j, the historical pair Pi_i loses at least two total support vertices. If i!=j, Pi_i strictly descends through H_i while its partner T_i is untouched, and Pi_j strictly descends through T_j while H_j is untouched. Thus a GG cell is certified historical pair descent, not a terminal fusion output.

(B) Row/column overcrowding. Suppose row i contains GB in both columns. Choose the off-diagonal cell (H_i,T_j) with j!=i and perform only its literal GB transfer from P454: the head vertex a of T_j is moved to the tail of H_i. Signed-Interval Rebirth in the accepted cell analysis says T_j loses exactly that unsigned head vertex and retains a nonempty signed descendant, while H_j is untouched. Hence the original pair Pi_j strictly descends. No final +2/reverse-trimer row test is needed. Dually, if one column j contains BG in both rows, choose the off-diagonal cell (H_i,T_j), i!=j; its literal BG transfer deletes the unsigned terminal vertex d from H_i while T_i is untouched, so Pi_i strictly descends. Therefore row/column overcrowding is also certified historical pair descent.

(C) BB cell. If both cell seams are bad, R3 gives the reverse turns bad? No: precisely (b,a,d) and (a,d,c) are tight, hence A=(b,a,d,c) is a tight reverse P4 as in P454. Read its first turn as saying the reversed terminal dimer D_T=(b,a) is tail-signed by d, and its second turn as saying the reversed terminal dimer D_H=(d,c) is head-signed by a. The dimers are vertex-disjoint because H and T were disjoint; their signs have opposite polarities; and their total support mass is four. Therefore R39 forms a direct ancestry-bearing balanced pair (D_H,D_T) and gives either a spanning two-cover or a mass-two ancestry-bearing floor. In the beyond-order-ten smallest-counterexample regime, a nonclosing BB floor enters the adaptive floor-aligned fixed-turn front door R249; R387 then yields a spanning two-cover, an explicit R173-admissible mate clause, or an accepted historical contact/pair exit. Thus BB is also downstream-consumable rather than a terminal bounded object.

After removing (A)-(C), the only outputs of the R440 proof not compiled here are the R436 witness/contact branch (strict growth, proper tight cycle, or reverse-contact/reversal geometry) and the finite quiet checkerboard descent to its small boundary. In the R442 predecessor-dimer specialization, R445 already consumes the quiet boundary after at most one quiet matching. This proves the stated paired-specialization compiler.
