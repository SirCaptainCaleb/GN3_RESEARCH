# R350 — Two-witness terminal-sign protected-contact compiler

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K=(k_0,...,k_m) be any proper tight path of order at least four. Fix either terminal physical dimer D of K and choose any two distinct exterior vertices e,f in V(H)\\V(K). No common tested orientation or polarity hypothesis is needed. For each witness separately, boundary antisymmetry on the reversal pair with the terminal carrier vertex as middle supplies a tight trimer having D as one terminal physical support, hence a valid signed incarnation of D with that witness. Delete D. R21 forces a selected crossing {x,z} from the nonempty surviving contiguous carrier A=K-D to the exterior. Since x lies on A and e,f lie outside K, at most one of e,f can equal z; therefore at least one admissible witness avoids the selected crossing. R37 captures the carrier-side endpoint x through that crossing. Because |A|>=2, x has a genuine historical adjacency on A, and after choosing an actual capture descendant and designated quiet continuation R312 forces an earlier protected contact or makes lawful R155 old-carrier mirror reentry the first contact. Consequently every terminal dimer of every proper tight path of order at least four with at least two exterior vertices is a protected-first-contact portal. In a smallest counterexample R169 supplies at least five exterior vertices automatically.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper tight path of order at least four; D is either terminal physical dimer; e,f are any two distinct exterior vertices.

## Scope
Order-free strengthening of R323 removing same-polarity and tested-orientation assumptions.

## Applicability
Use to enter the protected first-contact frontier from any proper tight path of order at least four. R199/global-longest endpoint shielding is unnecessary for this purpose.

## Persistence
The terminal dimer, chosen exterior witness identities, deletion crossing, capture event, surviving old carrier A, and lawful first-contact probe persist according to R21/R37/R312. The two sign incarnations need not share tested order or polarity.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the two sign certificates are same-polarity, does not create an R38 strict-path conclusion, and does not itself close the first-contact branch. The two witnesses are used only adaptively for capture.

## Proof
For the left terminal dimer D={k_0,k_1}, R3 makes exactly one of (e,k_0,k_1),(k_1,k_0,e) tight, and either orientation gives D as a terminal signed support with witness e; likewise for f. After deleting D, R21 selects {x,z} with x in A=K-D and z outside A. Since e,f are outside K while x is in K, the crossing can contain at most one of e,f, namely as z. The other witness is outside the selected pair, so R37 gives a valid capture of x. The terminal deletion leaves the contiguous path A of order at least two, hence x has an old adjacency. Apply R312.
