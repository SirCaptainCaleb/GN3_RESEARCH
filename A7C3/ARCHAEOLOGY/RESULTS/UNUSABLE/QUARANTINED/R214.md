# R214 — Mass-two singleton floor cannot be a literal signed-pair frame

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
In a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample, any literal signed-pair frame H=K sqcup P sqcup T of the form consumed by R171 must have historical pair mass M=|P|+|T| at least five. Indeed V(P) union V(T) is exactly the exterior of the proper tight path K, so R169 gives M>=5. Consequently no balanced pair of mass two, three, or four can itself be realized as the two literal pair components in an R171 frame. In particular, mass-two floors, direct mass-four dimer pairs such as R208, and every strict paid descendant of a mass-four pair are all too small for direct R171 entry unless a later remint/rebirth first increases the literal signed-support mass to at least five.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; H=K sqcup P sqcup T is proposed as a literal R171 signed-pair frame with K the third tight-path component.

## Scope
Sharp architecture/scope threshold for literal signed-pair-frame currentization in arbitrary-order smallest-counterexample work.

## Applicability
Use whenever a proof proposes feeding any low-mass balanced pair directly into R171. Pair mass below five is not merely hard to currentize: it is incompatible with the universal exterior floor.

## Persistence
Pure graph-intrinsic exterior-size obstruction; the prohibited low-mass literal representative cannot exist.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not prohibit historical low-mass balanced-pair ancestry, does not prohibit R171 at mass at least five, and does not prohibit a later remint/rebirth that increases the literal signed-support mass before R171 entry.

## Proof
Assume the hypotheses stated in R214: H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and H=K sqcup P sqcup T is proposed as a literal signed-pair frame of the form consumed by R171, with K the third tight-path component. No theorem-level use of R171 is needed. Because the displayed three paths are vertex-disjoint and spanning, V(P) union V(T)=V(H)\V(K), so the historical pair mass is M=|P|+|T|=|V(H)-V(K)|. The path K is proper, since P and T are nonempty signed supports. Accepted fully reconstructible R169 states that every proper tight path in a smallest counterexample has at least five exterior vertices. Therefore M>=5. Hence no literal frame satisfying these hypotheses can have pair mass two, three, or four. This proves the full R214 correction and its listed low-mass exclusions without using R171 as a premise.
