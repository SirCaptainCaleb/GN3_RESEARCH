# R489 — Bridge-neighbor correct-side exposure compiles to collision or exact endpoint-state reversal

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Retain the hypotheses and notation of R485 in its residual (P4) branch. Thus P=(a_0,z_1,...,z_{m-1},b_q) is a tight bridge with m>=3, C_L=(z_1,a_0,b_q) and C_R=(a_0,b_q,z_{m-1}) are tight, and D={a_0,b_q}. Put E=(a_0,b_q), let O_L=(a_0,z_1,z_2) be the original first bridge turn, and let O_R=(z_{m-2},z_{m-1},b_q) be the original last bridge turn. Let H-D=R sqcup S be any literal exact two-cover. (L) If one rail ends R=(...,y,z_1), then the literal three-cover R sqcup S sqcup E and its ordered R-to-E splice have the physical R453 mate clause (a_0,z_1,y) OR (b_q,a_0,z_1). Since C_L is tight, its exact reverse (b_q,a_0,z_1) is bad by R3, so (a_0,z_1,y) is forced tight. If y != z_2, this forced turn and O_L are same-polarity certificates on the identical oriented support (a_0,z_1) with distinct witnesses y,z_2, hence R38 gives a named source-adjacent collision. If y=z_2, the exact cover literally ends with the reversed old bridge state (z_2,z_1). (R) Dually, if one rail begins R=(z_{m-1},y,...), then the ordered E-to-R splice has mate clause (z_{m-1},b_q,a_0) OR (y,z_{m-1},b_q). Since C_R is tight, its reverse first literal is bad and (y,z_{m-1},b_q) is forced tight. If y != z_{m-2}, this and O_R give a named same-polarity source-adjacent collision on (z_{m-1},b_q); if y=z_{m-2}, the exact cover literally begins with the reversed old bridge state (z_{m-1},z_{m-2}). Consequently the cross-fiber target after R485 is only an endpoint-side bit: exposing z_1 at a rail tail or z_{m-1} at a rail head cannot terminate anonymously. It yields either a collision pinned to the original bridge boundary turn or the exact reversal of that old boundary state, with the pair-deletion cover and complete R453 clause retained.

## Hypotheses
All hypotheses of R485 including pc(H)>2 and m>=3; its P4 branch; H-D has the displayed exact two-cover; accepted R3,R38,R453.

## Scope
Order-free physical clause compiler specialized to the R485 bridge pair. It preserves the exact pair-deletion fiber, bridge-side endpoint, old bridge neighbor, and complete R453 splice certificate.

## Applicability
Use after R485/R486 when searching the exact H-{a_0,b_q} fiber. It is enough to steer z_1 to a rail tail or z_{m-1} to a rail head. A wrong predecessor/successor is productive by giving a source-adjacent collision; the only noncollision case is the exact reversed old endpoint state.

## Persistence
Retain D={a_0,b_q}; the original bridge P and old boundary turns O_L,O_R; the chosen exact H-D cover; the endpoint-side role of z_1 or z_{m-1}; the exact predecessor/successor; the literal three-cover E sqcup R sqcup S; and the complete ordered R453 splice clause. Collision or reversed-state outputs are graph-intrinsic once exhibited.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not force a correct-side endpoint exposure to exist. Does not say the resulting R38 collision or exact reversed state closes H. Does not preserve the rest of A or B inside the pair-deletion rails and does not by itself create an R173/R463 cycle.

## Proof
For (L), write R=(...,y,z_1). In the three-cover R sqcup S sqcup E with E=(a_0,b_q), the R453 formula for the ordered pair R-to-E gives the mate clause (a_0,z_1,y) OR (b_q,a_0,z_1). The second literal is the complete reversal of C_L=(z_1,a_0,b_q), hence bad by R3. Therefore (a_0,z_1,y) is tight. The old bridge supplies O_L=(a_0,z_1,z_2). If y is different from z_2, the two tight turns share the oriented dimer (a_0,z_1) in the same tail polarity and have distinct witnesses, so R38 gives the claimed collision. If y=z_2, the selected terminal state of R is z_2 z_1, the exact reversal of the old bridge state z_1 z_2. The right-hand statement is identical with R=(z_{m-1},y,...): the E-to-R splice gives (z_{m-1},b_q,a_0) OR (y,z_{m-1},b_q); the first is the reverse of C_R and therefore bad; the second is forced and compares with O_R=(z_{m-2},z_{m-1},b_q).
