# R888 — Integrable two-cover conjecture for edge-ordered complete graphs

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Conjecture. Every finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament whose comparison orientation Gamma(H) is acyclic has pc(H)<=2. Equivalently, for every total ordering of E(K_n), the vertex set of K_n can be partitioned into at most two vertex-disjoint increasing paths. A potentially useful strengthening is endpoint-flexibility: for every prescribed vertex v, such a two-path partition can be chosen with v exposed as an endpoint of one path.

## Hypotheses
Acyclic comparison orientation in the sense of the line-graph comparison representation, equivalently an edge-ordered complete graph. Paths are vertex-simple and increasing in the edge order; the two paths are vertex-disjoint and partition all vertices.

## Scope
New locality-escape conjecture suggested by the line-graph integrability representation. If true, any smallest counterexample to the main two-cover conjecture must have genuine comparison holonomy, so O4/O6 could restrict immediately to shortest comparison-cycle geometry.

## Applicability
Attack the acyclic/integrable branch independently of gate machinery. The globally largest edge and endpoint-flexibility are a promising induction handle, but no endpoint-flexibility theorem is currently proved.

## Persistence
Retain the equivalence with two increasing paths in an edge-ordered K_n and the stronger endpoint-flexibility candidate separately from the proved line-graph representation theorem.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Finite computation is evidence only. This revision does not claim the conjecture for n>=8, does not claim endpoint-flexibility, and does not infer anything about cyclic comparison orientations.
