# R424 — Universal O4 reduction to localized trimer interaction or common-shadow cross-state pair

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample beyond order ten. Fix any two distinct prescribed vertices u,v. Unless H has a spanning two-cover, there is a chosen certificate-retaining continuation with one of the following outcomes: (1) an accepted explicit historical pair/contact exit; or (2) an ancestry-bearing both-singleton balanced-pair floor whose singleton supports are exactly {u},{v}. In outcome (2) the floor retains one of the two typed O4 provenance packages of R417: Type I source-trimer provenance, or Type II common-shadow cross-state provenance. Indeed R417 first normalizes every nonclosing O4 survivor to an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. Choose any b distinct from u,v; by R170 exactly one of (u,b,v),(v,b,u) is a proper tight turn J with outer pair {u,v}. Apply R423 to the R417 floor. It either closes, takes an accepted explicit historical pair/contact exit, or steers the active floor to {u,v} while preserving the incoming ancestry ledger. At that aligned checkpoint the R423 fixed-turn endpoint budget applies: at most two aligned reentries can remain quiet before later aligned reentry forces closure or an explicit historical exit. Thus no physical target pair is intrinsically inaccessible from the normalized O4 floor; the scarce information is the retained Type I/II ancestry, not floor-coordinate availability.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample beyond order ten; provisional R417 and R423; accepted R170.

## Scope
Order-free strengthening of the top-level O4 normalization. The target pair {u,v} is prescribed before the reentry continuation and is independent of the incoming both-singleton floor coordinates.

## Applicability
Use after O4 normalization when a downstream consumer needs the floor aligned to a specific physical pair, especially the original source-trimer outer pair or the original common-shadow cross-state endpoints.

## Persistence
R423 preserves the incoming R417 ancestry ledger while floor coordinates change. Hence Type I source-trimer data or Type II common-shadow/cross-state data survive target-pair alignment. The chosen target turn J and its later endpoint-capture/contact history are retained within the J-lineage.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert arbitrary same-representative synchronization; does not say an explicit historical exit closes the conjecture; does not guarantee the target-aligned floor is reached if a productive exit occurs first; does not make floor coordinates scarce or canonically determined; does not make alternative target-pair descendants simultaneous.

## Proof
Assume H does not already have a spanning two-cover. By R417 choose a certificate-retaining continuation to a both-singleton floor S carrying either Type I source-trimer provenance or Type II common-shadow cross-state provenance. Fix distinct target vertices u,v and choose b outside {u,v}; the beyond-order-ten hypothesis supplies such b. By R170 exactly one of (u,b,v) and (v,b,u) is a proper tight turn J, whose unordered outer pair is {u,v}. R423 is turn-first and floor-independent: it applies to this preselected J from the arbitrary ancestry-bearing floor S. Along its chosen continuation, either a spanning two-cover or an accepted explicit historical pair/contact exit occurs, or Complete Floor Pair Steering aligns the active floor to {u},{v}. R423 explicitly preserves the complete incoming floor ancestry ledger while the active singleton coordinates change, so the R417 Type I/II provenance survives alignment. Its selected-incidence endpoint-anchor argument further implies that at most two aligned reentries can be quiet. This proves the claimed target-pair normalization and shows that coordinate accessibility is generic once the R417 floor is available.
