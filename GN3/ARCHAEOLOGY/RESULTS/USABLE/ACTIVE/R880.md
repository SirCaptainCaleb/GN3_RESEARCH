# R880 — Every surviving synchronized order-three R24 frame has long rail parameter m at least six

## Statement
Retain the accepted synchronized transitive order-three frame R594 with Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4, and the exhaustive aligned/crossed gate split R619. Then m>=6. Indeed m=4 is impossible in both gate phases by accepted R877. If m=5, the aligned phase is impossible by accepted R876 and the crossed phase is impossible by accepted R878. Therefore no synchronized transitive R24 frame exists for m in {4,5}; every surviving frame lies in the common long-core regime m>=6.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the accepted R594 synchronized transitive order-three frame. Use accepted exhaustive gate split R619 and accepted finite-base closures R876,R877,R878.

## Applicability

## Persistence
Retain the original R594 frame labels and the conclusion m>=6. The individual m=4 and m=5 phase certificates remain available as proof leaves but need not be carried as separate frontier cases.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close any m>=6 frame. Does not assert the aligned phase is impossible for m>=6. Does not replace the detailed R876/R877/R878 certificates when their literal path orders or Mate-Fork ancestry are needed.

## Proof
By R594 the surviving order-three frame has m>=4. If m=4, accepted R877 gives a contradiction in both the aligned and crossed gate phases, so m is not 4. Suppose m=5. Accepted R619 gives the exhaustive dichotomy ALIGNED or CROSSED. In the aligned phase, accepted R876 exhibits an immediate literal spanning 6+4 two-cover, contradiction. In the crossed phase, accepted R878 derives ten one-hole cap turns, six physical Mate-Fork companions, and a final literal 6+4 two-cover, contradiction. Thus m is not 5. Therefore every surviving R594 frame has m>=6.
