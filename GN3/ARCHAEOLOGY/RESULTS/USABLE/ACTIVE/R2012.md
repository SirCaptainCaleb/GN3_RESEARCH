# R2012 — At most one neutral component can capture the isolated singleton v

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789414485.925629`

## Statement

In the later non-loop OUT-`v` pre-loss state, there is at most one neutral symmetric-difference component `D_v` whose `F→J` toggle can destroy the isolated-singleton status of `v`.

If it exists, `D_v` is an alternating path with `v,in` as its J-only endpoint and the toggle merely transports that free IN defect to the opposite F-only endpoint. Consequently the R2011 neutral transport can avoid changing `v` until it either reaches `D_v`, reaches the flipped F-prefix of `C_*`, or produces a tight three-forest with the canonical first-open successor and `v` still isolated.

## Proof

Let `M=hat M_{r-1}` be the physically tight normalized pre-loss state. Audited R1060 gives both `v,out` and `v,in` unmatched in `M`. Its proof gives the stronger split-graph fact that F has no incidence at `v,in`; any noncommon J-incidence there, if one exists, lies on a delta-zero off-`C_*` component.

Because J is a matching, there is at most one selected edge at `v,in`. Hence there is at most one neutral component containing a `J\F` edge incident with `v,in`; call it `D_v`. If no such edge exists, no neutral toggle changes `v,in`.

Suppose `D_v` exists. At the split vertex `v,in`, the component has degree one in `F triangle J`, and its unique incident edge is J-colored because F has no incidence there. A delta-zero symmetric-difference component has equally many F-only and J-only edges. Thus `D_v` cannot be an alternating cycle. It is an alternating path whose opposite endpoint is F-only: an alternating path with equal color counts has oppositely colored terminal edges.

In the normalized F-state, `v,in` is free and the opposite endpoint is matched by its terminal F-edge. Toggling the whole component to J selects the J-edge at `v,in` and deletes the terminal F-edge at the opposite endpoint. Matching cardinality is unchanged; the free split copy is transported from `v,in` to that opposite endpoint.

Every other neutral component is disjoint from `v,in`, so toggling it leaves `v,in` free. Neutral components are also disjoint from `v,out`, whose unmatched status is carried by the distinguished component `C_*` at the pre-loss position. Hence every collection of neutral toggles avoiding `D_v` preserves both free copies of `v`.

Run the monotone blocker-transport procedure of R2011 but stop before toggling `D_v`. If the initial blocker component is already `D_v`, record immediate `D_v` contact. Otherwise toggle any required neutral component `E != D_v`; if the only required neutral component is `D_v`, stop and record that contact. Since each accepted step strictly enlarges the toggled neutral set, the procedure terminates in one of three states: no blocker remains, a blocker meets the flipped F-prefix of `C_*`, or the next forced neutral component is `D_v`. In the first state the result is a tight three-forest with the canonical first-open successor and, because `D_v` was never toggled, `v` remains isolated.
