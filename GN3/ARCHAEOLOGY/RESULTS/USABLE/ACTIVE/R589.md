# R589 — Aligned two-ended middle gates force a complete cross-end reverse fan

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2. Let Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=2, be a tight path and let X={a,b,c,z} be its four-vertex complement. Put L=q_0 and R=q_m. Assume the aligned complementary middle-gate packet Lbz,Lzb,acL,caL,bzR,zbR,Rac,Rca is tight. Then all eight cross-end middle turns are forced tight: aRz,cRz,aRb,cRb and bLc,zLc,bLa,zLa. Equivalently, every ordered cross pair p in {b,z}, q in {a,c} satisfies p L q tight, and every q in {a,c}, p in {b,z} satisfies q R p tight. Each forced turn is the exact reverse mate of a one-hole Hamilton-P6 candidate on X union {L,R}; the corresponding candidate identity is retained. Thus the aligned two-ended gate packet rigidifies into opposite complete 2-by-2 middle rectangles at the two rail ends.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; pc(H)>2; V(H)=X disjoint union V(Q) with |X|=4 and Q=(q_0,...,q_m) tight, m>=2; the eight displayed aligned gate turns are tight.

## Scope
Pure aligned-gate parent theorem. No smallest-counterexample minimality, R24, transitive matching-height classification, global-longestness, capture, or computation is used once the displayed gate packet and complementary tight rail are supplied.

## Applicability
In the accepted exact-two-bad transitive R24 cell R556, the required aligned gate packet is already present with L=q_0,R=q_m. Hence the full eight-turn cross fan follows immediately for every surviving rail length, including the long m>=6 residue of R574. Use these literal endpoint rectangles before invoking R582/R540: in particular cRb and bLa are forced simultaneously, not merely as alternatives.

## Persistence
Retain X,Q,L,R, the original eight aligned gate turns, each of the eight one-hole six-vertex candidates, its unique bad turn, and the exact reversed cross-fan mate. All resulting turn signs are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The eight-turn fan does not itself force a Hamilton P6; the local six-vertex orientation can remain P6-free. The middle rectangles are not R38 same-support collisions. No claim is made that the fan alone closes R24, creates a second Hamilton absorber, or supplies endpoint-compatible Hamilton multiplicity for R566.

## Proof
Because m>=2, Q^o=(q_1,...,q_{m-1}) is a nonempty tight path. Any tight Hamilton P6 on X union {L,R} would therefore form a spanning two-cover with Q^o, contradicting pc(H)>2. Now test the following eight P6 orders. (1) (L,b,z,R,a,c): Lbz,bzR,Rac are tight, so zRa is bad and aRz tight. (2) (L,b,z,R,c,a): Lbz,bzR,Rca are tight, so zRc is bad and cRz tight. (3) (L,z,b,R,a,c): Lzb,zbR,Rac are tight, so bRa is bad and aRb tight. (4) (L,z,b,R,c,a): Lzb,zbR,Rca are tight, so bRc is bad and cRb tight. Dually, (5) (R,a,c,L,b,z) forces bLc from bad cLb; (6) (R,a,c,L,z,b) forces zLc from bad cLz; (7) (R,c,a,L,b,z) forces bLa from bad aLb; and (8) (R,c,a,L,z,b) forces zLa from bad aLz. These are exactly the claimed two complete middle rectangles.
