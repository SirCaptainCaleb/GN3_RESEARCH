# R535 — Four-marker fragmentation identity and high-interleaving two-path normal form

## Statement
Let F=R sqcup S be a spanning two-path forest on W=X sqcup Y with |X|=4. Put p=e_F(X) and q=e_F(X,Y). For x in X let B_x=Y union {x}, let d_Y(x) be the number of selected F-adjacencies from x to Y, and let c_x=c(F[B_x]). Then c_x=p+q-d_Y(x)-1 for every x, and therefore sum_x c_x=4p+3q-4. If X supports no selected Hamilton P4 inside F, then p<=2. If additionally c_x>=4 for every x, then only (p,q)=(2,4),(1,6),(0,7),(0,8) are possible. Up to swapping and reversing the two rails and contracting each maximal Y-block to Y, the surviving forest words are exactly: for (2,4), Y | YXXYXXY and YXXY | YXXY; for (1,6), Y | YXXYXYXY, Y | YXYXXYXY, YXXY | YXYXY, YXXYXY | YXY; for (0,7), XY | YXYXYXY, XYXY | YXYXY, XYXYXY | YXY, XYXYXYXY | Y; for (0,8), Y | YXYXYXYXY, YXY | YXYXYXY, YXYXY | YXYXY. The corresponding multisets {c_x} are respectively {4,4,4,4}, {5,5,4,4}, {5,4,4,4}, and {5,5,5,5}.

## Hypotheses
F is a spanning forest with exactly two path components; X has four vertices. For p<=2 assume no rail contains all four X vertices consecutively as an X-only P4. For the high-interleaving classification assume c(F[Y union {x}])>=4 for all x.

## Scope
Pure path-forest combinatorics. No Strong orientation axiom is used except in applications to justify p<=2 from a no-P4 X-cell.

## Applicability
Use in R24-style four-marker deletion fibers or any two-path recompletion problem with four distinguished vertices. The pointwise formula is stronger than the summed 4p+3q bound and reduces the high-fragmentation branch to thirteen coarse rail words.

## Persistence
The selected forest F, X/Y partition, degrees, fragment counts, and contracted rail words are literal in the chosen representative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
The thirteen words classify only support/interleaving shape after maximal Y-block contraction; they do not encode turn orientations inside or across blocks. Matching-height information on X cannot eliminate a word merely from its support pattern when no XXX turn occurs.

## Proof
Let |W|=n. Since F is a spanning forest with exactly two path components, e(F)=n-2. Fix x in X and put A_x=X-{x}, B_x=Y union {x}. Because F[B_x] is a forest,
c_x=|B_x|-e_F(B_x).
Now |B_x|=n-3, while partitioning the selected edges gives e_F(B_x)=e(F)-e_F(A_x)-delta_F(A_x). Hence
c_x=(n-3)-(n-2-e_F(A_x)-delta_F(A_x))=e_F(A_x)+delta_F(A_x)-1.
Write d_X(x) and d_Y(x) for the selected F-degrees of x into X and Y. Since p=e_F(X), e_F(A_x)=p-d_X(x). Also delta_F(A_x) counts all X-Y edges except those incident with x, together with each X-X edge from x to A_x, so delta_F(A_x)=q-d_Y(x)+d_X(x). Therefore c_x=p+q-d_Y(x)-1.
Summing and using sum_x d_Y(x)=q gives sum_x c_x=4p+3q-4.

If p>=3, the graph F[X] is a forest on four vertices with at least three edges, hence is a tree. Because F itself has maximum degree two, F[X] is a four-vertex path. Its four X vertices occur consecutively on one F-rail and form a selected X-only P4. Thus in a no-P4 X application p<=2.

Assume now every c_x>=4. Then 4p+3q-4=sum c_x>=16, so 4p+3q>=20. The total selected degree at X is 2p+q<=8. With p<=2, the integer solutions are exactly (2,4),(1,6),(0,7),(0,8).

For (2,4), equality holds in the degree budget: every X-vertex has selected degree two. The pointwise formula c_x=5-d_Y(x)>=4 gives d_Y(x)<=1, while sum d_Y=4, so every d_Y(x)=1 and therefore every d_X(x)=1. Hence F[X] is a perfect matching and maximal Y-block contraction yields exactly the two path multisets Y | YXXYXXY and YXXY | YXXY. Every c_x=4.

For (1,6), again 2p+q=8, so every X-vertex has degree two. The two endpoints of the unique XX edge have d_X=1 and hence d_Y=1; the other two have d_X=0 and d_Y=2. Thus the c_x multiset is {5,5,4,4}. Contracting maximal Y-blocks and placing the unique XX pair among four X symbols in two paths with Y between all other consecutive X occurrences yields, up to rail swap/reversal, exactly Y | YXXYXYXY; Y | YXYXXYXY; YXXY | YXYXY; YXXYXY | YXY.

For (0,7), sum_X deg=7. Since every X-degree is at most two and no XX edges exist, exactly three X-vertices have degree two and one has degree one. Therefore {c_x}={4,4,4,5}. The two rails alternate X,Y after Y-block contraction and exactly one forest endpoint is X. According to whether the rail containing that X endpoint contains 1,2,3,4 X vertices, the four words are XY | YXYXYXY; XYXY | YXYXY; XYXYXY | YXY; XYXYXYXY | Y.

For (0,8), every X-vertex has degree two and both forest endpoints lie in Y. Both rails therefore begin and end in Y and alternate. Distributing four X vertices as 0+4,1+3,2+2 gives exactly Y | YXYXYXYXY; YXY | YXYXYXY; YXYXY | YXYXY. Here c_x=5 for every x. This exhausts the high branch.
