# R801 — The SHORTCUT-CUT P4 and P6 are X-blocked at both ends: no X vertex can seam onto K or J-sharp

## Statement
In the SHORTCUT-CUT branch of accepted R789, neither of the two distinguished tight paths can be extended by a vertex of X at either end. Precisely: (K-BLOCK) For every x in X, the turn (x,u,v) is bad and the turn (d,s,x) is bad. Hence no x in X prepends to K=(u,v,d,s) and no x in X appends to it. (J-BLOCK) For every x in X, the turn (x,L,u) is bad and the turn (s,R,x) is bad. Hence no x in X prepends to J#=(L,u,v,d,s,R) and no x in X appends to it. Both blocks are immediate from accepted universal star geometry together with boundary antisymmetry: accepted R723 gives (v,u,x) and (x,s,d) tight for every x in X, and accepted R594 gives (u,L,x) and (x,R,s) tight for every x in X, so R3 makes each displayed complete reversal bad. CONSEQUENCE for the X-ENDPOINT-CROSSING residue. In the SHORTCUT-CUT branch the natural spanning proposals are K together with the two rails of the exact cover T# of H-{u,v,d,s}, and J# together with a path on X union Q[3,m-3]. Neither proposal can be improved by seaming an X vertex onto an end of K or of J#. Therefore any spanning-two-cover splice in this branch must attach at a deep interval vertex of Q[3,m-3], or at the physical endpoints L,R in a manner not consisting of an X vertex adjacent to an end of J#, or must proceed by an interior rail exchange rather than an end seam. In particular the X-ENDPOINT-CROSSING alternative of R789, in which the selected {L,R}-to-X crossing lands in X rather than in deep Q, cannot be consumed by end-seaming that crossing partner onto either distinguished path.

## Proof
Work in the SHORTCUT-CUT branch of accepted R789, so A=(u,v,d) and B=(v,d,s) are tight, K=(u,v,d,s) is a tight P4, and J#=(L,u,v,d,s,R) is a tight P6.

K-BLOCK. Extending K at its initial end by a vertex y means asserting the tight path (y,u,v,d,s), whose initial turn is (y,u,v). Accepted R723 gives the tight trimer (v,u,x) for every x in X. By boundary antisymmetry R3, the ordered turn (a,b,c) is tight exactly when its complete reversal (c,b,a) is bad; applying this to (v,u,x) shows (x,u,v) is bad. Hence for y=x in X the required initial turn is bad and no such extension exists. Extending K at its terminal end by y means asserting (u,v,d,s,y), whose terminal turn is (d,s,y). Accepted R723 gives (x,s,d) tight for every x in X, so by R3 its complete reversal (d,s,x) is bad. Hence no x in X appends. This proves K-BLOCK.

J-BLOCK. Extending J# at its initial end by y requires the initial turn (y,L,u). Accepted R594 gives (u,L,x) tight for every x in X, so by R3 the complete reversal (x,L,u) is bad, and no x in X prepends. Extending J# at its terminal end by y requires the terminal turn (s,R,y). Accepted R594 gives (x,R,s) tight for every x in X, so by R3 the complete reversal (s,R,x) is bad, and no x in X appends. This proves J-BLOCK.

CONSEQUENCE. In this branch accepted R4 supplies an exact two-cover T# of H-{u,v,d,s}, and R789 forces a selected state crossing {L,R} against X union Q[3,m-3]. Suppose that crossing lands in X, which is the X-ENDPOINT-CROSSING residue named as the current frontier. The obvious closure-saturation move is to seam the X partner of that crossing onto an end of one of the two distinguished tight paths, producing a spanning proposal with fewer components. K-BLOCK and J-BLOCK show every such end seam is bad, for every vertex of X and at every one of the four ends. Hence no end-seam consumer of the X-ENDPOINT-CROSSING residue exists, and a successful splice in this branch must use a deep Q[3,m-3] attachment or an interior exchange. Note the blocks are symmetric across the two physical ends, so this is two-ended information and is not subject to the one-sided fences of accepted R626 and R721. This proves the negative result.
