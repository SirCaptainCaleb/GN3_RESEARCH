# R1027 — G36 source collision rotates to the selected far dimer; without a P4 the far rail is exactly a dimer

**Audit state:** PASS
**Source unit:** SV117618
**Proof provenance:** `A7C3/WORKSPACE/D17/429-g36-source-collision-rotation-terminal-wall-dimer.md`

## Statement
Retain the G36 source-gate wall packet after SV115482 lands in its source-dimer collision branch. In either orientation, the exact R516 signature-11 mapping rotates the anchor tests onto the actual selected far dimer. Either a labelled literal P4 occurs, or the two deleted source anchors become witnesses on that selected far dimer; any continuation of the far rail then concatenates to a P4. Hence, in the no-P4 residue, the far rail is exactly that dimer.

More precisely:

- **OUT.** The selected far boundary is `b->d`, the wall turn `(d,b,t)` and source turns `(A,t,C),(A,t,b)` are tight. Unless a P4 already appears, the four-set `{A,t,b,C}` is forced into R516 signature 11, yielding `(b,A,C)` and `(b,C,A)`. Testing `(d,b,A)` and `(d,b,C)`, any tight test gives a P4; if both are bad, R3 gives `(A,b,d)` and `(C,b,d)`, so `A,C` are head witnesses on the actual selected far dimer `(b,d)`. If the rail continued through `d->e`, then `(A,b,d,e)` is a P4. Thus the no-P4 far rail is exactly `(b,d)`.
- **IN.** Dually, the selected far boundary is `d->b`; unless a P4 already appears, R516 signature 11 and the corresponding anchor tests force `(d,b,A)` and `(d,b,C)`, so `A,C` are tail witnesses on the actual selected far dimer `(d,b)`. Any selected predecessor `e->d` produces a P4, so the no-P4 far rail is exactly `(d,b)`.

The surviving non-P4 packet is therefore an anchor-saturated wall dimer retaining the actual selected orientation and the identities of the deleted source anchors.

## Proof
### OUT orientation
Retain the selected far boundary `b->d` and tight turns

`(d,b,t), (A,t,C), (A,t,b)`.

Test `(b,t,C)`. If it is tight, `(d,b,t,C)` is a literal P4. Otherwise R3 gives `(C,t,b)` tight.

Now inspect `{A,t,b,C}` using `K=(A,t,b)`. Relative to the terminal dimers of `K`, the retained turns `(A,t,C)` and `(C,t,b)` put the fourth vertex `C` in the same tail and head polarities as the natural signs. If the four-set has a tight Hamilton P4, we are done. Otherwise R516 is forced into its exact signature-11 cell, giving

`(b,A,C), (b,C,A)` tight.

Test `(d,b,A)` and `(d,b,C)`. If the first is tight, `(d,b,A,C)` is a P4. If the second is tight, `(d,b,C,A)` is a P4. Therefore in the branch with no labelled P4 both tests are bad, and R3 gives

`(A,b,d), (C,b,d)` tight.

Thus the actual selected far dimer `(b,d)` carries `A,C` as distinct head witnesses. If the selected rail continued beyond `d` via `d->e`, then the rail turn `(b,d,e)` together with `(A,b,d)` gives the literal P4 `(A,b,d,e)`. Hence the no-P4 far component is exactly the dimer `(b,d)`.

### IN orientation
Retain the selected far boundary `d->b` and tight turns

`(A,t,C), (b,t,C), (t,b,d)`.

Test `(A,t,b)`. If it is tight, `(A,t,b,d)` is a literal P4. Otherwise R3 gives `(b,t,A)`. On the tight trimer `(b,t,C)` with fourth vertex `A`, the retained source turns force the exact R516 signature-11 cell whenever no P4 exists, yielding

`(C,A,b), (A,C,b)` tight.

Testing `(A,b,d)` and `(C,b,d)`, any tight test concatenates with these turns to a labelled P4. If both are bad, R3 gives

`(d,b,A), (d,b,C)` tight.

Thus the actual selected far dimer `(d,b)` carries `A,C` as two tail witnesses. If the rail had a predecessor `e->d`, the rail turn `(e,d,b)` together with either witness turn gives a P4. Hence the no-P4 far component is exactly `(d,b)`.
