# R341 — Hamilton-five density equality gives regular triangle-free trimer bad graphs with exact edge routing

## Statement
Assume equality in the Hamilton-five bad-family ceiling of accepted R331 on an r-vertex set W, and let B be the family of five-subsets of W that do not support a tight P5. Put q=r/5, so r is divisible by five. Fix any tight trimer support P subset W and let X=W\\P. Form the bad-extension graph G_P on X by joining distinct x,y exactly when P union {x,y} belongs to B. Then G_P is q-regular and triangle-free on r-3=5q-3 vertices. Moreover every bad edge xy has the following exact routing property. For each z in X\\{x,y}, the six-set U=P union {x,y,z} contains exactly one bad five-set other than P union {x,y}. If xz or yz is a bad edge of G_P, then exactly one of them is bad and that corresponding P-extension is the unique partner. If neither xz nor yz is bad, then exactly one of the three five-sets (P\\{p}) union {x,y,z}, p in P, is bad. Thus every equality-case bad edge routes each third outside vertex either to one adjacent bad edge in the fixed-trimer graph or uniquely through one deleted vertex of the trimer.

## Hypotheses
The bad five-set family B on W attains equality in accepted R331; P is a tight trimer support contained in W.

## Scope
Extremal structural refinement of R331 using the fixed-trimer Mantel theorem R328.

## Applicability
Use only in the exact R331 equality case. It converts the global paired 4-design-like normal form into a local regular triangle-free graph at every fixed trimer, together with an exact rule linking that graph to neighboring trimer supports.

## Persistence
B, q, the trimer support P, the graph G_P, its degrees, and the six-set partner assignments are graph-intrinsic combinatorial data on W.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that equality actually occurs in a smallest counterexample. Does not classify q-regular triangle-free graphs. Does not force bipartiteness, a P6, or a two-cover. The routing partner that drops a trimer vertex need not preserve the same trimer P.

## Proof
By R331, equality implies r=5q, every four-subset S of W lies in exactly q bad five-subsets, and every six-set containing one bad block contains exactly two bad blocks. Fix a tight trimer P and x in X. Bad neighbors y of x in G_P are in bijection with bad blocks containing the four-core P union {x}, so deg(x)=q. By R328 the graph is triangle-free. Now fix a bad edge xy and z outside P union {x,y}. The six-set U=P union {x,y,z} is occupied, hence by R331 has exactly one bad five-subset besides P union {x,y}. Its five alternatives are P union {x,z}, P union {y,z}, and the three sets (P\\{p}) union {x,y,z}. Since G_P is triangle-free, the first two cannot both be bad. Therefore if one is bad it is the unique partner; if neither is bad, exactly one of the three trimer-vertex-drop alternatives is bad.
