# R584 — Two common reverse-middle blockers force a literal reverse P4 ear

## Statement
Let A,C,x,y be four distinct vertices in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. If (A,x,C) and (A,y,C) are tight, then at least one of (A,x,C,y) and (A,y,C,x) is a tight Hamilton P4. More precisely, if (x,C,y) is tight the first path is tight; if it is bad, R3 makes (y,C,x) tight and the second path is tight. In the R583 gap notation, two reverse-middle blockers Bbar_i(x)=(q_{i+1},x,q_i) and Bbar_i(y)=(q_{i+1},y,q_i) therefore force a literal tight reverse P4 ear across the adjacent carrier state q_i q_{i+1}: either (q_{i+1},x,q_i,y) or (q_{i+1},y,q_i,x).

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) exact complete-reversal antisymmetry; four displayed vertices distinct.

## Scope
Pure four-vertex local lemma, order-free and independent of smallest-counterexample or deletion assumptions.

## Applicability
Use as the direct consumer of the reverse-middle branch of R583. It converts two middle blockers into a labelled P4 with the two Q contacts in reverse carrier order.

## Persistence
Retain the two blocker turns, the tested turn xCy and its status, and the resulting labelled P4.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not by itself splice the reverse P4 into Q, currentize it in a cover, or close H.

## Proof
Assume (A,x,C) and (A,y,C) are tight. Test the turn (x,C,y). If it is tight, then (A,x,C,y) is a tight P4 because its consecutive turns are (A,x,C) and (x,C,y). If (x,C,y) is bad, exact reversal antisymmetry R3 gives (y,C,x) tight; together with the given (A,y,C), this makes (A,y,C,x) a tight P4. Thus one of the two displayed labelled Hamilton P4s always exists. Substituting A=q_{i+1}, C=q_i gives the R583 reverse-ear form.
