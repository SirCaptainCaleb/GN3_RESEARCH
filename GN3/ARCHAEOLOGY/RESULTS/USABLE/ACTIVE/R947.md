# R947 — A longest increasing path can have non-Hamiltonian complement even when another one-vertex-swapped longest path has Hamiltonian complement

## Statement
There exists an edge ordering of K_16 with a globally longest increasing path P on 12 vertices such that the induced edge-ordered K_4 on V\\V(P) has no increasing Hamilton path. In the same ordering there is another globally longest increasing path P' whose support differs from P by exchanging one vertex and whose four-vertex complement does have an increasing Hamilton path. Hence the strengthening that EVERY globally longest increasing path has Hamiltonian complement is false, while this example does not refute the existential longest-complement conjecture.

## Hypotheses
Explicit matching-layer edge order on K_16 described in the proof: A={0,...,7}, B={8,...,15}; all internal edges precede all cross edges; cross edges are in cyclic perfect-matching blocks M_0<...<M_7.

## Scope
Exact finite fence for the longest-path-complement research direction inside the edge-ordered subclass.

## Applicability
Use to distinguish the false universal-longest-support statement from the still-open existential statement. It is also a reproducible model of the desired extremal support exchange: a bad longest support is repaired by one vertex swap.

## Persistence
Retain the exact internal-edge order, the paths P and P', the two four-vertex complements, and the R823 12-vertex global upper bound.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not refute the existential claim that SOME globally longest path has Hamiltonian complement; indeed this construction explicitly supplies such a good longest path. Does not prove that one-vertex support exchange always suffices.

## Proof
Let A={0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7} and B={8,9,10,11,12,13,14,15}. Put every edge internal to A or B before every A-B edge. Order the 56 internal edges, from least to greatest, as

27, 11-12, 15, 04, 36, 8-13, 47, 45, 57, 12, 35, 23, 07, 89, 16, 9-11, 14-15, 12-15, 11-13, 8-12, 02, 34, 06, 03, 37, 10-14, 12-14, 9-14, 10-12, 8-10, 67, 8-14, 11-14, 14, 56, 10-15, 10-11, 13-14, 10-13, 17, 24, 9-10, 26, 11-15, 01, 12-13, 05, 13-15, 9-15, 8-11, 9-13, 46, 13, 8-15, 9-12, 25.

(Here ij denotes the unordered edge {i,j}; hyphens are used only when a vertex has two digits.) For k=0,...,7 let M_k be the perfect matching of A to B consisting of {a, 8+((a+k) mod 8)} for a in A. Put all edges of M_k before all edges of M_l whenever k<l. The order inside one M_k is arbitrary.

This is exactly an R823 matching-layer ordering with m=8. Therefore every increasing path has at most 8+ceil(8/2)=12 vertices.

Consider
P=(12,14,13,15,8,0,9,7,10,5,11,4).
Its first four edges 12-14,14-13,13-15,15-8 occur in the displayed internal order at positions 27,38,48,54 respectively. Its remaining seven edges lie successively in cross blocks M_0,M_1,M_2,M_3,M_5,M_6,M_7. Hence P is increasing and has 12 vertices. By the R823 upper bound it is globally longest.

Its complement is X={1,2,3,6}. Restricted to X, the six edges occur in the order
36 < 12 < 23 < 16 < 26 < 13.
There is no increasing Hamilton path on X. To see this without computation, in any monotone Hamilton path on four vertices the middle of its three path-edges must have one smaller outer edge incident at one endpoint and one larger outer edge incident at the other, with the two outer endpoints distinct. The least edge 36 and greatest edge 13 cannot be the middle. If 12 is the middle, its only smaller edge is 36, which touches neither 1 nor 2. If 23 is the middle, choosing smaller outer edge 36 forces the remaining outer edge to be 12, also smaller; choosing smaller outer edge 12 forces the remaining outer edge to be 36. If 16 is the middle, the only possible smaller outer edges touching it are 36 and 12, and they force the opposite outer edge to be respectively 12 and 36, again smaller. If 26 is the middle, its only larger edge is 13, which touches neither 2 nor 6. Thus no middle edge works.

Now exchange 7 in the support of P with 6 and take
P'=(12,14,13,15,8,0,9,6,10,5,11,4).
The internal prefix is unchanged. The cross-block sequence is now M_0,M_1,M_3,M_4,M_5,M_6,M_7, so P' is again increasing on 12 vertices and hence globally longest. Its complement is X'={1,2,3,7}, and
1,2,3,7
is increasing because 12<23<37 in the displayed internal-edge order. Therefore X' has an increasing Hamilton path.

So P is a globally longest increasing path with non-Hamiltonian complement, while the one-vertex-swapped globally longest path P' has Hamiltonian complement. This disproves only the universal-longest-support strengthening and explicitly preserves the existential possibility.
