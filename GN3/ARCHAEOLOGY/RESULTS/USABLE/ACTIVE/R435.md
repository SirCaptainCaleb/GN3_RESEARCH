# R435 — Reverse Ear Lemma

## Statement
Let P=(v_0,...,v_k) and K be vertex-simple tight paths. Let E be a K-subpath from v_i to v_j such that v_i,v_j are consecutive K-contacts with P, so every internal vertex of E lies outside P, and assume i>j. If E is the single state v_i v_j with i=j+1, K already contains the reversal of the old P-state v_j v_i. Otherwise one obtains either a tight reverse trimer at one of the two P/E seams or a vertex-simple tight cycle formed by E together with the old P-segment from v_j through v_{i-1}. Consequently, outside a reversal/reverse-trimer or proper-cycle output, the P-vertices encountered along any tight path K occur in increasing P-order.

## Hypotheses
P and K are vertex-simple tight paths in a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. The displayed contacts v_i,v_j are consecutive along K and occur in reverse P-order i>j.

## Scope
Pure Strong Level-(1) path-interaction lemma. No smallest-counterexample, completion, CW, boundedness, selected-state persistence, or contradiction-from-cycle hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use to compare an ancestral path with a later path/marker. It converts a reverse-order encounter into an explicit reverse local cell or a constructive proper cycle, and otherwise gives order alignment.

## Persistence
The output reverse trimer/reversal cell and proper cycle are graph-intrinsic once exhibited. The theorem does not assert that either input path remains selected in later covers.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
A proper tight cycle is a constructive output, not a contradiction. If K has a bounded order, the reverse local cell is correspondingly bounded; the cycle itself need not be bounded. No representative synchronization is asserted.

## Proof
Let E be the K-subpath from v_i to v_j with no internal P-vertex and i>j. If E is exactly the state v_i v_j and i=j+1, this is already the reversal of the old P-state v_j v_i. Otherwise let x be the successor of v_i on E and y the predecessor of v_j on E; in the one-state nonadjacent case x=v_j and y=v_i. Since i>j, v_{i-1} and v_{j+1} exist. Test v_{i-1} v_i x and y v_j v_{j+1}. If the first is bad, R3 gives x v_i v_{i-1} tight. If the second is bad, R3 gives v_{j+1} v_j y tight. If both are tight, traverse E from v_i to v_j, then the old P-segment v_j,v_{j+1},...,v_{i-1}, and use the first tested seam to close back to v_i. Every turn is either on E, on P, or one of the tested seams, and vertex simplicity follows from the no-internal-P-contact condition. Thus a tight cycle results. Hence outside these outputs reverse-order consecutive contacts cannot occur, so all P-contacts along K follow increasing P-order.
