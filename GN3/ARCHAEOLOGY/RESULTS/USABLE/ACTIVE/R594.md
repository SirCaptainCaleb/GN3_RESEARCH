# R594 — R24 failure reduces noncircularly to the synchronized transitive order-three frame

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and suppose that for some vertex z an exact two-cover H-z=P⊔Q has a rail P of order at most three. Then the order-one and order-two cases close immediately. In the only surviving case, P has order three and Q has order at least five. Writing X=V(P)∪{z} and Q=(q_0,...,q_m) with m>=4, X is a no-P4 transitive matching-height four-cell. For every x∈X, Q together with any tight trimer on X-{x} is a literal exact two-cover of H-x. Both X∪{q_0} and X∪{q_m} are P5-free, and for every x∈X the endpoint reverse stars (q_1,q_0,x) and (x,q_m,q_{m-1}) are tight. After relabelling X as M(ab)=M_R>M(ac)=M_S>M(bc)=M_L, this is exactly the common transitive hard frame used by R543,R554,R576. No dependency in this reduction uses R24.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; H-z has a literal exact two-cover P⊔Q with |P|<=3. Accepted R3,R4,R193,R518,R590.

## Scope
Noncircular normal-form reduction for reconstruction of legacy R24.

## Applicability
Separates the fully reconstructed front half of R24 from the remaining transitive high-badness discharge and supplies all standing hypotheses of R543/R554/R576.

## Persistence
The four deletion roots are graph-intrinsic exact-cover facts on one fixed physical X,Q frame. Endpoint reverse stars and endpoint P5-free conditions hold simultaneously; no selected-state borrowing between deletion-root representatives is asserted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close the surviving transitive frame. Does not use R533,R169,R5,R24, or order-ten closure. Does not assert existence of a root with exactly two bad seams.

## Proof
Let H-z=P sqcup Q be the displayed exact two-cover. If |P|=1, adjoining z to P gives a tight dimer; if |P|=2, V(P) union {z} has a tight trimer by R3. Either path together with Q spans H, so |P|=3.

If |Q|=1, adjoining z to Q gives a dimer and P is the other rail. If |Q|=2, V(Q) union {z} has a tight trimer and P is the other rail. If |Q|=3 or 4, then |V(H)| is respectively 7 or 8. Choose any six vertices. R193 supplies a tight P5 on five vertices of that six-set. The remaining vertices form respectively a dimer or a three-set; the latter has a tight trimer by R3. Thus H has a spanning two-cover. Therefore |Q|>=5; write Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4.

Put X=V(P) union {z}. A Hamilton P4 on X together with Q would two-cover H, so X is P4-free. For each x in X, R3 gives a tight trimer on X-{x}; with Q this is a two-cover of H-x. It is exact by R4, since a Hamilton H-x plus singleton x would two-cover H.

A Hamilton P5 on X+q_0 with Q[1,m], or on X+q_m with Q[0,m-1], would two-cover H, so both endpoint five-sets are P5-free. If (x,q_0,q_1) were tight, then (x,q_0,...,q_m) plus a trimer on X-{x} would two-cover H. R3 therefore gives (q_1,q_0,x) tight for every x. Dually (x,q_m,q_{m-1}) is tight for every x.

R518 classifies the P4-free X as transitive matching-height or cyclic 00. Accepted R590 says every fifth vertex Hamilton-extends the cyclic 00 cell. Taking q_0 would contradict the P5-freeness of X+q_0. Hence X is transitive. Relabel M(ab)=M_R>M(ac)=M_S>M(bc)=M_L. This is exactly the synchronized transitive frame used by R543,R554,R576, obtained without R24 or any theorem depending on it.
