# R175 — One-Rail Unsigned-End Refund

## Statement
Let (alpha;(p)) and (T;beta) be a balanced opposite-sign historical pair in a smallest Strong counterexample, where the singleton support is (p) and T=(t_0,t_1,...,t_m) is nontrivial, m>=1, with its displayed signed end at t_0. Suppose a current literal outside frame contains a nontrivial tight path U=(u_0,u_1,...) vertex-disjoint from (p) and T, so u_0u_1 is a selected boundary. Then before any fresh recompletion or unrelated marker reset there is a proper tight trimer C_U equal to either (t_0,u_0,u_1) or (u_1,u_0,t_0). Its vertex set is exactly {t_0,u_0,u_1}; deleting it from the historical pair leaves the singleton support unchanged and the signed suffix T^-=(t_1,...,t_m), so historical pair mass drops exactly by one. The original tail witness beta remains valid. The exact head/tail dual also holds.

## Hypotheses
Smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; an ancestry-bearing balanced opposite-sign pair with one singleton support (p) and one nontrivial tail-signed support T=(t_0,...,t_m); a current literal outside nontrivial path U=(u_0,u_1,...) disjoint from both historical supports, with selected state u_0u_1.

## Scope
Strong-safe one-turn refund in the singleton/nontrivial historical-pair layer. The construction is performed in the current literal outside frame before fresh recompletion. Head/tail duality gives the corresponding refund at the opposite unsigned end.

## Applicability
Use to peel a nontrivial historical support by exactly one vertex while keeping the singleton side fixed. This is the certificate-preserving final layer of the chosen singleton/nontrivial pair descent.

## Persistence
The singleton support (p) survives literally. The descendant support is the historical suffix T^- and retains beta as its original tail witness. The trimer orientation may be either displayed orientation and must be retained if later orientation-sensitive calculations need it. The outside selected boundary u_0u_1 is the physical provenance of the refund.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close the both-singleton floor. Does not make arbitrary pair mass globally monotone across unrelated marker resets. Does not automatically close richer growth, cycle, or bounded-contact exits if one deliberately takes those branches. The witness beta is graph-intrinsic but need not lie in every residual induced subsystem. No CW-specific inference is used.

## Proof
Let (alpha;(p)) and (T;beta) be the literal historical pair of R175, with T=(t_0,t_1,...,t_m), m>=1, and let U=(u_0,u_1,...) be the displayed outside path, so u_0u_1 is selected. Apply Boundary Antisymmetry R3 to the triple (t_0,u_0,u_1). Exactly one of t_0u_0u_1 and u_1u_0t_0 is tight. In either case the resulting tight trimer C_U has vertex set {t_0,u_0,u_1}; hence it meets the historical pair exactly at the unsigned head t_0 of T and is disjoint from the fixed singleton (p). Delete V(C_U) from the active historical-support bookkeeping. By Signed-Interval Rebirth R425, the surviving suffix T^-=(t_1,...,t_m) is still tail-signed by the same old witness beta, while (p) is untouched. Therefore the descendant historical pair is (alpha;(p)),(T^-;beta) and its mass is exactly one less. The head/tail dual is identical after reversing the displayed roles. No recompletion, voluntary truncation, or hidden marker reset is used.
