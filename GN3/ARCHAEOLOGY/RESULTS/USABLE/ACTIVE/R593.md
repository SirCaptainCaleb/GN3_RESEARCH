# R593 — Every fifth vertex Hamilton-extends the cyclic no-P4 four-cell

## Statement
Let X={a,b,c,z} be the exact 00 no-Hamilton-P4 four-cell of R516; after labelling, its tight-turn representatives are abc,bca,cab,zba,azb,baz,acz,cza,zac,zcb,bzc,cbz. Then every fifth vertex d outside X lies in a Hamilton tight P5 on X∪{d}, and d can be placed one step from an end: its position is 1 or 3 in a five-vertex order numbered 0,...,4. Consequently, in an order-three singleton-deletion frame, X cannot be the cyclic 00 cell, because such a Hamilton P5 with d=q_0 together with the untouched suffix Q-q_0 would be a spanning two-cover.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry R3; X is the exact 00 signature from R516; d is distinct from X. For the R24 consequence, Q=(q_0,...,q_m) is the disjoint long rail and m>=1.

## Scope
Universal cyclic-four-cell fifth-vertex extension with near-end placement, plus its fixed-rail singleton-deletion specialization.

## Applicability
Restores the historically cited cyclic-four-set fifth-vertex extension including the near-end placement coordinate; eliminates the cyclic branch in R24 reconstruction.

## Persistence
The Hamilton P5 is graph-intrinsic and retains the identity and near-end position class of d. In the R24 specialization it coexists with the untouched complementary Q suffix.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not guarantee both near-end positions for a fixed orientation and does not prescribe which one occurs. Does not treat transitive no-P4 cells. Does not use legacy R24.

## Proof
Write uvw when the displayed turn is tight. Every candidate below places d in position 1 or 3. Assume none is a tight P5. Each candidate has one X-only turn certified by the 00 row of R516, so its failure converts a tight antecedent mixed turn into the tight complete reversal of its other mixed turn by R3.

Starting with dab:
dab ⇒ adz (zdabc) ⇒ czd (adzcb) ⇒ bdz (aczdb) ⇒ dba (cabdz) ⇒ bdc (cdbaz) ⇒ dbz (azbdc) ⇒ bda (adbzc) ⇒ cad (bdacz) ⇒ zda (bcadz) ⇒ bad (zdabc).

Starting with bad:
bad ⇒ cda (zbadc) ⇒ dcz (bzcda) ⇒ cdb (bdcza) ⇒ abd (cdbaz) ⇒ zdb (cabdz) ⇒ cbd (zdbca) ⇒ adb (zcbda) ⇒ daz (czadb) ⇒ adc (cdazb) ⇒ dab (zbadc).

The turns dab and bad are complete reversals, so exactly one is tight by R3. Either choice forces the other, contradiction. Hence at least one of the sixteen candidate orders is a tight Hamilton P5, and inspection of the displayed candidate names shows d is always in position 1 or 3.

For the R24 specialization take d=q_0. The P5 on X∪{q_0} and the tight suffix (q_1,...,q_m) are disjoint and spanning, contradicting pc(H)>2.
