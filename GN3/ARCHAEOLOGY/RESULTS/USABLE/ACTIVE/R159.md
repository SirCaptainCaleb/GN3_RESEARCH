# R159 — Punctured Component-Drop Pair Theorem

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament and let W be a proper vertex subset. Let R be a literal c-cover of H[W] and T a literal r-cover of H[W]. If r<c, then H contains a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair.

## Hypotheses
W is a proper subset of V(H); R and T are literal path covers of exactly W; T has fewer path components than R.

## Scope
Arbitrary finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournaments; no smallest-counterexample, extremality, endpoint, or deleted-marker hypothesis is needed beyond W being proper.

## Applicability
Generic compiler from any path-cover component drop on a proper induced subsystem to balanced-pair existence. In particular it applies whenever a fragmented complement is recompleted with fewer components.

## Persistence
The resulting balanced opposite-sign pair is graph-intrinsic historical information; it is not asserted to be selected by T or R.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the pair is unique, selected, current, or immediately productive; does not claim global monotonicity of pair mass. The proper-subset/spare-vertex condition is essential to the archived proof.

## Proof
Let R be a literal c-cover and T a literal r-cover of the same proper induced subsystem H[W], with r<c. Suppose for contradiction that no selected adjacent state of T has endpoints in two distinct R-components. Then every T-component is contained wholly in the vertex set of a single R-component: along an ordered T-path, leaving one R-component for another would create a first adjacent state crossing between them. Because the T-components cover all of W, every one of the c nonempty R-components contains at least one T-component. Distinct R-components are vertex-disjoint, so these T-components are distinct. Hence r>=c, contradicting r<c. Therefore T selects a state xy whose endpoints lie in two distinct components of R. Since W is a proper subset of V(H), accepted fully reconstructible R176 applies to the literal covers R,T and this selected cross-state. It yields a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair in H. This is exactly the R159 conclusion, with no smallest-counterexample, extremality, endpoint, or deletion-marker hypothesis beyond W being proper.
