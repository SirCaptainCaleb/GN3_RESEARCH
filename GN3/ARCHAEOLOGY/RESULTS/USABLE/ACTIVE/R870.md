# R870 — Crossed excess recompletions reduce to three binary Q-cell splits outside P4 or Reverse-Ear geometry

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed beyond-ten inward-anchor five-cell frame R862 with W=H-{q_2,q_{m-2}}, source cells G_0=Q[0,1], G_1=Q[3,m-3], G_2=Q[m-1,m], G_3={a,c}, G_4={b,z}, and let T be any exact two-cover of W. Suppose both X cells G_3,G_4 are unfragmented, i.e. each occurs as one contiguous two-vertex T-block. Then at least one of the following holds. (P4) H contains a source-labelled tight Hamilton P4 obtained from an incorrectly placed contiguous X block by the R649/R518 polarity argument. (RE) Comparing a T-rail with the original Q by R435 yields an explicit reversed old Q-state, reverse trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle. (BINARY-SPLIT) Neither geometric output occurs. Then G_4 is the initial block of its T-rail, G_3 is the terminal block of its T-rail, and after deleting these possible endcaps each T-rail consists only of Q vertices encountered in strictly increasing Q-index order. Consequently, for each i in {0,1,2}, the vertices of G_i occur in at most one T-block on each rail. Hence G_i contributes either one source-cell block total, if all of G_i lies on one rail, or exactly two source-cell blocks, if G_i is split nontrivially between the two rails. Therefore sigma_G(T) is exactly the number of Q-cells G_i that are split between the two rails. In particular sigma_G(T) is in {0,1,2,3}; if sigma_G(T)>0, all excess is encoded by at most three binary split decisions and t_G(T)=3+sigma_G(T)<=6.

## Hypotheses
Accepted crossed R621/R649 frame; accepted inward-anchor five-cell frame R862; accepted R518 and Reverse Ear R435; T is any exact two-cover of W and both physical X dimers are contiguous T-blocks.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed beyond-ten sigma compression. It treats every sigma_G(T), not only sigma=0, under the explicit unfragmented-X hypothesis. No payment, capture, floor steering, or finite forest enumeration is used.

## Applicability
Use at the start of sigma>0 analysis. If an X block is wrongly placed, retain the labelled P4. If Q order reverses, retain the R435 output. Otherwise replace the arbitrary excess block word by a 3-bit record indicating which of G_0,G_1,G_2 are split across the two rails.

## Persistence
Retain T, the actual X-dimer orientations and rail-end roles, any R435 output, and in BINARY-SPLIT the three split bits together with the two increasing Q-subsequences carried by the T-rails.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not treat fragmentation of G_3 or G_4 into singleton blocks. Does not claim a split pattern closes H. Does not require the Q vertices assigned to one rail to be consecutive in Q, only increasing. Does not consume P4 or Reverse-Ear outputs.

## Proof
Let the two X dimers be contiguous. For G_3 in its actual T-order S=(s_0,s_1), if a T vertex y follows S then (s_0,s_1,y) is tight. Accepted R649 supplies fixed head polarity on either orientation of G_3 with at least three named witnesses; choose w distinct from y with (w,s_0,s_1) tight. The y-sign is opposite in polarity on the terminal dimer, so R518 gives a Hamilton P4. Hence outside P4, G_3 is terminal. Dually, if a vertex y precedes contiguous G_4, then (y,s_0,s_1) is a head sign opposite the fixed R649 tail polarity; choosing a distinct fixed tail witness and applying R518 gives a Hamilton P4. Hence outside P4, G_4 is initial. Now compare each T-rail K with the original tight path Q by R435. If Q contacts occur in reverse index order, retain the corresponding R435 reversed-state/reverse-trimer/cycle output. Otherwise Q vertices along K occur in strictly increasing Q-index order. Since any G_4 block can occur only at the beginning and any G_3 block only at the end, the portion of K between those endcaps contains only Q vertices. Fix a Q cell G_i, which is an interval in the Q index order. Once K leaves that interval for a higher-index Q cell, increasing order forbids returning to G_i; and there is no internal X block available to separate two occurrences of G_i. Thus all G_i vertices assigned to K form one maximal G_i block, though they need not be consecutive indices in Q. Therefore each G_i contributes at most one block on each of the two T-rails: one total block if it lies wholly on one rail, exactly two if both rails meet it. The two X cells contribute exactly one block each by hypothesis. Relative to the five reference cells, sigma_G(T) is the number of extra blocks, hence precisely the number of Q cells represented on both rails. This number lies in {0,1,2,3}, and R862 gives t_G(T)=3+sigma_G(T).
