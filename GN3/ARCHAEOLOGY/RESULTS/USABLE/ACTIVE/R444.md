# R444 — Bare Both-Singleton Degeneracy

## Statement
Let p and t be distinct vertices of any Level-(1) boundary tournament. Under the accepted graph-intrinsic signed-support convention, the singleton supports (p) and (t) admit a formal balanced opposite-sign labelling: (t;(p)) is a head sign on (p) with witness t, and ((t);p) is a tail sign on (t) with witness p. This uses only that every ordered two-vertex path is automatically tight because it has no internal turn. Consequently the bare both-singleton balanced-pair label carries no turn information by itself. Any productive use of a mass-two floor must retain additional physical ancestry/certificates; distinct historical certificate labels on the same singleton cannot be declared a collision solely from singleton sign labels.

## Hypotheses
p and t are distinct vertices. The standard signed-support convention allows the witness of one singleton support to lie on the opposite support, while remaining outside its own support.

## Scope
Level-(1)-universal negative/degeneracy theorem. No smallest-counterexample, cover, payment, or CW hypothesis. Historically proved by R17 and independently audited in the subsequent R18/R20 audit chain.

## Applicability
Use as a guardrail whenever a signed-support or cross-generation argument reaches width one. It prevents treating an anonymous both-singleton pair, or mere reminting of sign certificates on a singleton, as new geometric progress without retained ancestry.

## Persistence
The statement is graph-intrinsic and representation-independent. If a particular floor has stronger birth/payment/capture ancestry, that ancestry remains separately available and may support stronger consumers.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor is useless. Does not say every later event involving the same singleton is quiet. Does not rule out same-polarity singleton collision from distinct physical witnesses when an actual three-vertex turn is tested. It only says singleton sign labels alone impose no internal-turn geometry and do not justify a universal opposite-polarity same-singleton collision theorem.

## Proof
Take distinct p,t. The ordered path (t,p) has two vertices and therefore has no internal turn to check, so it is tight. Hence (t;(p)) is a valid head-signed singleton with witness t. The same two-vertex path is also exactly the certificate required for ((t);p), so (t) is tail-signed by witness p. The supports are disjoint and the witnesses lie outside their own supports, so these form a formal balanced opposite-sign pair. Since the proof used no triple of vertices, the bare pair certifies no turn. Thus any later theorem claiming productivity from a both-singleton label must consume additional ancestry rather than the labels alone.
