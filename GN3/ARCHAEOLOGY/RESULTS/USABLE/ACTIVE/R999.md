# R999 — Every directed edge of a minimum R888 counterexample is a literal dimer rail of a spanning three-cover

## Statement
Let G be a minimum-order counterexample to R888. For every ordered pair of distinct vertices (x,y), there exists a spanning increasing three-cover D=A|B|(x,y) in which x->y is literally a dimer rail. More generally this is the dimer specialization of R998. If F is any spanning increasing three-cover in which x is a terminal and y is a source of different components, then relative to any such D the directed matching edge x_out y_in is an isolated D-only component of M_F triangle M_D, with weight +1.

## Hypotheses
G is a minimum-order counterexample to R888; x,y are distinct. For the isolated-gain clause F is any spanning increasing three-cover with x terminal and y source on different components.

## Scope
Pure edge-ordered minimum-counterexample completeness lemma, now explicitly based on the R888-specific prescribed-path restoration parent R998. No full boundary-tournament minimality is used.

## Applicability
Use to introduce any chosen fresh complete-graph edge as a literal dimer rail, and to realize a desired terminal-to-source joining edge as an isolated +1 symmetric-difference component before collision analysis.

## Persistence
Retain x,y, the literal dimer cover D, the current cover F when present, and the isolated component {x_out y_in}.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the isolated gain is collision-free or an upper set. Does not by itself reduce the number of paths. R980 is not needed for existence of the isolated component.

## Proof
By R998 applied to the proper increasing path K=(x,y), the complement G-{x,y} has an exact two-cover A|B. Therefore D=A|B|(x,y) is a spanning increasing three-cover containing x->y literally as a dimer rail. Now let F be any spanning increasing three-cover in which x is a terminal of one component and y is the source of a different component. In the directed matching M_F, x_out is unmatched because x is terminal, and y_in is unmatched because y is a source. In M_D, the dimer contributes the selected edge x_out y_in. Since a bipartite matching has degree at most one at each copy, no other edge of M_D triangle M_F can share x_out or y_in with x_out y_in. Hence {x_out y_in} is by itself a connected symmetric-difference component. It contains one M_D edge and no M_F edge, so its weight relative to F->D is +1. No collision-poset theorem is needed for this component-level fact.
