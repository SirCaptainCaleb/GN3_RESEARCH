# R885 — Fragmented-X five-cell excess has a bounded separator budget

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed beyond-ten inward-anchor five-cell frame R862 with W=H-{q_2,q_{m-2}}, source cells G_0=Q[0,1], G_1=Q[3,m-3], G_2=Q[m-1,m], G_3={a,c}, G_4={b,z}, and let T be any exact two-cover of W. Let f(T) be the number of X cells among G_3,G_4 that are fragmented in T, meaning that their two vertices do not form one contiguous T-block; thus f(T) is in {0,1,2}. Let b_i(T) be the number of maximal nonempty T-blocks lying in G_i. Then at least one of the following holds. (P4) An unfragmented X cell is placed away from its forced path end and accepted R837 yields a source-labelled tight Hamilton P4. (RE) Comparing a T-rail with Q by accepted R435 yields an explicit reversed old Q-state, reverse trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle. (BOUNDED) Neither geometric output occurs. Then every unfragmented G_4 block is initial on its T-rail, every unfragmented G_3 block is terminal, and the Q vertices encountered on each T-rail occur in strictly increasing Q-index order. Moreover b_3+b_4=2+f(T) and b_0+b_1+b_2<=6+2f(T). Consequently sum_i b_i<=8+3f(T). Since R862 gives t_G(T)=3+sigma_G(T), equivalently sum_i b_i=5+sigma_G(T), one has sigma_G(T)<=3+3f(T) and t_G(T)<=6+3f(T). In particular the contracted five-cell block forest has at most 8 blocks when f=0, at most 11 when f=1, and at most 14 when f=2. If Y=G_0 union G_1 union G_2 and r_Y is the number of maximal Y-blocks of T, then r_Y<=2+2f(T). Writing p=e_T(X) and q=e_T(X,Y) as in accepted R535, one has r_Y=p+q-2, hence p+q<=4+2f(T), and c_x=r_Y+1-d_Y(x)<=3+2f(T) for every x in X. In the exactly-one-fragmented case f(T)=1 there is a sharp dichotomy: either some x in X has c_x<=3, or (p,q)=(2,4) and c_x=4 for all four x. Thus the missing R535 high-interleaving floor is completely localized on f=1: if c_x>=4 for every x, the unique coarse parameter type is (2,4). Fragmented-X excess is therefore an m-independent bounded separator problem, with the one-fragmented and two-fragmented regimes having sharply different finite budgets, while f=0 recovers the numerical bound of R870.

## Hypotheses
Accepted crossed R862 inward-anchor five-cell frame and one exact two-cover T of W. Use accepted R837 for endcap placement of any contiguous X dimer, accepted Reverse Ear R435 for Q-contact monotonicity outside its explicit outputs, and accepted R535 for the optional X/Y fragmentation corollary.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed R24/O6 five-cell compression for arbitrary sigma_G(T), including fragmented X dimers. No payment, capture, floor steering, R436, R5, R168, R169, R224, R533, R350, generic R358, finite m-enumeration, or computation.

## Applicability
Use at the start of the previously unnamed fragmented-X excess branch. Split first by f=1 versus f=2. For f=1, either retain a named marker x with c_x<=3, or enter the unique extremal coarse type (p,q)=(2,4) with all c_x=4; also sigma<=6, t_G<=9 and there are at most 11 cell blocks. For f=2, sigma<=9, t_G<=12 and there are at most 14 cell blocks. Do not assume c_x>=4: on f=1 this theorem turns its failure into an explicit low-fragment marker and its validity into one unique R535 parameter type.

## Persistence
Retain T, the five physical source cells, f(T), all cell-block counts b_i, any R837 P4 or R435 output, and in the bounded branch the increasing Q-contact order together with the X/Y counts p,q,r_Y and d_Y(x),c_x when used.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not classify or close the f=1 or f=2 block forests. Does not prove c_x>=4. Does not assert that the 13 R535 high-interleaving words apply without their stated hypothesis. In f=1 it proves only the low-marker versus unique extremal-parameter dichotomy, not closure of either side. Does not turn a labelled P4 or Reverse-Ear output into closure by itself. The block bounds are upper bounds and do not claim every pattern is realizable.

## Proof
Let f=f(T). First suppose an unfragmented G_3 or G_4 occurs away from the path end prescribed by accepted R837. Then R837 gives the stated labelled P4, so assume this does not happen. Thus every intact G_4 block is initial and every intact G_3 block is terminal. Apply accepted Reverse Ear R435 to each T-rail K against the original path Q. If some pair of consecutive Q-contacts on K occurs in reverse Q-order, retain the corresponding R435 reversed state, reverse trimer/contact, or cycle. Hence in the remaining branch all Q contacts on each T-rail occur in increasing Q-index order.

For i=0,1,2, G_i is a Q-index interval. On a fixed T-rail, after deleting the fragmented-X singleton vertices, the Q contacts belonging to G_i form at most one contiguous G_i block: a lower-index Q cell cannot occur between two G_i contacts and a higher-index Q cell cannot be followed by a return to G_i because Q-contact order is increasing. Thus, across two rails, there are at most six baseline Q-cell blocks. Exactly 2f X vertices belong to fragmented X cells, and each such singleton occurrence can split at most one existing Q-cell block into two. Intact X dimers are endcaps in the present no-P4 branch and split none. Therefore b_0+b_1+b_2<=6+2f.

Each X cell has two vertices. If it is intact it contributes one cell block; if fragmented its two vertices contribute two singleton cell blocks. Hence b_3+b_4=2+f, and B:=sum_{i=0}^4 b_i<=8+3f. Along two nonempty T-rails, the number t_G(T) of selected transitions between distinct source cells is exactly B-2. Accepted R862 gives t_G(T)=3+sigma_G(T), so B=5+sigma_G(T). Therefore sigma_G(T)<=3+3f and t_G(T)<=6+3f. The displayed bounds 8,11,14 on the contracted block forest are immediate for f=0,1,2.

Now put Y=G_0 union G_1 union G_2. In the same monotone/endcap branch, deleting X from each T-rail leaves at most one Y-run, except that each of the 2f fragmented-X singleton occurrences can separate one additional Y-run. Hence r_Y:=c(T[Y])<=2+2f. Let p=e_T(X) and q=e_T(X,Y). Since T is a spanning two-path forest on X union Y with |X|=4, it has |X|+|Y|-2 edges, of which p+q are incident with X and the rest lie in Y. Therefore r_Y=|Y|-e_T(Y)=p+q-2. Thus p+q<=4+2f. Accepted R535 says c_x=p+q-d_Y(x)-1=r_Y+1-d_Y(x), so c_x<=3+2f for every x.

Finally take f=1. The crossed frame has X P4-free, so accepted R535 gives p<=2. Also p+q<=6 as just proved. Hence the R535 sum identity gives sum_x c_x=4p+3q-4=3(p+q)+p-4<=16. If some c_x<4, integrality gives a named x with c_x<=3. Otherwise all four c_x>=4, so their sum is at least 16. Equality must then hold throughout: sum_x c_x=16, p+q=6 and p=2. Thus q=4, and equality in the four lower bounds forces c_x=4 for every x. This is exactly the claimed f=1 dichotomy.
