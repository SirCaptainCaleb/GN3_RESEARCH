# R978 — The order-seven trimer/trimer residue cannot occur in a minimum R888 counterexample

## Statement
Let G be a minimum-order counterexample to R888 in the trimer-fiber setup G=X disjoint-union B, where |X|=4, X is non-Hamiltonian, B is Hamiltonian, and B+x is non-Hamiltonian for every x in X. Then |B| is not 3. Indeed if |B|=3, for any distinct x,y in X the two non-Hamiltonian four-sets B union {x} and B union {y} share the triangle B, so accepted R963 gives an increasing Hamilton path on B union {x,y}; the remaining two vertices X-{x,y} form a dimer, yielding a spanning two-path cover of G.

## Hypotheses
Minimum-order R888 counterexample in the accepted trimer-fiber setup; |B|=3 for contradiction.

## Scope
Pure edge-ordered trimer-fiber closure of the order-seven residual left outside R969.

## Applicability
Use with R969 to eliminate its explicit |B|=3 exception; every surviving trimer fiber has |B|>=4 and therefore enters the R969 gate/one-hole reduction.

## Persistence
Retain X, B, two chosen x,y, the two P4-free four-sets B+x and B+y, the R963 Hamilton P5 on their union, and the complementary X-dimer.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close the |B|>=4 one-hole R969 branch.

## Proof
Assume |B|=3 and choose distinct x,y in X. By the trimer-fiber hypotheses, both induced four-sets B union {x} and B union {y} are non-Hamiltonian. They share exactly the triangle B. Accepted R963 therefore gives an increasing Hamilton path K on B union {x,y}. The remaining vertex set is X minus {x,y}, which has exactly two vertices and hence forms a vacuously increasing dimer path D. The paths K and D are vertex-disjoint and span G, contradicting that G is a counterexample to R888. Thus |B| cannot equal 3.
