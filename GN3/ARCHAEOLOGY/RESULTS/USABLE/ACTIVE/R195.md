# R195 — Four-of-six Hamilton-five density

## Statement
Every six-vertex subset E of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament has at least four vertices e such that E\\{e} supports a tight five-vertex path.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; E is any six-element vertex set.

## Scope
Order-free local path-density strengthening of the six-set P5 fact.

## Applicability
Use whenever a six-vertex exterior or residual cell appears. It supplies at least four distinct Hamilton five-subsets, hence at least four choices of omitted marker.

## Persistence
The four-or-more Hamilton five-subset supports are graph-intrinsic on E.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert all six five-subsets are Hamiltonian and does not prescribe which at most two omissions fail.

## Proof
Let M={e∈E : E\\{e} supports a tight five-path}. Fix any three-subset T⊂E and put P=E\\T. By R8, P admits a tight trimer orientation. The three vertices of T are distinct and exterior to P, so R146 implies that at least one of the three five-sets obtained by adjoining two vertices of T to P is Hamiltonian. Those three sets are exactly E\\{t} for t∈T. Hence M∩T≠∅ for every three-subset T⊂E. If |E\\M|≥3, choosing T⊂E\\M of size three contradicts this. Therefore |E\\M|≤2, so |M|≥4.
