# R585 — Globally longest support admits no one-vertex Hamilton extension

## Statement
Let H be a finite tight-path system and let K be a globally longest vertex-simple tight path in H. Write X=V(K) and E=V(H)\X. Then for every r in E, the induced subsystem H[X union {r}] is non-Hamiltonian. Equivalently, X admits no one-vertex Hamilton extension into the exterior of K.

Consequently X cannot carry a boundary-reversed Hamilton dimer in the sense of R561 whenever E is nonempty: R561 would Hamilton-extend X by every exterior vertex and produce a tight path of order |X|+1, contradicting global-longestness of K.

In particular, in any tau=1 exact two-cover cell whose Hamiltonian absorber X=H[D union S] coincides with the vertex set of a globally longest path of H, the R561/R578/R581 reversed-dimer branch is unavailable. Dual-absorber or second-order search in that cell cannot proceed by manufacturing an R561 dimer on X.

## Hypotheses
Finite tight-path system; K globally longest; R561 only for the second paragraph (Strong Level-(1) / exact-reversal). The first paragraph is pure length.

## Scope
Order-free. Applies to every globally longest path, independently of deletion size, cover size, or smallest-counterexample minimality.

## Applicability
Use as a negative fence before applying R561, R156, R578, or R581 to an absorber that contains a globally longest path. Forces longest-path tau=1 cells to seek a second Hamilton order or an opposite-side absorber rather than a reversed dimer on X.

## Persistence
The non-extension of X and the unavailability of R561 on X are graph-intrinsic as soon as some globally longest path spans X.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that X is the unique maximum-order support. Does not forbid Hamilton cycles on X. Does not produce a second Hamilton order at a named crossing endpoint. Does not itself eliminate tau=1.

## Proof
Let K be globally longest of order |X|. Suppose there exists r notin X such that H[X union {r}] has a tight Hamilton path P. Then P is a tight path on |X|+1 vertices, contradicting global-longestness of K. This is independent of which Hamilton path of X is used: every Hamilton path of X union {r} has order |X|+1.

Now suppose E is nonempty and X carries a boundary-reversed Hamilton dimer in the sense of R561. Then every exterior vertex, in particular some r in E, Hamilton-extends X. The preceding paragraph yields a tight path of order |X|+1, contradiction.

If a tau=1 cell has Hamiltonian absorber X equal to the vertex set of a globally longest path, the second paragraph applies and the R561 hypothesis of R578 and R581 cannot hold on that absorber.
