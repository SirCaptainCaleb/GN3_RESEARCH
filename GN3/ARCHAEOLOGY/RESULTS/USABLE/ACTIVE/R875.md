# R875 — At the crossed m=6 gap, every R583 packet reduces to reverse-middle P4, fixed-cover seam/high-block, signed-rectangle weave, or core seam

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed m=6 frame Q=(L,u,v,w,d,s,R), X={a,b,c,z}, with corrected inward signed rectangles from accepted R849. Apply accepted R583 at the unique internal gap w|d. Then one of the following source-visible outcomes occurs.

(RM) A reverse-middle two-witness packet occurs, and accepted R584 gives a labelled Hamilton P4.

(RT) A right-terminal packet occurs: distinct x,y in X satisfy (s,d,x),(s,d,y). Accepted R723 also gives (x,s,d),(y,s,d), so both P4 orders (x,s,d,y) and (y,s,d,x) are tight. For any exact two-cover T of H-{s,d}, both rails are necessarily nontrivial; hence accepted R843 gives either b_T(x,y)>2 or an R833 source-visible reverse seam.

(LT-AC) The left-terminal witness pair is {a,c}. Then D=(w,v) is head-signed by a,c from R583 and tail-signed by b,z from accepted R849. For any exact two-cover T of H-D, both rails are nontrivial and accepted R846 gives HIGH, SEAM, CLOSE, or WEAVE. In WEAVE, accepted R847 further gives either explicit R435 reverse/cycle geometry or, up to interchanging rails, exactly one of the two monotone Q-contact partitions {L,d,R}|{u,s} or {u,d,R}|{L,s}.

(LT-BZ) The left-terminal witness pair is {b,z}. Accepted R849 tail-signs D=(w,v) by b,z, while R583 head-signs it by b,z, so both P4 orders (b,w,v,z),(z,w,v,b) are tight. Every exact H-D two-cover therefore gives HIGH or an R843 reverse seam.

(LT-MIX) The left-terminal witness pair is {h,k}, with h in {a,c} and k in {b,z}. If h=a, accepted R839(A2) gives either the literal core seam (v,a,c) or the additional tail sign (w,v,a); in the latter branch, R849 tail-signs k and the two opposite endpoint orders (a,w,v,k),(k,w,v,a) are both tight, so R843 gives HIGH or a reverse seam. If h=c, accepted R839(A1) gives either core seam (v,c,a) or tail sign (w,v,c), with the identical R843 conclusion.

Thus the unique crossed m=6 R583 packet has no anonymous terminal residue: it is a labelled P4, fixed-cover HIGH/SEAM geometry, the rigid R846/R847 rectangle WEAVE, or one of the two literal core seams (v,a,c),(v,c,a).

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in accepted crossed R621 at m=6. Uses accepted R3,R4,R583,R584,R723,R839,R843,R846,R847,R849. No R5,R24,R825,R508,capture,payment,protected contact,or path-growth theorem.

## Scope
Finite crossed m=6 terminal-gap compression with corrected provenance and complete fixed-cover hypotheses. Replaces old R845 dependence on the brittle R825 seven/eight-shape route.

## Applicability
Use first on the unique m=6 R583 packet. Reverse-middle and bidirectional terminal packets go directly to R584/R843. A full {a,c} head by {b,z} tail rectangle goes through R846 then R847. Mixed packets use the exact R839 A1/A2 core-seam clause before R843. Preserve all fixed-cover endpoint/block data.

## Persistence
Retain packet type, witness identities, tested central dimer, and for any fixed-cover branch the chosen exact deletion cover, its nontrivial rails, block count, selected reverse seam or WEAVE endpoint roles/Q-contact partition. In LT-MIX retain the exact R839 literal realized.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim a labelled P4, HIGH block count, reverse seam, R435 cycle/reversal, surviving WEAVE partition, or core seam closes H. Does not identify a canonical exact deletion cover. Does not use R825 or any seven-shape terminology.

## Proof

Retain the crossed m=6 frame Q=(L,u,v,w,d,s,R), X={a,b,c,z}. We use the R583 obstruction array at the unique internal gap w|d. The proof is organized by the packet R583 produces.

[cover-nontriviality]
We first record a local cover fact. Let D be any tight physical dimer and let T=T_1 sqcup T_2 be an exact two-cover of H-D supplied by R4. The deletion system H-D is not Hamiltonian, since a Hamilton path of H-D together with D would two-cover H. Neither T rail is singleton: if T_1={r}, then on V(D) union {r}, R3 supplies a tight trimer; that trimer together with T_2 would span H by two paths. Thus every exact dimer-deletion cover used below has two nontrivial rails.

[reverse-middle]
If R583 yields two reverse-middle blockers across w|d, R584 applies directly and produces a labelled tight reverse P4. This is outcome RM.

[right-terminal]
Suppose R583 yields distinct x,y with (s,d,x),(s,d,y). R723 supplies (x,s,d),(y,s,d), hence both P4s (x,s,d,y) and (y,s,d,x) are tight on common middle dimer (s,d). Choose any exact H-{s,d} two-cover T. By cover-nontriviality both rails are nontrivial, so R843 gives either b_T(x,y)>2 or a source-visible reverse seam. This is RT.

[core-middle-six-clauses]
We now prove locally the six m=6 core-middle clauses formerly isolated as R839. Put N=(v,w,d). R817 gives four core P4 rails A_1=(u,L,a,c), A_2=(u,L,c,a), B_1=(b,z,R,s), B_2=(z,b,R,s), with every A_i disjoint from every B_k and A_i sqcup B_k an exact two-cover of the order-eight core. Hence N together with any chosen A_i,B_k partitions V(H).

Concatenate N with these core rails. Every internal turn remains certified and exactly the two seam turns at the concatenation are new. If both seam turns were tight, the concatenated rail plus the untouched complementary core rail would two-cover H; therefore at least one seam turn is bad and R3 makes its reversal tight.

1. Append A_i after N. The only new turns are (w,d,u),(d,u,L), so we obtain (u,d,w) OR (L,u,d). Call this E_A.
2. Prepend A_1 before N: (u,L,a,c,v,w,d). The new turns are (a,c,v),(c,v,w), giving (v,c,a) OR (w,v,c). Call this A_1.
3. Prepend A_2 before N: (u,L,c,a,v,w,d). The new turns are (c,a,v),(a,v,w), giving (v,a,c) OR (w,v,a). Call this A_2.
4. Append B_1 after N: (v,w,d,b,z,R,s). The new turns are (w,d,b),(d,b,z), giving (b,d,w) OR (z,b,d). Call this B_1.
5. Append B_2 after N: (v,w,d,z,b,R,s). The new turns are (w,d,z),(d,z,b), giving (z,d,w) OR (b,z,d). Call this B_2.
6. Prepend B_k before N. The final seam window is (...,R,s,v,w,d), with new turns (R,s,v),(s,v,w), giving (v,s,R) OR (w,v,s). Call this E_B.

The hole lists are complete because the supports are literal disjoint spanning partitions. These six clauses are simultaneous physical facts in the fixed m=6 frame.

[left-terminal]
Now suppose R583 gives distinct head witnesses x,y on D_L=(w,v): (x,w,v),(y,w,v).

If {x,y}={b,z}, R849 tail-signs the same D_L by b,z. Thus both (b,w,v,z) and (z,w,v,b) are tight P4s. Any exact H-D_L cover has nontrivial rails, so R843 gives HIGH or a reverse seam. This is LT-BZ.

If {x,y}={a,c}, R849 tail-signs D_L by b,z, giving the full head/tail 2x2 signed rectangle. For any exact H-D_L cover T, cover-nontriviality applies and R846 yields HIGH, SEAM, CLOSE, or WEAVE. The quiet WEAVE branch is normalized below.

If {x,y} is mixed, write {h,k} with h in {a,c}, k in {b,z}. If h=a, local clause A_2 above says (v,a,c) OR (w,v,a). In the first case retain the literal core seam (v,a,c). In the second, a is also a tail witness on D_L. R849 already tail-signs k, while both a,k are head witnesses from R583, so both (a,w,v,k) and (k,w,v,a) are tight; R843 gives HIGH or reverse seam. If h=c, use local clause A_1: (v,c,a) OR (w,v,c), and the same argument gives either core seam (v,c,a) or a bidirectional same-dimer P4 pair followed by R843. This is LT-MIX.

[weave-normalization]
It remains to justify the precise quiet WEAVE alternative formerly isolated as R847. Let T=T_1 sqcup T_2 be a WEAVE cover of H-{w,v} from R846. Thus its two rail starts are exactly a,c, its two rail ends exactly b,z, it selects no X-X state, and each rail has the form h -> E_h -> t with a nonempty exterior block E_h drawn from E={L,u,d,s,R}; the two E-blocks partition E.

Compare each T rail with the old path Q using R435. If either comparison produces an old-state reversal, reverse trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle, retain that explicit R435 output. Otherwise the Q contacts along each T rail occur in increasing Q-index order.

Under this monotonicity there are three forbidden same-rail patterns. For every start h in {a,c}, R594 gives (u,L,h), so R3 makes (h,L,u) bad; hence L and u cannot lie on the same weave rail. For every end t in {b,z}, R594 gives (t,R,s), so (s,R,t) is bad; hence s and R cannot lie on one rail. R723 gives (t,s,d), so (d,s,t) is bad; hence d and s cannot lie together either. Each rail has a nonempty E-block and the blocks partition five vertices. A 1+4 split is impossible: whichever vertex is isolated, the four-block contains at least one forbidden pair L,u or s,R (and in the relevant cases d,s). Therefore the sizes are 2 and 3. Among d,s,R, two must share a rail; d,s and s,R are forbidden, so the shared pair is exactly {d,R}, while s is on the other rail. Finally L and u must split. Thus, up to interchanging rails, the only possible contact partitions are {L,d,R}|{u,s} and {u,d,R}|{L,s}, each in increasing Q order. This proves the full quiet WEAVE normalization.

[conclusion]
The R583 packet possibilities are exhausted. Reverse-middle gives a labelled P4 via R584. Right-terminal and LT-BZ give fixed-cover HIGH/SEAM via R843. LT-AC gives R846 HIGH/SEAM/CLOSE, or WEAVE followed by either explicit R435 geometry or one of the two monotone contact partitions just proved. LT-MIX gives either one of the literal core seams (v,a,c),(v,c,a) or HIGH/SEAM via R843. This is exactly R875.
