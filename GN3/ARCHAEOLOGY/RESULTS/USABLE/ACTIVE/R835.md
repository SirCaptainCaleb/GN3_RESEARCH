# R835 — The two inward stars of the R574 middle do not force Hamiltonicity at the first live order

## Statement
There exists a five-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament F on {a,c,v,w,d} such that N=(v,w,d) is tight and the four inward-star turns (w,v,a),(w,v,c),(a,d,w),(c,d,w) are all tight, but F has no tight Hamilton P5. Thus the local middle data retained by accepted R574 at m=6, namely Q[2,4]=(v,w,d) together with the two left witnesses a,c on the reverse dimer (w,v) and the two right witnesses a,c on the reverse dimer (d,w), do not by themselves force the complementary middle support {a,c,v,w,d} to be Hamiltonian. This remains true even with the stronger property that, for every fixed middle vertex y, the induced tournament on the other four vertices defined by x ->_y z iff (x,y,z) is tight is transitive. One explicit certificate is given by the following five local orders, declaring (x,y,z) tight exactly when x precedes z in the order attached to middle y: middle a: c>d>v>w; middle c: a>w>d>v; middle v: w>d>a>c; middle w: v>c>d>a; middle d: v>a>c>w.

## Hypotheses
Pure five-vertex Strong Level-(1) exact-reversal setting. The displayed local-order construction completely defines the ternary orientation.

## Scope
Finite local strategy fence for the aligned/exactly-two-bad R574 middle at the first surviving length m=6. It does not claim the full R574/R556 frame extends to this five-vertex certificate.

## Applicability
Use to reject any proof step that tries to close the m>=6 R574 residue solely from the facts that Q[2,m-2] is tight and a,c sign the first and last inward reverse dimers. Additional global shell, deletion-cover, gate-fan, or recompletion information is genuinely required.

## Persistence
Retain the explicit five local transitive orders and the five required R574 turns as a regression certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not construct a full order-eleven R24 counterexample or even extend to the entire X union Q frame. Does not show the aligned branch survives globally. It only disproves the local Hamilton-middle implication from the R574 retained middle data. No asymptotic claim is made.

## Proof
For each middle y, orient every reversal pair (x,y,z)/(z,y,x) by the displayed transitive order at y. This defines a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. The required turns are immediate: at middle w the order v>c>d>a gives (v,w,d); at middle v the order w>d>a>c gives (w,v,a),(w,v,c); at middle d the order v>a>c>w gives (a,d,w),(c,d,w). Direct inspection from the five local orders gives exactly the following tight P4s: (a,c,w,d), (a,c,d,w), (a,d,c,v), (c,a,d,w), (c,d,w,a), (v,w,c,d), (v,d,a,w), (v,d,w,a), (w,v,d,a), (w,v,d,c), (d,a,v,c), (d,v,a,w). Their respective missing vertices are v,v,w,v,v,a,c,c,c,a,w,c. Appending that missing vertex would require respectively the turns (w,d,v),(d,w,v),(c,v,w),(d,w,v),(w,a,v),(c,d,a),(a,w,c),(w,a,c),(d,a,c),(d,c,a),(v,c,w),(a,w,c), and each of these is bad by the displayed middle orders. Every Hamilton P5 would have a tight first four-vertex prefix and then append the unique missing vertex, so no Hamilton P5 exists.
