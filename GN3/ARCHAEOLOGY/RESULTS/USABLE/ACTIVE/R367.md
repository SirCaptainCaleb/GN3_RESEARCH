# R367 — P6-free Strong systems have asymptotically unit Hamilton-five density

## Statement
Let W be an r-vertex subset, r>=6, of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Then either W supports a tight P6 or the family B of five-subsets of W that do not support a tight P5 satisfies |B|/binom(r,5) <= 1/sqrt(r-4). Equivalently, every r-set satisfies the dichotomy: tight P6 exists, or Hamilton-five support density is at least 1-1/sqrt(r-4). Combining with the unconditional Johnson ceiling R326, in the no-P6 branch the bad density is at most min(r/[5(r-4)],1/sqrt(r-4)); the square-root bound is strictly stronger for r>20 and equal at r=20.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); W is any r-set, r>=6.

## Scope
Order-free global growth-or-density corollary.

## Applicability
Use when a proof can exploit either a P6 or extremely high P5 support density. The theorem is especially strong on large sets where P6 is excluded for some independent reason.

## Persistence
The path-support family is graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not infer P6 from high density alone; the no-P6 branch may have density 1 at small order as in R335.

## Proof
If W has a P6 we are in the first branch. Otherwise every physical three-set P supports a tight trimer by R8 and R366 gives at most (r-3)sqrt(r-4)/2 bad pairs outside P. Summing over all binom(r,3) triples counts each bad five-set exactly 10 times, so 10|B|<=binom(r,3)(r-3)sqrt(r-4)/2. Divide by binom(r,5) to obtain bad density <=1/sqrt(r-4). Compare with R326 as stated.
