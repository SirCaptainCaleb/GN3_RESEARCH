# R699 — The two crossed middle-gate deletions force mate geometry or an explicit opposite-sign X-anchored pair

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the accepted crossed R621/R649 frame with X={a,b,c,z} and physical Q endpoints L=q_0,R=q_m. Choose arbitrarily one exact two-cover T_ac of H-{a,c} and one exact two-cover T_bz of H-{b,z}. Then at least one of the following holds. (MATE) In T_ac or T_bz, one of the two X-complementary R649 packets lies in the R696 END branch, and therefore supplies a literal spanning two-path proposal with one or two holes, together with the corresponding forced reverse turn or physical two-hole mate clause. (PAIR) Otherwise there exist e in {b,z} and f in {a,c} such that an R649 packet on {a,c} has an R696 FREE crossing {e,x} in T_ac avoiding both L,R, while an R649 packet on {b,z} has an R696 FREE crossing {f,y} in T_bz avoiding both L,R. Use L as the exact witness on the head-signed {a,c}-side packet and R as the exact witness on the tail-signed {b,z}-side packet. Same-frame R527/R526 actualization gives a graph-intrinsic tail-signed dimer P_e containing e and a graph-intrinsic head-signed dimer P_f containing f. More precisely V(P_e) is either {e,x} or {e,L}, and V(P_f) is either {f,y} or {f,R}. These two opposite-polarity supports cannot meet in both vertices and cannot contain each other's X anchor. They are therefore either disjoint or meet in exactly one hinge. In the disjoint case accepted R39 gives a direct mass-four balanced pair and, in the hypothetical-smallest-counterexample setting, a finite continuation to a spanning two-cover or an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor. In the hinged case accepted R547 collapses them directly to the two-singleton floor on {e,f}. Thus the pair of physical crossed middle-gate deletions cannot have only anonymous R542 payment residue: before payment it exposes either a concrete mate proposal or an explicit opposite-sign dimer pair rooted at one X vertex from each complementary middle gate.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the accepted crossed R621/R649 frame. T_ac and T_bz are arbitrary exact two-covers of H-{a,c} and H-{b,z}. Use pending R698, accepted R527/R526 actualization, accepted R39, and accepted R547.

## Scope
Noncircular crossed-frame synthesis of the two physical middle-gate deletion covers. No five-cell classification, Reverse Ear, R5, R168, R169, R224, R533, generic R358, R436, or R450 is used. The result is source-local: its pair branch retains the two exact deletion covers, the two selected crossings, the chosen opposite endpoint witnesses L and R, and the two X anchors e,f.

## Applicability
Take any exact cover after deleting {a,c} and any exact cover after deleting {b,z}. Run the paired-orientation R698 test in each. If an END branch appears, keep its one/two-hole proposal. If neither deletion has END, choose the guaranteed FREE packet on each side, actualize the {a,c}-side with witness L and the {b,z}-side with witness R, and obtain opposite-signed X-anchored dimers that are automatically disjoint or singly hinged.

## Persistence

## Proof
Fix arbitrary exact covers T_ac of H-{a,c} and T_bz of H-{b,z}. Pending R698, which couples the two R649 orientations on a common deletion cover, says that T_ac has a non-sandwich X-complementary packet at some e in {b,z}, and T_bz has a non-sandwich X-complementary packet at some f in {a,c}; each such packet is END or FREE. If either is END, R696 supplies exactly the MATE conclusion, so assume both are FREE. Let D_e={e,x} be the FREE selected state in T_ac. By definition x is outside the R649 carrier and x notin {L,R}; because T_ac omits a,c and f belongs to {a,c}, also x!=f. Accepted R649 proves that whichever tested orientation of {a,c} produced e is head-signed by both L and R. Apply R527 to S_e with exact witness L and selected crossing D_e, orienting the marker to designate e. The R527 proof invokes R526. Since the marker has order two and the source is head-signed, the resulting opposite support is a tail-signed dimer P_e: if the terminal marker test is tight it is the marker dimer on {e,x}, while if the test is bad it is the short support on {e,L}. Thus V(P_e) is {e,x} or {e,L}. Similarly let D_f={f,y} be the FREE selected state in T_bz. Here y avoids L,R, and because T_bz omits b,z while e belongs to {b,z}, y!=e. R649 proves that whichever tested orientation of {b,z} produced f is tail-signed by both L and R. Apply R527 with exact witness R, capturing f. The head/tail-dual R526 actualization yields a head-signed dimer P_f whose support is either {f,y} or {f,R}. Hence P_e and P_f have opposite polarity and distinct anchors e!=f. The second vertex of P_e is x or L, neither equal to f. The second vertex of P_f is y or R, neither equal to e. Moreover x,y avoid L,R and L!=R. Therefore the only possible intersection between V(P_e) and V(P_f) is the equality x=y in the marker-marker outcome; otherwise the supports are disjoint. If disjoint, their total support size is four and R39 gives a direct balanced pair, with the accepted smallest-counterexample continuation to closure or an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor. If x=y and both actualizations use their marker supports, the dimers meet in exactly the single hinge x and R547 yields opposite-sign singleton supports on the nonhinge vertices e and f, i.e. the floor {e,f}. This proves PAIR and the theorem.
