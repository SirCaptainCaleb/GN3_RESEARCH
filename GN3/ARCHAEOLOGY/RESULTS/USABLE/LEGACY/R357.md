# R357 — Johnson density equality has macroscopic dual-clique deficit

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Assume equality in the global R326/R331 Hamilton-five bad-family ceiling on an r-vertex set W, and evaluate the R325 defect quantities V,D for k=5,t=2 on the same bad family B. Then V=0 but D is exactly the number of six-subsets containing no bad five-set, namely
D = [2(r-10)/(5(r-4))] * binom(r,6).
Equivalently the occupied six-set fraction is 3r/[5(r-4)] and the empty six-set fraction is 2(r-10)/[5(r-4)]. Thus R326 equality is not a near-equality regime for the weaker R322/R325 dual-clique inequality: after the parity fence R355 the first arithmetically admissible order is r=20, where already one quarter of all six-sets are empty, and the empty fraction tends to 2/5 as r grows. The R331 equality structure is 'uniform four-cores plus perfectly paired occupied six-sets', not 'nearly every six-set maximally filled'. Any use of R325 extremal stability in the R326 equality branch must respect this distinction.

## Hypotheses
Equality holds in R331; V,D are the R325 defects for the bad five-set family with k=5,t=2.

## Scope
Extremality-separation theorem for the quadratic dual-clique and linear Johnson bounds.

## Applicability
Use to prevent transferring R325 near-zero-defect conclusions into R326/R331 equality or near-equality without quantitative justification.

## Persistence
Four-core occupancies and six-set occupancies are graph-intrinsic support-family data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not refute R325 or R326. It shows their equality notions are different. Does not say R326 near-equality always has exactly this deficit; the formula is for exact R331 equality.

## Proof
R331 gives x_S=r/5 for every four-set, hence V=0. It also gives y_U∈{0,2}; therefore the R325 summand 1-binom(y_U,2) equals 1 exactly on empty six-sets and 0 on occupied ones, so D is the number of empty six-sets. If b=|B| and O is the number of occupied six-sets, then b(r-5)=sum_U y_U=2O. Using b=[r/(5(r-4))]binom(r,5) and binom(r,6)=binom(r,5)(r-5)/6 gives O/binom(r,6)=3r/[5(r-4)]. Subtract from one to obtain D/binom(r,6)=2(r-10)/[5(r-4)].
