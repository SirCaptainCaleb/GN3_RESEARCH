# R407 — Bidirectional same-witness dimer interaction compiler

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample of order at least six. Let D={a,b} be a physical dimer such that both tested orientations (a,b) and (b,a) carry signed certificates of the same polarity with one common witness u outside D. Then H carries an accepted fixed-trimer historical signed-support/anchor or cross-generation pair interaction at the same physical anchor u. Choose two fresh vertices r,s outside {a,b,u} and orient the unique tight u-middle trimer T on {r,u,s}. Its two terminal signed dimers have opposite polarities and common anchor u. Choose the terminal mate M of polarity opposite to the two D-incarnations. R39 forms two direct balanced-pair births, pairing M separately with (a,b) and (b,a). After either birth is historical first, R160 forces the other to be nonquiet before unrelated reset, yielding the interaction at u.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample with at least six vertices; D={a,b}; both tested orientations of D have one common polarity and common witness u; accepted R3,R39,R41,R160.

## Scope
Order-free local signed-support compiler; cap geometry, payment ancestry and deletion frames are unnecessary.

## Applicability
Use whenever bidirectional same-witness dimer saturation appears, including R400/R401 2+1 cap residues and R171-like orientation forks when the common-witness hypothesis is met.

## Persistence
D, its two tested orientations, common witness u, chosen u-middle trimer and fixed-anchor interaction are graph-intrinsic/historical. Pair payments are alternative descendants.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close the resulting interaction and does not apply when the two dimer orientations have different polarities or different witnesses.

## Proof
Choose r,s distinct from a,b,u. Boundary antisymmetry gives a tight u-middle trimer T. R41 gives its two terminal signed dimers opposite polarities with common anchor u. Select the terminal dimer M opposite to the common polarity of the two signed orientations of D. Since M is disjoint from D, R39 gives a balanced pair for each orientation. These are two pair births through the same fixed trimer T. By R160, after one is historical the other cannot be a quiet independent epoch and forces the accepted historical interaction at u.
