## Proof
Put L=q_0,u=q_1,v=q_2,w=q_3 and x_L=z. R596 normalizes the left high-side cell so the fully outgoing M_S edge is ac and its complementary M_S edge is bz. Accepted R536 first gives the two q_0-starting Hamilton P4s (L,a,c,b) and (L,c,a,z). Prepending u using the common endpoint reverse star uLx gives the tight P5s A_z=(u,L,a,c,b) and A_b=(u,L,c,a,z). A_z sqcup Q[2,m] is exactly R598. The supports of A_b and Q[2,m] partition H-b, so they form a literal two-cover of H-b. It is exact: if H-b were Hamiltonian, that Hamilton path together with singleton b would two-cover H, contradicting R4. This proves the second enlarged root. The inward-star clause of accepted R536, applied to the complementary M_S edge bz, gives (w,v,b) and (w,v,z) tight. It remains to prove the matching pair at (L,u). Suppose (b,u,L) were tight. Then (b,u,L,c,a,z) is a tight P6: besides the assumed first turn, its turns are uLc from the endpoint reverse star, Lca from full outgoing polarity of ac, and caz from the transitive matching-height rule M(ca)=M_S>M(az)=M_L. Together with the untouched tight suffix Q[2,m] this is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Hence buL is bad and R3 gives Lub tight. Similarly, if zuL were tight then (z,u,L,a,c,b) would be a tight P6, using uLa, Lac, and acb; together with Q[2,m] it would two-cover H. Therefore zuL is bad and R3 gives Luz tight. Thus b and z simultaneously tail-sign both (L,u) and (w,v), as claimed.
