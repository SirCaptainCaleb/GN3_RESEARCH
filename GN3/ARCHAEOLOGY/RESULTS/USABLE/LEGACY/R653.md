# R653 — Cross-end three-to-two component drop makes both deleted endpoints universal preserved floor coordinates

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the accepted transitive order-three R24 frame with V(H)=X disjoint union V(Q), X={a,b,c,z}, Q=(q_0,...,q_m), m>=4, and put L=q_0, R=q_m. Then each of the two deleted endpoints L and R is independently a universal preserved coordinate for ancestry-bearing mass-two floors, using only the fixed cross-end pair-deletion residue. More precisely, let W=H-{L,R}. The three disjoint tight paths Q^o=Q[1,m-1], D_1=(a,c), D_2=(b,z) form a literal three-cover of W. Let T be any exact two-cover of W. Since T has fewer components, the proof of R159 supplies a selected T-state xy crossing two components of this three-cover. Apply the fully reconstructed R176 proof to that same crossing. Because V(H)-W={L,R}, the arbitrary spare vertex d may be chosen as L or, in a separate alternative birth, as R. For either choice e in {L,R}, R176 produces a balanced opposite-sign pair with one signed support literally the singleton (e) and the opposite support of order at most two. Hence, unless H is already two-covered, accepted fixed-singleton descent R428 preserves (e) and reaches an ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. From that floor, for every prescribed vertex x!=e, accepted R432 preserves e while replacing only the other singleton, yielding either a spanning two-cover or a floor exactly {e,x}. Thus L and R are separately universal floor coordinates. The L-pinned and R-pinned births may use the same physical selected cross state in T but are alternative historical continuations, not simultaneous active pairs.

## Proof
Put W=H-{L,R}. Since m>=4, Q^o=Q[1,m-1] is a nonempty tight path. The physical dimers D_1=(a,c) and D_2=(b,z) are tight paths vacuously. Their supports are pairwise disjoint and together cover W, so R_0=Q^o sqcup D_1 sqcup D_2 is a literal three-component cover of W. Accepted Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429 supplies an exact two-cover T=T_1 sqcup T_2 of W.\n\nApply the component-drop argument of accepted R159 to R_0 and T. Because T has two components while R_0 has three, some selected adjacent state xy of T joins two distinct R_0 components: otherwise every T-component would lie wholly inside one R_0 component, forcing at least three T-components. Retain such xy.\n\nNow use the fully reconstructed R176 proof directly. Its spare vertex d may be chosen arbitrarily in V(H)-W. Here V(H)-W={L,R}. Fix e=L first and choose d=L. If the R_0-component containing x is nontrivial, R176 chooses an old neighbor p of x and uses R3 with witness y to sign a dimer on {x,p}; the automatic tight dimer through d=L and y gives the opposite singleton sign on (L). If that R_0-component is a singleton, R176 signs singleton (x) directly from xy and the same automatic L-y path gives the opposite singleton (L). Thus in all cases the birth has one signed support literally (L), while the other support has order one or two.\n\nIf the other support is already singleton, we are at an ancestry-bearing floor containing L. If it is a dimer, apply accepted R428 with L as the fixed singleton coordinate. Every nonclosing step preserves (L) and strictly pays the opposite support until both supports are singleton. Therefore closure occurs or a floor {L,v} is reached. For any prescribed target x!=L, accepted R432 may then be run with L as the already-shared target coordinate; its one-coordinate replacement macro preserves L and yields closure or the exact floor {L,x}.\n\nRepeat the identical construction from the same mathematical base residue and cross state xy but choose the R176 spare d=R. This gives an alternative birth with literal singleton (R), followed by R428 and R432 to closure or {R,x} for arbitrary x!=R. No simultaneous-currentness of the two births is asserted. This proves universal preservation for both endpoints.
