# R426 — Pair-Complement Sandwich and Fixed-Witness Descent to Width Two

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let (alpha;P),(T;beta) be a historical balanced opposite-sign pair. Local sandwich: when |P|,|T|>=3 and U is any nonempty tight path disjoint from both supports, testing the at-most-four new seam turns in the literal concatenation (P,U,T) gives exactly either (i) (P,U,T) tight, or (ii) an explicit reverse tight trimer meeting only unsigned support-end vertices and a descendant balanced pair with the same signed endpoints and witnesses alpha,beta and mass drop 1 or 2. Global fixed-witness descent: cover the outside reservoir H-(V(P) union V(T)) by at most two paths U sqcup V and apply the local sandwich to a nonempty component U. Hence before any unrelated marker reset one gets a spanning two-cover or a strict descendant balanced pair. Iterating while both supports have order at least three preserves the two original signed endpoints/witnesses and an ordered reverse-shell certificate stack, and terminates at a spanning two-cover or min{|P|,|T|}<=2.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong counterexample for the global/iterated statement. The pair supports P,T are vertex-disjoint tight paths carrying opposite graph-intrinsic signs (alpha;P) and (T;beta). The local sandwich part requires |P|,|T|>=3 and a nonempty tight path U disjoint from both supports.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), arbitrary order. The local sandwich itself is purely physical and does not use minimality; minimality is used only to obtain an at-most-two-path cover of the outside reservoir for the global descent.

## Applicability
Primary non-small balanced-pair payment engine. It converts complement-cover seams into literal reverse-trimer refund certificates and gives a chosen finite continuation to width at most two while retaining fixed witnesses and the reverse-shell ancestry ledger.

## Persistence
During the non-small phase the active head support is a prefix of its historical support, the active tail support a suffix, the original signed endpoints and witnesses alpha,beta remain fixed, and every paid deletion is recorded by an explicit reverse trimer in an ordered shell stack. The markers need not be simultaneously selected later.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Not a global scalar monotone under arbitrary proof operations; the chosen continuation is meaningful only with its certificate stack. Does not close support order one or two. Does not claim old refund markers stay current. Does not treat tight cycles as contradictions.

## Proof
Historical R12 certificate. Write P=(p_0,...,p_r), T=(t_0,...,t_s). For a singleton U=(u), the only new turns are p_{r-1}p_ru, p_rut_0, and ut_0t_1. A failed turn reverses to a tight trimer clipping respectively two vertices from P, one vertex from each support, or two vertices from T; Signed-Interval Rebirth gives the corresponding descendant pair, and the singleton-U drops are two units. For nontrivial U=(u_0,...,u_k), the only new seams are p_{r-1}p_ru_0, p_ru_0u_1, u_{k-1}u_kt_0, u_kt_0t_1. Failure gives respectively reverse trimers (u_0,p_r,p_{r-1}), (u_1,u_0,p_r), (t_0,u_k,u_{k-1}), or (t_1,t_0,u_k), and drops pair mass by 2,1,1,2. Because |P|,|T|>=3, these contacts do not swallow a support, and the surviving prefix/suffix retains alpha,beta exactly. If all seams are good, (P,U,T) is tight. In the smallest-counterexample setting take O=H-(P union T)=U sqcup V from minimality. If the sandwich succeeds, (P,U,T) sqcup V is a spanning two-cover; otherwise the reverse trimer pays the pair. Iteration strictly reduces mass and accumulates the physical shell certificates until one support has order at most two.
