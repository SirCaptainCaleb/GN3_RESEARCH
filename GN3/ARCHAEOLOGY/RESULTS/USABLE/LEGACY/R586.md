# R586 — Longest-path terminal-dimer puncture is a canonical tau>=1 Hamiltonian-absorber frame

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K=(k_0,...,k_m), m>=2, be a proper globally longest tight path, with exterior E=V(H)\V(K). Fix the left terminal dimer D={k_0,k_1}, and write S=V(K)\D={k_2,...,k_m} and C=E. Assume S is nonempty, i.e. m>=2. Then X=H[D union S]=H[V(K)] is Hamiltonian via K.

By R5, pc(H-D)=2. By R21 and R508, every exact two-cover T of H-D selects at least one S|C transition: the surviving carrier K-D is a nonempty absorbable block. Thus every such T has tau>=1.

If T is a tau=1 exact two-cover of H-D, the R560 unique-transition normal form and the R570 rigidity alternative apply: D union C = {k_0,k_1} union E is non-Hamiltonian, and outside an R566/R435 output there is at most one Hamilton path of X ending (CS) or starting (SC) at the unique S-endpoint x of the selected crossing. Moreover R197 tail-signs the reverse start dimer (k_1,k_0) by every vertex of E, and head-signs the reverse end dimer (k_m,k_{m-1}) by every vertex of E. By R585 the absorber X is R561-free.

The right terminal dimer D'={k_{m-1},k_m} yields the exact dual frame. Thus every globally longest path supplies two canonical common-deletion Hamiltonian-absorber frames in which tau=1 is the remaining low-transition target and reversed-dimer extension is forbidden.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper globally longest tight path of order at least three; D is a terminal physical dimer of K.

## Scope
Order-free smallest-counterexample parent. No R24 rail, transitive four-cell, or eight-vertex hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use as the concrete longest-path instance of the R570 tau=1 cell requested by current guidance. Subsequent work should attack this frame by a second Hamilton order of V(K) at x, or by forcing the opposite absorber {k_0,k_1} union E to be Hamiltonian (which R570 says would raise tau to at least 2).

## Persistence
Retains the globally longest path K, the chosen terminal dimer D, the partition (S,C), and any chosen exact two-cover T of H-D together with its unique crossing when tau=1.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that a tau=1 cover exists. Does not select which S-vertex is the crossing endpoint. Does not itself produce a second Hamilton order or close the cell. Does not apply R561 to X.

## Proof
Let K=(k_0,...,k_m), m>=2, be globally longest and D={k_0,k_1}. Then S={k_2,...,k_m} is nonempty and C=E is nonempty because K is proper. The path K is a tight Hamilton path of X=D union S. Pair-Deletion Rigidity R429 gives pc(H-D)=2, so an exact two-cover T of H-D exists, with both rails nontrivial. The path K-D=(k_2,...,k_m) spans S, hence Q=K is a tight path on D union S. R508 therefore forces every exact two-cover of H-D to select a state with one end in S and the other in C. Equivalently R21 applied to the puncture of K at D says every exact two-cover of H-D crosses from the nonempty surviving carrier to the exterior, at least pc(K-D)=1 time. Thus tau(T)>=1. Now suppose tau(T)=1. R560 supplies the unique-transition normal form: one mixed rail carries all of S together with one C-block, and the other rail is a pure C-path; in particular b_C(T)=2. R570 applies to the Hamiltonian absorber X and this T: H[D union C] is non-Hamiltonian, and outside an R566 two-witness collision or an R435 reverse-order output there is at most one Hamilton path of X in the crossing-compatible boundary role at the unique S-endpoint x. R197 applied to K says that every e in E tail-signs (k_1,k_0) and every e in E head-signs (k_m,k_{m-1}). These certificates live on the terminal dimers of K, not necessarily on the selected crossing of T. R585 applied to K says X admits no one-vertex Hamilton extension into E and therefore carries no R561 reversed Hamilton dimer. The R578/R581 branch is closed in this frame. The right-end dimer {k_{m-1},k_m} is the exact dual.
