# R607 — Minimum R24 cell forces the short-trimer cross-end bridge pattern

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the exact-two-bad transitive R24 cell of R556 with X={a,b,c,z}, L=q_0, R=q_m and m>=2. Then, in addition to the accepted R589 gate/cross-fan turns, the following eight turns are forced: aLc, zcL, baL, Rcb, Raz, aRc, RcL, RaL. Together with R591, the three vertices of the original short trimer therefore have the rigid cross-end middle pattern RaL, LbR, RcL. Equivalently a and c point from R to L while b points from L to R; the z-middle bridge is the only L/R middle orientation not determined here.

## Hypotheses
Accepted exact-two-bad R24 frame R556; X+L and X+R are P5-free; X union {L,R} is P6-free, since a Hamilton P6 there together with the nonempty Q^o=(q_1,...,q_{m-1}) would be a spanning two-cover; accepted R589 and R591; boundary antisymmetry R3.

## Exclusions / nonclaims

## Proof
All turns below except the displayed hole are already certified by R556/R589 and the matching-height cell X. Because X+L and X+R are P5-free, each listed P5 candidate must fail at its unique uncertified turn. (1) (b,c,L,a,z): bcL and Laz are tight, so cLa is bad and aLc is tight. (2) (b,L,c,z,a): bLc and cza are tight, so Lcz is bad and zcL is tight. (3) (z,L,a,b,c): zLa and abc are tight, so Lab is bad and baL is tight. (4) (a,b,c,R,z): abc and cRz are tight, so bcR is bad and Rcb is tight. (5) (c,z,a,R,b): cza and aRb are tight, so zaR is bad and Raz is tight. (6) (c,R,a,b,z): Rab and abz are tight, so cRa is bad and aRc is tight. Now X union {L,R} is P6-free: otherwise its Hamilton P6 together with Q^o=(q_1,...,q_{m-1}) two-covers H. Candidate (b,a,L,c,R,z) has baL,aLc,cRz tight, so LcR is bad and RcL is tight. Candidate (z,L,a,R,c,b) has zLa,aRc,Rcb tight, so LaR is bad and RaL is tight. Finally R591 supplies LbR. This proves the claimed backbone with no computation.
