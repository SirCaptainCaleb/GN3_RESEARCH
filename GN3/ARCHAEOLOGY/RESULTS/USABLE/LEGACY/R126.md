# R126 — Disjoint four-path plus trimer forces a five-path

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let Q=(a,b,c,d) be a tight four-vertex path in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, and let {x,y,z} be disjoint from Q and support a tight trimer. Then the seven-vertex set V(Q)∪{x,y,z} contains a tight path of order five.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; Q is a tight four-path; the disjoint three-set supports a tight trimer.

## Scope
Pure local path-amplification lemma. No order-ten minimality, deletion exactness, payment, or support-family assumptions are used.

## Applicability
Use whenever a displayed tight P4 has a disjoint tight trimer. In particular, any exact 4+3 cover supplies the hypotheses.

## Persistence
All asserted paths and forced turns are graph-intrinsic on the fixed seven vertices.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert a Hamilton path on all seven vertices, does not prescribe which five vertices support the resulting path, and does not claim an analogous P4 plus arbitrary three-set statement without first orienting that three-set as a tight trimer.

## Proof
Write Q=(a,b,c,d), so abc and bcd are tight. Orient the disjoint trimer as (x,y,z), so xyz is tight, and suppose for contradiction that V(Q)∪{x,y,z} contains no tight five-path.\n\nFirst obtain a universal three-turn packet for every w∈{x,y,z}. The candidate (w,a,b,c,d) cannot be a five-path, while abc and bcd are tight, so wab is bad and hence baw is tight by boundary antisymmetry. Likewise (a,b,c,d,w) cannot be a five-path, so cdw is bad and hence wdc is tight. Now (b,a,w,d,c) cannot be a five-path; since baw and wdc are tight, awd is bad and hence dwa is tight. Therefore, for every w∈{x,y,z},\n\n    baw,  dwa,  wdc\n\nare tight.\n\nIt remains only to use the local orientation type on {x,y,z}. Boundary antisymmetry chooses exactly one order from each reversal pair, so the eight possible local patterns form two isomorphism types under relabeling of x,y,z. Representatives are:\n\n(C) cyclic: xyz, yzx, zxy are tight;\n(T) transitive: xyz, xzy, yxz are tight.\n\nCase (C). Since baz and zxy are tight, the candidate (b,a,z,x,y) forces azx bad, hence xza tight. Since yzx and xdc are tight, (y,z,x,d,c) forces zxd bad, hence dxz tight. Since dxz and xza are tight, (d,x,z,a,y) forces zay bad, hence yaz tight. Since dya and yaz are tight, (x,d,y,a,z) forces xdy bad, hence ydx tight. But then\n\n    (y,d,x,z,a)\n\nis a tight five-path, because ydx, dxz, and xza are tight, contradiction.\n\nCase (T). Since bax and xzy are tight, (b,a,x,z,y) forces axz bad, hence zxa tight. Since yxz and zdc are tight, (y,x,z,d,c) forces xzd bad, hence dzx tight. Since dzx and zxa are tight, (d,z,x,a,y) forces xay bad, hence yax tight. Since dzx and zxa are tight, (y,d,z,x,a) forces ydz bad, hence zdy tight. But then\n\n    (z,d,y,a,x)\n\nis a tight five-path, because zdy, dya, and yax are tight, contradiction.\n\nThus a disjoint tight four-path and tight trimer always force a tight five-path.
