# R657 — Sibling extreme-edge capture fibers force dual root-anchor interactions

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain branch (B) of accepted R633. Then there are two explicit source-localized R436 anchor interactions, one at z and one at b, obtained before generic pair payment. At z, use the C_ba and C_bc short-carrier packets. In an exact H-{a,b} two-cover, R508 forces a selected crossing incident with the complementary singleton z; choose one of the C_ba witnesses L,c avoiding that crossing and use R527/R526 with marker endpoint z designated as the new signed anchor. Since C_ba is tail polarity, this gives a nontrivial head-signed dimer anchored at z. Independently, in H-{b,c}, the C_bc packet and witnesses R,a give by the same construction a nontrivial tail-signed dimer anchored at z. These two graph-intrinsic sibling-fiber supports both contain the historical anchor z and are nontrivial, so R436 applied to either one as the older signed support and the other as the later tight path has no stationary-singleton exception and yields strict tight-path growth, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a three-vertex reverse contact/reversal at z. Dually, C_za in H-{z,a} yields a tail-signed dimer anchored at b and C_zc in H-{z,c} yields a head-signed dimer anchored at b, forcing the analogous R436 event at b. Thus the saturated branch has two physically localized, non-replay root-anchor interactions tied to different sibling pair-deletion fibers.

## Proof
Assume branch (B) of R633. We give the z argument; the b argument is dual.

The packet C_ba says S_1=(b,a) is tail-signed by two distinct witnesses L,c. As checked directly from the matching-height frame, K_1=(a,b,z) is tight and S_1 is its reverse initial boundary dimer, with complementary carrier singleton z. Pair-delete D_1={a,b}. Accepted R508 applied to K_1 says every exact two-cover T_1 of H-D_1 selects a state on physical support {z,y_1} crossing the singleton {z} to the exterior of K_1. Since L,c are distinct, at least one of them, call it w_1, is not y_1 and hence avoids the selected crossing. Use accepted R527 with this witness and the two-vertex marker {z,y_1}; choose the marker order so that z is the actualization endpoint. Because S_1 is tail-signed, the exact tail/head dual of R526 produces a head-signed opposite support P_z with signed head anchor z. The marker has order two. In the good-test branch P_z is that marker dimer; in the bad-test branch P_z is the two-vertex support consisting of z and the chosen sign witness. Hence in either branch P_z is a nontrivial graph-intrinsic tight dimer anchored at z.

Now C_bc says S_2=(b,c) is head-signed by witnesses R,a. The tight carrier K_2=(z,c,b) has S_2 as its reverse terminal boundary dimer and complementary singleton z. Pair-delete D_2={b,c}. R508 forces a selected crossing {z,y_2}. Choose w_2 in {R,a} avoiding y_2 and apply R527/R526 with z designated as the marker endpoint. Since S_2 is head-signed, the opposite support T_z is tail-signed, nontrivial, and has signed tail anchor exactly z. Thus P_z and T_z are two graph-intrinsic nontrivial tight dimers from sibling deletion fibers, both containing z, with the complete C_ba and C_bc source ancestries retained.

Apply accepted R436 to the signed support P_z and the proper tight path T_z. The latter contains the signed anchor z of P_z. The only stationary exception in R436 is exact replay of the same singleton support with no new certificate data. Here both paths have order two, and moreover T_z comes from the distinct sibling deletion fiber and opposite source collision packet. Therefore the at-anchor branch is nonstationary and R436 yields strict growth of one of the tight paths, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a three-vertex reverse contact/reversal at z.

For b, C_za gives the head-signed dimer (z,a) with witnesses R,c and tight carrier K_3=(b,a,z); R508/R527/R526 in H-{z,a}, designating complementary singleton b, yields a nontrivial tail-signed dimer anchored at b. C_zc gives tail-signed (z,c) with witnesses L,a and tight carrier K_4=(c,z,b); the same construction in H-{z,c} yields a nontrivial head-signed dimer anchored at b. R436 on these two sibling-fiber supports gives the stated nonstationary event at b. This proves the corollary.
