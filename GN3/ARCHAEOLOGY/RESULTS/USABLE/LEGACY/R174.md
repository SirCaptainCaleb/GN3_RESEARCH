# R174 — Multi-Support Adjacent-Color Refund

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let S_1,...,S_r, r>=2, be pairwise vertex-disjoint nontrivial graph-intrinsic signed tight supports of arbitrary sign polarities. Suppose one tight path A contains the union of all V(S_i). Then there exist distinct i,j and a contiguous tight interval C of A such that C meets S_i in exactly one vertex, meets S_j in exactly one vertex, and meets every other S_k in no vertex. After Signed-Interval Rebirth on the two touched supports, total support mass sum |S_i| decreases by at least two. In particular any two disjoint nontrivial signed supports lying in one tight carrier admit an order-free two-support refund, independent of their relative sign polarities or historical order.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); S_i are pairwise disjoint, nontrivial graph-intrinsic signed tight supports; A is one tight carrier containing all support vertices; Signed-Interval Rebirth / Tight-Path Cut Memory is available for deleting a proper interval meeting a signed support partially.

## Scope
Order-free, arbitrary sign polarities, arbitrary number r>=2 of disjoint nontrivial signed supports. No smallest-counterexample assumption is needed for the adjacent-color combinatorics itself beyond whatever scope is required by the imported signed-support rebirth theorem.

## Applicability
Use whenever several historical signed supports have all been swallowed into one tight carrier. It pays support mass immediately without first aligning historical support order, running Reverse Ear, or classifying anchor order.

## Persistence
Untouched supports persist unchanged. Each touched support retains a nonempty signed descendant because the chosen interval meets it in exactly one vertex and the support is nontrivial. Tight-Path Cut Memory may shift the active witness to the appropriate surviving historical boundary; witness names are not fixed forever.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Requires every touched support to be nontrivial; do not apply the two-support refund when one support is singleton. Does not require opposite signs or a balanced pair. Does not itself close the support-mass floor. Does not make total pair/support mass globally monotone across unrelated new births or representative resets. Does not erase richer Reverse-Ear/contact ancestry when such geometry is independently useful.

## Proof
Let A be the tight carrier containing the pairwise disjoint nontrivial signed supports S_1,...,S_r, r>=2. Traverse A in its path order and retain only those vertices lying in the union of the support sets. Label each retained vertex by the unique support index i for which it belongs to S_i. Since every support is nonempty and r>=2, this finite color word uses at least two colors, hence contains two consecutive retained positions of different colors, say x in S_i followed by y in S_j with i!=j. Let C be the contiguous interval of A from x through y. By consecutivity in the retained support-encounter sequence, the interior of C contains no support vertex at all. Therefore C meets S_i exactly in {x}, meets S_j exactly in {y}, and misses every other S_k. Because S_i and S_j are nontrivial, each has at least one vertex outside C, so neither is swallowed. Apply fully reconstructible Signed-Interval Rebirth/Tight-Path Cut Memory R425 to each of the two signed supports after deleting C. Each touched support has a nonempty signed descendant obtained from a surviving old-path interval, with size at most |S_i|-1 and |S_j|-1 respectively; witness names may migrate to the appropriate cut neighbors exactly as R425 allows. Every untouched S_k persists unchanged. Hence the total support mass decreases by at least two. This proves the multi-support theorem. The r=2 statement is the same argument with only two colors and does not use relative sign polarity or historical support order.
