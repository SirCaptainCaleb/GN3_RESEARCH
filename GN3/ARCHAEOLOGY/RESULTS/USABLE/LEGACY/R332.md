# R332 — Boundary antisymmetry permits unit Hamilton-five density

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
For every finite vertex set V with a fixed total order <, define a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament by declaring an ordered triple (a,b,c) tight exactly when a<c. Then complete reversal (c,b,a) is tight exactly when c<a, so boundary antisymmetry holds. Moreover every nonempty vertex subset W admits a tight Hamilton path by listing its vertices increasingly. In particular every five-subset supports a tight P5, so Hamilton-five support density can equal 1 in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Therefore no upper obstruction to very high P5 density follows from boundary antisymmetry alone; any theorem forcing near-unit P5 density in a smallest counterexample must use global minimality/extremality or exact-cover structure.

## Hypotheses
Finite vertex set with a chosen total order.

## Scope
Order-free explicit Strong Level-(1) construction; strategic density fence, not a smallest-counterexample model.

## Applicability
Use when assessing whether very high Hamilton-five density is locally possible. It is: density one occurs. The research problem is forcing such density under smallest-counterexample hypotheses.

## Persistence
The construction and Hamiltonicity statement are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not produce a smallest counterexample and does not imply a smallest counterexample can have density one.

## Proof
Fix the chosen total order < on V and declare (a,b,c) tight iff a<c. For any three distinct a,b,c, exactly one of a<c and c<a holds. Hence exactly one of (a,b,c) and its complete reversal (c,b,a) is tight, so the declared turn system satisfies Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry. Now let W be any nonempty subset and list its vertices increasingly as w_1<...<w_k. For every consecutive triple w_i,w_{i+1},w_{i+2}, the first entry is smaller than the third, so (w_i,w_{i+1},w_{i+2}) is tight. Thus the increasing listing is a tight Hamilton path of W. In particular each five-subset has a tight P5, so the Hamilton-five support density is exactly 1. Therefore boundary antisymmetry alone cannot impose any upper obstruction below density one. No prior theorem is used; the proof is a direct verification from the displayed construction.
