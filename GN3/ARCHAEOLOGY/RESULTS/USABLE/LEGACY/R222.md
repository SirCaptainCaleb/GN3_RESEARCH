# R222 — Universal anchored-capacity reduction to contact or tiny swallow corner

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Every hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample H admits the following order-free reduction. Either a spanning two-cover occurs, or there is an anchored-capacity balanced-pair realization with reservoir of size at least two whose fixed capacity reverse dimer yields the R180 strict same-polarity two-witness extension/collision output or bounded reverse trimer/contact, or a one-residual capacity lineage terminates in bounded support swallow in one of exactly the small size corners: swallowed support order two with surviving third literal rail order three, or swallowed support order three with surviving third literal rail order two or three. Thus, modulo immediate closure, the arbitrary-order problem reduces to capacity-specific bounded contact/extension geometry or the finite 2+3 / 3+(2-or-3) swallow corner; no anonymous pair floor, exterior-slack case split, or indefinite pair-mass descent remains.

## Proof
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. We reconstruct the R222 reduction using only the modern clean pair machinery and the noncircular synchronized short-rail normalization.

INITIAL BALANCED PAIR. Fix any two distinct vertices p,x. Accepted R654 gives a finite certificate-retaining continuation with one of two outcomes: either H already has a spanning two-cover, in which case R222 is finished, or there is an ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced opposite-sign floor whose singleton supports are exactly {p,x}. Retain one such pair. This replaces the historical use of R198 entirely.

ANCHORED CAPACITY. Apply accepted R179 to the retained balanced pair and choose an anchored-capacity realization (alpha;P),(T;beta), maximizing |P|+|T| for the fixed signed anchors and witnesses. Let

  X = V(H) \ (V(P) union V(T) union {alpha,beta})

be its ordinary reservoir. Apply accepted R180.

If X is empty, R180 gives a spanning two-cover.

If |X|>=2, R180 gives exactly the capacity-specific branch stated in R222: the fixed capacity reverse dimer carries two same-polarity reservoir witnesses in one tested orientation, hence the strict two-witness extension/collision or bounded reverse-trimer/contact output of the accepted same-support interaction. This is the non-one-residual branch of R222.

It remains to treat |X|=1.

ONE-RESIDUAL LITERAL ENTRANCE. Accepted R217 converts this anchored-capacity one-residual realization into a literal spanning three-cover

  H = J_0 sqcup P_0 sqcup T_0

in the scope of R171, where P_0,T_0 are the historical opposite-signed supports and

  1 <= |J_0| <= 3.                                         (1)

Follow the actual literal peel mechanism of the fully reconstructible preferred proof P597 of R171. At each application, a good branch closes H by a spanning two-cover. A bad seam is reversed by R3 to a tight contact trimer. If that trimer contains an entire historical support, bounded support swallow occurs. Otherwise Signed-Interval Rebirth inside R171 leaves nonempty opposite-signed surviving intervals and strictly lowers the integer historical pair mass M, with no fresh completion or representative reset. The third rail remains a surviving literal interval of the previous third rail. Thus consecutive strict-descents stay inside one literal no-recompletion lineage.

Strict descent cannot continue indefinitely. Both historical supports remain nonempty, so M is a positive integer. More sharply, in any nonclosing literal frame M>=4: if the union of the two support vertex sets had order at most three, those vertices would support one Hamilton tight path, and together with the third rail would two-cover H. Therefore after finitely many strict descents there is a first non-descent R171 output.

If it is a spanning two-cover, stop. Assume it is bounded support swallow and write the terminal literal frame as

  H = J sqcup S sqcup T,

where S is the completely swallowed historical support and T is the untouched opposite-signed support. P597 interval persistence and (1) give

  1 <= |J| <= 3.                                           (2)

The R171 swallow clause itself gives

  1 <= |S| <= 3.                                           (3)

The set Y=V(S) union V(J) is exactly the physical complement of T. If |Y|<=3, then Y supports one Hamilton tight path: singleton/dimer trivially, and an order-three tight path by R3. That path together with T would be a spanning two-cover. Hence every nonclosing terminal swallow satisfies

  |S|+|J| >= 4.                                            (4)

MASS-FOUR EXTINCTION. Assume equality in (4). Because the R171 lineage retains graph-intrinsic sign ancestry, the untouched support T retains an exact sign witness beta outside V(T). Since H=J sqcup S sqcup T, beta lies in

  Y=V(S) union V(J),   |Y|=4.                              (5)

We construct an exact singleton-deletion cover H-z=Q sqcup T with |Q|=3 and V(Q) union {z}=Y.

If (|S|,|J|)=(1,3), let S={z} and Q=J.

If (|S|,|J|)=(2,2), write S={z,w}; by R3 the three-set V(J) union {w} has a tight trimer order Q.

If (|S|,|J|)=(3,1), write S={z,w_1,w_2}; by R3 the three-set V(J) union {w_1,w_2} has a tight trimer order Q.

In every case Q sqcup T is a literal two-cover of H-z. It is exact, since a Hamilton path on H-z together with singleton z would two-cover H.

Apply accepted R594 to this exact singleton-deletion row, keeping the literal terminal order T=(t_0,...,t_m) as the long rail. Its small opposite-rail cases close; therefore in the surviving counterexample normalization m>=4 and for every y in Y,

  (t_1,t_0,y) tight,       (y,t_m,t_{m-1}) tight.           (6)

Take y=beta from (5). If T is head-signed by beta, its retained sign gives (beta,t_0,t_1) tight, whose complete reversal (t_1,t_0,beta) is tight by (6), contradicting R3. If T is tail-signed by beta, its retained sign gives (t_{m-1},t_m,beta) tight, whose complete reversal (beta,t_m,t_{m-1}) is tight by (6), again contradicting R3. Hence |S|+|J| cannot equal four.

By (2),(3),(4) and the mass-four exclusion, the only possible nonclosing terminal swallow profiles are

  (|S|,|J|)=(2,3),(3,2),(3,3).

These are exactly the tiny swallow corners stated in R222. Combining them with the earlier R180 reservoir>=2 branch proves every alternative in R222. No R24, R5, R168, R169, R198, or R214 is used.
