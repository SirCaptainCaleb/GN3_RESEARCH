# R331 — Hamilton-five density equality forces a paired four-design normal form

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let W be an r-vertex subset, r>=6, of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, and let B be the family of five-subsets of W that do not support a tight P5. Then |B|/binom(r,5)<=r/[5(r-4)]. If equality holds, necessarily 5 divides r, every four-subset S of W lies in exactly r/5 members of B, and every six-subset U that contains at least one member of B contains exactly two members of B. Equivalently the extremal bad family is a 4-(r,5,r/5) design at the core-incidence level and is locally paired inside every occupied six-set. Hence failure of either core regularity or six-set pairing gives a strict density improvement below the R326 ceiling.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament; W any r-set; R195 gives at most two bad five-subsets in every six-set; R326 Johnson local-clique degree density principle and its equality conditions.

## Scope
Order-free structural equality corollary for Hamilton-five support density. Converts the scalar 4/5-asymptotic ceiling into a concrete design-like extremal normal form.

## Applicability
Use when a support-counting argument approaches the R326 ceiling. Instead of squeezing constants further, attack the forced four-core regularity or the exact two-bad-per-occupied-six-set pairing.

## Persistence
The bad-support family and its four-core/six-set incidence pattern are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert such extremal designs exist or are realizable by Strong Level-(1) systems. Does not classify near-equality families quantitatively. Does not imply P6.

## Proof
For the bad Hamilton-five family B, R195 supplies local six-set occupancy at most two. Equality in the R326 bad-density ceiling is therefore exactly the t=2 equality setting of R375 with k=5. Hence 5 divides r, every four-set lies in exactly r/5 bad five-sets, and every occupied six-set contains exactly two bad five-sets. This is precisely the paired four-design normal form asserted by R331.
