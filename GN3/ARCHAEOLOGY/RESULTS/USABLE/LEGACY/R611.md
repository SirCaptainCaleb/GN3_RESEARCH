# R611 — Exact adjacent-state reversal at the rotated return forces short-carrier payment

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the surviving exact-two-bad R24 cell with m>=6. In the right endpoint reversal residue of R605, an exact H-{L,R} cover has one rail beginning (b,q_{m-1},q_{m-2},...). Then R542 necessarily applies to one of the two orientations of the physical dimer {q_{m-1},q_{m-2}}. Put r_1=q_{m-1}, r_2=q_{m-2}, r_3=q_{m-3}. The current rail certifies b r_1 r_2 tight, while Q certifies r_3 r_2 r_1 tight. For each x in {a,c}, R3 chooses exactly one of x r_2 r_1 and r_1 r_2 x. If x r_2 r_1 is tight for at least one x, then it and r_3 r_2 r_1 give a same-head two-witness packet on tested dimer (r_2,r_1), which is a reverse boundary dimer of the tight carrier (b,r_1,r_2); the witnesses x,r_3 lie outside that carrier, so R542 applies. If neither x r_2 r_1 is tight, then r_1 r_2 a and r_1 r_2 c are both tight, giving a same-tail two-witness packet on tested dimer (r_1,r_2), which is a reverse boundary dimer of the tight carrier (r_3,r_2,r_1); witnesses a,c lie outside it, so R542 applies. The left reversal residue, where a rail ends (...,q_2,q_1,b), is the exact dual. Consequently the exact-state-reversal branch in the rotated endpoint normal form is always payable and is not a terminal R24 obstruction.

Surviving exact-two-bad R24 cell, m>=6; exact reversal residue from the explicit endpoint insertion analysis; R3 and R542.

## Proof
Right side. Let r_1=q_{m-1},r_2=q_{m-2},r_3=q_{m-3}. The endpoint-reversal cover selects b r_1 r_2, so K_+=(b,r_1,r_2) is tight. The old Q turn K_-=(r_3,r_2,r_1) is tight. For x=a,c, boundary antisymmetry on the reversal pair x r_2 r_1 / r_1 r_2 x gives exactly one tight turn. If x r_2 r_1 is tight for some x, then x r_2 r_1 and r_3 r_2 r_1 are both head certificates on the identical tested dimer D=(r_2,r_1), with distinct witnesses x,r_3. The dimer D is the reverse terminal dimer (c,b) of K_+=(b,r_1,r_2), and both witnesses are outside K_+, so R542 applies. Otherwise x r_2 r_1 is bad for both x=a,c, hence R3 makes r_1 r_2 a and r_1 r_2 c tight. These are two tail certificates on D'=(r_1,r_2) with witnesses a,c. The dimer D' is the reverse initial dimer (b,a) of K_-=(r_3,r_2,r_1), and a,c lie outside K_-, so R542 applies. The left side is obtained by reversing the notation: current turn q_2 q_1 b and old Q turn q_1 q_2 q_3 play the roles of K_+,K_-, and the same a,c dichotomy pays either (q_1,q_2) or (q_2,q_1).
