# R770 — Internal-block puncture forces one-step path extension or a reverse endpoint contact

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2, and let J=(j_0,j_1,...,j_r) be a proper vertex-simple tight path with r>=2 and nonempty exterior E=V(H)\\V(J). Put D={j_1,...,j_{r-1}} and S={j_0,j_r}. Suppose T is a literal exact two-cover of H-D. Since J is a tight path on exactly D union S, accepted R508 forces T to select a physical state with one endpoint in S and the other in E. Let h in E be the exterior endpoint. If the touched endpoint is j_0, boundary antisymmetry on {h,j_0,j_1} gives exactly one of (h,j_0,j_1) and (j_1,j_0,h) tight. In the first case (h,j_0,j_1,...,j_r) is a literal tight path extending J by one vertex at its head; in the second case (j_1,j_0,h) is a reverse head-contact trimer. If the touched endpoint is j_r, boundary antisymmetry on {j_{r-1},j_r,h} gives exactly one of (j_{r-1},j_r,h) and (h,j_r,j_{r-1}) tight. In the first case (j_0,...,j_r,h) extends J by one vertex at its tail; in the second case (h,j_r,j_{r-1}) is the dual reverse tail-contact trimer. Thus every supplied exact internal-block-deletion two-cover of a proper tight path yields either a one-vertex extension of that path or a named reverse endpoint contact. In a hypothetical smallest counterexample, D itself is a proper graph-intrinsic tight path (a singleton when r=2, otherwise the contiguous internal subpath), so accepted R4 supplies such an exact two-cover and the extension-or-reversal alternative is unconditional for every proper tight path.

## Hypotheses
Finite Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament H with pc(H)>2; J=(j_0,...,j_r) is a proper tight path with r>=2 and nonempty exterior; T is a supplied exact two-cover of H-{j_1,...,j_{r-1}}. For the unconditional smallest-counterexample corollary, use accepted R4.

## Scope
Order-free and path-length-free recompletion parent theorem. The local implication uses only R508 and R3 once the internal-block deletion cover is supplied. Minimality is used only through R4 to supply that cover. No longest-path assumption, R5,R168,R169,R224,R533,generic R358,R436,R312,Reverse Ear,or computation.

## Applicability
Use on any proper role-labelled tight path before invoking path-length-specific puncture lemmas. Repeated use gives a finite growth-or-reverse-contact process: successful branches strictly enlarge the physical path by one vertex; failure branches expose a graph-intrinsic reverse contact at a terminal physical dimer. R769 is the P5/internal-trimer specialization and should be read through this parent.

## Persistence
Retain J, its internal block D, endpoint pair S, exact cover T, selected endpoint-to-exterior crossing, touched endpoint, exterior vertex h, and resulting extended path or reverse endpoint-contact trimer. Selectedness is T-relative; the tight extension/contact is graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert which endpoint is touched, that both endpoints have crossings in one cover, that extension can continue indefinitely without a reverse-contact branch, or that a reverse endpoint contact alone closes H. Does not imply all exterior vertices reverse-contact a non-longest path; compare R199 for the globally longest universal shield.

## Proof
Let D={j_1,...,j_{r-1}}, S={j_0,j_r}, and E=V(H)\\V(J). Because r>=2, D is nonempty; because J is proper, E is nonempty. The path J is vertex-simple and tight and has vertex set exactly D union S. Hence accepted R508 applies to every exact two-cover T of H-D and forces a selected state crossing S|E. Let h be its E-endpoint. If the S-endpoint is j_0, apply R3 to {h,j_0,j_1}. Exactly one of (h,j_0,j_1) and (j_1,j_0,h) is tight. The former concatenates with all old turns of J to give the one-vertex head extension (h,j_0,j_1,...,j_r); the latter is the named reverse head contact. If the S-endpoint is j_r, apply R3 to {j_{r-1},j_r,h}. Exactly one of (j_{r-1},j_r,h) and (h,j_r,j_{r-1}) is tight. The former yields the one-vertex tail extension (j_0,...,j_r,h); the latter is the reverse tail contact. These cases exhaust the R508 crossing. In a hypothetical smallest counterexample, D is itself a proper graph-intrinsic tight path: for r=2 it is the singleton (j_1), and for r>=3 it is the contiguous tight subpath (j_1,...,j_{r-1}). Accepted R4 therefore gives an exact two-cover of H-D, making the alternative unconditional.
