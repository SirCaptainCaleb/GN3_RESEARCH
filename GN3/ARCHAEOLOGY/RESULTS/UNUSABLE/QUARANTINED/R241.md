# R241 — Five-exterior dense column forces widespread exact-frame carrier capture

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the non-Hamiltonian five-exterior equality face. Let K=(k_1,...,k_m), m>=6, have exterior E. Then there is a hole z in E and a successful set S subseteq V(K), |S|>=ceil(4m/5), such that Q_k=(E\\{z}) union {k} supports a tight P5 for every k in S. Put F=E\\{z}. For each k in S choose a tight ordering of Q_k and choose one consecutive tight turn in that ordering in which k is an outer witness and the other two vertices form a physical dimer D(k) contained in F. There are only six possible physical dimers D(k). Every internal k in S whose physical-dimer class contains another carrier vertex has a historical same-frame capture certificate at k in the exact D(k)-deletion root K sqcup (E\\D(k)). Consequently at least max(0,ceil(4m/5)-8) distinct carrier vertices admit such exact-frame historical capture certificates.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample in the five-exterior equality face; E is non-Hamiltonian; accepted R232 dense-hole column, R8 trimer existence, R5 pair-deletion exactness, and witness-adaptive capture R37.

## Scope
Arbitrary-order carrier-ancestry amplification at the five-exterior floor. This strengthens R239 by pigeonholing only the six physical dimers of the exterior four-set, using the fact that R37 is adaptive across different tested orders and polarities on the same physical support.

## Applicability
Use when replacement density must be converted into carrier-position capture. Compared with R239, the capture loss is only six singleton physical-dimer classes plus two carrier endpoints, giving the sharp contract-level bound ceil(4m/5)-8.

## Persistence
The hole z, four-core F, P5 supports Q_k, physical dimers D(k), exact D-deletion roots, selected carrier-cut crossings, and capture events have the persistence of their parent theorems. For one fixed D, all captures are sourced from the same literal exact root K sqcup (E\\D), though payment descendants are not simultaneous.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not force adjacent captured vertices, does not make different capture descendants simultaneous, does not by itself create a longer carrier or spanning two-cover, and does not feed low-mass ancestry directly into R171 contrary to R214. The strengthening does not require the two witnesses on one physical dimer to have the same tested order or polarity.

## Proof
Fix the dense hole z supplied by R232 and put F=E-{z}. For each k in the successful set choose any tight P5 ordering of F union {k}. In every position of a five-path, k is an outer vertex of at least one consecutive tight turn, so that turn has the form (k,u,v), (u,v,k), or its head/tail-signed equivalent with u,v in F. Record only the physical dimer D(k)={u,v}. There are exactly six possible dimers in a four-set F. Now let k be internal on K and suppose k prime != k has D(k prime)=D(k)=D. Delete D. The remaining three vertices E-D form a tight trimer by R8, hence K sqcup (E-D) is a two-cover of H-D; R5 makes it exact. Relative to the retained P5 Q_k, deleting D leaves k separated from the other exterior vertices, so the two selected K states incident with k are carrier/exterior crossings for Q_k-D. The distinct witness k prime lies on at most one of those two selected states. Choose the other crossing. The P5 Q_k prime supplies an admissible signed incarnation of the same physical support D witnessed by k prime. R37 explicitly permits witness-adaptive capture across different signed incarnations of one physical support, so no equality of tested order or polarity is required. Since k prime is outside the chosen selected pair, R37 yields historical capture of carrier vertex k in this same exact D-deletion frame. Thus every internal member of every non-singleton physical-dimer class is capturable. At most six successful vertices can occupy singleton dimer classes and at most two successful vertices are carrier endpoints. With |S|>=ceil(4m/5), at least max(0,ceil(4m/5)-8) successful vertices are captured.
