# R141 — Type-(2,2,3) common-trimer universal shield and terminal saturation

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the minimum-overlap-two type-(2,2,3) motif of R130/R131: L=X∪Y∪{p}, B=X∪Z, D=Y∪Z, with |X|=|Y|=2, |Z|=3, exterior vertices e,f, and shared short-trimer support P={p,e,f}. Put U=V(H)\\P=X∪Y∪Z. Then no five-set P∪{u,v}, for distinct u,v∈U, supports a tight five-path. Consequently, fix any tight orientation P=(a,b,c) and define E_left={u∈U:(u,a,b) is tight} and E_right={u∈U:(b,c,u) is tight}. There are no distinct x∈E_left and y∈E_right. Hence either E_left is empty, or E_right is empty, or E_left=E_right={q} for one q∈U. By boundary antisymmetry, in the first case (b,a,u) is tight for every u∈U; in the second case (u,c,b) is tight for every u∈U; and in the singleton/singleton case both families of reverse turns hold for every u∈U\\{q}. Thus every tight orientation of the common trimer has either one terminal dimer with seven common outside reverse witnesses or both terminal dimers with six common outside reverse witnesses.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; minimum longest-support overlap equals two; accepted type-(2,2,3) normalization R130/R131; boundary antisymmetry R3 and longest-path value R9.

## Scope
Support-level and ordered-turn compression of the shared-short-trimer branch. No payment, actualization, or representative coupling is used.

## Applicability
Use as the canonical fixed-trimer interface for the type-(2,2,3) motif. It upgrades the two-root shared trimer to a universal five-support shield and a near-total terminal reverse-star system before any descendant is opened.

## Persistence
The non-Hamiltonicity of all P-plus-two supports is graph-intrinsic. For each chosen tight orientation of P, the extender sets and forced reverse turns are graph-intrinsic ordered-turn facts.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert which terminal side is saturated, does not assert a common saturated side across different tight orientations of P, does not couple paid descendants, and does not itself assert a spanning 5+5 cover.

## Proof
Let U=X∪Y∪Z=V(H)\\P. Suppose for contradiction that S=P∪{u,v}, with distinct u,v∈U, supports a tight five-path. By R9, S is a global-longest five-support. Since e,f lie in none of L,B,D and P∩L={p}, P∩B=P∩D=∅, minimum overlap two with B forces both u and v to lie in B=X∪Z. Minimum overlap two with D likewise forces both u and v to lie in D=Y∪Z. Therefore u,v∈B∩D=Z. But then S∩L={p}, of size one, contradicting minimum overlap two. Hence no P∪{u,v} is Hamiltonian. Now fix a tight orientation P=(a,b,c). If there were distinct x∈E_left and y∈E_right, then (x,a,b), (a,b,c), and (b,c,y) would all be tight, so x,a,b,c,y would be a tight five-path on P∪{x,y}, contradiction. Thus every member of E_left equals every member of E_right whenever both sets are nonempty. Hence either one set is empty, or both are the same singleton {q}. If u∉E_left, then (u,a,b) is bad, so by R3 its complete reversal (b,a,u) is tight. If u∉E_right, then (b,c,u) is bad, so (u,c,b) is tight. The stated seven-witness and six-witness alternatives follow immediately.
