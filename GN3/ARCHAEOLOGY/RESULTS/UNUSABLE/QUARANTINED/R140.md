# R140 — Type-(2,2,3) shared-trimer endpoint replacement rule

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the minimum-overlap-two type-(2,2,3) motif of R130/R131: L=X∪Y∪{p}, B=X∪Z, D=Y∪Z with |X|=|Y|=2, |Z|=3, exterior vertices e,f, and common short trimer support P={p,e,f} in the exact roots D⊔P after deleting X and B⊔P after deleting Y. Fix any tight path ordering of D. For each endpoint t of that ordering there exists w∈P such that (D\\{t})∪{w} is a global-longest five-support. If t∈Y, then necessarily w=p. Symmetrically, for each endpoint t of any tight ordering of B there exists w∈P such that (B\\{t})∪{w} is a global-longest five-support, and if t∈X then necessarily w=p.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; minimum longest-support overlap equals two; accepted type-(2,2,3) support and exact-root normalization R130/R131.

## Scope
Support-level radius-one consequence inside the shared-short-trimer overlap-two motif. It is proved directly from accepted inputs and does not depend on returned R132 or pending R138.

## Applicability
Use to rotate the type-(2,2,3) motif through long-rail endpoints. A private endpoint forces replacement by p; a Z-endpoint admits a replacement by one of p,e,f.

## Persistence
The generated replacement support is a graph-intrinsic global-longest support. The chosen endpoint and path ordering belong to the displayed root representative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify the replacement witness for a Z-endpoint, does not assert both endpoint replacements use distinct witnesses, and does not couple the B-root and D-root replacements in one representative.

Every P-plus-two-D support is non-Hamiltonian by minimum-overlap-two incidence; if all Q-plus-w supports also failed, the support-tracked R126 forcing would contradict those exclusions. ## Proof
Work first in the exact root D⊔P after deleting X, where P={p,e,f}. Choose a tight ordering D=(d1,d2,d3,d4,d5) and an orientation (x,y,z) of P that is a tight trimer. Let t=d5 and Q=(a,b,c,d)=(d1,d2,d3,d4); the other endpoint is handled by the last four-window identically.\n\nFirst, no support P∪{u,v} with u,v∈D can support a tight P5. Indeed any such P5 is global-longest by R9. Since B=X∪Z and P is disjoint from B, minimum overlap two forces u,v∈Z. But then L=X∪Y∪{p} meets P∪{u,v} only in p, giving overlap one, contradiction. Thus every P-plus-two-D support is non-Hamiltonian.\n\nSuppose for contradiction that none of Q∪{x},Q∪{y},Q∪{z} is Hamiltonian. For every w∈P, failure of (w,a,b,c,d) and (a,b,c,d,w) gives baw and wdc tight by R3; failure of (b,a,w,d,c) then gives dwa tight. Thus baw,dwa,wdc are tight for every w∈P. The three reversal pairs on the trimer have, up to relabeling, cyclic and transitive types. In the cyclic type xyz,yzx,zxy are tight. The candidates (b,a,z,x,y), (y,z,x,d,c), (d,x,z,a,y), and (x,d,y,a,z) lie respectively on P∪{a,b}, P∪{c,d}, P∪{a,d}, P∪{a,d}; all these supports were just shown non-Hamiltonian. Their successive failures force xza, dxz, yaz, ydx tight, after which (y,d,x,z,a) is a tight P5 on P∪{a,d}, contradiction. In the transitive type xyz,xzy,yxz are tight; the analogous four failed candidates force zxa, dzx, yax, zdy tight, after which (z,d,y,a,x) is a tight P5 on P∪{a,d}, contradiction. Therefore Q∪{w} supports a tight P5 for some w∈P. By R9 its support C=(D\\{t})∪{w} lies in the global-longest family F.\n\nIf t∈Y, then D\\{t} consists of the other Y vertex together with all three Z vertices. Hence C∩L has size one when w∈{e,f} and size two when w=p. Since the global minimum overlap is two, w=p is forced.\n\nThe same proof applies to the other endpoint of D. For the B-root after deleting Y, interchange X with Y and B with D. Any P-plus-two-B support meeting D twice must use two Z vertices and then meets L only at p, so the same forcing gives an endpoint replacement B\\{t}+w; if t∈X, minimum overlap with L forces w=p.
