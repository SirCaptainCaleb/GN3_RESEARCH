# R131 — Minimum-overlap-two triangle exact-root normalization

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case and minimum longest-support overlap two. Choose L,B,D as in R129, with X=L∩B and Y=L∩D disjoint 2-subsets of L. Put Z=B∩D. Then |Z| is 2 or 3. If |Z|=2, there are unique vertices p,b,d and one exterior vertex e such that L=X∪Y∪{p}, B=X∪Z∪{b}, D=Y∪Z∪{d}; deleting X,Y,Z respectively gives exact 5+3 covers D ⊔ {p,b,e}, B ⊔ {p,d,e}, and L ⊔ {b,d,e}, after orienting each displayed three-set as a tight trimer. If |Z|=3, there is a unique p∈L\\(X∪Y) and two exterior vertices e,f such that L=X∪Y∪{p}, B=X∪Z, D=Y∪Z; deleting X and Y respectively gives exact 5+3 covers D ⊔ {p,e,f} and B ⊔ {p,e,f}. Thus the (2,2,2) motif carries three cyclic 5+3 roots whose short trimers share one exterior vertex, while the (2,2,3) motif carries two 5+3 roots with one common physical short trimer.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; minimum longest-support overlap equals 2; accepted R129.

## Scope
Support/deletion normalization of the overlap-two disjoint-core fork.

## Applicability
Use to attack minimum overlap two through synchronized 5+3 roots. The 2-2-2 branch has a common short-trimer vertex; the 2-2-3 branch has an identical short-trimer support across two roots.

## Persistence
The support coordinates and deleted pairs are graph-intrinsic. Each short three-set can be oriented as a tight trimer by R8; the resulting exact covers are separate certificates.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert common orientations across distinct three-sets in the 2-2-2 branch, simultaneous currentness of the exact covers, or immediate 5+5 closure.

## Proof
Take L,B,D from R129 and set X=L∩B, Y=L∩D. As in the direct set calculation, Z=B∩D is disjoint from L and has size 2 or 3. Suppose first |Z|=2. Since L has five vertices and X,Y are disjoint pairs, L=X∪Y∪{p} for a unique p. Similarly B=X∪Z∪{b} and D=Y∪Z∪{d} for unique b,d. These nine vertices are distinct, so let e be the unique vertex outside L∪B∪D. Delete X. The support D is disjoint from X and is a tight five-path support. The remaining three vertices of H-X outside D are exactly {p,b,e}; by R8 they admit a tight trimer orientation. Hence D and that trimer form an exact 5+3 cover of H-X. Cyclically, deleting Y gives B together with {p,d,e}, and deleting Z gives L together with {b,d,e}. Now suppose |Z|=3. Again L=X∪Y∪{p}. Since |B|=5 and B contains the disjoint sets X and Z of total size five, B=X∪Z; similarly D=Y∪Z. The union has eight vertices, so let e,f be the two exterior vertices. Deleting X leaves D intact and the remaining three vertices are {p,e,f}; orient them by R8 to obtain an exact 5+3 cover. Deleting Y gives B with the same physical short triple {p,e,f}. This proves the root normalization.
