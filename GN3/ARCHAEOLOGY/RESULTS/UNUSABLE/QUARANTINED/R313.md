# R313 — Every proper tight path of order at least six has a puncture-forced first-contact front door

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K=(k_1,...,k_m) be any proper tight path of order m>=6. Puncture the central adjacent dimer D={k_3,k_4}. Then either H has a spanning two-cover, or accepted R293 supplies an exact D-deletion frame with a selected crossing {b,z} from the surviving historical carrier K-D to the exterior and a valid historical capture of the carrier-side endpoint b through that crossing. The two surviving historical runs are (k_1,k_2) and (k_5,...,k_m), both nontrivial. Hence whichever run contains b, the captured vertex b has at least one genuine historical adjacency E on that same retained run. After choosing an actual capture descendant and designated quiet continuation, R312 applies: lawful R155 mirror reentry along E either reveals an earlier contact at b or is itself the first contact. Thus every proper tight path of order at least six is an order-free entry to a puncture-indexed protected first-contact event; no fresh-support ancestry transport is used.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper tight path of order at least six; accepted R293 and provisional R312.

## Scope
Order-free role-sensitive puncture front door correcting the generic interaction route.

## Applicability
Use to reduce any branch possessing a proper P6-or-longer to an actual capture/contact lineage. The only residual longest-path regime not covered by this theorem has maximum proper path order at most five.

## Persistence
K,D, the two surviving historical runs, selected crossing, captured endpoint, and first-contact history are retained according to R293/R312. The exact deletion representative and protected continuation remain lineage-relative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the exposed contact closes the conjecture. Does not apply to P5 where one surviving run may be a singleton. Does not use generic fresh-spoke interactions as progress.

## Proof
Because m>=6, deleting k_3,k_4 leaves the two historical K-runs A_L=(k_1,k_2) and A_R=(k_5,...,k_m), each of order at least two. R293 applies to this internal dimer puncture. In its nonclosing branch an exact D-deletion frame selects a carrier/exterior crossing {b,z} with b in the surviving carrier and gives a valid historical capture of b. The vertex b lies on exactly one of A_L,A_R. Since both runs are nontrivial, every vertex of either run has at least one physical adjacency inside that same historical run. Let E be such an adjacency at b. This is precisely the hypothesis needed by R312. Choose the actual capture descendant and protected quiet continuation; then lawful R155 reentry along E either encounters an already exposed earlier contact or constitutes the first contact at b.
