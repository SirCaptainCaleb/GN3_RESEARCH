# R465 — Exact-support cross-generation overlap is fresh-certificate-blind

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the fixed-trimer remint configuration of R458: J=(a,x,c) is a proper tight trimer, D is a signed dimer disjoint from J carrying one signed certificate with witness x, and M is the unique terminal signed dimer of J of polarity opposite to D. Let y be any distinct witness for a second certificate of the same polarity on the same oriented support D for which the corresponding signed turn is tight. Then:

(1) The R458 remint conclusion is invariant under replacing the original opposite-rail witness w by y. Pairing M with the x-signed copy of D and with the y-signed copy of D gives two direct balanced-pair births by R39, and R160 forces the same nonquiet fixed-trimer historical interaction. This proof uses only the fixed trimer J, the physical support D, the common polarity, and witness distinctness; it does not use that y is an endpoint of the opposite residual rail or that y arose from a literal splice proposal.

(2) If these two births are then spent only through the exact-support overlap branch of R441/R436, the discharge can be made without using the y-certificate at all: on either repeated nontrivial support M or D, R436 may return the old signed extension from the first birth exactly as in the exact-replay case. Hence the composite R458 -> R441/R436 route remains valid after arbitrary replacement of the second witness by any y satisfying the same-polarity certificate condition, and it cannot by itself force any output whose novelty depends on the opposite-rail role or splice provenance of the original w. In particular it cannot by itself force a new literal sibling proposal or the simultaneous two-endpoint adjacency cuts required by R461.

Thus the current remint-plus-overlap chain is a genuine interaction certificate but is not yet a consumer of the cross-partition geometry carried specifically by the R455 opposite-rail witness.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1). Accepted R39, R160, R436, R441, R458, and R461. In the abstract replacement statement, J is a proper tight trimer, D is physically disjoint from J, D has two same-polarity signed certificates with distinct witnesses x and y, and M is the opposite-polarity terminal signed dimer of J. For the R458 specialization, x is the source middle and the original second witness w comes from the R455 residual-rail splice.

## Scope
Order-free role-consumption fence for the fixed-trimer remint architecture and its exact-support R441/R436 spend. It strengthens the exact-replay fresh-certificate-blindness observation by showing that the preceding R458 remint itself is insensitive to the geometric role of the second witness.

## Applicability
Use whenever an R455/R458 collision is claimed to create cross-partition or sibling-proposal progress. Credit the opposite-rail/splice coordinate as consumed only after a later theorem uses that coordinate essentially, for example by constructing a path surgery whose validity changes under witness replacement.

## Persistence
The role-blindness is logical and survives later currentization. J, x, D, the second witness, M, and any original splice proposal may remain as historical coordinates, but this theorem records that the R39/R160 remint and exact-replay R441/R436 discharge do not use the second witness's residual-rail or splice role.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not invalidate R455 or R458, and does not say the named opposite-rail witness is useless. A different consumer may use its physical location or splice provenance essentially. Does not rule out non-exact-support R441 contact, a certificate-sensitive interaction theorem, or a genuine two-endpoint path surgery. Does not assert that every arbitrary vertex supplies the required same-polarity signed certificate on D.

## Proof
Let the first certificate on D have witness x and the second have witness y distinct from x, with the same tested orientation and polarity. Since D is disjoint from J, and M is the terminal signed dimer of J of opposite polarity, R39 applies to M together with either signed copy of D. Thus B_x=(M,D_x) and B_y=(M,D_y) are direct balanced-pair births. The hypotheses of R160 depend on the same fixed trimer J, one of its terminal signed dimers M, and a partner signed dimer D physically disjoint from J. They do not require the partner witness to lie on a designated residual rail or to come from a splice proposal. Therefore, reading B_x first and B_y second, R160 forces the same nonquiet historical signed-support/anchor or cross-generation pair interaction for every such y. This proves (1).

For (2), both births have exactly the same two nontrivial physical supports M and D. Hence any R441 comparison has cross-generation overlap. Choose either repeated oriented support P in {M,D} and view the later copy as Q=P. Apply R436 to the old signed certificate (z;P) from the first birth and Q=P. Because P is nontrivial, the singleton replay exception is irrelevant. In the at-anchor branch, the already-tight first old signed turn makes R436 return the already-known strict extension (z,P) of Q. This discharge uses only first-birth data and the equality Q=P; the second witness y and its certificate are not referenced. Thus the R441/R436 overlap route can be completed without consuming y.

Consequently the composite remint-plus-overlap argument is invariant under replacing the original R458 witness w by any y that supplies the same-polarity second certificate on D. Any conclusion whose novelty specifically requires w to be an opposite-rail endpoint, or to carry the literal R455 splice provenance, cannot follow from this chain alone. By R461, an exact complementary sibling of a native splice literal must abandon both incident source endpoint adjacencies. Nothing in the witness-substitution-invariant chain constructs such a proposal or uses both endpoint adjacencies, so that two-cut geometry requires an additional consumer.
