# R502 — Anti-aligned bridge forces a bridge-interior cross-state pair birth

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Let A=(a_0,a_1,...,a_p) and B=(b_0,...,b_{q-1},b_q) be vertex-simple tight paths with p,q>=1, and suppose H has the anti-aligned bridge three-cover P sqcup A^+ sqcup B^- of C388, where P=(a_0,z_1,...,z_{m-1},b_q) is tight with m>=3, A^+=(a_1,...,a_p), B^-=(b_0,...,b_{q-1}), and P^o=(z_1,...,z_{m-1}). Put D={a_0,b_q} and W=H-D. Then every exact two-cover T of W selects a directed state xy with one endpoint in V(P^o) and the other in V(A^+) union V(B^-). Consequently accepted R176 yields a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair whose birth retains the exact pair-deletion fiber D, the selected cross state xy, and whether the nonbridge endpoint lies on A^+ or B^-. In particular a named P^o cross-state pair birth is automatic in every anti-aligned bridge cell; no endpoint-universality, R485 wrap analysis, R493 collision split, or R495 DR/HF/TF split is needed for this output.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; p,q>=1; m>=3; P sqcup A^+ sqcup B^- is the literal anti-aligned bridge three-cover described above. Accepted R5 and R176.

## Scope
Order-free smallest-counterexample consumer for the full anti-aligned bridge configuration. It preserves the bridge-interior ancestry of the selected cross-state that births the pair.

## Applicability
Use immediately whenever an anti-aligned bridge P sqcup A^+ sqcup B^- appears in a smallest counterexample. Choose any exact two-cover of H-{a_0,b_q}; before doing endpoint-side, wrap, DR, or one-hole-fork analysis, extract a selected state crossing P^o to an ancestral residual rail and apply R176.

## Persistence
Retain D={a_0,b_q}, the literal source partition A^+ sqcup P^o sqcup B^-, the chosen exact two-cover T of W, the selected directed cross state xy, and the identity of its bridge-interior endpoint and ancestral side. The resulting balanced pair is graph-intrinsic; T and xy are the birth certificate/provenance.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the resulting balanced pair itself closes H, is current in a common cover, or has a prescribed support size. Does not say every cross state of T touches P^o; only that at least one does. Does not provide the next physical spend of the pair. Outside smallest-counterexample scope one must separately assume an exact two-cover of H-D.

## Proof
Let D={a_0,b_q} and W=H-D. By R5, pc(W)=2, so choose any literal exact two-cover T=T_1 sqcup T_2 of W. The retained source pieces A^+, P^o, B^- are three nonempty vertex-disjoint tight paths spanning W. Suppose for contradiction that T selects no directed state with exactly one endpoint in V(P^o) and the other in V(A^+) union V(B^-). Then no T-component can contain both a P^o vertex and a vertex outside P^o, because along the ordered path from one type to the other there would be a first selected transition crossing the partition. Since P^o is nonempty, at least one T-component lies entirely in V(P^o). Because A^+ and B^- are also nonempty and T has only two components, exactly one component, say T_P, has vertex set V(P^o), while the other component T_{AB} has vertex set V(A^+) union V(B^-). Thus T_{AB} is a vertex-simple tight Hamilton path on the whole ancestral residual support A^+ union B^-. But the original bridge P=(a_0,z_1,...,z_{m-1},b_q) is a tight path on D union V(P^o), disjoint from T_{AB}; therefore P sqcup T_{AB} is a literal spanning two-cover of H, contradicting that H is a counterexample. Hence every exact two-cover T of W contains a selected cross state xy incident with P^o and one of A^+,B^-. Since W is proper in H, accepted R176 applies to the literal source cover R=A^+ sqcup P^o sqcup B^- and the exact cover T and upgrades xy to a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair, retaining the cross-state provenance. This proves the claim.
