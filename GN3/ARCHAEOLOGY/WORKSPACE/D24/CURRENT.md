# D24 — Five/six-exterior replacement saturation and specialized carrier funnels

Specialized five/six-exterior development: replacement-density laws, global-longest endpoint shields, puncture/capture funnels, synchronized exterior grids, negative density fences, collision webs, anchored-capacity/tiny-swallow reductions, and their distinct physical constructions.

## Minimum-overlap-one and four-of-five replacement saturation

R184 and R189 are the two historical minimum-overlap-one replacement families.

R184 starts with a global-longest P5 carrier A and a five-vertex non-Hamilton exterior E. Fix a tight trimer inside E, leaving holes x,y. For every carrier vertex a, apply the exact pair-extension theorem R146 to the trimer together with x,y,a. The alternative producing E itself as a P5 is impossible because E is non-Hamiltonian. Hence at least one one-hole replacement `(E-y)+a` or `(E-x)+a` is a P5. Pigeonhole gives one hole that works for at least three carrier vertices.

R189 rotates this over all hole pairs. For a fixed carrier vertex a, at most one hole can fail, so at least four of the five one-hole replacements are P5s. Across five carrier vertices this gives at least twenty distinct longest-five supports meeting the old carrier in exactly one vertex. This is a genuine saturation theorem, not merely a counting heuristic.



### Order-free four-of-five form

R192 removes the special ordering assumptions from the previous fan. Let K be proper with exterior E of size five. Minimal-counterexample exterior bounds make K global-longest in the relevant regime, and E cannot be Hamiltonian. For any k in K and any two holes x,y in E, orient the remaining three exterior vertices as a tight trimer and apply R146. Since E itself cannot be a P5, at least one of the two one-hole replacements succeeds. Therefore a fixed row k cannot have two failed holes: **every carrier row has at least four successful holes out of five**.

This `4/5` law is the basic replacement-density invariant used throughout the next generation. It is exact and survives later failures of stronger density arguments.

R194 combines a successful replacement with an internal carrier vertex. If k is internal in K and `Q=(E-z)+k` is a successful P5, the complement `(K-k)+z` has a literal three-cover from the two K intervals plus z. Minimality gives a two-cover of the same proper residue, so the component-drop theorem R159 produces a balanced pair. Thus successful replacement rows have a direct signed-pair consumer.



## Six-exterior puncture fan

R196 is the six-exterior analogue. Let `|E|=6`. Define `M={e : E-e is a P5}`. Every three-subset of E meets M: orient its complementary triple, apply R146 to the resulting five-set alternatives, and use non-Hamiltonicity to rule out the forbidden branch. A three-uniform hitting argument then gives `|M|≥4`. Hence at least four omitted vertices produce P5s `Q_e=E-e`.

For each good e, `K ⊔ Q_e` is an exact two-cover of H-e; if the complement were Hamiltonian, adding singleton e would give a two-cover of H. For an internal k of K, replacing k by e creates a literal three-cover of the complement of Q_e, and R159 again yields a balanced pair. This is the six-exterior pair fan.



## Global-longest endpoint shields and specialized terminal-dimer floor

R197 is the global-longest terminal-dimer capture gateway. Let K be global-longest with at least five exterior vertices. Direct endpoint extension by an exterior vertex is impossible, so boundary antisymmetry gives a reverse terminal-dimer sign from every exterior vertex. Delete one terminal dimer. Exact completion plus puncture crossing gives a selected crossing from the surviving carrier to the exterior. Because the exterior witness pool has at least five vertices, one may choose a sign witness avoiding that crossing; witness-adaptive capture then captures whichever crossing endpoint is useful. The aligned payment theorem starts a paid descendant unless closure occurs.

R199 isolates the endpoint-shield part: every exterior vertex signs the two reverse terminal dimers of a global-longest K, with opposite end polarities. R202 combines those two terminal signs inside K: the opposite-signed terminal dimers form a balanced mass-four pair, and the carrier interval between them refunds to a mass-two floor on the two physical endpoints of K. This is an ancestry-bearing floor, not a claim that the endpoints remain simultaneously selected with the old K.

Generic mass-two-floor-to-fixed-turn entry is developed in D16 (R170/R249); this section retains the stronger global-longest terminal labels that precede that generic interface.

## Six-exterior synchronized holes, rectangles, and central-anchor grids

R203 gives a common-hole synchronization principle: if each carrier row has at most one failed hole, then for any t≤4 rows there remain at least `5-t` holes successful for all of them. Thus four carrier vertices share a successful hole. R207 converts this density into an exterior-dimer collision count. Each successful P5 row signs at least one exterior dimer with the carrier vertex as witness. A fixed dimer can be associated with at most three holes, so four successes force at least two exterior dimers per row. Six rows create at least twelve row-dimer incidences over ten physical exterior dimers; some dimer repeats with distinct witnesses and the same-support interaction theorem fires.

R208 records a different six-exterior rectangle. In the root `K ⊔ Q_e ⊔ {e}`, direct endpoint attachments of e to K are blocked by global longestness and direct attachments to Q_e are blocked because they would make E Hamiltonian. Boundary antisymmetry therefore signs all four reverse terminal dimers of K and Q_e with the same witness e. The left-side signs share one polarity and the right-side signs the opposite, giving a `2×2` family of cross-rail balanced pairs.

R209 applies the endpoint-refund mechanism separately to K and Q_e, producing alternative endpoint-floor lineages. R213 uses the cross-diagonal rectangle directly as two terminal-payment channels, again as separate descendants.

R211 is the carrier-by-exterior central-anchor grid. At least four good omissions produce a union of internal exterior vertices of size at least four. Pair an internal carrier vertex with a suitable internal exterior vertex, choose disjoint centered trimer windows, and invoke the fixed-trimer central-anchor theorem. This gives a dense family of named anchor interactions but not simultaneous currentness.



## What replacement density does not force

Several accepted negative results are essential boundaries.

R201 is a scope fence: a literal six-exterior singleton-root cover has rails of prescribed sizes, but that does **not** automatically satisfy the maximin hypotheses of older root-reverse-star theorems. Literal exactness and maximin extremality are different data.

R205 gives an abstract contract-level countermodel: the support/certificate outputs of the five/six-exterior pair theorems alone do not imply a new longer carrier. A separate theorem linking a replacement support to actual carrier extension is required.

R206 is an explicit nine-vertex local countermodel: a four-core F, a tight exterior trimer, and three successful `F+e` P5 replacements can coexist while the obvious carrier extension remains non-Hamiltonian. Thus even a common-core replacement packet does not force local P6 growth.

These are not dead ends to omit. They explain why the later program turns replacement density into signed interactions/payment rather than trying to infer carrier growth directly.



## Collision webs and the ancestry-sensitive capture funnel

R215 observes that every exterior vertex signs the same oriented terminal dimer of a global-longest K in the same polarity. With six exterior vertices, every witness pair yields a same-support collision: fifteen pairwise collision certificates at each end. The abundance is graph-intrinsic; usefulness still depends on a consumer that needs the named support/witness identities.

R210 is a more ancestry-sensitive route. The R197 puncture/capture gateway captures a chosen carrier endpoint x. Lawful mirror reentry through a genuine carrier adjacency creates a mirror trimer with x as middle. The historical paid-pair machinery can then be steered to that trimer's outer pair and the fixed-turn clock started. The theorem is a funnel to a finite historical interaction/contact lineage, not a proof that the first captured state remains current forever.



## Literal pair-frame size fence and tiny support-swallow corners

R212/R214 are scope fences for the literal same-representative pair discharge R171. In a literal frame `H=K⊔P⊔T` with K proper, the pair mass `|P|+|T|` is exactly the exterior size of K and hence at least five. Therefore historical mass-two, mass-three, or mass-four pairs cannot simply be reinterpreted as the two literal pair components in R171.

R217 handles the unique-residual anchored-capacity case: if only one reservoir vertex remains, it plus the two witnesses forms a path of size at most three, so `H=J⊔P⊔T` enters the literal R171 discharge. R220 iterates strict mass descent; since literal pair mass is at least five, only finitely many descents occur before cover or support swallow.

R221 classifies the support-swallow corner. If R171 swallows a support of size one, the exact deletion rails contradict the small-rail floor. If the swallowed support has size two or three, the third rail has size three or two/three respectively. Thus only `(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)` remain. R222 packages this as the anchored-capacity parent reduction.

R223 shows that a swallowed dimer carries a bidirectional endpoint-refund fork: the incoming signed orientation and the reverse contact created by the bad swallow seam let one run alternative payment lineages preserving opposite physical endpoints. Endpoint-selective paid-floor preservation is developed in D16 as R224 and is the generic interface used after the specialized R223 dimer fork.

R225/R226 reconnect the tiny corners to the exterior gateways. In `(2,3)` or `(3,2)` the untouched size-three support sees a five-vertex exterior and hence the `4/5` replacement theorem R192. In `(3,3)` it sees a six-vertex exterior and R196 applies. R227 is the resulting historical parent reduction from arbitrary balanced pair to capacity/collision, one-residual discharge, or five/six-exterior tiny corner. It remains valid as a historical decomposition but is superseded as the canonical front door by later more direct reductions.



R218 is the historical universal anchored-capacity composition: start from an ancestry-bearing pair, maximize the two signed supports, then obtain either collision/contact output or, in the one-residual case, enter the literal discharge. It is superseded as a front door by the protected-contact development but remains a distinct order-free composite route.

## Dependency-sensitive specialized route worth preserving

**R210 has both a wrapper and an explicit ancestry route.** P210 packages the result as a composition of the global-longest terminal capture gateway R197 with the then-current captured-middle fixed-turn injection. P596 spells out the physical mechanism: obtain the real carrier/exterior crossing and same-frame capture from R197, use lawful carrier mirror reentry to make the captured endpoint the middle of a trimer, finish the paid pair to a floor, steer to the trimer outer pair, and enter fixed-turn descent. The explicit route is preferable when capture ancestry and the carrier adjacency must be inspected; the wrapper remains a concise historical derivation.

These proof-route distinctions do not change the accepted theorem statements or their lifecycle. They record dependency strength and retained physical information.
