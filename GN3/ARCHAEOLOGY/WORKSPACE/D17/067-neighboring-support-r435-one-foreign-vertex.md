# One-for-one neighboring Hamilton supports have only one-foreign-vertex Reverse-Ear geometry

**Workspace:** D17
**State:** established
**Key:** `neighboring-support-r435-one-foreign-vertex`

**Summary:** Let P be Hamilton on S+x and K Hamilton on S+y with x!=y. In any R435 comparison of K against P, every K-ear between consecutive P-contacts has at most one internal vertex, namely y, because K-P={y}. Consequently every R435 conflict between one-for-one neighboring Hamilton supports is generated by either a literal common-support chord/reversal or a two-state detour through the single exchanged label y. Any proper cycle output is therefore an old P-segment closed by either one selected chord or one y-detour; no long anonymous foreign ear can occur. This arbitrary-order reduction is the structural reason complete neighboring-support conflict is a plausible holonomy-contraction target.

### 1. Neighboring-support setup
Let

  X=S union {x},    Y=S union {y},    x!=y,

and let P be a vertex-simple Hamilton tight path on X and K a vertex-simple Hamilton tight path on Y. Compare K with P by accepted R435, using P as the reference path.

The common vertices are exactly S, and

  V(K)-V(P)={y}.                                         (OF.1)

### 2. Every Reverse-Ear has at most one foreign internal vertex
Take consecutive K-contacts u,v with P which occur in reverse P-order, and let E be the K-subpath from u to v appearing in R435. By definition E has no internal P-contact. Every internal vertex of E therefore lies in V(K)-V(P). By (OF.1) there is at most one such vertex. Hence exactly one of the following holds:

1. E is the single selected state u->v, with no internal vertex; or
2. E is the two-state detour u->y->v, whose unique internal vertex is the exchanged label y.

There is no longer foreign ear.                                      (OF.2)

### 3. Consequences for the three R435 outputs
Apply the exact proof mechanism of R435 to E. In the zero-internal case, any selected-state reversal or proper-cycle output is supported entirely on the old P support, with the cycle obtained by closing an old P segment using the single selected chord u->v. In the one-internal case, every seam reverse trimer uses y, and any proper cycle is obtained by closing an old P segment with the two-state detour u->y->v.

Thus a neighboring-support R435 conflict has only two physical holonomy shapes:

  COMMON-SUPPORT CHORD,
  ONE-FOREIGN-VERTEX DETOUR.                               (OF.3)

In particular a complete conflict between one Hamilton representative and an entire neighboring one-for-one support family cannot hide arbitrarily long ears; all order activity is localized to the one exchanged label and the reference interval it jumps across.

### 4. Scope
This is an arbitrary-order path-interaction reduction. It uses no smallest-counterexample, fixed-complement, critical-block, or order-five hypothesis. It does not consume the resulting reversal, reverse trimer, or proper cycle. Its purpose in the current Common-Complement Holonomy Contraction program is to reduce every neighboring-support conflict to a minimal interval problem with one foreign label, so any genuine obstruction must come from how these localized intervals coexist across the common-complement family, not from uncontrolled ear interiors.
