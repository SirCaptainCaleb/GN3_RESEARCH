# D19 — Crossed capture packets, carrier saturation, and inward five-cell exchange


This development asks how the **crossed R24 source geometry** can be converted into source-labelled capture, collision, floor, and recompletion information without silently losing the deletion roots, inward Q coordinates, or exact-cover provenance that make those packets usable. Its strongest established outputs are distributed across a few connected mechanisms: **`collision-box`** gives endpoint collision and sibling-fiber interaction packets; **`carrier-saturation`** isolates the order-free carrier/witness saturation alternatives and the XET exceptional rail; **`crossed-puncture-repair-genealogy`** and **`inward-anchor-repair-genealogy`** record the repaired nonreplay puncture mechanisms; **`first-inward-portals`** retains same-frame first-inward capture/contact portals; and **`five-cell-inward`** connects the standard D1 five-cell recompletion theory back to the physical crossed-source labels and older capture constructions.

The exact unresolved connection is between these **local source-labelled outputs on a chosen exact cover** and a consumer that forces global closure or synchronizes the needed information across different exact covers. The limits are recorded in **`first-inward-unresolved`** and **`unresolved-five-cell-fences`**: some historical floor transports remain blocked by provenance or dependency defects, and P4/Reverse-Ear/two-witness outputs are not contradictions by themselves. The development therefore preserves what each construction actually gives and where it lives, while leaving tactical choices about which packet to attack on the shared editorial surfaces rather than in this mathematical introduction.

Several historical descendants remain needs-more-work because they layered additional steering claims on top of these accepted packets: R637/R639/R641/R642/R650/R651/R652. ## Carrier/witness saturation and the XET exceptional rail

R686 is the reusable counting theorem behind many source-specific capture arguments. Fix one signed support S, one exact two-cover T of H-V(S), k retained one-vertex extensions K_i=S plus c_i, and a witness set W. R508 forces a selected carrier/exterior crossing from each complementary singleton c_i. A fixed witness can spoil capture for a carrier only when it is the other endpoint of that selected crossing; because T is a two-path forest, that witness has selected degree at most two. Thus with at least three carriers no single witness can evade all carriers, and more generally the failed-capture incidence is degree-bounded. R687 applies this to crossed middle-gate dimers, which possess three explicitly named carriers, so a single exterior witness always has a capture route. R688 makes the exceptional XET alternative witness-exclusive: if one witness forces both endpoint carriers to use it, a second admissible witness cannot do so and must be captured. R684 identifies the exceptional structure exactly: the relevant exact deletion cover has one entire rail (L,v,R) or (R,v,L), with the other rail Hamiltonian on the complement. R690 then consumes that XET rail itself. One terminal dimer has the source witness and, by the crossed endpoint star, a second witness, hence is R542-ready. R693 abstracts the degree-one/degree-two dichotomy of a short-carrier deletion cover: complementary singleton e has T-degree one or two; degree one gives a complete physical mate clause, degree two gives an internal singleton with two selected incidences. R696 adds two witnesses and refines the possibilities to END mate geometry, FREE witness-avoiding capture, or the tightly controlled SANDWICH branch. R700 and R768 supply the clean two-probe amplifier: a trimer plus two probes gives a P4, or both reverse boundary dimers simultaneously acquire the two witnesses; if neither four-set has a P4, R522 additionally produces a Hamilton P5. R752 is the one-witness many-carrier version: among k>=3 complementary singleton carriers, at least k-2 are capture-capable in the same exact cover.

**Direct inward-anchor capture (R691).** The same-polarity anchor-exchange theorem is stronger than a mere witness count. For an oriented crossed middle-gate dimer S and inward vertex v=q2 (dually q_{m-2}), the anchor-exchange hypothesis gives a tight three-vertex carrier K on S union {v}. Puncture the appropriate accepted R621 singleton-deletion weave at the other vertex of S. The resulting exact H-S cover contains the old selected Q state {v,q3} (dually {q_{m-3},v}), which is a carrier/exterior crossing for K. R649 supplies a far physical endpoint as an exact same-polarity witness on S, and that witness avoids the selected crossing. R527 can therefore orient the actualizing marker so that v itself becomes the captured signed anchor. The genuine S-v edge of K then licenses the R312 protected first-contact continuation. No reminting or generic floor steering is used.

R689 was an attractive shape-free attempt to apply the same idea to every minimum Q-X transition; it remains needs-more-work and is not used as an interface. R694 similarly has a transparent mirror calculation after R691, but its deposited route depended on invalid R658; if revived its signed-dimer input must be replaced by R849. R706 is a valid abandoned complementary-X version. These records are retained here because they show exactly which parts of the capture mechanism are generic and which still depend on source-specific signed packets.


## Inward signed pairs: exact repair genealogy and provenance fences

This era contains several examples where the turn geometry was correct but the floor semantics were not. The original R658 high-side rectangle claim was invalid. Accepted repair R849 retains the exact signed rectangles: on the left (L,u) and (q3,q2) are tail-signed by b,z; on the right (s,R) and (q_{m-2},q_{m-3}) are head-signed by a,c. R664 likewise overclaimed the simultaneous depth-two singleton pair; accepted R850 repairs it by one fixed contiguous long-rail cut, yielding exactly {q2,q_{m-2}} with the four named witnesses b,z,a,c. R663 made a different orientation error: in its coincident-support branch it treated certificates on opposite tested orientations of the same physical dimer as a same-oriented R523/R518 collision. It is invalid. Accepted R783 preserves the distinct-support combined-provenance floor and leaves the coincident reverse-tested case as an R657 interaction rather than fabricating a collision. The same semantic fence explains R772 and R774. R774 then incorrectly treated such a raw pair as a steerable paid floor and invoked R432. The geometry survives; the steering claim does not. R717 has the same historical laundering hazard in its floor branch and remains needs-more-work; its exact first-inward diagonal/Reverse-Ear geometry is retained without upgrading the raw pair.

**Orientation-sensitive sibling-fiber repair.** R663 claimed that the two root-anchored dimers produced by sibling fibers either cut to a combined-provenance floor or, when they share the same nonroot endpoint, form a same-support collision. The second branch is false. Accepted R783 is the exact correction. Write the two certificates as P=(r,p), head-signed by alpha, and T=(t,r), tail-signed by beta. If p!=t, the supports meet only at r and have opposite polarity, so R547 cuts the hinge and leaves singleton supports p,t while retaining both sibling-fiber ledgers. If p=t, however, the certificates are tested on opposite orientations (r,p) and (p,r). R523 explicitly requires one fixed tested orientation; unordered support equality is insufficient. The only licensed conclusion in that branch is the already-established R657 interaction. R783 is developed here because its central mechanism is the orientation-sensitive floor repair; D20 cites it only where consumed.

R716/R717 preserve a useful first-inward diagonal dichotomy: either a cross-end trimer already triggers R435, or the endpoint geometry produces opposite terminal signs. The step that turns the resulting raw singleton pair into a steerable floor is not justified by the deposited lineage, so R717 remains needs-more-work. Its Reverse-Ear/terminal-sign geometry may be used independently; its floor consumer may not.

## Five-cell inward-anchor normal forms and source-decorated exchange


### Relation to the standard five-cell theory

The **standard five-cell residue, minimum classification, and current restoration proofs live in D1**, not here. Use **D1/DR1.7 § `setting`** for the five source cells
\[
G_0=Q[0,1],\quad G_1=Q[3,m-3],\quad G_2=Q[m-1,m],\quad G_3=(a,c),\quad G_4=(b,z),

together with the exact transition identity \(t_G(T)=3+\sigma_G(T)\). Use **D1/DR1.7 § `minimum`** for the complete \(\sigma_G=0\) contracted-forest classification, the elimination of the internal-X shapes, and the **current accepted shape-A and shape-C proofs R855/R856** (with the current shape-D rebase R882 and exhaustive parent R883). Use **D1/DR1.7 § `interfaces`** for the current R869 BAD-seam probe interface. Those arguments are not repeated here.

The purpose of the present section is different: retain the **extra crossed-source information and alternative constructions** that are invisible if one reads only the quotient classification.

### Mixed-root staircase and source-labelled five-cell birth

R620 is the earlier accepted mixed-root five-cell staircase. R728 is the preferred source-labelled formulation. Starting from one left and one right crossed singleton-deletion weave and deleting the mixed root pair, the two literal three-covers have common refinement

Pair-deletion exactness then forces an exact two-cover of the residue, and the five-component deficit forces at least three selected transitions across those **specific physical source cells**. ### Historical shape E/F seam calculators

The old shape-E/F calculations R677/R678 contain useful explicit seam algebra, but their deposited proofs P755/P756 are invalid because they cite invalid R658. The dependency-migrated replacements R853/R854 are valid and abandoned. Their reusable content is local and exact.

### Scope and limitation

This section does **not** re-prove the standard five-cell classification or the current A/C closure calculations; those are authoritative in D1/DR1.7 § `minimum`. Its contribution is the additional crossed-source data: which singleton roots and Q coordinates produced the five cells, which selected transition or seam carries a packet, which older construction reaches the same local geometry by a different route, and why certain attractive reflections or floor descendants fail.

All such data remain attached to the exact cover on which they were produced. Nothing here licenses combining packets from different exact covers merely because their quotient cells have the same names. That synchronization requires a separate common-fiber or exchange theorem, and the blocked descendants are recorded in `unresolved-five-cell-fences` rather than silently treated as part of the current D1 classification.

## Crossed puncture packets: exact repair genealogy

Several crossed-frame constructions were mathematically useful before their first deposited proofs became canonically usable. Their repair history should be read as one sequence rather than as unrelated failed claims.

**Root-pinned puncture.** R628 identified the correct physical mechanism: preserve a deleted X-root as a historical singleton, steer to a root-pinned floor, puncture a deep R621 cover, and use the forced recompletion crossing to create a later selected-incidence event through the same root. **Shallow endpoint-star clauses.** R629 discovered four explicit inward mate clauses obtained by puncturing the four shallow R621 deletion covers. ## Inward Q-anchor non-replay: from witness change to genuine support change

R659-R661 isolate an instructive repair principle. The target is to turn the fixed inward Q coordinates q_2 and q_{m-2} into nonstationary events while preserving their original crossed-frame sign witnesses.

Two defects remained: the m>=7 preservation step used R224 through a circular R169/R24 lineage, and the final R436 step treated a second singleton q_2 with a different witness as automatically nonstationary.

For m>=7, an explicit R425 cut removes the unwanted endpoint while leaving the opposite inward dimer untouched, and dependency-clean R428 pays the opposite support while preserving q_2 (dually q_{m-2}). Accepted R661 makes the decisive repair. It is a provenance-rich local birth, not by itself a completed payment or closure.

R760/R780 and R761/R782 try to transport mixed-sibling provenance to a floor containing q2 and then graft it onto the inward five-cell transition fan. They remain needs-more-work. R762 isolates the exact R176 combinatorics that survives: each selected transition is either GOOD, producing a spare-free cross-witness pair, or BAD, producing one reverse trimer that can be paired with either deleted inward anchor as a pin. With at least three transitions this yields a finite GOOD/BAD counting problem, but no generic R436 conclusion follows automatically. These distinctions are preserved so the unresolved work is visible without making the current development depend on it.


## Five-cell descendants that remain noncanonical or blocked

Not every attractive five-cell descendant was repaired to a usable theorem, and the migration must preserve that distinction.

R673 says that in the sigma_G(T)=0 minimum five-cell frame every selected Q-X transition is decorated either by a same-tested-order collision or by a labelled Hamilton P4, using the fixed crossed-frame witnesses. The local decoration argument itself is meaningful, but its accepted proof P751 depends on R667, whose own proof depends on unusable R665. Consequently R673 is currently unusable. It may be used only after the five-cell minimum-frame dependency is independently repaired; this development does not silently promote the decoration theorem.

R666 is similar but more severe. It attempts to use three or more transitions in one common pair-deletion frame to avoid both inherited two-witness sets and create nontrivial extension dimers at q_2 and q_{m-2}. Its selected proof P744 is invalid because exact dependency R664 is invalid. A later repaired formulation was explored in R863/P938, but that revision was abandoned/pending rather than canonically accepted. The salvageable idea is the pigeonhole/witness-avoidance pattern: three directed transitions can beat a specified two-vertex witness set, and an R176 birth can produce a genuine extension dimer. The simultaneous inward-floor premise needed to instantiate that idea remains unproved in this lineage.

By contrast, source-specific high-side puncture geometry that was successfully repaired should be cited through accepted R713, and the order-free opposite-polarity dimer crossing parent through accepted R872. These later interfaces are safe replacements for several older P4-puncture and opposite-sign recompletion attempts. The status boundary is intentional: useful local mechanisms are retained, but blocked premises remain blocked.

R766 is the needs-more-work predecessor of accepted R872. Its load-bearing idea is completely valid once stated without the old dependency: opposite-polarity certificates (x,a,b) and (a,b,y) concatenate by R523 to the P4 (x,a,b,y). In any exact cover of H-{a,b}, R508 applied to that P4 forces a selected state crossing the surviving endpoint pair {x,y} to the exterior. R872 is the accepted order-free repair and should be cited. Thus the recompletion-crossing mechanism is not a remaining gap even though R766 itself is.
