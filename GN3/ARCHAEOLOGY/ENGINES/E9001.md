# E9001: Marked low-transition exchange compressor

## Theorem

Let `G=H-{A,C}=B⊔X`, where `X={v,s1,s2,s3}` and `B` is a nonempty tight Hamilton spectator path. Tight turns are invariant under cyclic rotation, and the complete reversal of every bad turn on three distinct vertices is tight. For each retained source `s_i`, `(A,s_i,C)` is tight, while `X` has no tight Hamilton path on all four of its vertices. Every physical exact two-cover arising in the retained low-transition frame has two nontrivial rails.

Let `F` be a physical exact two-cover of `G` in which all three sources are internal and, on one `F`-rail,

`B_L -> s1 -> s2 -> s3 -> B_R`,

with `B_L,B_R∈B`. The only `X|B` transition edges of `F` are the two displayed boundary gates and the unique `v-B` incidence; `v` has no other `F`-incidence.

Let `J` be a physical spanning three-forest of `G` with `τ(J)=1`. In the split-copy symmetric difference `F△J`, let `C*` be the unique `F`-heavy alternating path and let every other alternating component be balanced, meaning it contains equally many `F`- and `J`-edges. Write the zipper on `C*` as

`e_i=a_{i-1}->b_i` for `1≤i≤m`,

`f_i=a_i->b_i` for `1≤i<m`.

Its transition-weight excess is two, i.e.

`Σ_i χ(e_i)-Σ_i χ(f_i)=2`.

The `v-B` transition and at least one boundary gate lie on `C*`; the `F/J` transition counts on `C*` are either `(3,1)` or `(2,0)`. In the `(2,0)` case the `v-B` transition occurs before the opposite boundary gate.

Keep every balanced component initially in its `J`-state. For `0≤q<m`, let `M_q` select `e_1,...,e_q,f_{q+1},...,f_{m-1}` on `C*`, and put

`S_q=Σ_{i≤q}(χ(e_i)-χ(f_i))`,

where `χ(x->y)=1` iff `x,y` lie on opposite sides of `X|B`. Let `r` be the first index with `S_r>0`; hence `S_q≤0` for `q<r`. The free split copies of `M_q` are `a_q,out` and `b_m,in`; for distinct physical labels define

`g_q=a_q->b_m`.

Assume `M_0=J` is physical.

For every physical pre-`r` prefix, the process below exits as one of:

1. a first nonphysical ordinary prefix whose every new blocker contains its named installed edge;
2. a canonical closure with only named endpoint-seam debt;
3. a rank-zero entrance state at `q=r-1`;
4. a zero-success source-source failure certificate; or
5. the rigid one-success first-positive kernel of Lemma 12.

These are compressor outputs; no downstream consumption is asserted.

## 1. Prefix and canonical-closure ledger

### Lemma 1. Marked prefixes

For `q<r`,

`τ(M_q)=1+S_q`

and `S_q∈{-1,0}`. If

`M_h=M_{h-1}-{f_h}+{e_h}`

is the first nonphysical ordinary prefix, every newly created blocker contains `e_h`.

**Proof.** Relative to `J=M_0`, only the zipper exchanges `f_i->e_i` for `i≤q` have changed, so

`τ(M_q)=1+S_q`.

Since `S_q≤0` and transition count is nonnegative, `S_q∈{-1,0}`. At a first failed prefix, deleting `f_h` cannot create a bad turn, selected backtrack, or directed cycle. Any new blocker not containing `e_h` would therefore already occur in physical `M_{h-1}`. ∎

### Lemma 2. Exact canonical ledger

If `q<r` and the free physical labels are distinct, `M_q+g_q` has exact-two-cover cardinality and transition count exactly one.

**Proof.** Modulo two, the weight-two condition gives

`Σχ(e_i)+Σχ(f_i)=0`.

Cancellation of internal zipper labels leaves `σ(a_0)=σ(b_m)`, where `σ` is the side label. Likewise,

`S_q ≡ Σ_{i≤q}(χ(e_i)+χ(f_i)) ≡ σ(a_0)+σ(a_q) ≡ χ(g_q) (mod 2)`.

Thus `χ(g_q)=0` when `S_q=0` and `χ(g_q)=1` when `S_q=-1`, so

`τ(M_q+g_q)=1+S_q+χ(g_q)=1`.

The edge `g_q` joins the two free split copies, raising a three-forest matching to exact-two-cover cardinality. This is a matching statement; physicality is treated next. ∎

## 2. Entrance topology

Fix a physical `M_q`, `q<r`.

### Lemma 3. Canonical topology

Exactly one of the following occurs.

1. The free physical labels coincide. Then both directed ports of that vertex are free, so it is a singleton rail and the formal canonical edge is a forbidden loop.
2. The labels are distinct but lie on the same rail. Then `g_q` closes that rail into a directed cycle, with a two-edge backtrack as the shortest case.
3. The labels are distinct and lie on different rails. Then `g_q` joins the terminal vertex of one rail to the initial vertex of another, creating neither a directed cycle nor a backtrack. All inherited turns remain tight, so the only possible new bad turns are the endpoint seams adjacent to `g_q`. If those seams are tight, Lemma 2 makes the result a physical exact two-cover with `τ=1`.

**Proof.** A physical selected matching is a disjoint union of directed tight paths. The free copies are one free OUT and one free IN. Their locations on the path components give the three cases immediately. In the cross-rail case the new edge concatenates two different paths, so only its adjacent turns are new. ∎

Call the loop and same-rail cases *topological entrance debt*.

### Lemma 4. One-step topological transfer

Suppose `q<r-1` and `M_q` has topological entrance debt. For

`M_{q+1}=M_q-{f_{q+1}}+{e_{q+1}}`,

either `M_{q+1}` is nonphysical, in which case every new blocker contains `e_{q+1}`, or `M_{q+1}` is physical and its two free physical labels are distinct and lie on different rails. Consequently the next canonical closure is topologically clean and, by Lemma 2, has matching transition count one; any remaining failure is seam-only.

**Proof.** First suppose the old free labels are distinct on the same rail `P`. Because `b_m,in` is free and `a_q,out` is free, `P` runs from `b_m` to `a_q`. Put

`f=f_{q+1}=a_{q+1}->b_{q+1}`, `e=e_{q+1}=a_q->b_{q+1}`.

If `f` lay on `P`, the directed order would contain

`b_m -> ... -> a_{q+1} -> b_{q+1} -> ... -> a_q`.

Deleting `f` leaves the suffix `b_{q+1}->...->a_q`, and adding `a_q->b_{q+1}` closes that suffix into a directed cycle. Hence physical `M_{q+1}` forces `f` onto another rail `R`.

Deleting `f` splits `R` into a prefix ending at `a_{q+1}` and a suffix beginning at `b_{q+1}`; adding `e` joins `P` to that suffix. Thus `b_m` lies on the joined rail while `a_{q+1}` is terminal on the separate `R`-prefix. They are on different rails. They are also distinct: if `a_{q+1}=b_m`, then the selected edge `f` would leave the initial vertex `b_m` of `P`, putting `f` on `P`, already excluded.

Now suppose the old state is a loop, `a_q=b_m=s`. Both ports of `s` are free, so `s` is a singleton rail. The selected edge `f` cannot start at `s`, since `s,out` is free. Thus `f` lies on another rail. Deleting it again makes a prefix ending at `a_{q+1}` and a suffix beginning at `b_{q+1}`; installing `e=s->b_{q+1}` joins the singleton `s` to the suffix. The new free labels `s` and `a_{q+1}` lie on different rails and are distinct, because equality would again force `f` to start at `s`.

If instead `M_{q+1}` is nonphysical, Lemma 1 localizes every new blocker at `e_{q+1}`. ∎

Every nonterminal loop, cycle, or backtrack state therefore either exits at once through a named `e_{q+1}` blocker or transfers, in the same fixed frame, to a later cross-rail canonical candidate. Since `q` increases, this cannot recur indefinitely before `r`. At `q=r-1` the topological state is recorded as the rank-zero output. A cross-rail nonphysical closure is already seam-only debt; a physical cross-rail closure enters the source chase below.

## 3. Physical canonical covers and the controlled source chase

Assume the canonical closure `T=M_q+g_q` is physical. By Lemma 2, `T` is an exact two-cover of `G` with `τ(T)=1`.

### Lemma 5. Tau-one source exposure

The two rails have block shape

`mixed X-B (or B-X) | isolated X`,

and at least two distinct retained sources are rail endpoints.

**Proof.** Let `b_X,b_B` be the total numbers of maximal `X`- and `B`-blocks on the two rails. Since both rails are nontrivial,

`τ(T)=b_X+b_B-2`.

If `b_X=1`, all four vertices of `X` occur consecutively in one tight block, giving a tight Hamilton path on `X`, forbidden by hypothesis. Hence `b_X≥2`; also `b_B≥1` because `B` is nonempty. Since `τ(T)=1`, necessarily `(b_X,b_B)=(2,1)`, giving exactly one mixed rail and one all-`X` rail.

The isolated `X` rail is nontrivial and therefore has a source endpoint, because `v` is the only nonsource in `X`. The mixed rail has one outer `X` endpoint. If it is a source, that is a second exposed source. If it is `v`, then `v` is absent from the isolated rail, so both endpoints of that rail are sources. ∎

### Lemma 6. Global neutral-frontier invariant

During the controlled chase below, every exposed source vacancy off `C*` lies at a frontier of a balanced alternating path component. On each such component the selected state is an `F`-prefix followed by a `J`-suffix, with the unique vacancy between them. A successful elementary pivot deletes the next selected `J\F` edge and installs the next previously unselected `F\J` edge. Consequently no later pivot removes an installed `F\J` edge.

**Proof.** First consider an exposed source `s` in the initial physical canonical cover. On `C*`, the closed prefix selects

`e_1,...,e_q,f_{q+1},...,f_{m-1},g_q`.

These edges occupy exactly the same split copies as the full `F`-state `e_1,...,e_m`: every `a_i,out` and every `b_i,in` is occupied. Common `F∩J` incidences are selected as well. Since `s` is internal in `F` but is an endpoint of the current cover, exactly one `F`-port of `s` is vacant. That port lies neither on `C*` nor on a common edge. Its `F`-incidence is therefore on a balanced component of `F△J`.

At that vacant split copy the component has an `F`-incidence but no selected `J`-incidence. Thus the component is an alternating path, initially in `J`-state, with an `F`-only endpoint at the vacancy and a `J`-only endpoint at the other end. Replacing the first selected `J\F` edge by its paired `F\J` edge moves the vacancy one step along the path. Inductively, after any number of successes on that component, the portion behind the vacancy is `F`-selected and the portion ahead is `J`-selected.

Now allow the chase to switch exposed sources and components. Every controlled pivot occurs off `C*`, so the `C*` occupancy never changes. On a previously untouched balanced component the preceding argument starts at its `F`-only endpoint. On a partially changed component, all nonfrontier split copies are occupied by exactly one of their `F`- or `J`-incidences; hence a vacant source port carrying an `F`-incidence can occur only at that component's existing frontier. The next legal pivot again replaces a selected `J\F` edge by an unselected `F\J` edge, moving the frontier forward. Therefore every component remains of `F`-prefix/`J`-suffix form and no installed `F\J` edge is ever deleted. ∎

Define

`μ = # {selected F\J edges on balanced components}`.

Every successful controlled pivot increases `μ` by exactly one, and `μ` is finitely bounded.

A source-incident missing `F`-edge off `C*` is one of the two source-source mates `s1->s2`, `s2->s3`, or a boundary source gate. The policy is to attempt an exposed source-source frontier; if an exposed source instead presents a boundary gate, reselect another exposed source as in the next lemma.

### Lemma 7. GATE avoidance and tau-one persistence

Whenever an exposed source presents a missing boundary gate, the current physical cover still has `τ=1` and another exposed source presents a missing source-source edge. Thus the controlled policy never needs to attempt a boundary gate. Every successful controlled pivot is source-source and leaves the next physical state at `τ=1`.

**Proof.** A missing gate cannot lie on `C*`, because `C*` keeps full `F` split-copy occupancy throughout the chase. Hence the `(3,1)` placement is impossible: there all three `F`-transitions lie on `C*`. We are in the `(2,0)` placement. Thus the `v-B` transition and the opposite boundary gate lie on `C*`, while the missing gate is the unique `F`-transition off `C*`. Since `τ(J)=1` and `C*` carries no `J`-transition in this cell, there is exactly one selected `J`-transition off `C*` initially.

Inductively all earlier successful controlled pivots were source-source and hence same-side. None can have deleted that unique `J`-transition: if one did, replacing it by a same-side `F`-edge would produce a physical exact two-cover with `τ=0`, whereas every physical exact two-cover here satisfies `τ=b_X+b_B-2≥1`. Therefore the unique transition remains selected and the current physical state has `τ=1`.

Lemma 5 now gives at least two exposed sources. The unique missing gate is incident with only its outer source. Choose a different exposed source `t`. Its vacant `F`-port is off `C*` by Lemma 6 and is source-incident. It cannot be that same gate, so its missing edge is one of the two source-source mates. Hence a source-source attempt is always available instead of GATE.

Conversely, a successful source-source pivot inserts a same-side edge. It cannot delete the unique transition, again because a physical `τ=0` exact two-cover is impossible. Hence every successful controlled state remains at `τ=1`. ∎

### Lemma 8. Finite chase and at most one successful SS pivot

The controlled chase reaches a failed source-source pivot after finitely many attempts, and before that failure at most one source-source pivot succeeds.

**Proof.** Every successful pivot raises the bounded integer `μ` from Lemma 6 and no later pivot lowers it. After every success the state is physical with `τ=1` by Lemma 7, so Lemma 5 again supplies exposed sources and Lemma 7 supplies another controlled source-source attempt. Therefore an all-success trajectory is impossible.

For the sharper bound, suppose both old source-source `F`-edges were simultaneously selected in a physical controlled state. Then `s2` has both ports occupied. Each outer source has its inner source-block port occupied. At least one of the two boundary gates lies on untouched `F`-like `C*`, so at least one outer source also has its boundary port occupied. Thus at most one retained source can be an endpoint. This contradicts Lemma 5, which requires at least two exposed sources in every physical `τ=1` state.

An installed source-source `F`-edge is never removed by Lemma 6. Hence two successful controlled pivots would force both source-source mates to be selected, impossible. If one mate was already `F`-like selected before the chase, the other therefore cannot succeed; that case belongs to the zero-success branch. Thus the first failed source-source pivot occurs after exactly zero or one successful controlled source-source pivots. ∎

## 4. Zero-success exit

Suppose the first controlled source-source attempt already fails. In the IN-vacancy orientation write

`e=x->d`, `f=x->d'`,

where `d,x` are consecutive retained sources, `e` is the missing old-`F` edge, `f` is the selected mate being deleted, and the physical predecessor rail `V` begins at `d`.

### Lemma 9. Zero-success certificate

If `f` lies on `V`, installing `e` creates a directed cycle. If `f` lies on the other rail, the switch is an acyclic tail swap; no backtrack is possible, and the only possible new bad turns are

`(p,x,d)` when the predecessor `p` of `x` exists, and

`(x,d,y)` when the successor `y` of `d` exists.

At least one existing seam is bad. Complete reversal turns a bad left seam into the tight cap `(d,x,p)` and a bad right seam into `(y,d,x)`. The OUT-vacancy orientation is the directional dual.

If the failed acyclic candidate has `τ=0`, its two rails are side-pure, one spanning all of `X` and the other all of `B`; the failed installed edge is source-source, and exactly one adjacent seam is bad.

**Proof.** Deleting `f` alone cannot create a blocker. If `f` lies on `V`, the directed segment of `V` from `d` through `x` survives the deletion, and `x->d` closes that segment into a directed cycle.

If `f` lies on the other rail, deleting it splits that rail into a prefix ending at `x` and a suffix beginning at `d'`; installing `x->d` joins the prefix to `V`. The joined pieces were disjoint directed paths, so the result is acyclic. A selected two-edge backtrack would require the same pair of physical vertices to have occupied both original rails, impossible. Every inherited turn remains tight, so only the two seams adjacent to `e` can newly fail. Since this is the first failed pivot, at least one existing seam is bad, and complete reversal gives the displayed caps.

For the sharpening, `τ=0` means there are no `X|B` transitions. Because the candidate is an exact two-cover spanning both sides, one rail contains all four `X`-vertices and the other all of `B`. If `e=x->d` is internal on the all-`X` rail and both adjacent seams were bad, their complete reversals would be `(d,x,p)` and `(y,d,x)`, which concatenate as the tight Hamilton path `y->d->x->p` on all four vertices of `X`, contrary to hypothesis. Thus at most one seam is bad; failure gives at least one. At an endpoint there is only one seam to begin with. ∎

We record the cycle geometry needed for the one-success branch.

### Lemma 10. SS-cycle reverse dimer

In a physical `τ=1` predecessor, if a source-source frontier `e=x->d` fails by CYCLE, then the vacancy rail beginning at `d` starts with the selected reverse edge `d->x`.

**Proof.** In the cycle case the selected mate `f=x->d'` lies on the vacancy rail `V` beginning at `d`.

If `f` is a transition, then `d'∈B`. Since the whole cover has only one transition, the part of `V` before `f` is entirely in `X`. The other rail is the nontrivial isolated-`X` rail of Lemma 5 and therefore contains at least two `X`-vertices. As `V` already contains the distinct `X`-vertices `d,x`, there is room for no further `X`-vertex before `f`; hence `V` begins `d->x->d'`.

If `f` is same-side, then `d'∈X`. The rail `V` cannot be the mixed rail, because it would already contain three distinct `X`-vertices while the nontrivial isolated-`X` rail requires at least two more. Hence `V` is itself the isolated-`X` rail. The mixed rail contains at least one `X`-vertex, so `V` has at most three; therefore `V=(d,x,d')`. Again its first edge is `d->x`. ∎

## 5. One-success rigidity

### Lemma 11. No two-source isolated dimer

A physical `τ=1` exact two-cover cannot have an isolated two-vertex `X`-rail `(s,t)` consisting of retained sources.

**Proof.** Let `Q` be the other rail. If `{A,C,s,t}` had a tight Hamilton path, that path together with `Q` would be a spanning two-cover of `H`. But such a path always exists. If `(A,s,t)` is tight, then `(C,A,s,t)` is tight because `(C,A,s)` is a cyclic rotation of `(A,s,C)`. If `(A,s,t)` is bad, complete reversal gives `(t,s,A)` tight, hence cyclic rotation gives `(s,A,t)` tight; together with `(A,t,C)` this gives the tight path `(s,A,t,C)`. Contradiction. ∎

### Lemma 12. Rigid one-success kernel

Suppose exactly one controlled pivot succeeds. Up to reversing the source-block orientation, it installs `s1->s2`. Then the remaining mate `s2->s3` is the failed controlled edge. The last physical predecessor is exactly

`(s1,s2,B_T) | (s3,v)`

for a nonempty tight `B`-path `B_T`; the failed candidate is

`(s1,s2,s3,v) | B_T`,

has `τ=0`, and has unique bad turn `(s2,s3,v)`. Hence `(v,s3,s2)` is tight. Moreover,

`e_r=a_{r-1}->v`,

`f_r=s3->v`,

`e_{r+1}=s3->B_R`.

The reverse source orientation gives the directional dual.

**Proof.** By Lemma 6 the installed edge `s1->s2` is never removed. Since no second SS pivot can succeed and GATE is never attempted, the remaining SS mate `s2->s3` must be the failed controlled edge.

It cannot fail by CYCLE: Lemma 10 would force selected `s3->s2`, colliding at `s2,in` with permanent `s1->s2`. Thus the failure is acyclic. One adjacent seam is the literal old-`F` turn `(s1,s2,s3)`, which is tight. Since the attempted candidate nevertheless fails, its other, outer seam exists and is the unique bad seam.

We next identify the second exposed source. In the IN presentation the vacant port is `s3,in`; then `s2` is internal, with `s2,in` occupied by `s1->s2` and `s2,out` by the selected mate being deleted. In the OUT presentation the vacant port is `s2,out`; `s3,in` is occupied by the selected mate and `s3,out` is occupied because the outer bad seam exists, so `s3` is internal. In either presentation Lemma 5 requires a second exposed source, and the only possibility is `s1`.

Because `s1,out` is occupied by `s1->s2`, its missing old-`F` port is its IN boundary gate `B_L->s1`. Lemma 6 places this missing gate off `C*`. Hence the `(3,1)` cell is impossible and we are in `(2,0)`: the `v-B` transition and opposite gate `s3->B_R` lie on `C*`, while `B_L->s1` is the unique `F`-transition off `C*`.

The successful SS pivot is same-side and, by Lemma 7, did not delete the unique off-`C*` `J`-transition. That transition therefore exhausts the current `τ=1` budget, so no `F`-transition on `C*` has yet been selected. In particular the original canonical coordinate satisfies `q<r` and all controlled moves remain off `C*`.

At the split copy `s3,out`, the later `F`-edge on `C*` is the right gate `s3->B_R`; before it is selected, the current selected incidence there is a `J`-edge `s3->w`. The `(2,0)` cell has no `J`-transition on `C*`, so `w∈X`. It is not `s1` because `s1,in` is free, not `s2` because `s2,in` is occupied by `s1->s2`, not `s3` because loops are forbidden. Thus `w=v`. Hence `s3->v` is selected, the unique outer bad turn is `(s2,s3,v)`, and complete reversal gives `(v,s3,s2)` tight.

The OUT presentation is impossible. If `s2,out` were the vacant source port, the selected mate entering `s3` would be `d'->s3`. Its tail cannot lie in `X`: `s1,out` is already occupied, `s2,out` is free, `d'=s3` is a loop, and `d'=v` would form a selected backtrack with `s3->v`. Hence `d'∈B`, so `d'->s3` is the unique transition of the `τ=1` predecessor. Every other selected edge is same-side. The rail containing `s1->s2` is therefore entirely in `X`; since `s3,v` lie on the other rail, that isolated `X`-rail is exactly `(s1,s2)`, contradicting Lemma 11. Thus the failed attempt is IN-presented at `s3`.

Its selected mate sharing `s2,out` is therefore `s2->d'`. The head `d'` cannot lie in `X`: `s1,in` is the exposed free gate port, `d'=s2` is a loop, `s3,in` is the vacancy, and `v,in` is occupied by `s3->v`. Hence `d'∈B`; write `d'=b0`. Thus `s2->b0` is the unique transition of the physical predecessor.

The rail beginning `s3->v` cannot continue into `B`, which would create a second transition, and cannot continue to another `X`-vertex because `s1,s2` lie on the other rail. Hence that rail is exactly `(s3,v)`. Since `s1,in` is free, the other rail begins at `s1`; after `s1->s2` it must enter `B` through `s2->b0` and then remain in `B`. Therefore it is exactly `(s1,s2,B_T)` with nonempty tight `B`-path `B_T=(b0,...)`.

Replacing `s2->b0` by the failed edge `s2->s3` gives

`(s1,s2,s3,v) | B_T`.

Both rails are side-pure, so this candidate has `τ=0`. The source-block turn `(s1,s2,s3)` is tight; the only other new turn is the already identified bad turn `(s2,s3,v)`. This proves the exact predecessor, failed matching, and unique cap.

It remains to locate the kernel on `C*`. Write the right gate as `e_k=s3->B_R`. The `J`-edge of `C*` sharing its OUT copy is `f_{k-1}=s3->b_{k-1}`. We have shown that the selected incidence at `s3,out` is `s3->v`, so `b_{k-1}=v`. The `F`-edge sharing `v,in` is therefore `e_{k-1}`. By hypothesis `v` has only one `F`-incidence, the unique `v-B` transition. In the `(2,0)` cell there are no `J`-transitions on `C*` and every earlier `F`-edge is same-side, so the cumulative zipper sum stays zero until this `v-B` edge and becomes positive there. Hence this edge is exactly `e_r`; thus `k-1=r`. Consequently,

`e_r=a_{r-1}->v`, `f_r=s3->v`, `e_{r+1}=s3->B_R`,

as required. ∎

## Completion

Sections 1 and 2 send every physical pre-`r` entrance state to a named first-prefix blocker, seam-only canonical debt, rank-zero entrance state, or a physical canonical exact two-cover with `τ=1`. Section 3 gives a finite controlled chase on every physical canonical output: all successful states remain `τ=1`, GATE is avoided, and at most one SS pivot succeeds. Section 4 gives the complete zero-success CYCLE/LEFT/RIGHT certificate, including the `τ=0` one-cap sharpening. Lemma 12 gives the exact one-success first-positive kernel and its directional dual. The cases are exhaustive and the fixed marked ancestry is preserved throughout. Therefore the theorem holds. ∎
