# E9005: Canonical defect exchange and sharp-root reduction

## Theorem

Let `H` be a smallest Strong Level-(1) boundary-tournament counterexample with `|H|>10`, and write

`G=H-{A,C}=X⊔B`, `X={v,s_1,s_2,s_3}`, `B=(b_0,...,b_m)`.

Assume `B` is tight, `X` has no Hamilton `P4`, `(A,s_i,C)` is tight for every source, and `F` is a physical exact two-cover of `G` in which all three sources are internal. Assume also

`τ(F)≥3`, `d_F(v)∈{1,2}`,

and that at least two sources are `B`-active. Let `J` be a physical spanning three-path forest with `τ(J)=1`, carrying the marked source ancestry supplied by the canonical low-cut construction.

Then the comparison of `F` and `J` yields one of the following exchange-level interfaces:

1. an exact-two-cover-cardinality matching `M` with `τ(M)<τ(F)`, physical when its selected turns are tight and its selected graph is acyclic, and otherwise carrying an explicit bad-turn, selected-backtrack, or directed-cycle defect;
2. the unique singleton-defect loop interruption;
3. a sharp source-rooted first-loss `K_{2,2}` rectangle, provided both adjacent closures exist; or
4. the sharp `v`-rooted marked package

   `τ(F)=3`, `τ(J)=1`, `w(C*)=2`,

   with physical `J`, a unique source-bearing `F`-heavy alternating path `C*`, `d_F(v)=1`, singleton `v`-block, a literal ordered three-source block, the exact `(2F,0J)/(3F,1J)` transition ledger on `C*`, the `v-B` edge as the first-positive pivot, and a later `X`-mated source boundary gate.

The fourth interface is the structural input required by E9001.

## 1. Symmetric-difference contraction

Compare `F` and `J` as directed split-vertex matchings. For every connected component `C` of `F△J`, define

`δ(C)=|F∩C|-|J∩C|∈{-1,0,1}`,

`w(C)=τ(F∩C)-τ(J∩C)`.

Since `F` has two path components and `J` has three,

`Σδ=1`.

Put

`d:=τ(F)-τ(J)=τ(F)-1≥2`.

Then `Σw=d`.

For any union `S` of whole alternating components, flip `S` from its `J`-state to its `F`-state. The result is again a directed matching and satisfies

`|M_S|=|J|+Σ_S δ`,

`τ(M_S)=1+Σ_S w`.

If some `S` has `Σ_Sδ=1` and `Σ_Sw<d`, then `M_S` has exact two-cover matching cardinality and `τ(M_S)<τ(F)`. If its selected graph is a tight acyclic two-path forest, it is a physical exact two-cover. Otherwise matching compatibility leaves a concrete physicality defect: a bad selected turn, a selected two-edge backtrack, or a directed cycle. This is interface 1.

Assume no such subfamily exists. If a zero-`δ` subfamily had positive weight, its complement would have `δ=1` and weight less than `d`; therefore every zero-`δ` subfamily has nonpositive weight. Let `P,N` be the numbers of `+1,-1` components. Then `P=N+1`, and every `+1` component has weight at least `d`.

Every unit of negative transition weight must spend a `J`-transition, while `J` has only one. Hence the total contribution of all nonpositive-weight components is at least `-1`, and therefore

`d=Σw≥Pd-1`.

Thus

`(P-1)d≤1`.

Since `d≥2`, we obtain `P=1` and `N=0`.

Hence there is a unique `F`-heavy alternating path `C*`, while every other alternating component is matching-balanced. Each balanced component has nonpositive weight. A balanced component not containing the unique `J`-transition has no `J`-transition, and therefore no `F`-transition either. At most one balanced component carries transition data, and it contains at most one `F`-transition.

Consequently `C*` contains at least `τ(F)-1≥2` old `F`-transitions. Since at least two distinct sources are `B`-active in `F`, at least one actual source-to-`B` incidence of `F` lies on `C*`. The unresolved exchange has therefore contracted to one source-bearing alternating path.

## 2. The zipper and the unique loop

Orient

`C*=e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2,...,f_{m-1},e_m`,

with `e_i∈F-J`, `f_i∈J-F`, and choose split-copy coordinates

`e_i=α_{i-1}->β_i`, `f_i=α_i->β_i`.

For a selected physical edge `g`, let `χ(g)=1` exactly when it crosses `X|B`, and define

`S_q:=Σ_{i≤q}(χ(e_i)-χ(f_i))`.

Replacing the first `q` `J`-edges of `C*` by the first `q` `F`-edges gives a proper-prefix matching of the same cardinality as `J` and transition count

`1+S_q`.

Because `w(C*)≥d≥2` while the terminal edge contributes at most one transition, some proper prefix has positive `S_q`. Let `r` be the first such index. Then

`S_r=1`, `χ(e_r)=1`, `χ(f_r)=0`.

Let `β_m` be the fixed far unmatched split copy of the alternating path. Whenever `α_q≠β_m` as physical labels, close the two unmatched copies by

`g_q=α_q->β_m`.

At `q=r` this gives exact two-cover matching cardinality.

Give each physical vertex its shore bit `σ(x)∈{0,1}` according as `x∈X` or `x∈B`. Since

`χ(u->v)=σ(u) xor σ(v)`,

telescoping modulo two gives

`S_q mod 2 = σ(α_0) xor σ(α_q)`,

`w(C*) mod 2 = σ(α_0) xor σ(β_m)`.

Hence

`χ(g_q)=(S_q mod 2) xor (w(C*) mod 2)`.  (1)

At the first positive prefix, the closure therefore has transition count `2` when `w(C*)` is odd and `3` when it is even.

The balanced components have nonpositive aggregate weight, while their total negative weight is at least `-1` because `J` has only one transition. Since

`d=w(C*)+Σ_D w(D)`,

we obtain

`d≤w(C*)≤d+1`.  (2)

Since `τ(F)=d+1`, the first-positive closure has transition count strictly below `τ(F)` except in the unique numerical cell

`τ(F)=3`, `τ(J)=1`, `w(C*)=2`.  (3)

Outside (3), the closure is interface 1: if physical, it is a lower-transition exact cover; otherwise it is the corresponding exact-cardinality matching together with its explicit physicality defect.

A closing edge fails to exist exactly when the two unmatched split copies have the same physical label, `α_q=β_m`; the proposed edge would then be a physical loop. The physical labels `α_q` are pairwise distinct along the alternating path, so this can happen for at most one index. Retain the corresponding prefix matching, in which that physical vertex has both split copies unmatched. This is interface 2, the unique singleton-defect loop.

It remains to analyze the sharp cell (3), assuming the closures needed below are not interrupted by that loop.

## 3. The sharp closure staircase

Normalize every matching-balanced component completely to its `F`-state. Let

`W_0:=Σ_D w(D)≤0`

be the aggregate balanced-component transition weight. In the sharp cell, `d=2` and `w(C*)=2`, so

`W_0=0`.  (4)

Let `\widehat M_q` denote the normalized prefix on `C*`. It has exact three-forest matching cardinality and

`τ(\widehat M_q)=1+S_q`.

Whenever the closure exists, define

`\widehat N_q=\widehat M_q+g_q`, `γ_q=χ(g_q)`.

Then

`T_q:=τ(\widehat N_q)=1+S_q+γ_q`.  (5)

Consecutive defined closures differ on exactly four split copies:

`\widehat N_q=\widehat N_{q-1}-{f_q,g_{q-1}}+{e_q,g_q}`.  (6)

Thus they form one literal `K_{2,2}` switch. By (1), since `w(C*)=2` is even,

`γ_q=S_q mod 2`.

Therefore

`T_q-T_{q-1}∈{-2,0,2}`,

and an upward `+2` step occurs exactly when the removed pair `{f_q,g_{q-1}}` consists of two same-shore edges while the installed pair `{e_q,g_q}` consists of two `X|B` edges.

For every `q<r`, first-positive minimality gives `S_q≤0`, while the selected normalized prefix has nonnegative transition count `1+S_q`; hence

`S_q∈{-1,0}`.

Whenever its closure exists, (5) gives `T_q=1`. At `r`, `S_r=1`, and if `g_r` exists then

`T_r=3=τ(F)`.

Thus `r` is exactly the first numerical loss of strict transition descent.

A first-loss rectangle is asserted only when both adjacent closures `g_{r-1}` and `g_r` exist. If either is the unique forbidden loop, interface 2 has already occurred. Otherwise (6) at `r` is the switch

`{f_r,g_{r-1}} <-> {e_r,g_r}`

from two same-shore edges to two crossing edges. Shore parity forces the old diagonal to contain one `X-X` edge and one `B-B` edge. Let `z∈X` be the physical `X`-endpoint of the newly installed old `F`-transition `e_r`; the old `X-X` member is incident with `z`.

If `z` is one of the three sources, retain this marked source-rooted first-loss rectangle. This is interface 3.

If `z` is not a source, then `z=v`, and we enter the `v`-rooted sharp cell.

## 4. The `v`-rooted marked package

In the `v`-rooted sharp cell, `e_r` is the distinguished selected `v-B` edge of `F`. Put `d_v=d_F(v)` and let `e_XX` be the number of selected `X-X` edges of `F`. The three sources are internal and contribute total selected degree six on `X`, so

`6+d_v=2e_XX+τ(F)=2e_XX+3`.

Since `d_v∈{1,2}`, parity forces

`d_v=1`, `e_XX=2`.  (7)

Thus the number of maximal `X`-blocks is

`b_X=4-e_XX=2`,

and from `τ(F)=b_X+b_B-2` we obtain `b_B=3`.

The unique selected edge incident with `v` is the displayed crossing edge `e_r`, so `{v}` is one `X`-block. The other `X`-block contains all three sources. Since all three are internal in `F`, that block is itself internal on a rail and, after complete reversal if necessary, occurs literally as

`B_L -> s_1 -> s_2 -> s_3 -> B_R`.  (8)

Hence the three `F`-transitions are exactly the unique `v-B` edge and the two source boundary gates `B_L->s_1` and `s_3->B_R`. Each source gate is `X`-mated by the adjacent internal source edge.

Let `n_F,n_J` be the numbers of `F`- and `J`-transitions on `C*`. Since `w(C*)=2`, while globally `F` has three transitions and `J` has one,

`n_F-n_J=2`, `0≤n_F≤3`, `0≤n_J≤1`.

Therefore

`(n_F,n_J)=(2,0)` or `(3,1)`.  (9)

The `v-B` edge `e_r` is the first-positive pivot. In the `(2,0)` case no negative `J`-transition occurs on `C*`; if an `F`-transition preceded `e_r`, a positive prefix would occur before `r`. Thus the other `F`-transition on `C*` is a source boundary gate strictly after `e_r`.

In the `(3,1)` case, if both source gates preceded `e_r`, their two positive contributions could be offset by at most the unique `J`-transition, again forcing a positive proper prefix before `r`. Hence at least one source boundary gate lies strictly later than the `v`-transition, and any source gate before `e_r` is neutralized by the unique `J`-transition.

We have therefore obtained interface 4:

`τ(F)=3`, `τ(J)=1`, `w(C*)=2`,

with physical `J`, a unique source-bearing `F`-heavy alternating path `C*`, `d_F(v)=1`, singleton `v`-block, the literal ordered source block (8), precisely the unique `v-B` transition and the two source boundary transitions in `F`, the `v-B` edge as first-positive zipper pivot, the alternatives (9), and a later `X`-mated source gate. The balanced components have aggregate transition weight zero; no componentwise physical normalization claim is needed.

Finally, every exact two-cover of `G=H-{A,C}` has two nontrivial rails. If `G` were Hamiltonian, its Hamilton path together with `(A,C)` would two-cover `H`. If an exact two-cover had singleton rail `{x}`, boundary antisymmetry on `{A,x,C}` would give a tight trimer on those three vertices; together with the other rail it would again two-cover `H`.

Thus interface 4 supplies exactly the nonambient input required by E9001: physical `J` with `τ(J)=1`, matching-balanced residual components, unique heavy path `C*`, first-positive `v`-index, the exact `(2F,0J)/(3F,1J)` ledger, singleton `v`-block and ordered source block, a later `X`-mated source gate, and nontriviality of every exact pair-deletion two-cover. Initializing every balanced component in its `J`-state makes the initial prefix literally `J`, and hence physical.

The proof uses only the canonical defect packet and performs no first-source phase-descent argument. Its four outputs are exchange interfaces; their downstream consumption lies outside this Engine. ∎
