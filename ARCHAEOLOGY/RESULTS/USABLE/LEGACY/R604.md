# R604 — Cross-end rotated return has no quiet internal-middle replay

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Retain the surviving exact-two-bad R24 cell, write L=q_0 and R=q_m, and let J_*=(L,b,R) be the tight cross-end trimer from R591. The long rail supplies graph-intrinsic signed terminal dimers T_L=(q_1,L), tail-signed by witness b, and H_R=(R,q_{m-1}), head-signed by witness b. Let H-{L,R}=U sqcup V be any exact two-cover in which b is internal on one selected rail, with predecessor r and successor s. Then either H has a spanning two-cover, or at least one of the following explicit non-replay anchor contacts occurs: (L) rLb is bad, so R3 gives bLr tight; the proper dimer (b,L) meets the old tail-signed support T_L at its signed anchor L and is physically different from T_L, so R436 yields strict growth, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a reverse-contact/reversal cell at the retained L-anchor. (R) bRs is bad, so R3 gives sRb tight; the proper dimer (R,b) meets the old head-signed support H_R at its signed anchor R and is physically different from H_R, so R436 yields the corresponding explicit nonquiet output at R. Indeed if neither seam is bad, replacing the selected singleton occurrence b in its rail by the tight segment L,b,R produces a spanning two-cover. Therefore every internal-b exact return of the rotated outer pair {L,R} is zero-quiet and consumes the original long-rail endpoint ancestry; the old b<->z source-replay mechanism cannot survive this rotated checkpoint.

## Hypotheses
Surviving exact-two-bad R24 cell with m>=6; endpoint stars q_1 L x and x R q_{m-1}; accepted cross-end trimer R591; exact pair-deletion cover supplied by R429; R3 and R436.

## Scope
Specialized R24 replay-breaker. Fully R5-free and representative-safe: the exact H-{L,R} cover is arbitrary and only its selected local neighbors of b are used.

## Applicability
Use after rotating to the cross-end outer pair {L,R}. If b is internal in any exact pair-deletion return, there is no protected quiet episode to budget: closure or an immediate physical anchor-contact certificate is forced.

## Persistence
Retain Q, J_*=LbR, old terminal signed supports (q_1,L) and (R,q_{m-1}) with common witness b, the exact H-{L,R} cover, b's selected neighbors r,s, the failed seam, its R3 mate dimer (b,L) or (R,b), and the R436 contact certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not consume the case in which b is an endpoint in the chosen H-{L,R} cover. Does not claim an R436 growth/cycle/reversal output alone closes H. Does not require the old Q terminal dimers to be selected in the new pair-deletion cover.

## Proof
The endpoint stars give q_1Lb and bRq_{m-1} tight. Hence the dimer T_L=(q_1,L) is tail-signed by b with signed anchor L, while H_R=(R,q_{m-1}) is head-signed by b with signed anchor R. By R429 choose any exact H-{L,R}=U sqcup V and suppose b is internal on one rail with selected predecessor r and successor s. Since J_*=(L,b,R) is tight, replacing the occurrence b by the three-vertex segment L,b,R changes only the two attachment turns at the old neighbors: rLb on the left and bRs on the right. If both are tight, the modified rail together with the untouched other rail is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Thus at least one attachment turn is bad. If rLb is bad, R3 gives bLr tight. Then Q_L=(b,L) is a proper tight dimer meeting T_L exactly in L, which is the signed anchor of T_L. Its support differs from T_L because b is in X while q_1 lies on Q. Apply R436 to the older tail-signed support T_L and later proper path Q_L. The exact-replay singleton exception is irrelevant because both are dimers, so R436 gives strict full-T_L growth, strict Q_L growth, a vertex-simple tight cycle, or a three-vertex reverse-contact/reversal cell. This is an explicit nonquiet L-anchor output. If bRs is bad, R3 gives sRb tight. Then Q_R=(R,b) is a proper dimer meeting H_R exactly in its signed anchor R and physically differing from H_R, so the head-side dual of R436 gives the corresponding nonquiet R-anchor output. Hence every internal-b return closes or consumes one of the original long-rail endpoint anchors.
