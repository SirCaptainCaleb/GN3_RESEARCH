# R314 — Smallest-counterexample order is at most twice longest proper-path order plus one

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample of order n, and let L be the maximum order of a proper tight path in H. Then n<=2L+1. For any vertex v, minimality gives a spanning two-cover of H-v by two tight paths P,Q. Both are proper tight paths of H, so |P|,|Q|<=L, while |P|+|Q|=n-1. Hence n-1<=2L. In particular L<=5 implies n<=11.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; L is the maximum order of a proper tight path in H.

## Scope
Order-free minimality/size inequality.

## Applicability
Use whenever another theorem bounds the global longest proper-path order.

## Persistence
The inequality depends only on n,L and smallest-counterexample minimality.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not itself bound L or close any fixed order.

## Proof
Fix v in V(H). Since H is a smallest counterexample, the proper induced subsystem H-v has a spanning cover by at most two tight paths P,Q. Each nonempty P,Q is also a tight path of H and is proper because v is omitted, so each has order at most L. Their vertex sets cover H-v and are disjoint as a path cover, hence |P|+|Q|=n-1. Therefore n-1<=2L and n<=2L+1.
