# R221 — One-residual terminal swallow reduces to the 2+3 / 3+(2-or-3) corner

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
In the anchored-capacity one-residual branch of a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample, follow the literal R171 peel lineage until its first non-descent output. If that output is bounded support swallow rather than a spanning two-cover, then the swallowed literal signed support S has order two or three. If |S|=2, the surviving third literal rail J has order exactly three. If |S|=3, J has order two or three. Thus the entire nonclosing one-residual branch terminates in the finite local size corner 2+3 or 3+(2 or 3).

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; an anchored-capacity balanced pair has ordinary reservoir of size one; the literal R171 peel continuation is followed until its first non-descent output.

## Scope
Order-free size compression of the only nonclosing one-residual anchored-capacity output.

## Applicability
Use after the one-residual capacity branch has been converted to a literal R171 frame. It replaces arbitrary bounded swallow by two explicit small-size cases suitable for local trimer/dimer analysis.

## Persistence
The size conclusion concerns the terminal literal peel frame. No claim is made that the original capacity supports or witnesses remain current after intermediate peel steps.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close either size corner. Does not assert the swallowed support is the original pre-peel capacity support. Does not apply to bounded contacts arising outside the one-residual literal R171 lineage.

## Proof
Start in the anchored-capacity one-residual branch of R221. Accepted R217 supplies a literal spanning three-cover

  H = J_0 sqcup P_0 sqcup T_0

in the scope of R171, where 1<=|J_0|<=3 and P_0,T_0 are the historical opposite-signed supports.

Follow the literal R171/P597 peel lineage through consecutive strict pair-mass descents. The mechanism of P597 is important here. A bad displayed seam is reversed by R3 to a tight contact trimer. If that trimer swallows an entire historical support, R171 is already in its bounded-swallow output. Otherwise R425 rebirths nonempty signed surviving intervals of the contacted supports and strictly lowers the integer pair mass M, with no fresh completion or representative reset. The good geometry remains in the same literal frame; the third rail is a surviving literal interval of the previous third rail. Thus successive strict descents may be iterated inside one literal no-recompletion lineage. Since both historical supports remain nonempty, M is a positive integer bounded below by two; indeed in any nonclosing literal frame M>=4, because if the union of the two support vertex sets had order at most three, those vertices would themselves admit one Hamilton tight path (dimer at order two, or an R3 trimer at order three), which together with the third rail would two-cover H. Therefore strict descent cannot continue indefinitely. A first non-descent output occurs after finitely many steps.

If that output is a spanning two-cover, we are done. Assume it is bounded support swallow. Write the terminal literal frame as

  H = J sqcup S sqcup T,

where S is the completely swallowed historical signed support and T is the untouched opposite-signed support. By the literal interval persistence just described, J is a surviving interval of J_0, so

  1 <= |J| <= 3.                                           (1)

By the bounded-swallow clause of R171, the failure/contact trimer contains all of S, hence

  1 <= |S| <= 3.                                           (2)

Also X:=V(S) union V(J) is exactly the physical complement of T. If |X|<=3, then X supports one Hamilton tight path: the order-one/two cases are vacuous singleton/dimer paths and the order-three case has a tight trimer orientation by R3. That Hamilton path together with T is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Thus

  |S|+|J| >= 4.                                            (3)

It remains to exclude equality in (3). Assume |X|=4. The R171 lineage retains the graph-intrinsic historical sign of the untouched support T. Let beta be its retained sign witness. By definition beta lies outside V(T); because H=J sqcup S sqcup T, we have beta in X.

We construct an exact singleton-deletion cover H-z=P sqcup T with |P|=3 and V(P) union {z}=X.

If (|S|,|J|)=(1,3), let S={z} and take P=J.

If (|S|,|J|)=(2,2), write S={z,w}. The three-set V(J) union {w} admits a tight trimer order P by R3.

If (|S|,|J|)=(3,1), write S={z,w_1,w_2}; the three-set V(J) union {w_1,w_2} admits a tight trimer order P by R3.

In every case P sqcup T is a literal two-cover of H-z. It is exact: if H-z were Hamiltonian, that Hamilton path together with singleton z would be a spanning two-cover of H.

Apply accepted R594 to this exact singleton-deletion row, using the same literal order

  T=(t_0,...,t_m)

from the terminal R171 frame as its long rail. R594 closes its small opposite-rail cases; since H is a counterexample, we are in its surviving normalization, where m>=4 and for every x in X the simultaneous endpoint reverse stars

  (t_1,t_0,x) tight,      (x,t_m,t_{m-1}) tight             (4)

hold.

Set x=beta. If T is head-signed by beta, then the retained historical sign extension gives

  (beta,t_0,t_1) tight,

while (4) gives its complete reversal (t_1,t_0,beta) tight, contradicting R3. If T is tail-signed by beta, the retained sign gives

  (t_{m-1},t_m,beta) tight,

while (4) gives its complete reversal (beta,t_m,t_{m-1}) tight, again contradicting R3. Therefore |X| cannot equal four.

Combining (1),(2),(3) with the exclusion of total four gives |S|+|J|>=5 while each coordinate is at most three. The only possibilities are

  (|S|,|J|)=(2,3),(3,2),(3,3).

This is exactly the conclusion of R221. No R24, R5, R168, R169, or R214 is used.
