# R563 — Trimer pure-rail both-bad CS cell at j=2 admits a suffix-preserving one-hole splice

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement

Retain a full R513 both-bad CS packet with (j,s)=(2,2). Thus the pure C-rail is the trimer K=(c_0,c_1,c_2), the Q-prefix of the seam is the dimer (q_0,q_1), the seam four-set is F={a,b,c,d} with a=q_0, b=q_1, c=c_0, d=c_1, both seam holes (a,b,c) and (b,c,d) are bad, and g=(b_r,q_2,q_3) exists as the isolated mixed-seam hole of the parent R511 proposal (since 2=j<=m-1 forces m>=3). R3 supplies the forced tight reverses (c,b,a) and (d,c,b), hence K*=(d,c,b,a)=(c_1,c_0,q_1,q_0) is tight. Write rho=(b,a,c_2)=(q_1,q_0,c_2) for the unique R513 right outer turn at s=2.

(1) The R513 reverse-four-segment proposal
U*=(c_1,c_0,q_1,q_0,c_2),\nV=(b_0,...,b_r,q_2,...,q_m)
has complete uncertified-turn set {g,rho}. If rho is tight, this is a literal one-hole proposal with sole hole g, so g is bad and reverse(g)=(q_3,q_2,b_r) is tight. If rho is bad, reverse(rho)=(c_2,q_0,q_1) is tight.

(2) The unique Hamilton P4 on F that retains the oriented K-boundary dimer (c,d)=(c_0,c_1) as its suffix is P_R=(b,a,c,d)=(q_1,q_0,c_0,c_1). It is tight if and only if (q_1,q_0,c_0) and (q_0,c_0,c_1) are tight.

(3) If P_R is tight, the spanning proposal
U_R=(q_1,q_0,c_0,c_1,c_2),\nV=(b_0,...,b_r,q_2,...,q_m)
has complete uncertified-turn set {g}: the two P_R turns are tight by hypothesis and (c_0,c_1,c_2) is a K-turn. Hence this is a literal one-hole proposal, g is bad, and reverse(g) is tight.

(4) If (q_1,q_0,c_0) is bad, then (c_0,q_0,q_1) is tight. If (q_0,c_0,c_1) is bad, then (c_1,c_0,q_0) is tight.

Therefore the R513 (2,2) cell reduces to a one-hole proposal with unchanged V and sole hole g unless both rho and P_R fail, in which case the exact missing turns are rho together with at least one P_R turn, each with its displayed R3 reverse. The construction rewrites only the spanning proposal and does not require D/S membership of q_0 or q_1.

## Hypotheses

H is finite Strong Level-(1) with pc(H)>2. The full accepted R513 both-bad CS packet is retained with the additional parameter restriction (j,s)=(2,2). Tight/bad are complementary by R3.

## Scope

Order-free coordinate-preserving refinement of the accepted R513 CS residue at the unique shallow cell where the pure C-rail is a trimer and the Q-seam prefix is a dimer. It does not treat j>=3 or s>=3, and it does not claim an SC dual beyond the R513 (s,i)=(2,m-2) cell.

## Applicability

In an R513 both-bad CS cell, read (j,s). If (j,s)=(2,2), first test rho=(q_1,q_0,c_2) on the R513 reverse splice; if it is tight, keep U* sqcup V as a one-hole proposal. Independently test the two P_R turns; if both are tight, use U_R sqcup V as a one-hole proposal. If both tests fail, retain the displayed reverse mates as the exact irreducible certificate. If (j,s) is not (2,2), this theorem does not apply.

## Persistence

Retain D,S,Q,T,K,B,A, the unique crossing orientation, cut index j=2, both bad seam holes, g, rho with its tight/bad status, the two P_R turns with their statuses, and the chosen one-hole proposal when certified. Forced reverses are graph-intrinsic; proposal coordinates are representative-relative.

## Exclusions / nonclaims

Does not assert that rho or P_R is always tight, that the both-bad residue is realizable, or that T decreases. Does not splice leftover vertices when j>=3 or s>=3. Does not enumerate anonymous P4 orders other than K* and the unique (c_0,c_1)-preserving order. Does not treat the SC side. Does not use R435, balanced-pair birth, or an unlabeled reverse trimer as an output.

## Proof

Work in the retained R513 both-bad CS packet with j=2 and s=2. Then a=q_0, b=q_1, K=(c_0,c_1,c_2), F={q_0,q_1,c_0,c_1}, the turns (q_0,q_1,c_0) and (q_1,c_0,c_1) are bad, and R3 gives (c_0,q_1,q_0) and (c_1,c_0,q_1) tight. In particular K*=(c_1,c_0,q_1,q_0) is a tight Hamilton P4 on F. The genuine three-hole CS range forces j<=m-1, so m>=3 and g=(b_r,q_2,q_3) is a defined mixed attachment on the unchanged rail V=(B followed by Q[2,m]).

(1) The R513 substitution at these parameters is U*=(c_1,c_0,q_1,q_0,c_2). Its consecutive triples are (c_1,c_0,q_1) (a K* turn), (c_0,q_1,q_0) (a K* turn), and rho=(q_1,q_0,c_2). Every consecutive triple of V is a B-turn, a Q-turn, or g. Thus the complete uncertified-turn set is {g,rho}. If rho is tight, the set reduces to {g}. A one-hole spanning proposal in this packet cannot have its unique hole tight: otherwise U* sqcup V would be a spanning two-cover, contradicting pc(H)>2. Hence g is bad and R3 gives reverse(g)=(q_3,q_2,b_r) tight. If instead rho is bad, R3 gives reverse(rho)=(c_2,q_0,q_1) tight.

(2) Any Hamilton P4 on F with suffix (c_0,c_1) has the form (x,y,c_0,c_1) with {x,y}={q_0,q_1}. The choice (q_0,q_1,c_0,c_1) begins with the bad seam hole (q_0,q_1,c_0). The remaining choice is P_R=(q_1,q_0,c_0,c_1), tight exactly when (q_1,q_0,c_0) and (q_0,c_0,c_1) are tight.

(3) Assume P_R is tight. Form U_R=(q_1,q_0,c_0,c_1,c_2). Its first two triples are the turns of P_R, hence tight. Its last triple is (c_0,c_1,c_2), a K-turn. The rail V is unchanged, with sole mixed attachment g. There is no omitted vertex: V(U_R)= {q_0,q_1} union V(K) and V(V)=V(B) union V(Q[2,m]), which together exhaust V(H) because {q_0,q_1}=Q[0,j-1] at j=2. Therefore the complete uncertified-turn set is {g}, g is bad, and reverse(g) is tight.

(4) If (q_1,q_0,c_0) is bad, R3 gives (c_0,q_0,q_1) tight. If (q_0,c_0,c_1) is bad, R3 gives (c_1,c_0,q_0) tight.

No vertex of T is moved, and the D/S membership of q_0,q_1 is unused, because both displayed proposals rewrite the spanning R511/R513 rails rather than exchanging blocks of H-D.
