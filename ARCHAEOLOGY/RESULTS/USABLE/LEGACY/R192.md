# R192 — Five-exterior four-of-five replacement saturation

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K be a proper tight path whose exterior E=V(H)\\V(K) has exactly five vertices. Then K is globally longest. If E itself is not Hamiltonian, then for every k in V(K), at least four of the five sets (E\\{z}) union {k}, z in E, support tight five-vertex paths. Equivalently, for each carrier vertex k there is at most one exterior hole z for which the one-vertex exterior replacement E-z+k fails to support a P5.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; K is a proper tight path with exactly five exterior vertices E. The nontrivial branch assumes E is not Hamiltonian, since otherwise K and E already form a spanning two-cover.

## Scope
Arbitrary order. Support-level amplification at the universal five-exterior floor; no order-ten, sign, payment, or exact-return hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use in any higher-order smallest-counterexample analysis once a proper path reaches the universal five-exterior floor. It produces a dense carrier-by-hole grid of exterior-side P5 replacements; a single complementary carrier-side replacement K-k+z would immediately give a spanning two-cover.

## Persistence
K, E, and all forced five-path supports are graph-intrinsic. The result is independent of representative changes.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the complementary set (K\\{k}) union {z} is a tight path. Does not imply that the forced P5 is globally longest when |H|>10. Does not prove existence of a five-exterior path in every smallest counterexample.

## Proof
Let E be the five-vertex exterior of K. By R23, K is globally longest. If E supports a tight five-path, then K and that path form a spanning two-cover, so in a smallest counterexample E is non-Hamiltonian. Fix k in K. For arbitrary distinct x,y in E, let P=E\\{x,y}. By R8, P has a tight trimer orientation. Apply R146 to this trimer and x,y,k. One of E, (E\\{y}) union {k}, (E\\{x}) union {k} supports a tight P5. The first is impossible, hence at least one of the two replacement supports is tight. Therefore no two distinct holes can both be failed replacement columns for the same k. Thus at most one z in E fails, and at least four of the five supports (E\\{z}) union {k} are tight P5 supports.
