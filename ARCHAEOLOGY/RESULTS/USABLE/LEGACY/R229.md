# R229 — Small-complement balanced pairs recycle to the five/six-exterior gateways

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let (alpha;P),(T;beta) be any balanced opposite-sign pair with vertex-disjoint tight supports. Put W=V(H)\\(V(P) union V(T)). If 1<=|W|<=3, then there is a finite chosen continuation with the following outcome: either a spanning two-cover occurs, or the terminal literal pair frame has an untouched opposite support whose exterior has size five or six and therefore enters the accepted R192 five-exterior replacement-saturation gateway or the accepted R196 six-exterior four-root gateway. More precisely, after finite R171 descent any nonclosing bounded swallow has size pattern (swallowed support, surviving third rail) equal to (2,3),(3,2), or (3,3); the first two give a five-exterior carrier, while the last gives a six-exterior carrier.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; P,T are vertex-disjoint historical opposite-signed tight supports; their physical complement W has order one, two, or three.

## Scope
Order-free balanced-pair small-complement theorem. No anchored-capacity, exterior-slack, order-ten, or prescribed-root hypothesis.

## Applicability
Use whenever pair growth/capacity leaves at most three ordinary physical vertices outside the two signed supports. It converts near-spanning pair geometry directly into the existing five/six-exterior gateway programs.

## Persistence
The initial signs are historical. A tight path on W makes the initial R171 frame literal. Strict peel descendants stay within that chosen literal lineage. The final five/six-exterior carrier and its exterior are literal in the terminal frame; R192/R196 then have their standard persistence.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close R192 or R196. Does not apply when the physical complement has four or more vertices. Does not make pair mass globally monotone outside the chosen R171 peel lineage.

## Proof
Retain the hypotheses of R229: H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample, (alpha;P),(T;beta) is a balanced opposite-sign historical pair with vertex-disjoint nonempty tight supports, and

  W = V(H) \ (V(P) union V(T))

has order between one and three.

INITIAL LITERAL FRAME. Choose one tight path J_0 spanning W. For |W|=1 this is the singleton, for |W|=2 the dimer, and for |W|=3 an appropriate tight trimer orientation exists by R3. Hence

  H = J_0 sqcup P sqcup T

is a literal spanning three-cover in the scope of accepted R171, and |J_0|<=3.

FINITE LITERAL DESCENT. Follow the fully reconstructible preferred R171/P597 peel lineage. A spanning-two-cover output closes. Every strict-descent output is generated by an exact reversed seam/contact trimer and Signed-Interval Rebirth inside the same literal no-recompletion lineage; it keeps both historical opposite-signed supports nonempty, leaves the third rail as a surviving interval of the previous third rail, and strictly lowers the integer pair mass. Consequently strict descent cannot continue indefinitely. There is a first non-descent output.

Assume this output is bounded support swallow rather than a spanning two-cover. Write the terminal literal frame

  H = J sqcup S sqcup T,

where S is the completely swallowed historical signed support and T is the untouched opposite support. Literal interval persistence gives

  1 <= |J| <= |J_0| <= 3,                                 (1)

and the R171 swallow clause gives

  1 <= |S| <= 3.                                           (2)

Put E=V(S) union V(J), the exact physical exterior of T. If |E|<=3, then E supports a Hamilton tight path, by trivial singleton/dimer structure or R3 in order three. That path together with T would be a spanning two-cover. Hence every nonclosing terminal swallow has |E|>=4.

EXCLUDE |E|=4. The R171 lineage preserves the graph-intrinsic historical sign ancestry of the untouched support T. Let beta' be its retained exact sign witness. Since beta' lies outside T and H=J sqcup S sqcup T, we have beta' in E.

If |E|=4, construct an exact singleton-deletion two-cover H-z=Q sqcup T with |Q|=3 and V(Q) union {z}=E. For profile (1,3), take z to be the singleton S and Q=J. For (2,2), delete one S-vertex z and orient the other S-vertex together with the dimer J as a tight trimer Q by R3. For (3,1), delete one S-vertex z and orient the other two S-vertices together with singleton J as a tight trimer Q by R3. In each case Q sqcup T is exact because Hamiltonicity of H-z together with singleton z would two-cover H.

Apply accepted R594 to Q sqcup T, retaining the literal terminal order T=(t_0,...,t_m) as the long rail. In the surviving counterexample normalization R594 gives for every e in E

  (t_1,t_0,e) tight,      (e,t_m,t_{m-1}) tight.            (3)

Set e=beta'. If T is head-signed by beta', its retained sign contains (beta',t_0,t_1), while (3) contains the complete reversal (t_1,t_0,beta'), contradicting R3. If T is tail-signed by beta', its retained sign contains (t_{m-1},t_m,beta'), while (3) contains the complete reversal (beta',t_m,t_{m-1}), again contradicting R3. Therefore |E| is not four.

By (1),(2), every nonclosing terminal exterior E has order exactly five or six. If |E|=5 and E itself supports a Hamilton P5, that P5 together with T two-covers H. Otherwise accepted R192 applies to the proper tight carrier T with non-Hamiltonian five-vertex exterior E and yields the five-exterior four-of-five replacement-saturation gateway. If |E|=6, accepted R196 applies to T and E and yields the six-exterior four-root singleton fan.

Thus the finite chosen R171 continuation from the original small-complement balanced pair ends either in a spanning two-cover or in exactly the accepted R192/R196 five/six-exterior gateways, proving R229. No R24, R5, R168, R169, or R214 is used.
