# R238 — Three consecutive common-four-core completions do not locally force P6

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
There exists a seven-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with F={0,1,2,3} and a tight turn (4,5,6) such that each of F union {4}, F union {5}, and F union {6} supports a tight P5, but the system contains no tight P6. Explicit P5 orders are (1,3,2,0,4), (3,1,2,0,5), and (1,0,3,2,6). Hence the local support pattern supplied by an R237 consecutive-success triple cannot by itself be converted into a six-path; any O3 use of R237 must additionally consume global-longest carrier order, exact-cover minimality, ancestry, or another global constraint.

## Applicability

## Proof
The listed tournament tables define a complete Level-(1) boundary-antisymmetric turn system. Direct substitution verifies (4,5,6), (1,3,2,0,4), (3,1,2,0,5), and (1,0,3,2,6) are tight. Exhaustive checking of the 7P6=5040 ordered six-tuples shows none has all four consecutive turns tight. Thus all three common-four-core one-vertex completions coexist with the tight carrier triple but no P6 exists.
