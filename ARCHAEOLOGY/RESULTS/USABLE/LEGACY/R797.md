# R797 — The R795 two-stage bridge is a short endpoint-X-middle path or a nontrivial X-split

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Apply accepted R435 to the two actual T rails relative to the old path Q. Then at least one of the following holds. (EAR) R435 yields an explicit old-state reversal, reverse trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle. (BRIDGE) Some T rail contains a literal tight subpath either (L,x_1,...,x_k,q_i) or (q_i,x_1,...,x_k,R), where 3<=i<=m-3, 1<=k<=4, and x_1,...,x_k are distinct vertices of X. The displayed endpoint and q_i are consecutive Q-contacts along that rail, so every internal vertex of the bridge lies in X. (SPLIT) The two T rails separate the physical endpoints from the middle interval: for a nonempty proper partition X=X_E disjoint-union X_M, one rail has vertex set {L,R} union X_E and the other has vertex set M union X_M. Outside EAR the endpoint rail encounters L before R, and the middle rail encounters its Q-vertices in the literal increasing order q_3,...,q_{m-3}. In particular the arbitrary TWO-STAGE geometry collapses to a bounded endpoint-to-middle X-bridge or a three-size X-split residue.

## Hypotheses
Retain accepted R795 in its TWO-STAGE branch in the accepted crossed frame, m>=6. Thus D={u,v,d,s}, M=Q[3,m-3], T is the fixed exact two-cover of H-D, T selects an endpoint-X state between {L,R} and X, T selects an X-M state, and T has no selected endpoint-M state.

## Scope
Same-cover normalization of accepted R795 TWO-STAGE. Uses only path-cover connectivity and accepted Reverse Ear R435. No transition minimization, R7, capture, payment, R436, R508 beyond accepted R795, or computation.

## Applicability
Use after accepted R795 TWO-STAGE. First compare each actual cover rail with Q via R435. Outside reverse geometry, if a rail meets both a physical endpoint and M, retain the shortest segment between consecutive such Q-contacts as BRIDGE. If no rail meets both, retain the forced nontrivial X partition between the endpoint rail and middle rail.

## Persistence
Retain the exact cover T and actual rail orientations. In BRIDGE retain the endpoint, q_i, ordered internal X list, and the fact that they are consecutive Q-contacts. In SPLIT retain X_E,X_M, both complete rail vertex sets, L-before-R order, and the increasing order of all M contacts.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim BRIDGE or SPLIT alone closes H. Does not prescribe the internal X order in SPLIT, does not say the endpoint-X and X-M states from R795 share an X vertex, and does not claim the two R795 selected states lie on one rail. A vertex-simple tight cycle from R435 is constructive output, not contradiction.

## Proof
Compare each of the two actual T rails with the original tight path Q using accepted R435. If either comparison has consecutive Q contacts in decreasing Q order, retain the corresponding R435 reversal, reverse-trimer/contact, or vertex-simple tight cycle and we are in EAR. Hence assume no such output. R435 then guarantees that the Q vertices encountered on each T rail occur in increasing Q order. Suppose first that some T rail contains both a physical endpoint from {L,R} and a vertex of M. Along this rail list its Q contacts in their actual order. Since they are increasing, if L occurs on the rail then the first M contact after L, say q_i, is adjacent to L in the Q-contact list; if R occurs, the last M contact before R is adjacent to R in that list. At least one such endpoint-middle adjacent pair exists. The rail segment between the pair is a literal tight subpath. By adjacency in the Q-contact list it has no internal Q vertex, so all internal vertices lie in X. Because the TWO-STAGE hypothesis excludes a selected endpoint-M state, the segment has at least one internal X vertex. Since X has four vertices, it has at most four. Its actual orientation is therefore either (L,x_1,...,x_k,q_i) or (q_i,x_1,...,x_k,R), yielding BRIDGE. Now suppose no T rail contains both a physical endpoint and a vertex of M. If L and R lay on different rails, every rail would contain an endpoint, leaving no rail able to contain the nonempty set M without violating the supposition. Thus L and R lie on the same rail T_E. Since no endpoint rail meets M, all vertices of M lie on the other rail T_M; M cannot split across the two rails because T_E would then meet M. The remaining vertices X partition according to the two rails, say X_E on T_E and X_M on T_M. Accepted R795 supplies a selected endpoint-X state, so X_E is nonempty. It also supplies a selected X-M state, so X_M is nonempty. Hence X_E,X_M form a nonempty proper partition of X. Outside EAR, R435 gives L before R on T_E and increasing Q-contact order q_3,...,q_{m-3} on T_M. This is SPLIT and exhausts the possibilities.
