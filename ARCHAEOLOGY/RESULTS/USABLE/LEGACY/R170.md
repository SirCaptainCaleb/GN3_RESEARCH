# R170 — Universal Proper-Turn Front Door

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Every hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample H contains a proper graph-intrinsic tight turn J=(a,b,c). More strongly, for any three distinct vertices a,b,c, exactly one of (a,b,c) and (c,b,a) is tight; since |H|>3, either orientation is a proper tight path whenever it is the tight one.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample to the Strong Level-(1) Two-Cover Conjecture.

## Scope
Global entry lemma for the Strong smallest-counterexample setting.

## Applicability
Allows any argument whose only entry requirement is a chosen proper tight turn to start immediately, without first producing a longest-path portal, fan, pivot, marker, collision, or paid pair. It is the upstream entry theorem referenced by the historical fixed-turn lineage.

## Persistence
The chosen turn is graph-intrinsic and remains a valid tight path after representative changes. Any fixed-turn lineage built on it must keep the same physical turn identity if its own theorem requires that.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not itself produce a balanced pair, descent, endpoint exposure, exact deletion frame, or two-cover. It does not say the fixed-turn lex clock controls arbitrary representative resets.

## Proof
Take any three distinct vertices a,b,c. By Boundary Antisymmetry R3, exactly one of abc and cba is tight, so H contains a tight trimer J on those vertices. It remains only to see that J is proper. A graph with at most two vertices has a spanning cover by at most two one- or two-vertex tight paths. A graph on exactly three vertices has a spanning tight trimer by the same antisymmetry argument. Therefore a counterexample has more than three vertices, and J is a proper tight path.
