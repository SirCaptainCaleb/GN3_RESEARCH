## Statement

Probe the same trimer by rho=q_{m-3}. If C_L union {rho} supports a Hamilton P4, retain it. Otherwise both L and rho are no-P4 extensions of the same tight trimer C_L, so their exact R516 signatures agree by accepted R522. For the L-cell, the R516 A-bit is A_L=[(v,u,L) tight]. But the old Q turn (L,u,v) is tight, so boundary antisymmetry makes (v,u,L) bad and A_L=0. Hence A_rho=0 as well: (v,u,rho) is bad, and R3 forces the explicit all-Q trimer (rho,u,v)=(q_{m-3},q_1,q_2) tight. Along this trimer the consecutive Q contacts rho then u occur in reverse Q-order and are nonadjacent because m>=6. Accepted R435 therefore yields a genuine nonadjacent Reverse-Ear output: a tight reverse trimer at one of the corresponding Q seams or a vertex-simple tight cycle; the automatic adjacent-old-state reversal branch is impossible here. RIGHT dually: suppose C_R=(v,s,d)=(q_2,q_{m-1},q_{m-2}) is tight and its probe by R=q_m is P4-free. Probe by w=q_3. If P4 occurs, retain it. Otherwise the two no-P4 probes have the same R516 signature. For the R-cell the B-bit is B_R=[(R,s,d) tight], but Q contains (d,s,R), so R3 gives B_R=0. Hence B_w=0, so (w,s,d) is bad and R3 forces the explicit all-Q trimer (d,s,w)=(q_{m-2},q_{m-1},q_3) tight. Its consecutive contacts s then w are reverse and nonadjacent on Q, so R435 again yields a reverse trimer or vertex-simple tight cycle, never merely an adjacent-state reversal. Thus the double-no-P4 terminal residue of R781/R786 is already P4 or specific nonadjacent Q-relative Reverse-Ear geometry, without constructing or enumerating the R522 Hamilton P5.

## Proof
LEFT. Let C=(a,b,c)=(v,u,d) and let y=L, z=rho. By hypothesis C union {y} is P4-free. Apply R518 to C with z; if P4 occurs, stop. Otherwise C union {z} is also P4-free. Accepted R516 assigns signatures sigma_y,sigma_z in {00,01,10,11}; accepted R522 proves that two no-P4 extensions of one tight trimer have the same signature. In the R516 notation A_y means (a,b,y)=(v,u,L) tight. The old Q turn (L,u,v) is tight, and it is the complete reversal of (v,u,L), so R3 makes (v,u,L) bad. Thus A_y=0, hence A_z=0. Therefore (v,u,rho) is bad, so R3 gives its complete reversal (rho,u,v) tight. Set K=(rho,u,v). All three vertices lie on Q, and along K the first two contacts occur at Q-indices m-3 then 1. Since m>=6, m-3>1+1, so this is a reverse nonadjacent pair of consecutive K-contacts. Apply accepted R435 to P=Q and K. Its adjacent-reversal special case cannot apply; hence the output is a tight reverse trimer at a Q/K seam or a vertex-simple tight cycle. RIGHT. Let C=(a,b,c)=(v,s,d), y=R, z=w. If the z-probe gives P4, stop; otherwise the y- and z-cells are P4-free and have the same R516 signature by R522. Here B_y means (y,b,c)=(R,s,d) tight. Q contains (d,s,R), its complete reversal, so R3 makes (R,s,d) bad and B_y=0. Hence B_z=0, so (w,s,d) is bad and R3 gives (d,s,w) tight. For K=(d,s,w), the consecutive contacts s=q_{m-1} then w=q_3 occur in reverse Q-order with gap at least two because m>=6. R435 again lies in its nonadjacent branch and yields a reverse trimer or vertex-simple tight cycle. This proves the dual conclusion.
