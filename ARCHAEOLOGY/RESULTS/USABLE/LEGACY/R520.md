# R520 — Common-anchor strict-spoke count fails without tested-order alignment

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
The quantitative strict-spoke conclusion of historical R252 is false without tested-order alignment. Already for t=3, the exact R516 doubly-misaligned 00 no-P4 signature gives a Strong four-vertex common-anchor cell in which every spoke receives the two expected outer-witness certificates but the two certificates on each spoke use opposite tested dimer orders. Hence no R38 interaction on that spoke is forced from those two certificates alone. In particular R252's claimed lower bound of at least t-2=1 opposite-polarity strict spoke would imply, via R263 on a common tested order, a Hamilton P4 on the same four vertices, contradicting the exact no-P4 cell. The defect is the parity comparison across repeated occurrences of one physical spoke while allowing tested dimer order to change. The tested-order-safe replacement is R519: every common-anchor spoke has certificates from all other outer witnesses for t>=3; when t>=4, pigeonhole among the two tested dimer orders guarantees an R38 interaction on every spoke; when t=3 even that interaction need not be forced. No uniform lower bound on strict opposite-polarity spokes follows without extra alignment hypotheses.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry. Historical R252 is the object under correction. Accepted R263 and corrected R516/R519 are available.

## Scope
Reconstructible correction fence for the invalid strict-count theorem and for the overstrong t=3 interaction residue that survived into R307/R308.

## Applicability
Use to block any descendant that imports R252's strict-spoke count or assumes every t=3 spoke has a comparable witness pair. Use R519 for the exact tested-order-safe replacement.

## Persistence
The R516 00 cell is a graph-intrinsic counterexample. The corrected R519 witness certificates retain their tested orders; for t>=4 the chosen same-order witness pair and R38 output are graph-intrinsic.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not rule out strict-spoke counts under stronger geometry or explicit tested-order alignment. Does not deny the individual witness certificates. For t>=4 it does not deny, and R519 proves, an R38 interaction on every spoke.

## Proof
Take the exact R516 signature 00 on {a,b,c,y} and choose common anchor b with outer set {a,c,y}. The three centered b-middle trimers are exactly the common-anchor fan data. Inspect the explicit 00 row. On each physical b-spoke, its two outer-witness certificates use opposite tested dimer orders. Thus no pair of certificates on that spoke is automatically a same-oriented-dimer input to R38. This already shows why the historical parity multiplication is ill-posed. Moreover historical R252 claims that for t=3 at least one spoke is forced into the strict opposite-polarity branch. Such a strict same-oriented-dimer pair would satisfy R263 and produce a Hamilton tight P4 on these four vertices, contradicting the exact no-P4 property of R516. Hence the quantitative strict-count theorem is false. The correct general theorem is R519. Every spoke always receives all t-1 outer witnesses with tested orders retained. At t=3 no interaction need be forced, as the 00 cell demonstrates. At t>=4, at least three certificates lie on each physical spoke, so two share one of the two tested orders and R38 applies. No strict-versus-collision count follows without additional alignment.
