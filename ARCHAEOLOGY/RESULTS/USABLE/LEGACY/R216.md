# R216 — Two-thirds Hamilton-five density in every vertex set

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let W be any r-vertex subset, r>=6, of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Let h_5(W) be the number of five-subsets of W that support a tight five-vertex path. Then h_5(W) >= (2/3) binom(r,5). Equivalently, at least two-thirds of all physical five-subsets of W are Hamiltonian P5 supports.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; W is any finite vertex subset of order r>=6; accepted four-of-six Hamilton-five density R195.

## Scope
Order-free local density amplification by pure double counting.

## Applicability
Use for arbitrary exterior slack. Any exterior or residual vertex set of size at least six is not merely guaranteed to contain one P5: a uniform two-thirds fraction of all its five-subsets are P5 supports. This gives a scalable combinatorial reservoir for synchronized-root and replacement arguments.

## Persistence
The Hamiltonian five-support count is graph-intrinsic on W.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert a P6 or longer path, does not prescribe compatible P5 orderings, does not imply a common omitted vertex, and does not by itself yield exterior-size descent or a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
Let I be the set of pairs (S,F) with F subset S subset W, |S|=6, |F|=5, and F supporting a tight P5. By R195, each six-set S contains at least four Hamiltonian five-subsets, so |I|>=4 binom(r,6). On the other hand, each Hamiltonian five-subset F of W is contained in exactly r-5 six-subsets S=F union {x}, x in W-F. Hence |I|=(r-5) h_5(W). Therefore (r-5)h_5(W)>=4 binom(r,6). Using binom(r,6)=binom(r,5)(r-5)/6 and r>=6, divide by r-5 to obtain h_5(W)>= (4/6)binom(r,5)=(2/3)binom(r,5).
