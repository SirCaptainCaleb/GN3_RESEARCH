# R320 — Dual-clique Hamilton-five density amplification

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let W be any r-vertex subset, r>=6, of a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Let B be the family of five-subsets of W that do not support a tight P5, and write b=|B| and M=binom(r,5). Then\n\n b/M <= beta_r := [15 + sqrt(225+60(r-4)(r-5))]/[30(r-4)].\n\nEquivalently the Hamiltonian five-support density is at least 1-beta_r. Combining with R216 gives bad-density at most min(1/3,beta_r). For every r>10 one has beta_r<1/3, so this strictly improves the two-thirds Hamilton-five density theorem. In particular for r=11, b<=148 and therefore at least 314 of the 462 five-subsets support tight P5s. As r tends to infinity, beta_r tends to 1/sqrt(15), so the Hamiltonian five-support density is asymptotically at least 1-1/sqrt(15) approximately 0.7418.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry; W is an r-set with r>=6; accepted four-of-six Hamilton-five density R195.

## Scope
Order-free support-density theorem. Pure finite set-system/Johnson-graph counting; no smallest-counterexample, carrier, deletion, sign, payment, or representative hypotheses.

## Applicability
Use for higher-order exterior/residual sets when the coarse R216 two-thirds density is insufficient. The structural proof retains both union-six-set and intersection-four-core clique localization, making it suitable for occupancy/generalization arguments. A separate spectral proof route should also be retained because it may generalize differently.

## Persistence
The bad-support family B, its Johnson edges, six-set clique occupancies, and four-core occupancies are graph-intrinsic finite-set data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not force P6 or longer paths, does not prescribe compatible P5 orderings, does not by itself yield a two-cover, and does not assert the density bound is sharp. For r<=10 the displayed beta_r bound need not improve R216, so the combined min(1/3,beta_r) is the recommended numerical form.

## Proof
Write B for the non-Hamiltonian five-subsets of W and b=|B|. Regard the five-subsets as vertices of J(r,5). Every Johnson edge A-Aprime has |A union Aprime|=6 and belongs to the unique union-indexed K6 consisting of the six five-subsets of that six-set. By R195, every six-set contains at most two bad five-subsets, hence each such K6 contains at most one edge of the induced graph on B. Therefore e(B)<=binom(r,6). Now decompose the same Johnson edges by their unique four-set intersection. For each four-set S subset W put x_S=|{A in B:S subset A}|. The five-sets containing S form a clique K_{r-4}, and every Johnson edge has exactly one four-set intersection, so e(B)=sum_S binom(x_S,2). Also sum_S x_S=5b because each bad five-set contains five four-sets. Hence 2e(B)=sum_S x_S^2-5b >= (sum_S x_S)^2/binom(r,4)-5b =25b^2/binom(r,4)-5b by Cauchy. Combining with e(B)<=binom(r,6) gives 25b^2/binom(r,4)-5b<=2binom(r,6). Put M=binom(r,5), use binom(r,4)=5M/(r-4) and binom(r,6)=(r-5)M/6, and set alpha=b/M. After division by M this becomes 15(r-4)alpha^2-15alpha-(r-5)<=0. The positive root is beta_r=[15+sqrt(225+60(r-4)(r-5))]/[30(r-4)], proving alpha<=beta_r. At r=11 the resulting real upper bound is b<149, so b<=148 and h_5(W)>=462-148=314. Finally beta_r<1/3 exactly when r>10, and beta_r tends to 1/sqrt(15).
