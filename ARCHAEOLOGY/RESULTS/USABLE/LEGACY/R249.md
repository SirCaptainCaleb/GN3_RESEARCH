# R249 — Adaptive floor-aligned fixed-turn universal front door

**Record status:** `USABLE/LEGACY`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. More strongly than the original existential formulation: given any ancestry-bearing mass-two balanced-pair floor whose two singleton supports are {a} and {c}, choose any vertex b distinct from a,c. Exactly one of (a,b,c) and (c,b,a) is a tight proper turn J by boundary antisymmetry. Its unordered outer pair is exactly the floor pair {a,c}. Entering the R172 pair-bearing fixed-turn lineage at this floor with no pre-entry capture credit gives Psi_J=(2,0,0). Hence every first quiet, nonclosing, non-exit macro step retaining J must strictly lower epsilon_J and therefore acquire at least one of the two floor vertices a,c as a captured outer endpoint before pair-mass remint or target-distance debt can be charged. In particular, the original R198 existential front door follows by first producing any mass-two floor.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; an ancestry-bearing mass-two floor with singleton supports {a},{c} is given; accepted R3 and R172. The standard smallest-counterexample size floor guarantees a third vertex b and properness of the chosen turn.

## Scope
Order-free universal conditional strengthening of R198. The floor-to-turn step works for every ancestry-bearing mass-two floor, not merely the specific floor produced in the original proof.

## Applicability
Use after any endpoint-selective or prescribed-coordinate floor construction, including R224/R248. A chosen floor vertex automatically becomes one outer target of a fixed-turn lineage; the first quiet step must capture one of the two floor coordinates.

## Persistence
The given floor pair, chosen proper turn J, and its outer target pair are retained in the chosen lineage. No pre-entry capture is credited. Later conclusions remain lineage-relative as in R172.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not guarantee which of the two floor vertices is captured first, does not preserve an earlier source representative, does not make Psi global across turn changes, and does not assert explicit R172 exits close the conjecture.

## Proof
Let the given ancestry-bearing mass-two floor have singleton supports {a} and {c}. First observe directly, without any deletion-floor theorem, that a counterexample H must have at least five vertices. Indeed every vertex set of size at most four can be partitioned into at most two parts of size at most two, and every singleton or ordered dimer is vacuously a tight path; hence pc(H)<=2 for |H|<=4. Thus choose b distinct from a,c. Since |H|>=5, the three-set {a,b,c} is a proper subset of V(H). By R3 exactly one of (a,b,c) and (c,b,a) is tight; retain that orientation as the proper turn J. Its unordered outer pair is exactly {a,c}, the given floor pair. Enter the R172 pair-bearing fixed-turn lineage at this floor with no pre-entry capture credit. Then A_J is empty, so epsilon_J=2; the current balanced-pair mass is M=2, so M-2=0; and the current floor S={a,c} equals the target outer pair, so delta_J=2-|S intersect {a,c}|=0. Hence Psi_J=(2,0,0). By R172 every first quiet, nonclosing, non-exit macro retaining J must strictly decrease Psi_J. Since its second and third coordinates are already zero, that first decrease must lower epsilon_J, i.e. acquire at least one of a,c as a captured historical signed anchor before any quiet remint or target-distance debt can be charged. This proves the full R249 contract from R3 and R172 alone.
