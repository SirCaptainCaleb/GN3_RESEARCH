# R2011 — Neutral-component toggles transport off-component physical obstruction to C_*

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** workspace root `1789414372.145159`

## Statement

In a physically tight later-loss normalized prefix, after an off-`C_*` first-open blocker component is toggled to its `J`-state, further whole neutral-component `F→J` toggles can be chosen monotonically so that either a tight three-forest with the canonical first-open successor is obtained or every remaining bad turn, backtrack, or directed cycle meets the flipped `F`-prefix of `C_*`.

Thus the physical off-component obstruction cannot remain trapped entirely among neutral components.

## Proof

Let `M=hat M_{r-1}` be the physically tight normalized pre-loss three-forest and let `D` be the off-`C_*` neutral component carrying the exceptional first-open blocker. By R2009 and R2010, toggling `D` from its F-state to its J-state is matching-valid, preserves cardinality, and installs the canonical successor `c->b_{t+2}` at `c,out`; terminality at `c,out` is impossible. Keep the partial state of `C_*` fixed: its already-flipped prefix stays in F-state and its future suffix stays in J-state.

For any set `S` of neutral components containing `D`, let `M(S)` be the matching obtained from `M` by putting exactly the components in `S` into J-state and every other neutral component into F-state. Every neutral component has delta zero, so all `M(S)` have the same three-forest matching cardinality. Split-graph components are disjoint, so toggling one component never changes the already-installed successor at `c,out` unless it is `D`; since `D` remains in `S`, the canonical successor persists throughout.

Take a physical blocker of `M(S)` that avoids the flipped F-prefix of `C_*`.

For a bad turn or two-edge backtrack, if both selected incidences at its middle physical vertex are J-like (a common edge, a component in `S`, or the unflipped J-suffix of `C_*`), the same two incidences occur in the tight forest `J`, impossible. If both are F-like neutral incidences from components outside `S`, the same two incidences occur in the original physically tight normalized prefix `M`, impossible. Hence the blocker is mixed: one incidence is J-like and the other lies on some neutral component `E` outside `S` still in F-state. Toggle `E` to J. The exact local blocker disappears, and at that vertex the two incidences now agree with J.

For a directed cycle, suppose it uses no selected edge from an F-state neutral component outside `S`. Since it also avoids the flipped F-prefix of `C_*`, every selected edge on the cycle is then a J-edge or common edge. The identical directed cycle would occur in `J`, impossible. Therefore the cycle contains an F-selected edge from some neutral component `E` outside `S`; toggling `E` removes that edge and destroys the exact cycle.

Thus every blocker avoiding the flipped `C_*` prefix permits a toggle of a new neutral component, strictly enlarging `S` and destroying that exact blocker. There are finitely many neutral components, so the process terminates. If no blocker remains, the matching is an acyclic tight matching of three-forest cardinality and hence a tight three-forest. Otherwise every remaining bad turn, backtrack, or cycle meets the flipped F-prefix of `C_*`.

No transition-count monotonicity is claimed for these reverse neutral toggles. The conclusion is only physical-obstruction transport: either the off-component residue becomes an actual tight three-forest with the first-open successor, or obstruction support is forced back onto the distinguished augmenting component `C_*`.
