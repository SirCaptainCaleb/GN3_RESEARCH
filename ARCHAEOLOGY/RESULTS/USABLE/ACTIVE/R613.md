# R613 — One-sided gap-1 EAR data do not force the first R24 zipper switch

## Statement
There exists a Strong Level-(1) orientation F on V=X∪{L,u,v,w}, where X={a,b,c,z}, with all of the following simultaneous properties.

(1) X is the transitive matching-height four-cell M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}.

(2) (L,u,v,w) is tight; (u,L,x) is tight for every x∈X; Lbc,Lcb,Lac,Lca are tight; and wvz is tight. In particular z has exactly the left high-side R596/R598 seed order (u,L,a,c,b) and the inward anchor wvz, with L=q_0,u=q_1,v=q_2,w=q_3.

(3) X∪{L} is Hamilton-P5-free.

(4) At the gap u|v, with original insertion turns A_x=(L,u,x), B_x=(u,x,v), C_x=(x,v,w), the tightness rows are (T,F,T) for x=a,b,c and (T,T,F) for x=z. Thus there are no reverse-A witnesses, the only reverse-C witness is the distinguished anchor z, and the three reverse-middle turns vau,vbu,vcu are tight. The R583/R588 outcome is therefore genuinely the EAR branch, not a hidden terminal two-witness branch; indeed for every x<y in {a,b,c}, (v,y,u,x) is a literal tight P4 ear.

(5) More strongly, every tight path in F ending with v,w has order at most four. The only such paths of order four are (c,u,v,w),(z,u,v,w),(L,u,v,w). Consequently, for every nonempty Y⊆X there is no Hamilton path on {L,u,v,w}∪Y ending in v,w. This realizes the full one-sided prefix-subset obstruction through q_3 that would follow in the R24 frame by concatenation to Q[4,m].

(6) For every root r∈X there is no Hamilton path on (X−{r})∪{L,u,v} beginning at v. Hence none of the four proposed advanced states L(2,r) exists.

Therefore the left seed R598, its distinguished R596 anchor, the complete gap-1 R583 clause, even a literal R588 EAR, and all one-sided prefix-compatible nonextension information through q_3 do not by themselves compile to a first root-switched zipper state. Any valid zipper theorem must use additional retained data—such as a nonlocal/right-root coordinate, a payment descendant with an explicit cover interpretation, or a stronger state invariant—not merely the one-sided local packet.

## Hypotheses
Only Strong Level-(1) boundary antisymmetry. The construction is a finite eight-vertex orientation and does not assume a smallest counterexample.

## Scope
Durable R24 strategy fence. It refutes the proposed local inference from the one-sided R596/R598 seed plus the gap-1 R583/R588 EAR data, even after adding every prefix-subset obstruction visible on X∪Q[0,3].

## Applicability
Use when designing the carrier-indexed zipper. A sound first transition must retain and consume data absent from this model, most plausibly the synchronized right root or an explicitly currentized R542 descendant. Do not spend further effort trying to derive L(2,r) solely from the left eight-vertex packet.

## Persistence
Retain the physical labels X,L,u,v,w; root z; seed order (u,L,a,c,b); gap index u|v; exact A/B/C tightness rows; the three reverse-middle witnesses and literal EARs; and the two obstruction families in (5),(6).

## Exclusions / nonclaims
This is not a counterexample to R24, does not have pc(F)>2, and does not claim the local orientation extends to a hypothetical smallest counterexample. It does not refute a nonlocal exchange using the simultaneous right root, exact pair-deletion covers, or the full internal structure of an R542 payment continuation. It refutes only conclusions whose used hypotheses are contained in the explicitly realized one-sided packet.

## Proof
Order the vertices
a<b<c<z<L<u<v<w.
For a middle vertex y and endpoints p<q, the following table lists exactly those canonical turns pyq that are tight. Every unlisted canonical turn pyq is bad, and its complete reversal qyp is tight. Thus the table defines a Strong Level-(1) orientation by construction.

middle a: bac, baz, baL, bau, caz, Lau.
middle b: abc, abz, abL, abu, zbL, zbu.
middle c: acb.
middle z: azv, bzL, bzv, czL, czv, Lzv, uzv.
middle L: aLb, aLz, aLv, bLv, cLz, cLv, zLv, uLv.
middle u: cuv, zuv, Luv.
middle v: avw, bvw, cvw, uvw.
middle w: none.

Restricting the table to X gives exactly the matching-height rule M_R>M_S>M_L. It also gives Luv,uvw; uLx for all x∈X; Lbc,Lcb,Lac,Lca; and wvz. Hence (u,L,a,c,b) is tight: its turns are uLa,Lac,acb.

We first verify X+L is P5-free without an oracle. The complete list of tight P4s in this five-set is
abzL, aLbc, baLz, czbL, czLb, cLaz, cLza, zLbc, Lacb, Lcaz, Lcza, Lczb.
For these twelve orders, respectively, the unused vertex would require the final turn
zLc, bcz, Lzc, bLa, Lba, azb, zab, bca, cbz, azb, zab, zba.
Every displayed required turn is bad in the table. Any Hamilton P5 has a tight P4 prefix, so none exists.

At gap u|v, direct table lookup gives
(A_x,B_x,C_x)=(T,F,T) for x=a,b,c,
and (A_z,B_z,C_z)=(T,T,F).
Reversal therefore gives vau,vbu,vcu and wvz tight, with every x u L bad and wav,wbv,wcv bad. There are no two reverse-terminal witnesses. For x<y in {a,b,c}, vyu is one of the three reverse-middle turns and yux is the reversal of the unlisted canonical turn xuy; hence (v,y,u,x) is a tight P4 ear.

The prefix-subset obstruction is also read off completely. The tight paths ending v,w of order three are
avw,bvw,cvw,uvw,
and the tight ones of order four are
cuvw,zuvw,Luvw.
There is no order-five continuation: a predecessor of cuvw would require pcu, a predecessor of zuvw would require pzu, and a predecessor of Luvw would require pLu; every such turn with a still-unused p is bad in the table. The only listed path containing both L and u is Luvw, and it contains no X-vertex. This proves (5).

Finally fix a deleted root r and inspect tight paths on S_r=(X−{r})∪{L,u,v} that begin at v. The complete lists of order-five prefixes are as follows; after the slash is the unused sixth vertex and the required final turn, which is bad:

r=a:
vczbL / u, bLu;
vczbu / L, buL;
vczLb / u, Lbu;
vcubL / z, bLz.

r=b:
vcLaz / u, azu;
vcLau / z, auz;
vcLza / u, zau;
vcuaz / L, azL.

r=c:
vabzL / u, zLu;
vbaLz / u, Lzu;
vbuaz / L, azL.

r=z:
vaLbc / u, bcu;
vbuac / L, acL;
vcLau / b, aub;
vcubL / a, bLa;
vuacb / L, cbL.

Successive extension using the middle-tournament table gives exactly these prefixes. Since every Hamilton P6 beginning at v would have one of them as its first five vertices, and every listed final turn is bad, no such P6 exists for any r. This proves (6) and the claimed insufficiency fence.
