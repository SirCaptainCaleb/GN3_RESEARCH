# R866 — Minimum inward-anchor five-cell residue has only six typed forest shapes and three structural regimes

## Statement
Retain the sigma_G(T)=0 minimum five-cell setting of accepted R865. Contract each complete contiguous cell block G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3,G_4 in the exact two-cover T to a vertex, writing Q for any of G_0,G_1,G_2 and X for either of G_3,G_4. The contracted graph F_T is a two-component path forest on three Q-vertices and two X-vertices with exactly three edges, no X-X edge, and c_X(T) in {2,3} in the genuinely new residue. Up to permutation of the three Q labels and interchange of the two X labels, exactly six typed forest shapes occur. For c_X=2: (A) X-Q-Q-X plus an isolated Q; (B) Q-Q-X-Q plus an isolated X; (C) Q-Q-X plus Q-X; (D) Q-Q plus X-Q-X. For c_X=3: (E) X-Q-X-Q plus an isolated Q; (F) Q-X-Q plus Q-X. Consequently the entire c_X=2,3 minimum residue falls into only three structural regimes. (I) Internal-X regime: some X-dimer cell has degree two in F_T; this is every c_X=3 cover and c_X=2 shape (B). (II) Same-rail endpoint-X regime: both X-dimer cells have degree one and lie on the same T-rail; these are c_X=2 shapes (A) and (D). (III) Split-X regime: both X-dimer cells have degree one and lie on different T-rails; this is only c_X=2 shape (C). In regime (I), the degree-two X block is literally preceded and followed by Q blocks on one oriented T-rail. In regime (II), orienting the common rail makes one X block its first endpoint and the other its last endpoint. No other minimum c_X=2 or 3 topology exists.

## Hypotheses
Accepted R865 sigma=0 five-cell packet, with c_X(T) in {2,3}.

## Scope
Pure finite path-forest classification inside the noncircular crossed R24 five-cell frame. No sign/payment theorem is used.

## Applicability
Replace ad hoc enumeration of c_X=2/3 block arrangements by the three regimes Internal-X, Same-rail endpoint-X, and Split-X. Combine only afterward with transition-direction/source-decoration data.

## Persistence
Retain the exact cover T, physical identities of G_0,...,G_4, all three selected intercell transitions, and the contracted two-component path forest. The six shapes are quotient types; physical Q/X labels and rail directions remain retained separately.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not classify orientations of the T rails, Hamilton orders inside Q-cell blocks, or the collision/P4 decorations of Q-X transitions. Does not claim any shape closes H. Q-cell permutations and X-cell swaps are only quotient symmetries for listing shapes; physical cell identities remain distinct downstream.

## Proof
By accepted R865, in the sigma_G(T)=0 case each of the five cells is one complete contiguous T-block and T has exactly three intercell transitions. Contracting the blocks gives a graph F_T with five vertices and three edges. Each T rail contracts to a path, so F_T is a forest with exactly two path components and maximum degree at most two. R865 also excludes the X-X edge G_3G_4. We work only in the new c_X=2,3 residue.

CASE c_X=2. Exactly one of the three edges is Q-Q and two are Q-X. Let e be the unique Q-Q edge. Because maximum degree is two, the two Q-X edges can be placed relative to e in only the following quotient-distinct ways.
(1) Attach one X to each endpoint of e. This gives the four-vertex path X-Q-Q-X and leaves the third Q isolated: shape A.
(2) Attach one X to an endpoint of e and attach the second Q-X edge from that same X to the third Q. This gives Q-Q-X-Q and leaves the other X isolated: shape B. Equivalently this is the unique c_X=2 shape with an X vertex of degree two.
(3) Attach one X to one endpoint of e and use the other Q-X edge between the third Q and the other X. This gives Q-Q-X plus Q-X: shape C.
(4) Use both Q-X edges at the third Q, joining it to both X vertices. Then e is a separate Q-Q component and the other component is X-Q-X: shape D.
These exhaust the possibilities. To see this formally, each Q-X edge either meets an endpoint of e or the third Q. If neither meets the third Q, maximum degree forces them to hit different endpoints, giving A. If exactly one meets the third Q, then the other either meets the same X, giving B, or the other X, giving C after quotient relabelling. If both meet the third Q, they must use the two distinct X vertices, giving D.

CASE c_X=3. There is no Q-Q edge. Thus F_T is a bipartite path forest with three Q vertices, two X vertices, three edges. Since the sum of X-degrees is three, one X has degree two and the other has degree one. The degree-two X is joined to two Q vertices. The remaining Q-X edge either attaches the degree-one X to one of those two Q vertices, producing the four-vertex path X-Q-X-Q and leaving the third Q isolated (shape E), or attaches it to the third Q, producing Q-X-Q plus Q-X (shape F). These are quotient-distinct and exhaustive.

The structural regimes now follow immediately. Shapes B,E,F contain an X vertex of degree two, and no other shape does; E,F are exactly all c_X=3 shapes. Shapes A,D have both X vertices degree one in the same nontrivial path component, hence on the same T rail, with one at each end after orienting that rail. Shape C has its two degree-one X vertices in the two different path components, hence on different T rails. This proves R670.
