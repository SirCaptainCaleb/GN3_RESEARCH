# R1018 — Four-deletion exactness is equivalent to the singleton rail-order-four floor

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. The following are equivalent: (A) every exact one-vertex-deletion two-cover H-z=P sqcup Q has |P|,|Q|>=4; (B) for every four-vertex set D, pc(H-D)=2. Moreover the equivalence is direct and certificate-preserving: an order-three rail P in a failed singleton-deletion cover produces the four-deletion Hamiltonian residue H-(V(P) union {z})=Q, while a four-deletion Hamiltonian residue K on H-D and any z in D produce an exact singleton-deletion cover K sqcup T where T is a tight trimer on D-{z}.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. Use boundary antisymmetry R3 and smallest-counterexample minimality R4.

## Scope
Exact interface theorem identifying the quarantined core. This theorem does not prove either equivalent condition; it shows that repairing R5 at deletion size four and proving the operative R24 singleton rail floor are the same mathematical obligation.

## Applicability
Use to transfer a counterexample certificate or a proof between the R24 singleton-floor formulation and the R5 four-deletion formulation. In repair work, this licenses treating the unresolved |D|=4 case as exactly R24 rather than as an additional descendant obligation.

## Persistence
The deleted singleton z, the three-vertex short rail P or trimer T, the four-set D, and the Hamiltonian complementary rail Q or K are retained literally under the two translations.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not establish R24 or four-deletion exactness. Does not identify the stronger historical packaging beyond the operative rail-order floor. The already-clean deletion sizes one through three are separate and do not require this equivalence.

## Proof
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample.\n\n(A) implies (B). Assume every exact singleton-deletion two-cover has both rails of order at least four. Let D be any four-vertex set. By R4, pc(H-D)<=2. Suppose for contradiction that H-D is Hamiltonian, with Hamilton tight path K. Choose any z in D. By R3, the three-set D-{z} has a tight trimer T. Then K sqcup T is a literal two-cover of H-z. It is exact: if H-z were Hamiltonian, that Hamilton path together with singleton z would be a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Thus K sqcup T is an exact singleton-deletion two-cover with a rail T of order three, contradicting (A). Therefore H-D is non-Hamiltonian. Together with R4 this gives pc(H-D)=2, proving (B).\n\n(B) implies (A). Assume pc(H-D)=2 for every four-set D. Let H-z=P sqcup Q be any exact singleton-deletion two-cover. We show both rails have order at least four. By symmetry it suffices to treat P. If |P|=1, then adjoining z to P gives a tight dimer; that dimer together with Q is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. If |P|=2, then V(P) union {z} is a three-set, so R3 gives a tight trimer on those three vertices; together with Q this again spans H by two tight paths, contradiction. If |P|=3, put D=V(P) union {z}. Then |D|=4 and H-D has vertex set V(Q). Since Q is already a Hamilton tight path on that residue, pc(H-D)=1, contradicting (B). Hence |P|>=4. The same argument applies to Q, so every exact singleton-deletion two-cover has both rails of order at least four, proving (A).\n\nThe translations preserve the physical certificate. In the first direction, a four-deletion Hamiltonian residue K and chosen z produce the exact short-rail singleton-deletion cover K sqcup T. In the reverse direction, a three-vertex short rail P and its deleted singleton z produce the four-set D=V(P) union {z} whose complement is literally the other Hamilton rail Q. Thus the unresolved four-deletion case of R5 and the operative singleton rail floor of R24 are exactly the same obligation, not merely adjacent consequences.
