# R581 — Reversed Hamilton dimer plus universal-extension rigidity forbids singleton residual rails

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let X be a nonempty proper vertex set that carries a boundary-reversed Hamilton dimer in the sense of R561: there exist distinct u,v in X and Hamilton tight paths of X beginning with (u,v) and ending with (v,u). Then every exterior vertex r notin X Hamilton-extends X, so the universal-extension hypothesis of R156 holds. Consequently pc(H-X)=2, and every exact two-cover of H-X has both rails of order at least two: no exact two-cover of the complement has a singleton component.

In particular, in a tau=1 exact two-cover cell with Hamiltonian absorber X=H[D union S] that additionally carries a boundary-reversed Hamilton dimer, the R560 complement C admits the exact two-cover consisting of the mixed C-block B_C and the unique pure C-rail, and both of those C-blocks have order at least two. Thus neither C-block can be a singleton. This forbids the mixed singleton B_C={y} already ruled out order-free by R578, and additionally forbids a singleton pure C-rail, which R578 does not address.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample. X is nonempty and proper and carries an R561 boundary-reversed Hamilton dimer. The tau=1 specialization additionally assumes a partition V(H)=D sqcup S sqcup C with X=H[D union S] Hamiltonian, and a literal exact two-cover of H-D with exactly one S|C transition, so R560 supplies b_C=2 with one mixed C-block and one pure C-rail.

## Scope
Smallest-counterexample consumer of R561+R156, with a tau=1 block-size corollary from R560. Order-free in |X| and |H| except for the standing smallest-counterexample deletion floor used by R156. Complements the order-free spanning obstruction R578, which kills only the mixed singleton and does not use minimality.

## Applicability
Use after an R561 certificate has been manufactured on an absorber or other proper set. The no-singleton residual is the correct first consumer of universal extension before any attempt to merge C-blocks or to run R567 against D union C. In tau=1 cells it gives a uniform floor |B_C|>=2 and |pure C-rail|>=2.

## Persistence
The reversed dimer on X, the universal one-vertex extensions, pc(H-X)=2, and the no-singleton residual are graph-intrinsic. In the tau=1 specialization the two C-blocks of T remain representative-relative to T.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not produce the reversed Hamilton dimer. Does not make D union C Hamiltonian. Does not force a second Hamilton order at a crossing endpoint. Does not by itself span H. Does not apply outside smallest counterexamples, except that the mixed-singleton spanning obstruction remains available from R578 without minimality.

## Proof
By R561, a boundary-reversed Hamilton dimer on X makes X a universal one-vertex absorber: for every r outside X, exactly one of the antisymmetric turns (r,u,v) and (v,u,r) is tight, and that tight turn Hamilton-extends X by prepending r to the start-path or appending r to the end-path. This is precisely the universal-extension hypothesis of R156. R156 therefore yields pc(H-X)=2, and moreover every exact two-cover of H-X has both rails nontrivial.

Now specialize to a tau=1 cell. Let V(H)=D sqcup S sqcup C with S,C nonempty, X=H[D union S] Hamiltonian, and T a literal exact two-cover of H-D with exactly one selected S|C transition. R560 gives the unique-transition normal form: exactly one rail of T is mixed and consists of one S-block covering all of S together with one C-block B_C, and the other rail is a pure C-rail. Those two C-blocks partition C and are themselves tight paths, so they form an exact two-cover of H-X=H[C]. The no-singleton clause of R156 applied to this cover forces |B_C|>=2 and forces the pure C-rail to have order at least two.

The mixed singleton B_C={y} is already impossible on order-free grounds by R578 whenever the same reversed dimer is present, because R561 would then Hamilton-extend X by y and the remaining pure C-rail would complete a spanning two-cover of H. The present theorem adds the symmetric singleton prohibition on the pure C-rail, which requires the smallest-counterexample rigidity R156 rather than a direct spanning construction.
