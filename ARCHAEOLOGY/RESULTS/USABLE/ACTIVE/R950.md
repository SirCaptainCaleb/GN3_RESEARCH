# R950 — Failure of every one-vertex longest-rail substitution creates two parity replacement barriers

## Statement
Let P=(p_0,...,p_m), m>=2, be an increasing path in an edge-ordered complete graph, with e_i=p_ip_{i+1}. Let x be outside P. If replacing no single p_i by x in the displayed order gives an increasing path, then on each parity class of positions i there is a canonical internal position rho_r(x) with the following properties. Writing A_i=p_{i-1}x and B_i=xp_{i+1}, the outer substitution inequalities hold at i, but the middle one is forced to reverse: B_i<A_i. More precisely rho_r(x) is the least position i of parity r for which the right outer condition is feasible; propagation by steps of two makes the left outer condition feasible there. Moreover, if rho_r(x)<rho_r(y) for two nonreplaceable exterior vertices on the same parity, then x,p_{rho_r(x)+1},y is an increasing three-vertex path.

## Hypotheses
Pure edge-ordered complete graph setting; P increasing of order at least three; x outside P and no literal one-position substitution p_i->x preserves the displayed increasing order.

## Scope
Second-order analogue of the R895 insertion barrier, designed for longest-support exchange rather than insertion.

## Applicability
Use when a longest-support extremal argument needs to consume failure of every one-vertex support substitution. It turns that failure into two parity-indexed canonical obstruction coordinates and cross-turn witnesses.

## Persistence
Retain the definitions of left/right outer substitution feasibility, the two-step propagation L_i and not R_i implies L_{i+2}, and the same-parity strict-separation witness.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say that every exterior vertex has a replacement slot. Does not by itself imply a Hamilton complement, and the parity barrier witnesses cannot in general be concatenated through one exterior vertex because its middle substitution inequality is reversed.

## Proof
Write e_i=p_ip_{i+1}, so e_0<...<e_{m-1}. For a proposed substitution of x for p_i define

L_i(x): true if i=0 or i=1, and e_{i-2}<p_{i-1}x if i>=2;
R_i(x): true if i=m or i=m-1, and xp_{i+1}<e_{i+1} if i<=m-2.

For 1<=i<=m-1, the displayed path with p_i replaced by x is increasing exactly when L_i(x), p_{i-1}x<xp_{i+1}, and R_i(x) all hold. At i=0 substitution works exactly when R_0(x) holds; at i=m it works exactly when L_m(x) holds.

The key propagation is by two positions. Suppose L_i(x) holds and R_i(x) fails, with i<=m-2. Failure of R_i gives e_{i+1}<xp_{i+1}. Since e_i<e_{i+1}, we have e_i<p_{i+1}x, which is precisely L_{i+2}(x). Thus
L_i and not R_i imply L_{i+2}.

Fix a parity r in {0,1} that occurs among the positions 0,...,m. The first position of that parity, r, has L_r automatically. Along that parity class there is eventually a right-feasible position, because the last one or two positions m-1,m have R automatically. Let rho_r(x) be the least position of parity r with R true. Repeated two-step propagation shows L_{rho_r(x)} true. If rho_r(x)=0, then R_0 holds and x replaces p_0, contrary to hypothesis. If rho_r(x)=m, then L_m holds and x replaces p_m, again contrary to hypothesis. Hence rho_r(x) is internal. Since both outer conditions hold there but substitution is impossible, the middle comparison must fail, and strict totality gives
xp_{rho_r(x)+1}<p_{rho_r(x)-1}x.
This proves the two parity replacement barriers.

Finally let x,y be two nonreplaceable exterior vertices and suppose on parity r that i=rho_r(x)<j=rho_r(y). Since the positions have the same parity, i<=j-2, hence i<=m-2. Right feasibility of x at i gives
xp_{i+1}<e_{i+1}.
Because i is an earlier position of the same parity than the least right-feasible position j for y, R_i(y) fails, so
e_{i+1}<yp_{i+1}.
Therefore
xp_{i+1}<yp_{i+1},
and (x,p_{i+1},y) is increasing.
