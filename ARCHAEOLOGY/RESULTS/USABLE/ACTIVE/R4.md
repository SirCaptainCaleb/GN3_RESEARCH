# R4 — Smallest-counterexample minimality

## Statement
In a smallest counterexample H, pc(H)=3 and every nonempty proper induced subsystem has path-cover number at most two. Every proper graph-intrinsic tight path has an exact two-cover of its complement, and restoring it gives a minimum spanning three-cover containing it literally.

## Proof
Let H be chosen with minimum vertex order among counterexamples to the Strong Level-(1) spanning two-cover conjecture. By definition H itself has no spanning cover by at most two tight paths. Every nonempty proper induced subgraph H[S] has smaller order, so it is not a counterexample and therefore has a spanning cover by at most two tight paths.

H nevertheless has a spanning cover by three tight paths: choose any vertex v. The proper induced graph H-v has a cover by at most two tight paths by the preceding paragraph, and adjoining the singleton path (v) gives a cover of H by at most three paths. Since H has no two-cover, its path-cover number is therefore exactly three.

Now let K be any proper graph-intrinsic tight path of H. Its complement H-V(K) is a proper induced subgraph, so it has a cover by at most two tight paths. It cannot be Hamiltonian: if one tight path Q covered H-V(K), then K and Q would be a spanning two-cover of H. Thus the complement has path-cover number exactly two. Restoring K literally to any exact two-cover of its complement gives a spanning three-cover of H containing K as the specified current component; because the path-cover number of H is exactly three, that restored cover is minimum. No endpoint prescription or other current state is asserted. This proves the full R4 contract from the definition of a minimum-order counterexample and the elementary fact that a singleton is a tight path.
