# R1028 — G35 same-rail order-two suffix is a transient reverse dimer, not an R3 seam

**Audit state:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Proof provenance:** `A7C3/WORKSPACE/D17/424-g35-successful-pivot-far-star-persistence.md`

## Statement
Retain two consecutive successful G35 defect-pivot states `M_q,M_{q+1}` with moving terminals `a_q,a_{q+1}`, fixed far source `b_m`, fixed selected successor `c` of `b_m`, and closing shortcuts `g_i=a_i->b_m`. Assume the two closures are not singleton-loop coincidences, so `a_q,a_{q+1} != b_m`, and retain the physical successful-pivot fact `a_q != a_{q+1}`.

For either state `i`, exactly one of the following far-boundary forms occurs.

1. If `c != a_i`, then `(a_i,b_m,c)` is a genuine three-distinct-vertex seam. If that seam is bad, R3 applies legally and gives the reverse-star turn `(c,b_m,a_i)` tight.
2. If `c = a_i`, then there is no three-vertex seam at all. The already selected edge `b_m->a_i` and the shortcut `a_i->b_m` form the explicit order-two reverse dimer/backtrack `b_m->a_i->b_m`, and R3 is not invoked.

The reverse-dimer case cannot occur at both consecutive successful states, because `a_q != a_{q+1}` while `c` is fixed. Consequently any far-seam obstruction that genuinely persists across the two closures lies in the first case at both indices and yields the two reverse-star turns `(c,b_m,a_q)` and `(c,b_m,a_{q+1})`. Thus the missing same-rail order-two suffix is one-step debt and does not alter the G35 persistent-far-star conclusion or the G36 consumer of genuinely persistent fixed-wall debt.

## Hypotheses
The consecutive successful-pivot G35 setup of D17.424; `b_m` has fixed selected successor `c`; neither closure is the singleton-loop coincidence; R3 is available only for genuine three-distinct-vertex triples.

## Scope
This is the audited repair of the same-rail order-two suffix in the G35 persistence argument. It certifies the legality of every R3 use at the far boundary and separates the repeated-vertex case into an explicit reverse-dimer branch.

## Applicability
Use whenever the G35/G36 corridor argument treats the fixed far successor `c`. Before invoking R3 on `(a_i,b_m,c)`, test `c=a_i`. Equality is the transient reverse-dimer branch; inequality is the genuine seam branch. Persistent fixed-wall debt across consecutive successful pivots is therefore always represented by reverse-star turns on distinct vertices.

## Persistence
The physical vertices `a_q,a_{q+1},b_m,c`, the selected far edge `b_m->c`, and the two closing shortcuts are retained literally. No anonymous replacement of the repeated-vertex case by a trimer is permitted.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim that every closure fails, that every failure is a far-seam failure, or that the resulting reverse star by itself closes the proof. It repairs only the omitted same-rail order-two branch. Near-seam and ordinary shortcut-cycle transport remain governed by the surrounding G35 argument.

## Proof
Because `b_m->c` is a selected path edge, `b_m != c`. At state `i`, the closing shortcut is `g_i=a_i->b_m`, and by the nonloop hypothesis `a_i != b_m`.

First suppose `c != a_i`. Then `a_i,b_m,c` are pairwise distinct, so `(a_i,b_m,c)` is a legitimate three-vertex seam. If it is bad, boundary antisymmetry R3 applies to this actual triple and gives its exact reversal `(c,b_m,a_i)` tight. There is no repeated-vertex use of R3 in this branch.

Now suppose `c=a_i`. The selected far edge is then literally `b_m->a_i`, while the shortcut is its reverse `a_i->b_m`. Adding the shortcut therefore creates the two-edge backtrack `b_m->a_i->b_m`. The notation `(a_i,b_m,c)` would repeat `a_i` and is not a trimer, so R3 is not applicable and is not used. This is an explicit reverse-dimer branch rather than a seam branch.

The successor `c` of `b_m` is unchanged by the successful pivot: the pivot does not alter the selected outgoing edge of `b_m` in the G35 setup. The moving terminal does change, so `a_q != a_{q+1}`. Hence `c=a_q` implies `c!=a_{q+1}`, and `c=a_{q+1}` implies `c!=a_q`. The reverse-dimer branch therefore cannot persist across the two consecutive successful states.

Suppose now that the same physical far-seam obstruction is bad at both states. Since the reverse-dimer branch is not a three-vertex seam and cannot occur at both states, persistence as a far seam forces

`c!=a_q` and `c!=a_{q+1}`.

Both triples are therefore genuine and R3 yields

`(c,b_m,a_q)` tight and `(c,b_m,a_{q+1})` tight.

These are exactly two spokes of the fixed far reverse star anchored at `(c,b_m)`. Thus the only correction needed in D17.424 is the explicit order-two backtrack branch. It is transient and cannot furnish the persistent fixed-wall debt consumed by the G36 corridor. The persistent branch, when present, is unchanged and all of its R3 invocations are on distinct vertices.
