# R2027 — Source-source singleton exclusion

**Status:** PASS
**Source:** Slack workspace R2027

## Statement

In the audited R2025 source-anchor lift

`N=(A,s,C) | R | Q`,

suppose the nonanchor rail `R` is the singleton `(t)` and `t` is a second retained source spoke, distinct from `s`. Then `H` has a spanning exact two-cover, contradiction. Consequently no singleton nonanchor rail in an R2025 lift can be another source spoke.

In particular, if the source exposed by R2024 lies on an isolated two-vertex X-rail `{s,t}`, then the other vertex is `v`.

## Proof

Let `S=(A,s,C)` and `R=(t)`, with `t!=s` another retained source spoke. The retained source turn `(A,t,C)` is tight.

Consider the ordered cross-rail join `t->A` from the singleton rail `R` into the anchor rail `S`. By the audited R2025 lift, this is an acyclic exact-two-cover-cardinality candidate. Because `R` is a singleton, its only possible new seam is `(t,A,s)`.

If `(t,A,s)` were tight, the joined path together with `Q` would be a spanning exact two-cover of `H`, impossible in the retained counterexample. Hence `(t,A,s)` is bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 gives the complete reversal `(s,A,t)` tight.

Now `(s,A,t,C)` is a literal tight four-vertex path: its consecutive trimers are `(s,A,t)` and the retained source trimer `(A,t,C)`. It uses exactly the vertices of `S union {t}`. The untouched rail `Q` covers every remaining vertex, so

`(s,A,t,C) | Q`

is a spanning exact two-cover of `H`, contradiction.

Thus the singleton nonanchor rail cannot be a second source spoke. If an exposed T-rail is an isolated two-vertex X-rail, its other vertex belongs to `X={v,p,q,r}`. The three source-spoke possibilities are excluded, leaving only `v`.

## Dependencies and scope

Trusted dependencies: `R2025`, R3, the retained source trimers `(A,x,C)` for `x in {p,q,r}`, and the smallest-counterexample assertion that `H` has no spanning exact two-cover.

Provisional dependencies: none.

The symmetric ordered join `C->t` yields the same contradiction but is unnecessary.
