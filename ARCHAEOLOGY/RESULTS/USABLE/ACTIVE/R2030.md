# R2030 — Two-vertex source-rail anchor caps

**Status:** PASS_ADJUSTED
**Source:** Slack workspace R2030 re-audit

## Repaired statement

Let `T` be a literal exact two-cover of `G=H-{A,C}` in the retained smallest-counterexample source frame. Suppose one T-rail has exactly two vertices `{s,u}`, where `s` is a retained source spoke.

Then the four-set `{A,C,s,u}` is P4-free and both

`(s,A,u)` and `(u,C,s)`

are tight.

The original claim for an arbitrary longer rail with source endpoint `s` is withdrawn.

## Proof

Let `Q` be the other T-rail. If `{A,C,s,u}` contained any tight Hamilton P4, that P4 together with `Q` would be a literal spanning exact two-cover of `H`, contradicting counterexample status. Thus the four-set is P4-free.

The retained source turn `(A,s,C)` is tight.

If `(s,A,u)` were bad, complete-reversal R3 would make `(u,A,s)` tight. Then

`(u,A,s,C)`

would be a tight P4 because its consecutive turns are exactly `(u,A,s)` and `(A,s,C)`. Contradiction. Hence `(s,A,u)` is tight.

If `(u,C,s)` were bad, R3 would make `(s,C,u)` tight. Then

`(A,s,C,u)`

would be a tight P4 because its consecutive turns are exactly `(A,s,C)` and `(s,C,u)`. Contradiction. Hence `(u,C,s)` is tight.

No cyclic rotation of a tight turn is used.

## Removed overclaim

For a longer rail beginning `(s,u,...)` or ending `(...,u,s)`, the former proof tried to extend the rail using `(C,A,s)` or `(s,C,A)` as a cyclic rotation of the retained source turn `(A,s,C)`. Such rotations are not automatic under R3. Therefore the arbitrary-length endpoint claim is not certified and is removed.

## Dependencies and scope

Trusted dependencies: retained source turn `(A,s,C)`, R3, and counterexample status of `H`.

Provisional dependencies: none.

This repaired result is exactly the two-vertex endpoint-cap fact needed in the short-rail geometry and is independent of rail orientation.
