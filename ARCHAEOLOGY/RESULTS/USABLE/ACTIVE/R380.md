# R380 — Johnson paired-design equality has a biregular girth-eight Levi geometry

## Statement
Assume the t=2 equality setting of R375 and write r=kq. Form the bipartite incidence graph Lambda whose left vertices are the selected k-sets F in B, whose right vertices are all physical (k-1)-subsets S of the ground set, and where F is adjacent to S exactly when S subset F. Then Lambda is (k,q)-biregular: every selected block contains k cores and every core lies in q selected blocks. Moreover Lambda has girth at least eight. Equivalently it has no 4-cycles and no 6-cycles. The point graph on the left side is exactly the induced Johnson graph from R379, while the line-intersection graph on the right is q(k-1)-regular with each core-neighborhood decomposing into q cliques K_{k-1}.

## Hypotheses
Finite k-uniform family in the t=2 equality case of R375; r=kq.

## Scope
Pure finite incidence-geometry theorem.

## Applicability
Use to study equality families via biregular girth methods, incidence spectra, partial linear spaces, or generalized-polygon style constraints.

## Persistence
The incidence graph and its point/line graphs are intrinsic finite combinatorial data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the Levi graph has girth greater than eight or classify all such incidence geometries.

## Proof
Biregularity follows from R375: every selected k-set contains k different (k-1)-cores and every core lies in q=r/k selected blocks. A 4-cycle would give two distinct selected k-sets containing two distinct common (k-1)-cores; the union of those two cores has size k, forcing the two k-sets to coincide, contradiction. A 6-cycle would give three selected blocks F1,F2,F3 that are pairwise Johnson-adjacent through three distinct cores. In J(r,k), a triangle not contained in one core-clique is necessarily a top triangle: there is a (k+1)-set U with Fi=U\\{u_i} for three distinct omitted vertices. Then U contains three selected k-sets, contradicting the local occupancy cap two. Hence girth is at least eight. For the right-side line-intersection graph, fix a core S. Its q incident selected blocks each contain k-1 other cores; cores arising from the same block form a K_{k-1}, while cores arising from different blocks cannot be adjacent through a second selected block without creating a 4-cycle in Lambda. Thus the neighborhood is q disjoint K_{k-1} and the degree is q(k-1).
