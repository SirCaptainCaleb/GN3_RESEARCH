# R171 — Literal Signed-Pair Frame Complete Discharge

## Statement
Let H be in the Strong smallest-counterexample program. Suppose H=K sqcup P sqcup T is a literal spanning three-cover, K is a nonempty tight path, and (a;P),(T;b) is a graph-intrinsic opposite-sign historical pair with historical pair mass M=|P|+|T|. Then at least one of the following occurs: (i) a spanning two-cover; (ii) strict historical pair-mass descent; (iii) bounded support swallow. In the swallow branch generated by the interval-peel proof, an order-three failure/contact trimer contains an entire historical support, so that support has order at most three.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample to the two-cover conjecture; H is displayed literally as the three current tight-path components K,P,T; K is nonempty and proper; P,T are vertex-disjoint historical opposite-signed supports with graph-intrinsic witnesses a,b and M=|P|+|T|. The proof uses the accepted witness-order seed constructions, Signed-Interval Rebirth/Tight-Path Cut Memory, and complete historical-pair marker-contact descent.

## Scope
Strong Level-(1), no CW assumption. The interval-peel part does not require a uniform bound on |K|. Literal means the displayed current representative is exactly H=K sqcup P sqcup T and the peel consumes literal intervals of that same K without a fresh minimality completion or representative reset.

## Applicability
Use when a retained opposite-sign historical pair and a third current path exhaust the vertex set. It eliminates the full-reservoir literal-frame recurrence before fresh recompletion.

## Persistence
The historical signs are graph-intrinsic and survive as ancestry. A failed peel produces an order-three tight contact marker; Signed-Interval Rebirth may replace a deleted signed endpoint by the appropriate immediately deleted historical neighbor, so witness names need not persist. Successful peels remain in the displayed literal frame. No new completion is introduced during the peel.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not say bounded support swallow is closure; it means one historical support is completely swallowed by the bounded order-three contact marker and is therefore of order at most three. Does not make pair mass globally monotone across unrelated resets. Does not preserve original witness names after rebirth. Does not use a proper tight cycle as contradiction. Does not assert any CW-specific conclusion. The theorem is not a generic statement for arbitrary three paths lacking the historical opposite-sign pair.

## Proof
Write the literal frame as H=K sqcup P sqcup T, with K=(k_0,...,k_m), P=(p_0,...,p_r), T=(t_0,...,t_s), all nonempty, and retain the opposite signs (a;P) and (T;b). Thus a is outside P, b is outside T, (a,P) is a tight head extension, and (T,b) is a tight tail extension. Put M=|P|+|T|.

We use the following seam observation throughout. Whenever already-tight vertex-disjoint blocks are concatenated, the only uncertified turns are the one or two turns crossing each displayed block junction. In every junction used below, each uncertified turn contains one or two vertices of P or T. If such a turn h is bad, R3 makes its complete reversal C=bar(h) a tight trimer on the same three vertices. If C contains an entire contacted historical support, then that support has order at most three and R171 output (iii) holds. Otherwise apply Signed-Interval Rebirth R425 to every historical support met by C, taking its signed-side surviving interval. At least one support strictly shortens, neither surviving support is empty, and the head/tail polarities remain opposite. Hence the historical pair mass strictly decreases, giving output (ii). This is a graph-intrinsic historical descent; no fresh cover completion is invoked. Therefore in each construction below we may assume every displayed seam is tight, because the first bad seam already proves (ii) or (iii).

Step 1: eliminate cross-support witnesses. Because H is partitioned by K,P,T and a is outside P, either a lies in K or a lies in T. Suppose a lies in T. If |P|>=2, the first sign turn (a,p_0,p_1) is tight. Use this tight trimer itself as a marker. If it contains all of P (in particular |P|<=2) or all of T, output (iii). Otherwise R425 applied after deleting its three vertices leaves a nonempty head-signed descendant of P and a nonempty tail-signed descendant of T, and P loses at least p_0,p_1, so M strictly decreases: output (ii). If |P|=1, choose any two distinct vertices outside P (they exist in the displayed three-cover) and use R3 to orient a tight trimer containing the unique vertex p_0; this trimer contains all of P, giving (iii). Thus a in T is completely discharged. The exact dual argument discharges b in P: when |T|>=2 use the inherited tight terminal sign turn (t_{s-1},t_s,b), and when |T|=1 use any R3 tight trimer containing the singleton T. Consequently the only remaining case has a,b both in K.

Step 2: forward witness order. Suppose a=k_i and b=k_j with i<j. Decompose K in its old order as
K = L, a, N, b, R,
where L=(k_0,...,k_{i-1}), N=(k_{i+1},...,k_{j-1}), and R=(k_{j+1},...,k_m), omitting empty blocks. Consider the two literal vertex-disjoint spanning path proposals
A = (L,a,P,N),
B = (T,b,R).
All turns internal to L,N,R are inherited from K; (a,P) and (T,b) are the retained sign extensions. The only uncertified turns are: the single L-a-P junction turn when L is nonempty; the at-most-two P-N junction turns when N is nonempty; and the single T-b-R junction turn when R is nonempty. Every one of these junction turns meets P or T, so by the seam observation a first bad one gives (ii) or (iii). If all are tight, A and B are tight, disjoint, and cover every vertex of H. Thus they are a literal spanning two-cover, output (i).

Step 3: reverse witness order. Suppose b=k_j occurs before a=k_i, so j<i, and write
K = L, b, N, a, R
with the evident possibly empty intervals. Consider the single literal spanning order
Q = (L,T,b,N,a,P,R).
The inherited data certify every turn except those at four kinds of junction: the at-most-two L-T turns; the one turn joining the tail extension (T,b) to the next K vertex toward a when such a vertex exists; the one turn joining the last K vertex before a to the head extension (a,P) when such a predecessor exists; and the at-most-two P-R turns. (When b and a are adjacent, the two middle junction turns are simply (t_s,b,a) and (b,a,p_0).) Every uncertified turn again meets T or P. Hence any bad junction gives (ii) or (iii) by the seam observation. If all are tight, Q is a literal Hamilton tight path on H, in particular a spanning cover with at most two paths, so output (i) holds.

Step 4: coincident witnesses. Suppose a=b=k_i and write K=L,a,R. Consider
Q = (L,T,a,P,R).
The only uncertified turns are the at-most-two L-T junction turns, the central bridge turn (t_s,a,p_0), and the at-most-two P-R junction turns. The sign data already certify the turns immediately inside T,a and a,P. A bad outer junction gives (ii) or (iii) by the seam observation. If the central bridge is bad, R3 gives the tight reverse trimer (p_0,a,t_s), which meets both historical supports. If either contacted support is entirely contained in this trimer, output (iii); otherwise R425 rebirths both to nonempty opposite-signed descendants and strictly lowers M, giving (ii). If every junction is tight, Q is a literal Hamilton tight path on H, hence output (i).

These cases exhaust all possible witness locations and relative orders. Every construction uses only the original literal frame, inherited contiguous K/P/T intervals, the two original sign extensions, boundary antisymmetry, and Signed-Interval Rebirth. No minimality recompletion, unrelated representative, marker reset, or transported currentness is used. In the good branch the displayed block paths are literal paths on the original vertex partition; in the bad branch the exact reversed seam trimer is retained as the physical contact/refund certificate. Thus the reconstruction preserves precisely the historical same-frame/no-recompletion strength of R171 while proving its three stated alternatives.
