# R736 — R727 P4 geometry alone does not force any first-step root P6

## Statement
There exists a seven-vertex Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament F on X union {L,u,v}, with X={a,b,c,z}, having all of the following properties simultaneously. (1) X is the transitive matching-height no-P4 cell M_R={ab,cz}>M_S={ac,bz}>M_L={bc,az}. (2) (L,u,v) is tight, (u,L,x) is tight for every x in X, and the full left gate turns Lbc,Lcb,Lac,Lca,bzL,zbL,czL,zcL are tight. The R626 blocker turns visible on these seven coordinates, Luz,vuz,aLz,Lub,vub,cLb, are also tight. (3) The endpoint cell X union {L} is Hamilton-P5-free. (4) The full R723 first-inward star holds: (v,u,x) is tight for every x in X. (5) The R725 left saturation turns (b,a,u) and (z,c,u) are tight. (6) Both R727 candidate four-sets are genuinely Hamiltonian: {a,b,c,u} contains the tight P4 (a,c,u,b), while {c,z,a,u} contains the tight P4 (c,z,a,u). Nevertheless, for every root r in X, the six-set {L,u,v} union (X-{r}) has no tight Hamilton P6. Hence even the simultaneous presence of R723 universal inward saturation, the complete left endpoint/gate packet, L-SAT, and actual labelled P4s on both R727 supports does not locally force a first-step cover-valued root advance. Any consumer of the R727 P4 branch must use something other than bare Hamilton extension on the seven-coordinate end gadget, such as same-residue cover comparison, nonlocal/right-end data, or provenance-bearing pair interaction.

## Hypotheses
Explicit finite Strong Level-(1) orientation. No smallest-counterexample assumption is used; the result is a local obstruction to an inference from the listed crossed-frame turn data.

## Scope
Strategy fence for the first-step zipper program. It does not say that R727 P4s are useless; pending R733 gives a nonlocal-in-the-cover-space pair consumer. It only rules out deducing a Hamilton P6 on any root support from the seven-coordinate local turn packet itself.

## Proof
Specify F by its local tournaments T_m on the other six vertices, with x m y tight iff x beats y in T_m. Six local tournaments are transitive with the following orders: T_L: u>a>c>v>b>z; T_u: L>v>c>z>b>a; T_v: u>z>b>c>a>L; T_a: b>L>c>z>v>u; T_b: a>z>v>u>L>c; T_z: c>b>v>a>u>L. The local tournament T_c on {L,u,v,a,b,z} has arcs u>L, v>L, L>a, L>b, z>L, u>v, a>u, u>b, z>u, a>v, v>b, z>v, a>b, z>a, z>b. This completely defines a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Direct restriction verifies the matching-height orders on X: at middles a,b,c,z the X-orders are respectively b>c>z, a>z>c, z>a>b, c>b>a. The displayed orders/arcs immediately verify Luv, every uLx, all eight listed left-gate turns, the six listed R626 blockers, every vux, and the two L-SAT turns bau,zcu. Exhaustive inspection of the 5!=120 orders on X union {L} finds no Hamilton P5. The two displayed P4s are immediate from the local tournaments. Finally, for each r in {a,b,c,z}, exhaustive inspection of all 6!=720 orders on {L,u,v} union (X-{r}) finds zero Hamilton P6s. Thus all stated positive packet data coexist while every possible first-step root P6 is absent.
