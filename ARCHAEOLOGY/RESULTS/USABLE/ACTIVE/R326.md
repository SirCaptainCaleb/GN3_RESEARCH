# R326 — Johnson local-clique degree density principle

## Statement
Let B be a family of k-subsets of an r-element ground set, r>=k+1, and suppose every (k+1)-set contains at most t members of B. Write p=|B|/binom(r,k). Then\n\np <= [k+(t-1)(r-k)]/[k(r-k+1)].\n\nMore precisely, the induced subgraph J(r,k)[B] has maximum degree at most (t-1)(r-k), while the intersection-clique decomposition forces average degree at least k(r-k+1)p-k. Comparing them gives the stated ceiling. Equality requires both mechanisms to be tight: every member F of B must lie in exactly t members of B inside every (k+1)-superset U of F, and the (k-1)-core occupancies x_S=|{F in B:S subset F}| must all be equal. For k=2,t=2 the bound becomes p<=r/[2(r-1)], the usual Mantel density scale. For k=5,t=2 it gives p<=r/[5(r-4)].

## Hypotheses
Integers r>=k+1 and 1<=t<=k+1; B subseteq binom([r],k); every (k+1)-set contains at most t members of B.

## Scope
Pure finite-set / Johnson-graph theorem. Stronger parent of the R322 quadratic dual-clique ceiling; it uses the same two clique systems but retains the local degree information instead of averaging it away.

## Applicability
Use whenever a support family has a local occupancy cap inside every one-vertex extension. Especially useful when t is small. The theorem turns a local forbidden-density rule into a sharp-scale global density ceiling and equality regularity.

## Persistence
The family B, induced Johnson graph, union-clique local occupancies, and intersection-core occupancies are intrinsic finite combinatorial data.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert equality configurations exist for general parameters. Does not imply path growth or Strong-specific closure. Does not invalidate R322/R325; those remain distinct structural/stability and spectral proof resources.

## Proof
Let b=|B| and M=binom(r,k). Fix F in B. Every Johnson neighbor G of F has a unique union U=F union G of size k+1. There are r-k such supersets U of F, and each contains at most t members of B total, hence contributes at most t-1 neighbors G of F. Thus deg_B(F)<= (t-1)(r-k), so 2e(B)<=b(t-1)(r-k). For each (k-1)-set S let x_S count members of B containing S. The intersection-clique decomposition gives 2e(B)=sum_S x_S(x_S-1)=sum_S x_S^2-kb. Since sum_S x_S=kb, Cauchy gives sum_S x_S^2>=k^2b^2/binom(r,k-1). Hence 2e(B)>=k^2b^2/binom(r,k-1)-kb. Comparing and dividing by b>0 gives k^2b/binom(r,k-1)-k <= (t-1)(r-k). Using binom(r,k-1)=M*k/(r-k+1) and p=b/M yields k(r-k+1)p-k <= (t-1)(r-k), hence the claimed bound. Equality forces equality in the pointwise degree bound for every F and equality in Cauchy, giving the stated regularity.
