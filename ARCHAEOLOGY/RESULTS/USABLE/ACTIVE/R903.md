# R903 — Edge-orderability has arbitrarily large vertex-minimal obstructions, even with transitive middle tournaments

## Statement
For every integer n>=4 there is a boundary 3-tournament H_n on n vertices such that (i) its comparison orientation Gamma(H_n) has exactly one directed simple cycle, of length n; (ii) every proper induced subsystem is edge-orderable; (iii) every local middle-vertex tournament is transitive; and (iv) H_n has a cyclic tight Hamilton ordering, hence pc(H_n)=1. Consequently edge-orderability of boundary tournaments has no characterization by forbidden induced subsystems of bounded vertex order: for every fixed k, some non-edge-orderable boundary tournament has every induced subsystem of order at most k edge-orderable.

## Hypotheses
n>=4. Boundary means exactly one of (u,v,w) and (w,v,u) is tight for each ordered triple of distinct vertices. Edge-orderability means a total order on E(K_V) realizes tightness by uv<vw.

## Scope
Order-free obstruction family for local-to-global integrability. Independent of smallest-counterexample, R24/R5, gate phases, and pending five-vertex R902.

## Applicability
Rules out inferring global edge-orderability from bounded-size induced edge-orderability, even when all local middle tournaments are transitive. In particular, accepting the proposed five-vertex nonHamilton-implies-edge-orderable result would not supply this missing local-to-global inference. Hamiltonicity is explicit, so the family itself cannot occur as a smallest two-cover counterexample.

## Persistence
Retain the labelled ordinary Hamilton cycle e_i={v_i,v_{i+1}}, its cyclic comparison orientation, and the common total ordering of all noncycle ordinary edges.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not refute the two-cover conjecture: each constructed H_n is Hamiltonian. Does not rule out an integrability theorem with extra smallest-counterexample or exact-cover hypotheses. Does not establish or use R902. No canonical promotion or acceptance is requested by the producer.

## Proof
Let V={v_0,...,v_{n-1}}, with indices modulo n, and let e_i={v_i,v_{i+1}}. For n>=4, the vertices e_i induce a chordless n-cycle in L(K_V). Let D=E(K_V)\{e_0,...,e_{n-1}} and choose any total order on D. Orient every adjacency between members of D from earlier to later. Orient every adjacency between d in D and e_i from d to e_i. Finally orient e_i -> e_{i+1} for all i modulo n. These rules orient every edge of L(K_V) exactly once. Define (u,v,w) tight iff {u,v}->{v,w}; opposite reversals are complementary, so this defines a boundary tournament, with the comparison dictionary of accepted R887.

A directed cycle cannot use both D and its complement because there is no arc from a cycle-edge vertex back into D. The orientation on D is acyclic because it follows one total order. The orientation induced by {e_i} is exactly the directed n-cycle. It follows that this is the unique directed simple cycle in Gamma(H_n).

If U is a proper subset of V, an omitted original vertex removes its two incident cycle edges from E(K_U). The restriction Gamma(H_n)[E(K_U)] therefore cannot contain the unique directed cycle and is acyclic. By R887, H_n[U] is edge-orderable. This also covers the trivial small U cases.

For a fixed original vertex v_i, the incident ordinary edges form a clique of L(K_V). All incident members of D precede both cycle edges in their common total order, and the last two edges satisfy e_{i-1}->e_i. Thus this clique is transitively oriented, proving transitivity of each local middle tournament directly.

Finally e_i->e_{i+1} means (v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2}) is tight for every cyclic index. Hence (v_0,...,v_{n-1}) is a tight Hamilton path, and every rotation is as well. The full H_n is not edge-orderable because its comparison orientation is cyclic. Given any fixed k, take n>max(k,3). All subsystems on at most k vertices are proper and edge-orderable, but H_n is not. Any supposed bounded-order forbidden-induced characterization would therefore fail on H_n.
