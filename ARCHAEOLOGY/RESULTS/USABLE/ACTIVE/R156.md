# R156 — Universal-extension deletion rigidity

## Statement
Let H be a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let X be a nonempty proper vertex set such that, for every r in V(H)\\X, the induced subsystem H[X union {r}] has a Hamilton tight path. Then pc(H-X)=2. Moreover every exact two-cover H-X=U⊔V has both U and V nontrivial; equivalently, no exact two-cover of H-X has a singleton component.

## Hypotheses
H is a smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; X is nonempty and proper; for every r outside X, X union {r} supports a graph-intrinsic Hamilton tight path. X itself need not be Hamiltonian.

## Scope
Arbitrary-size deletion under a universal one-vertex Hamilton-extension hypothesis. This is not restricted to |X|<=4 and is therefore not subsumed by universal small-deletion exactness R5. It also adds a no-singleton rigidity conclusion for every exact residual two-cover.

## Applicability
Use in universal-extension modules to obtain a genuine exact two-rail residual frame and rule out degenerate singleton rails before maximin normalization, fixed-rail graft surgery, endpoint-role analysis, or other residual-cover consumers.

## Persistence
The universal-extension property is graph-intrinsic. The conclusion pc(H-X)=2 and the absence of singleton rails are graph-intrinsic statements about the residual subsystem. A chosen exact two-cover remains representative-relative; the theorem does not select a canonical residual cover or preserve rails across later recompletions.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that X itself is Hamiltonian. Does not give rail-size bounds stronger than at least two vertices each. Does not provide a canonical exact two-cover, fixed-rail persistence, endpoint exposure, selected crossing states, capture/protection ancestry, or a spanning two-cover of H. Without the universal-extension hypothesis the theorem does not apply.

## Proof
Recovered from INCREMENTAL_HANDOFF_RESEARCHER_34_CYCLIC_K4_NEAR_END_GRAFT_FOUR_DELETION_RIGIDITY_2026-08-24.md, Theorem 6.1. Smallest-counterexample minimality gives pc(H-X)<=2. Suppose H-X has a Hamilton path Q and choose an endpoint r. The hypothesis supplies a Hamilton tight path K_r on X union {r}. If Q=(r), then K_r spans H, contradiction. Otherwise Q-r is a nonempty tight path disjoint from K_r and K_r⊔(Q-r) is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Thus pc(H-X)=2. Now suppose an exact two-cover has a singleton component, H-X=(r)⊔Q. Again K_r exists, and K_r⊔Q is a spanning two-cover of H, contradiction. Therefore both residual rails are nontrivial.
