# R415 — Certificate-retaining balanced-pair descent to the both-singleton floor

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let Pi be a historical balanced opposite-sign pair equipped with a retained physical birth/refund certificate stack in the sense of the signed-support payment machinery. Then there is a chosen certificate-retaining continuation along which either H acquires a spanning two-cover or the active pair support mass descends to two, so both active signed supports are singletons. Every credited mass decrease is backed by a named physical turn/selected outside boundary/Signed-Interval Rebirth certificate, and the pre-existing graph-intrinsic birth ancestry of Pi remains retained as historical certificate data throughout the chosen descent. In particular the endpoint case with one singleton support and one nontrivial support admits a direct one-unit refund using one selected boundary of a nontrivial outside rail; the complementary outside-cover shapes are handled by the previously audited singleton analysis. The resulting both-singleton label by itself is not a contradiction and may be formally cheap; the durable output is the both-singleton floor together with the full retained birth/refund ancestry stack.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; Pi is an ancestry-bearing balanced opposite-sign pair in the accepted historical signed-support convention; the standard certificate-retaining pair-payment/refund machinery is available.

## Scope
Historical Strong Level-(1) signed-payment theorem. The continuation is lineage-relative and preserves graph-intrinsic certificate ancestry, not one current cover through all steps.

## Applicability
Use after an R176 cross-state pair or any other balanced-pair birth whose provenance matters. It converts pair birth into a finite paid checkpoint: spanning two-cover or ancestry-bearing both-singleton floor. Do not use bare pair mass as a global monotone across unrelated births.

## Persistence
The initial pair birth certificate, any cross-state/reverse-trimer orientation supplied at birth, each chosen refund marker/blade, descendant signed intervals, and final singleton supports are retained as historical truth in the chosen ledger. Active representatives may change.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not close the both-singleton layer. Does not claim pair mass is globally monotone across unrelated remints. Does not assert all balanced-pair continuations follow the same representatives. Does not permit discarding the birth/cross-state ancestry and retaining only anonymous singleton labels.

## Proof
Let Pi be the given ancestry-bearing balanced opposite-sign pair with retained birth/refund certificate stack. If both active supports are already singleton, the conclusion holds. If one support is singleton and the other is nontrivial, apply fully reconstructible R428. Along its chosen nonclosing continuation the singleton support is preserved, the opposite support strictly shortens at each refund, and either a spanning two-cover occurs or both supports become singleton; its refund/rebirth certificates append to the incoming Pi ledger. Suppose instead that no support is singleton. While both supports have order at least three, apply fully reconstructible R426. Its global fixed-witness descent gives either a spanning two-cover or a strict descendant balanced pair, recording each mass decrease by an explicit reverse-trimer shell/refund certificate and retaining the pre-existing pair ancestry. Iterating this chosen nonclosing phase terminates with at least one support of order at most two. If a singleton has appeared, apply R428 to the singleton/nontrivial pair, unless both supports are already singleton. Otherwise some support has order exactly two. Apply fully reconstructible R427 to that ancestry-bearing pair. It gives either a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced pair with a singleton support, retaining the old dimer, swallowing/reverse-trimer data, and the prior ancestry stack. In the nonclosing branch apply R428 to that singleton/nontrivial pair; if the opposite support is already singleton there is nothing to do, otherwise R428 terminates at a both-singleton pair while preserving the fixed singleton and appending its ordered refund ledger. Thus in every case there is a finite chosen continuation ending in a spanning two-cover or active pair mass two. Every credited mass decrease is certified by one of R426,R427,R428, and concatenating their persistence ledgers retains the initial pair birth plus all subsequent physical refund/rebirth certificates as historical truth. Because the continuation is chosen lineage-relative, this proves no global monotonicity across unrelated pair births or remints, exactly as fenced in R415.
