# R515 — Trimer central-anchor interaction

## Statement
Let T=(a,b,c) and U=(p,q,r) be vertex-disjoint tight trimers in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. Each trimer has its two natural terminal signed dimers: (a,b) is tail-signed by c and (b,c) is head-signed by a, while (p,q) is tail-signed by r and (q,r) is head-signed by p. Hence the crossed pairings Pi_1=((a,b),(q,r)) and Pi_2=((b,c),(p,q)) are two graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pairs of total support mass four. Both pair births carry the same unordered pair of middle anchors {b,q}. If the two births are retained in sequence before unrelated reset, then applying R448 with fixed trimer T gives an explicit at-anchor growth/cycle/reversal interaction at b, and applying R448 with fixed trimer U gives the dual interaction at q. More generally, if a tight path is vertex-disjoint from a fixed tight trimer, every consecutive trimer window of the path gives the same two-middle-anchor construction with the fixed trimer; these are graph-intrinsic historical interaction certificates, not simultaneous paid descendants and not a closure theorem.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament. T and U are vertex-disjoint graph-intrinsic tight trimers. For the path version, every selected consecutive three-vertex window is a tight trimer and is disjoint from the fixed trimer.

## Scope
Modern reconstructible formulation of the legacy central-anchor interaction theorem. It removes obsolete remint/payment shorthand and states the exact crossed pair births and R448 local interaction certificate.

## Applicability
Use whenever two disjoint tight trimers, or a fixed trimer and a disjoint path window, need a role-preserving interaction at their middle vertices. The output is local historical interaction geometry, not automatic progress.

## Persistence
Retain both trimers, their middle vertices, the four natural terminal signed dimers, the two crossed balanced-pair birth certificates, and the two R448 at-anchor interaction certificates. Alternative later representatives need not coexist.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim the R448 growth output is fresh rather than source-trimer replay. Does not claim the two pair births are simultaneously paid, that pair mass descends, or that either interaction closes the conjecture.

## Proof
For T=(a,b,c), accepted R448 records the natural terminal signed dimers D_T^-=(a,b), tail-signed by c, and D_T^+=(b,c), head-signed by a; both contain the signed anchor b. For U=(p,q,r), the same theorem records D_U^-=(p,q), tail-signed by r, and D_U^+=(q,r), head-signed by p, both anchored at q. Because T and U are vertex-disjoint, D_T^- is physically disjoint from D_U^+ and their polarities are opposite, so by the project signed-pair definition they form a graph-intrinsic balanced opposite-sign pair Pi_1 of mass four. Likewise D_T^+ and D_U^- form Pi_2. Each birth therefore uses one terminal dimer of T and one terminal dimer of U, so both births have the same unordered middle-anchor pair {b,q}. Retain Pi_1 and Pi_2 before unrelated reset. From the T viewpoint, the earlier and later births each use one of T's terminal dimers and a partner support disjoint from T; R448 applies and gives its explicit at-anchor growth/cycle/reversal certificate at b. From the U viewpoint the same two births satisfy the dual hypotheses, so R448 gives the corresponding certificate at q. If K is a tight path disjoint from T, every consecutive three-vertex window of K is a tight trimer disjoint from T, so the identical argument applies independently to each window. Nothing asserts simultaneous current paid descendants or freshness of the returned growth branch.
