# R527 — Same-frame crossing, capture, concentration, and refund

## Statement
Let H be a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, let K be a retained vertex-simple tight carrier path, and let S be a graph-intrinsic signed support with V(S) contained in V(K) and exact sign witness w outside V(S). Put C=V(K)\V(S) and E=V(H)\V(K). Let T=U sqcup V be one exact two-cover of H-V(S). A selected carrier/exterior crossing means a selected adjacent state xy of T with one endpoint in C and the other in E.

(Capture/refund branch.) If some selected carrier/exterior crossing xy avoids w, then the physical dimer {x,y} is disjoint from S union {w} and may be used as the actualizing marker in the same deletion frame. Either physical endpoint of {x,y} may be designated as the captured signed anchor by choosing the corresponding marker orientation before actualization. Applying R526 to S and this oriented dimer marker produces an explicit opposite-polarity signed support and a balanced-pair birth while retaining S, its tested sign orientation and witness w, the original selected physical crossing, the chosen marker orientation, the captured endpoint, and the terminal actualization test as historical certificate data. If S is a dimer and H is additionally a hypothetical smallest counterexample, the aligned payment clause of R526 gives a finite certificate-retaining continuation to either a spanning two-cover or singleton width. Thus the old aligned-refund ancestry is retained explicitly.

(Concentration branch.) Suppose instead that every selected carrier/exterior crossing of T meets w. Delete w from the two ordered rails of T and split at w. The resulting path forest has at most three nonempty components: the rail not containing w remains one component, while the rail containing w splits into at most two. Every surviving component is cut-pure with respect to C|E. Indeed, if one surviving component contained both a C-vertex and an E-vertex, some adjacent state of that component would cross C|E; that state was already a selected state of T and does not contain w, contradicting the hypothesis that every selected crossing meets w. Hence deleting w leaves a literal path cover of H-(V(S) union {w}) by at most three C/E-pure path components. This is the exact same-frame concentration bound.

The two branches are local to this exact support-deletion representative. The selected crossing is current only in T. Capture, balanced-pair birth, and any paid descendant are later historical/certificate data. Reorienting the two-vertex marker for actualization does not assert that the reversed marker orientation was selected in T.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); K is a retained tight carrier; S is a signed support contained in K with witness w outside S; T is an exact two-cover of H-V(S). For the paid singleton continuation in the capture branch, additionally H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample and S has order two.

## Scope
Full-contract reconstructible resurrection of legacy R36/R33. It preserves both historical alternatives: same-frame capture with aligned refund ancestry, and witness concentration. The concentration constant is made explicit as three and follows directly from deleting one vertex from an exact two-cover. The local capture/concentration theorem does not require smallest-counterexample minimality; only the optional aligned dimer payment continuation does.

## Applicability
Use when a signed support and selected carrier/exterior crossing genuinely coexist in one exact support-deletion two-cover. If a selected crossing avoids the sign witness, capture either physical crossing endpoint in that same frame and retain refund ancestry. If all selected crossings hit the witness, use the explicit at-most-three cut-pure-component concentration certificate after deleting the witness.

## Persistence
Retain the original exact deletion cover T, partition C|E, signed support S with tested orientation and witness, and in the capture branch the exact selected crossing, chosen actualization orientation, captured endpoint, R526 actualization/birth certificate and any later refund certificates. In the concentration branch retain T, w, and the at-most-three cut-pure path components of T-w. No later descendant is asserted to preserve the original selected crossing as current.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Concentration is an exact-frame structural alternative, not a graph-level obstruction. Capture is not permanent protection. Reorienting the dimer marker for actualization does not reverse a selected state in T. The theorem does not assert the capture and concentration branches coexist. For a non-dimer S it records actualization/capture ancestry but does not invoke the dimer-to-singleton payment clause.

## Proof
Let T=U sqcup V be the exact support-deletion two-cover. First suppose T selects a carrier/exterior state xy with w not in {x,y}. Because T lies in H-V(S), the marker {x,y} is disjoint from S, and by assumption it is also disjoint from w. A two-vertex path is tight in either orientation. Choose the marker order so that the desired endpoint is the actualization endpoint appropriate to the polarity of S. Apply accepted reconstructible R526. Its local actualization clause produces an opposite-polarity signed support with that chosen marker endpoint as the signed anchor, while retaining the marker order and terminal test. Together with S this is a graph-intrinsic balanced-pair birth. The selected state xy remains a fact about the original T-frame; the chosen marker order is an actualization choice and need not equal its selected direction. If S has order two in a hypothetical smallest counterexample, R526's payment clause further supplies the claimed aligned paid continuation and refund ancestry. Now suppose every selected C|E crossing of T contains w. Since w lies outside S, w is a vertex of H-V(S) and occurs exactly once in the path forest T. Delete w from its rail. A vertex deletion from one directed path leaves zero, one, or two path pieces, while the other T-rail is unchanged, so T-w has at most three nonempty path components. Assume one such component is not C/E-pure. Along its ordered vertex sequence, pass from a vertex in C to a vertex in E or conversely; at the first color change there is an adjacent selected state ab crossing C|E. This adjacency survived deletion of w, so neither endpoint is w. But ab was already a selected carrier/exterior crossing of T, contradicting the assumption that every such crossing meets w. Hence every component of T-w lies wholly in C or wholly in E. Therefore T-w is a path cover by at most three cut-pure components, proving the concentration branch and the full theorem.
