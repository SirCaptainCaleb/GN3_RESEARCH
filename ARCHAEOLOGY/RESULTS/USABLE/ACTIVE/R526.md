# R526 — Signed-support actualization and aligned payment

## Statement
Let P be a graph-intrinsic signed tight support in a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament, with exact sign witness w, and let M=(m_0,...,m_r), r>=1, be a tight marker path disjoint from V(P) union {w}. Then M admits an explicit opposite-polarity actualization against P with the marker orientation, witness, and resulting signed anchor retained. If P is head-signed by w, test the terminal marker turn (m_{r-1},m_r,w). If it is tight, M itself is tail-signed by w with signed tail anchor m_r. If it is bad, R3 gives (w,m_r,m_{r-1}) tight, so the short support (w,m_r) is tail-signed by witness m_{r-1}, again with signed tail anchor m_r. The tail-signed case is the exact head/tail dual at m_0. Thus actualization never means free truncation: it is certified by one displayed terminal marker test and produces a physically disjoint opposite-polarity support, hence a balanced-pair birth with P. The marker orientation used for this test is part of the certificate and need not have been the orientation selected in an earlier cover.

In the aligned dimer payment scope, suppose H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and P has order two. The actualized opposite support together with P is then a balanced pair with a width-two support. Accepted R427 therefore gives a finite chosen certificate-retaining continuation yielding either a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced pair with a singleton support. Retain separately the original sign orientation and witness, marker and its chosen orientation, terminal actualization test, the actualized opposite support, the pair-birth event, each refund certificate supplied by the width-two descent, and the resulting singleton descendant. This is the modern explicit form of signed-support actualization and aligned payment.

## Hypotheses
Local actualization clause: Strong Level-(1); P is a signed tight support with named witness w; M is a nontrivial tight marker disjoint from both P and w. Payment clause: additionally H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and P has order two.

## Scope
Equal-strength reconstructible replacement for legacy R35. The local actualization clause is purely Strong Level-(1) and does not use minimality. The payment conclusion is only the historical aligned dimer scope and uses smallest-counterexample width-two descent. Head/tail duals and marker orientation are explicit; no support is truncated for free.

## Applicability
Use whenever a signed support has a disjoint marker. For capture-oriented uses, orient a dimer marker so the desired endpoint occupies the actualization endpoint. When the original signed support is a dimer in a smallest counterexample, follow the resulting balanced-pair birth through R427 to obtain a spanning two-cover or singleton-width descendant.

## Persistence
Retain the exact original signed support orientation and witness, marker support and chosen marker orientation, terminal marker test, actualization outcome and signed anchor, balanced-pair birth, and any later R427 refund/descent certificates. The marker or pair supports need not remain current in the original deletion representative after actualization/payment.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim a marker orientation was previously selected merely because it is chosen for actualization. Does not permit free endpoint truncation. Does not claim both marker orientations or head/tail payment descendants coexist. Does not assert payment for a non-dimer original support by this theorem. Does not close a resulting singleton floor by itself.

## Proof
Assume first that P is head-signed by w and orient the disjoint tight marker as M=(m_0,...,m_r), r>=1. Test the ordered turn (m_{r-1},m_r,w). If it is tight, then M is tail-signed by witness w: its terminal sign turn is exactly (m_{r-1},m_r,w), and the signed tail anchor is m_r. Since M is disjoint from P, P and M are disjoint opposite-polarity signed supports. If the tested turn is bad, boundary antisymmetry R3 gives its complete reversal (w,m_r,m_{r-1}) tight. Hence the two-vertex support Q=(w,m_r) is tail-signed by witness m_{r-1}, again with signed tail anchor m_r. Because w and M are disjoint from P, Q is disjoint from P. Thus in either branch the displayed marker test creates a graph-intrinsic opposite-polarity support Q disjoint from P, and therefore a balanced-pair birth. The tail-signed case is dual: test (w,m_0,m_1). If tight, M is head-signed by w with head anchor m_0; if bad, R3 gives (m_1,m_0,w) tight, so (m_0,w) is head-signed by witness m_1. This proves the local actualization clause and records exactly which physical marker endpoint is the new signed anchor. Now assume H is a hypothetical smallest counterexample and P is a dimer. The balanced pair just constructed has at least one support of order two, namely P. Accepted fully reconstructible R427 applies and yields, along a finite chosen certificate-retaining continuation, either a spanning two-cover or a descendant balanced pair with a singleton support. R427's refund/rebirth ledger is retained together with the already graph-intrinsic actualization certificate, so the sign orientation, witness, marker orientation, actualization event, paid descendant, and singleton are separate historical ledger entries exactly as claimed. No currentness of the original marker after the later descent is asserted.
