# R144 — Type-(2,2,3) reduces to type-(2,2,2) or an exterior-dimer collision

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the accepted minimum-overlap-two type-(2,2,3) motif L=X∪Y∪{p}, B=X∪Z, D=Y∪Z with |X|=|Y|=2, |Z|=3 and exterior pair {e,f}, with exact root B ⊔ {p,e,f} after deleting Y. Fix any tight path ordering of B and let t1,t2 be its two distinct endpoints. Then either there exists a global-longest support E such that L,D,E form a type-(2,2,2) triangle with three pairwise-disjoint overlap-two cores, or the exterior physical dimer {e,f} carries a same-polarity two-extension collision with distinct witnesses among {p,t1,t2}. More precisely, apply R140 at each endpoint ti to obtain Ei=(B\\{ti})∪{wi}, wi∈{p,e,f}. If some wi∈{e,f}, then necessarily ti∈Z and L∩D=Y, D∩Ei=Z\\{ti}, L∩Ei=X, so L,D,Ei is type (2,2,2). Otherwise w1=w2=p. Then after deleting Y the complements of E1,E2 are the tight trimer supports {t1,e,f} and {t2,e,f}; together with the original short trimer {p,e,f}, these give three signed certificates on the same physical dimer {e,f} with distinct witnesses p,t1,t2. Two certificates share a polarity, so R38 forces the accepted same-polarity two-extension collision package on {e,f}.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; minimum longest-support overlap equals two; accepted type-(2,2,3) support/exact-root normalization R130/R131 and endpoint replacement rule R140.

## Scope
Parent reduction of the entire type-(2,2,3) motif. It uses one long-root ordering and no payment or representative coupling.

## Applicability
Use to remove endpoint case trees from the type-(2,2,3) branch. Subsequent work need only close the type-(2,2,2) motif and the named same-polarity exterior-dimer collision alternative.

## Persistence
All generated longest supports, pairwise core identities, and the exterior-dimer collision certificate are graph-intrinsic. The three trimer orientations are separate certificates.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert that the type-(2,2,2) branch is impossible, does not spend the exterior-dimer collision, and does not assert simultaneous currentness of the three short trimers or replacement roots.

## Proof
Let B=(b1,b2,b3,b4,b5) be any tight ordering and put t1=b1,t2=b5. For each i, R140 supplies wi∈P={p,e,f} such that Ei=(B\\{ti})∪{wi} is a global-longest support. If wi is e or f, R140's private-endpoint clause shows ti cannot lie in X, hence ti∈Z. Then Ei=X∪(Z\\{ti})∪{wi}. Since wi is exterior to L∪D, we have L∩D=Y, D∩Ei=Z\\{ti}, and L∩Ei=X. These are three pairwise-disjoint 2-subsets, so L,D,Ei is a type-(2,2,2) minimum-overlap triangle. Assume therefore that neither endpoint produces this alternative. Then w1=w2=p. For each i, Ei=(B\\{ti})∪{p} is a tight five-path support. In the fixed deletion H−Y, the physical complement of Ei is exactly Ti={ti,e,f}; by R8 it supports a tight trimer. The original root has short trimer T0={p,e,f}. For each of T0,T1,T2 prescribe e as the middle. By R3 exactly one of the two complete reversals with middle e is tight, and R41 supplies signed terminal dimers. In every case {e,f} is a terminal physical dimer, with witness respectively p,t1,t2. The witnesses are distinct because t1,t2 are distinct vertices of B and B is disjoint from {p,e,f}. Thus {e,f} has three signed certificates with distinct witnesses. There are only two polarities, so two certificates have the same polarity; R38 then forces the accepted same-polarity two-extension collision package on the physical support {e,f}.
