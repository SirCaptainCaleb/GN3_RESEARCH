# R400 — Common-shadow three-cap occupancy normal form

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let T={a,b,c}. Suppose one exact two-cover H-T=U sqcup V supports endpoint-restoration caps for all three deleted vertices. Then, unless H has a spanning two-cover, the cap-end occupancy is forced into one of two structured residues. (I) All three caps concentrate at the same physical end of U or V. (II) Exactly two deleted vertices, say a,b, cap the same physical end and the third caps a different end; then the physical deleted dimer {a,b} carries both opposite tested orientations as signed supports of the same polarity with the same witness equal to the terminal vertex of the duplicated cap end. Concretely, if U=(u_1,u_2,...) and a,b both cap the left end, then in the nonclosing branch (a,b,u_1) and (b,a,u_1) are both bad, hence (u_1,b,a) and (u_1,a,b) are tight. The right-end dual gives the opposite common polarity.

## Hypotheses
H is a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample; T is a triple; one exact H-T cover supports all three endpoint caps.

## Scope
Order-free refinement of R396 retaining the cap-end occupancy and the exact bidirectional deleted-dimer residue.

## Applicability
Use after common-shadow cap alignment. It reduces nonclosure to either three-cap concentration at one physical end or a bidirectionally signed dimer on two deleted vertices with a named terminal witness.

## Persistence
The common residual cover, cap targets, duplicated end, deleted dimer, witness, tested orientations, and signs are literal/graph-intrinsic in the displayed frame.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the bidirectional dimer closes, that the concentrated case is impossible, or that cap representatives always align.

## Proof
There are four physical ends because R168 gives both residual rails order at least two. If the three caps target three distinct ends, the three endpoint attachments commute and give a spanning two-cover, impossible. Suppose exactly a,b target one left end U=(u1,u2,...) and c targets a different end. The cap turns (a,u1,u2) and (b,u1,u2) are tight. If (a,b,u1) is tight, then (a,b,u1,u2,...) is a tight extension of U using both a,b; attaching c at its distinct capped end commutes and closes H. Likewise if (b,a,u1) is tight. Hence in a surviving counterexample both stack turns are bad. Boundary antisymmetry gives (u1,b,a) and (u1,a,b) tight, which are same-polarity signed certificates on the two tested orientations (b,a) and (a,b) with common witness u1. The right-end case is the reversal-dual. If all three target the same end, retain that as the concentrated residue.
