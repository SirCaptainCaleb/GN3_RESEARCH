# R453 — Three-cover six-splice mate-clause fan

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a Strong Level-(1) boundary tournament with pc(H)>2, and let H=A sqcup B sqcup C be a literal spanning three-cover in which A,B,C are nontrivial tight paths. For every ordered pair of distinct components, say A=(a_0,...,a_p) and B=(b_0,...,b_q) with p,q>=1, the literal spanning two-path proposal (a_0,...,a_p,b_0,...,b_q) sqcup C has complete hole set exactly h_1=(a_{p-1},a_p,b_0) and h_2=(a_p,b_0,b_1). Since H has no spanning two-cover, at least one hole is bad, so boundary antisymmetry yields the physically certified mate clause bar(h_1) OR bar(h_2), i.e. (b_0,a_p,a_{p-1}) tight OR (b_1,b_0,a_p) tight. Repeating over the six ordered pairs of components gives six explicit R173-admissible two-hole mate clauses attached to the same literal three-cover. In particular, if H is a smallest Strong counterexample and J is any proper tight trimer, an R18 currentization H=J sqcup U sqcup V has |U|,|V|>=2 by R168 and therefore carries the full six-splice clause fan.

## Hypotheses
Strong Level-(1); pc(H)>2; H=A sqcup B sqcup C is a literal spanning three-cover; all three components are nontrivial. For the trimer-currentization specialization, H is a smallest counterexample, J is proper, and R18/R168 are available.

## Scope
Order-free physical clause compiler. The generic six-splice statement uses only a literal nontrivial three-cover, pc(H)>2, and boundary antisymmetry. The R18/R168 specialization applies to every proper tight trimer in a smallest counterexample.

## Applicability
Use when R173-compatible physical clauses are required. It shows that clause existence is abundant at any nontrivial three-cover; downstream progress must therefore come from complement/mate linkage among clauses or from retained structural roles, not from producing one isolated clause.

## Persistence
Each clause retains its exact ordered component pair, literal spanning two-path proposal, and complete two-hole list. The six proposals are alternative proof certificates built from the same three graph-intrinsic component paths; they are not asserted simultaneously current after later representative changes.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not assert the six clauses contain a Physical Mate-Cycle, that any two clauses have complementary holes, or that a single clause closes H. Does not license reversal of an entire component path. Does not preserve unrelated ancestry under an R18 currentization.

## Proof
Fix an ordered pair A,B and leave C untouched. Write A=(a_0,...,a_p) and B=(b_0,...,b_q), with p,q>=1. In the concatenated path A*B=(a_0,...,a_p,b_0,...,b_q), every consecutive triple lying wholly inside A or wholly inside B is tight by hypothesis. The only new consecutive triples are h_1=(a_{p-1},a_p,b_0) and h_2=(a_p,b_0,b_1). Thus (A*B) sqcup C is a literal spanning two-path proposal whose complete hole set is exactly {h_1,h_2}. If both holes were tight, it would be a spanning two-cover of H, contradicting pc(H)>2. Hence at least one h_i is bad. Boundary antisymmetry R3 says exactly the complete reversal of each bad ordered triple is tight, so bar(h_1)=(b_0,a_p,a_{p-1}) or bar(h_2)=(b_1,b_0,a_p) is tight. This is precisely a physically certified two-hole mate clause of the kind admissible to R173. There are six ordered pairs among A,B,C, and the same argument applies to each. For the proper-trimer specialization, R18 supplies H=J sqcup U sqcup V and R168 gives |U|,|V|>=2; J has order three, so all three components are nontrivial and the generic clause fan applies.
