# R101 — Transversal triangle three-frame deficit kernel

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the transversal alternative of R98. Then the three supports can be coordinatized as A=T∪{x,y,alpha}, B=T∪{x,z,beta}, C=T∪{y,z,gamma}, where |T|=2 and the remaining vertices are delta,epsilon. For the three minimum-overlap pairs AB, AC, BC, let S_x=T∪{x}, S_y=T∪{y}, S_z=T∪{z}. Every exact two-cover of H-S_x, H-S_y, or H-S_z has profile 4+3 and satisfies the corresponding three-block deficit identity t=1+sigma. In the x-frame, sigma=0 exactly when the three-vertex R-block {gamma,delta,epsilon} is one tight-trimer component and the other component is a tight four-path formed by concatenating the wing dimers on {y,alpha} and {z,beta} with exactly one inter-wing transition. Cyclically, the y-frame zero packet concatenates the wing dimers on {x,alpha} and {z,gamma} and has trimer {beta,delta,epsilon}; the z-frame zero packet concatenates the wing dimers on {x,beta} and {y,gamma} and has trimer {alpha,delta,epsilon}. Consequently either at least one of the three frames admits a positive-excess exact two-cover, or all three displayed zero-excess concatenated 4+3 packets exist.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; A,B are globally minimum-overlap with |A∩B|=3; C is a transversal support in R98 alternative (i).

## Scope
Finite support/deletion kernel for the transversal branch.

## Applicability
Use before reopening representative-dependent return machinery: positive excess supplies a crossing-rich frame; failure of positive excess leaves the explicit three-packet equality kernel.

## Persistence
The three physical deletion frames and their zero-excess packets are support-level; sigma remains representative-relative to the chosen exact cover.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not claim that positive excess itself gives overlap descent, nor that the simultaneous three-packet equality kernel is already impossible.

## Proof
By R98, write C as two vertices of S=A∩B together with one vertex from each of U=A\B, V=B\A, and R=V(H)\(A∪B). Thus |A∩C|=|B∩C|=3. Since |A∩B|=3 is globally minimum, all three pairs A,B,C are minimum-overlap pairs. Let T=A∩B∩C, so |T|=2. Let x be the unique vertex of (A∩B)\C, y the unique vertex of (A∩C)\B, z the unique vertex of (B∩C)\A, and let alpha,beta,gamma be the private vertices of A,B,C respectively. The three supports are therefore A=T∪{x,y,alpha}, B=T∪{x,z,beta}, C=T∪{y,z,gamma}. Their union has eight vertices; denote the two exterior vertices by delta,epsilon.

Consider first the pair A,B. Its common triple is S_x=T∪{x}, its two wings are {y,alpha} and {z,beta}, and its R-block is {gamma,delta,epsilon}. By R5, H-S_x has an exact two-cover. More generally let T_x be any exact two-cover of H-S_x. R11 says its profile is 5+2 or 4+3. If it had a five-rail D, then R9 makes D a global-longest support in F. But D is disjoint from S_x and hence meets A only inside the two-vertex wing {y,alpha}, so |D∩A|≤2, contradicting global minimum overlap three. Therefore every T_x has profile 4+3.

For the physical partition {{y,alpha},{z,beta},{gamma,delta,epsilon}}, the two wings have intrinsic path-cover number one and the R-block has intrinsic path-cover number one by R8. R7 therefore gives t=1+sigma with sigma≥0. If sigma=0, each class restriction is one path and there is exactly one inter-class transition. Since the cover profile is already 4+3, the only possible grouping is the union of the two two-vertex wings versus the three-vertex R-block. More precisely, the R-block is the tight-trimer component and the four-component concatenates the tight dimer on {y,alpha} with the tight dimer on {z,beta} using exactly one inter-wing transition. Conversely, an exact cover of precisely this form has minimal class restrictions and t=1, so sigma=0 by R7.

The identical argument for the pair A,C gives common triple S_y=T∪{y}, wings {x,alpha} and {z,gamma}, R-block {beta,delta,epsilon}, and its zero packet concatenates the two wing dimers with exactly one inter-wing transition while {beta,delta,epsilon} is the trimer component. For B,C one gets S_z=T∪{z}, wings {x,beta} and {y,gamma}, R-block {alpha,delta,epsilon}, with the analogous one-transition wing concatenation and trimer component.

Finally, if any of the three frames admits an exact two-cover with sigma≥1, the first alternative of the claimed dichotomy holds. Otherwise every exact two-cover in each frame has sigma=0. By R5 at least one exact two-cover exists in each frame, so the corresponding canonical zero packet exists in all three frames. This proves the three-frame deficit kernel.
