# R294 — Pairwise internal-dimer puncture synchronization through one fixed exterior trimer

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Let H be a hypothetical smallest Strong Level-(1) counterexample and let K be a proper tight path with two distinct internal adjacent dimers D,D'. Apply the corrected puncture theorem R293 separately. Unless a spanning two-cover occurs, obtain puncture-indexed historical capture vertices b,b' on K, each with its own valid original-frame capture certificate. Independently choose five distinct exterior vertices d,d',y,r,s. The spokes S={b,d} and S'={b',d'} carry signed certificates with the same witness y via the unique d-middle and d'-middle tight trimers. Let T be the tight trimer on {r,y,s} with middle y. Each spoke forms a direct mass-four balanced pair with a terminal dimer of T of opposite polarity, and after one pair birth is chosen first the second is nonquiet by R160. Thus the two punctures share one physical exterior witness y and one fixed-trimer interaction packet while their capture ancestries remain separate and attached only to the original puncture frames.

## Hypotheses

## Persistence

## Proof
Apply R293 to D and D'. If either branch closes, stop. Otherwise retain the historical capture vertices b,b'. R169 supplies at least five exterior vertices; choose distinct d,d',y,r,s. R3 gives one tight d-middle trimer on {b,d,y} and one tight d'-middle trimer on {b',d',y}; R41 signs S,S' with common witness y. R3 also prescribes the y-middle tight orientation of {r,y,s}, call it T. Its terminal dimers have opposite polarities by R41. Choose for each spoke a terminal mate of opposite polarity; the spokes are disjoint from T. R39 gives two direct pair births and R160 makes the second nonquiet. No capture-to-fresh-support transport occurs.
