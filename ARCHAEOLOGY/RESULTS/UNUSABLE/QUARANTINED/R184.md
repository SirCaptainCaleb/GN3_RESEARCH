# R184 — Minimum-overlap-one two-hole replacement fan

**Record status:** `UNUSABLE/QUARANTINED`

## Statement
Assume the standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case and let A be a global-longest five-support with five-vertex exterior E=V(H)\\A. Suppose the global minimum overlap between distinct longest-five supports is one. Choose any tight trimer support P contained in E and write E\\P={x,y}. Then for every a in A, at least one of E\\{x}∪{a} and E\\{y}∪{a} is a global-longest five-support. Consequently one of the two four-sets E\\{x}, E\\{y} is contained in at least three distinct global-longest five-supports obtained by adjoining three distinct vertices of A.

## Hypotheses
Standing order-ten smallest-counterexample case; global minimum overlap among distinct longest-five supports equals one; accepted R8, R9, R13 and recovered three-exterior pair-extension lemma R146.

## Scope
Support-level gateway for the minimum-overlap-one branch. No sign, payment, exact-return, or representative synchronization is used.

## Applicability
Use as the first normalization once minimum overlap one is established. It turns an arbitrary overlap-one pair into a radius-one fan over one of two four-vertex exterior cores.

## Persistence
All resulting five-supports and the common four-core are graph-intrinsic. The chosen tight trimer orientation is only a proof certificate.

## Exclusions / nonclaims
Does not identify which of the two holes is used for a given a, does not assert one common hole works for all five vertices of A, and does not itself force a spanning two-cover.

## Proof
Let A be a global-longest five-support and E=V(H)\\A. Since H is a counterexample to a spanning two-path cover, E cannot support a tight five-path: otherwise A together with a tight path on E would be a spanning 5+5 cover. Choose a three-subset P of E and orient it as a tight trimer using R8. Write the remaining two vertices of E as x,y. Fix any a∈A. Apply R146 to P and the three distinct exterior vertices x,y,a. At least one of P∪{x,y}=E, P∪{x,a}=E\\{y}∪{a}, or P∪{y,a}=E\\{x}∪{a} supports a tight five-path. The first support E is impossible, so at least one of the two one-vertex replacement supports is Hamiltonian. By R9 it is global-longest. This holds for each of the five choices a∈A. Each a therefore chooses at least one of the two holes x,y; assigning one available hole to each a and applying pigeonhole, one hole is used for at least three distinct vertices of A. Equivalently one of E\\{x},E\\{y} lies in at least three distinct global-longest five-supports obtained by adjoining distinct A-vertices.
