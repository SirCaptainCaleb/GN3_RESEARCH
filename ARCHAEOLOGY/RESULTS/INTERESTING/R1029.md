# R1029 — D17.430 five-set data do not force a Hamilton P5 with the wall vertex as an endpoint

**Audit state:** PASS
**Proof provenance:** Slack `#a7c3-workspace` root `1789280602.868739`

## Statement
There is a five-vertex boundary tournament on `Z={A,C,t,b,d}` satisfying all four retained OUT star-cycle turns from D17.430,

`(d,b,t), (b,t,A), (A,t,C), (C,t,b)` tight,

but having no tight Hamilton P5 with the wall vertex `d` as an endpoint. Therefore the retained D17.430 five-set data alone do not imply an endpoint-controlled Hamilton P5 usable as a far-wall insertion port.

## Construction
For each middle vertex `v`, write `x->y` in `T_v` when `(x,v,y)` is tight. Define the five local tournaments as follows.

- `T_A`: transitive `C->b->d->t`.
- `T_C`: transitive `b->A->d->t`.
- `T_b`: transitive `A->C->d->t`.
- `T_d`: transitive `t->b->C->A`.
- `T_t`: `d` dominates `{A,C,b}`, while `A->C->b->A` is the directed triangle on the other three vertices.

These five local tournaments specify a boundary tournament on `Z`: every ordered triple with distinct vertices is decided by the tournament at its middle vertex, and complete reversal has the opposite value.

## Verification of the D17.430 packet
At middle `b`, `T_b` has `d->t`, so `(d,b,t)` is tight. At middle `t`, the directed triangle has `b->A`, `A->C`, and `C->b`, so `(b,t,A)`, `(A,t,C)`, and `(C,t,b)` are all tight. Thus the exact OUT star-cycle packet retained by D17.430 is realized.

The only cyclic local star in this construction is `T_t`; the other four local tournaments are transitive. In particular the model is compatible with the star-cycle branch rather than introducing an unrelated local defect.

## Endpoint check
Direct inspection of the local tournament tables gives exactly three tight four-vertex paths beginning at `d`:

`(d,A,t,C)`, `(d,C,t,b)`, `(d,b,t,A)`.

Their unique unused vertices are respectively `b,A,C`. Extending them to Hamilton P5s would require, respectively,

`(t,C,b)`, `(t,b,A)`, `(t,A,C)` tight.

All three are bad in the displayed local tournaments: at middle `C`, `b->...->t` places `b` before `t`; at middle `b`, `A->...->t` places `A` before `t`; and at middle `A`, `C->...->t` places `C` before `t`. Hence no Hamilton P5 starts at `d`.

Dually, direct inspection gives exactly three tight four-vertex paths ending at `d`:

`(A,b,C,d)`, `(C,A,b,d)`, `(b,C,A,d)`.

Prepending the respective unused vertex `t` would require

`(t,A,b)`, `(t,C,A)`, `(t,b,C)` tight.

Each is bad in the corresponding transitive local tournament. Hence no Hamilton P5 ends at `d`.

Therefore this boundary tournament has no tight Hamilton P5 with `d` as either endpoint.

For completeness, the model is nevertheless Hamiltonian, as D17.430/R902 predicts in the star-cycle branch. For example `(b,d,A,t,C)` is a tight Hamilton P5. The counterexample is specifically to endpoint control, not to existence of a Hamilton P5.

## Scope
This result fences only the inference from the retained five-set D17.430 data to a Hamilton P5 whose endpoint is the fixed far-wall vertex. It does not rule out endpoint control after adding source-gate ancestry, zipper data, or other information outside the five-set, and it does not invalidate the D17.430 Hamilton-5/twin-anchor-wall alternative itself.

## Applicability
Any G37 splice requiring `d` as an insertion port must use more than the four OUT star-cycle turns retained in D17.430, or use a different splice geometry. The construction is an explicit witness that a purely local strengthening of the five-set packet cannot supply that endpoint for free.

## Dependencies
- R3 / boundary antisymmetry
- D17.430 retained OUT star-cycle packet
