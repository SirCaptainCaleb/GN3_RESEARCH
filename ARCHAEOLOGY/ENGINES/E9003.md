# E9003: First-source prepayment phase descent

## Theorem

Let `H` be a hypothetical smallest counterexample to the two-path-cover theorem, with `n=|H|`. Let

`F_1,...,F_r`

be a finite retained packet of literal phase-one maximum spanning three-forest checkpoints participating in one certificate-retaining prepayment analysis. In `F_i` mark a longest rail of order `M_i` and assign the coarse phase-one rank

`rho(F_i)=(1,n-M_i)`.

Put

`M_*=max_i M_i`.

Suppose that, before any phase-zero transition, the retained analysis itself emits either a graph-intrinsic proper tight path `K`, or a proper tight cycle together with a named cyclic break whose deletion gives such a path `K`. The participating source checkpoints and the physical event producing `K` remain in the retained ancestry. Then either `H` has a spanning two-cover, or the analysis reaches a retained checkpoint of phased rank strictly below every `rho(F_i)`.

More precisely, the descending checkpoint may be chosen either in phase one with a marked rail of order at least `M_*+1`, hence rank at most `(1,n-M_*-1)`, or at the certified terminal-wall entrance to phase zero constructed below, whose leading rank coordinate is `0`.

The assertion is source-relative. An unrelated proper path elsewhere in `H`, an arbitrary balanced pair, a support change, or a fresh maximum three-forest does not satisfy the hypothesis. Nor is the theorem a replay rule from a parent checkpoint already in phase zero.

## 1. Currentization

If the emitted object is a cycle, cut it at the certified break and call the resulting proper tight path `K`. If `K` spans `H`, we are done. Otherwise `H-V(K)` is a proper induced subsystem, so minimality gives it a path cover by at most two tight paths. It cannot be Hamiltonian, because a Hamilton path of the complement together with `K` would already two-cover `H`. Thus

`H-V(K)=U|V`

is an exact two-cover, and

`G=K|U|V`

is a literal spanning three-path forest carrying the retained source ancestry.

Mark a longest rail `A` of `G` and let `L=|A|`. If `L>M_*`, then

`(1,n-L)<(1,n-M_*)<=rho(F_i)`

for every source `F_i`, so the currentized checkpoint already gives the required descent. Hence assume `L<=M_*`. We show that the marked rail either grows through the old packet ceiling, closes `H`, or reaches a certified terminal wall.

## 2. Marked-rail growth and the terminal wall

Write the current forest as

`A|B|C`, `A=(a_0,...,a_s)`,

with `A` longest. If `|A|=1`, then all three rails are singletons and `H` has order three, hence is itself a tight trimer; so we may assume `|A|>=2`.

Consider another rail `X=(x_0,...,x_m)`. At the right end of `A`, test

`alpha=(a_{s-1},a_s,x_0)`.

If `alpha` is tight and `X` is a singleton, adjoining `x_0` to `A` merges two rails and gives a spanning two-cover with the third rail. If `X` is nontrivial, test `(a_s,x_0,x_1)`. If it is tight, the concatenation `AX` together with the third rail two-covers `H`. If it is bad, delete `x_0x_1` and add `a_sx_0`; the new forest is

`(a_0,...,a_s,x_0)|(x_1,...,x_m)|Y`,

and the marked rail has grown by one.

Therefore, whenever no rightward growth is available, `alpha` is bad and boundary antisymmetry gives

`(x_0,a_s,a_{s-1})`

tight. Thus the donor source `x_0` certifies the reverse terminal dimer

`D_R=(a_s,a_{s-1})`.

Dually, at the left end, if `(x_m,a_0,a_1)` is tight, then either `X` is singleton and we two-cover, or deleting `x_{m-1}x_m` and adding `x_ma_0` grows the marked rail. Hence failure of every leftward growth gives

`(a_1,a_0,x_m)`

tight, so the donor terminal `x_m` certifies the reverse initial dimer

`D_L=(a_1,a_0)`.

Greedily perform any available growth. Every successful move transfers exactly one endpoint from a donor rail to the marked rail, so the marked rail grows by one and remains longest. The process is finite. Stop immediately if a spanning two-cover appears or if the marked rail first reaches order `M_*+1`. Otherwise we arrive at a terminal forest

`F^*=A^*|B^*|C^*`

with no inward endpoint growth at either end of `A^*`. At such a wall, the sources of `B^*,C^*` certify `D_R` in one polarity and their terminal vertices certify `D_L` in the opposite polarity.

The terminal marked rail has order at least three. If `|A^*|=2`, say `A^*=(a_0,a_1)`, every donor rail has order at most two. If a donor `B^*=(b_0,b_1)` is nontrivial, terminality gives both

`(b_0,a_1,a_0)` and `(a_1,a_0,b_1)`

tight, so `(b_0,a_1,a_0,b_1)` is a tight `P4` spanning `A^* union B^*`; together with `C^*` it two-covers `H`. If both donors are singletons, `A^*` together with the dimer on the two donor vertices is already a two-cover. Thus every genuine terminal wall satisfies `|A^*|>=3`.

## 3. The certified terminal-wall phase-zero entrance

We now construct the terminal-wall phase transition itself. This is not the rule “balanced pair implies phase zero”; it is a specific certified birth attached to the no-slide wall.

First suppose `|A^*|>=4`, with `A^*=(a_0,...,a_s)`. The reverse boundary dimers

`D_L=(a_1,a_0)`, `D_R=(a_s,a_{s-1})`

have disjoint physical supports. Terminality supplies witnesses of opposite endpoint polarity: a donor source `x_0` gives `(x_0,a_s,a_{s-1})` tight, while a donor terminal `x_m` gives `(a_1,a_0,x_m)` tight. Thus `D_R` and `D_L` form a certified opposite-polarity `2+2` balanced pair. Retain this pair, its named donor witnesses, and one actual consecutive tight turn `J` of `A^*`.

Now suppose `|A^*|=3`, say

`A^*=(a_0,a_1,a_2)`.

Delete the hinge `a_1`. The residue `H-a_1` inherits the literal four-cover

`{a_0}|{a_2}|B^*|C^*`.

Minimality gives a path cover by at most two paths, and the residue is not Hamiltonian because a Hamilton path there together with singleton `(a_1)` would two-cover `H`. Hence it has an exact two-cover `T`.

Some selected edge `xy` of `T` joins two distinct components of the inherited four-cover; otherwise two connected `T`-rails could cover at most two of the four nonempty components. Boundary antisymmetry on `{a_1,x,y}` gives exactly one of

`(a_1,x,y)`, `(y,x,a_1)`

tight.

If `(a_1,x,y)` is tight, retain the selected dimer `(x,y)` head-signed by witness `a_1`; the singleton support `{a_1}` carries the opposite tail polarity through the witnessed extension `(a_1,x,y)`. If `(y,x,a_1)` is tight, retain the reverse dimer `(y,x)` tail-signed by `a_1`, while singleton `{a_1}` carries the opposite head polarity through the same witnessed triple. In either orientation we obtain a physically disjoint certified opposite-polarity `1+2` pair. Its birth certificate retains the hinge, the selected crossing state and its direction, the two crossed four-cover components, and the fixed tight turn

`J=(a_0,a_1,a_2)`.

Accordingly, in both terminal-wall cases we have the same legal phase-transition data: the no-slide forest `F^*`, a fixed graph-intrinsic turn `J` of the marked rail, and a certified opposite-polarity pair born from that wall. Define the resulting retained state

`Phi^*=(F^*,J,C_wall)`

to be the terminal-wall phase-zero entrance checkpoint.

The coarse phased rank is lexicographic: a marked phase-one forest `A|B|C` has rank `(1,n-|A|)`, while a terminal-wall phase-zero checkpoint has rank `(0,...)`, with `0<1`. The phase change is therefore attached to the certified terminal-wall birth just constructed, not to arbitrary balanced geometry elsewhere.

The greedy continuation now has exactly three outcomes: a spanning two-cover; first arrival at marked order `M_*+1`; or the certified terminal phase-zero entrance. In the second case the rank is `(1,n-M_*-1)`, strictly below every source rank `(1,n-M_i)`. In the third case the leading coordinate drops from `1` to `0`, again strictly below every participating source. The original source packet and the physical birth of `K` remain in the retained ancestry throughout. This proves the core descent theorem.

## 4. Canonical exact-cover entry portals

Once a legal source-labelled prepayment analysis has produced an eligible proper path or certified cycle break, the theorem above takes over. We record two standard comparison mechanisms that manufacture such births.

### Pair-deletion crossing portal

Suppose the retained analysis produces an exact two-cover of `H-{p,t}` and identifies a selected physical state `xy` as the relevant source-labelled crossing state. Restore the deleted dimer `(p,t)` in the retained packet. Boundary antisymmetry on the distinct vertices `{p,x,y}` makes exactly one of

`(p,x,y)` and `(y,x,p)`

tight. Since `t` is absent, the resulting trimer is a proper tight path of `H`, and its birth retains the deleted pair, the selected portal state, and the source ancestry. It is therefore an eligible input to the descent theorem.

### Two nonidentical exact covers of one residue

Let `D` have order two or three and carry a retained tight Hamilton path. Let

`R=R_1|R_2`, `T=T_1|T_2`

be exact two-covers of `W=H-D` in the same retained prepayment packet. Restore the same tight path on `D` to both covers.

If the unordered rail-support bipartitions differ, some selected state `zw` of one cover, say `T`, joins the two support components of `R`. In the qualification applications, `|H|>10` and `|D|<=3`, so `|W|>=8`; hence the two crossed `R`-components cannot both be singletons. Choose the crossing endpoint `z` in a nontrivial `R`-component and an inherited `R`-neighbor `p` of `z`. Boundary antisymmetry on `{p,z,w}` makes exactly one of

`(p,z,w)`, `(w,z,p)`

tight. This proper trimer uses literally the retained `R`-adjacency and the `T`-crossing, so its birth is source-visible and prepayment.

Suppose instead that the support bipartitions agree but one common support has different literal Hamilton orders. Let

`P=(v_0,...,v_k)`

and let `Q` be another tight Hamilton order on the same support with `Q!=P`. Read the `P`-indices in their order along `Q`. Since this permutation is not increasing, two consecutive `Q`-vertices are `v_i,v_j` with `i>j`.

If `i=j+1`, `Q` selects the reverse of the consecutive `P`-state `v_jv_i`; this selected reversal dimer is already a proper packet-born path. If `i>j+1`, test

`(v_{i-1},v_i,v_j)` and `(v_i,v_j,v_{j+1})`.

A bad first turn gives the tight reverse trimer `(v_j,v_i,v_{i-1})`; a bad second gives `(v_{j+1},v_j,v_i)`. If both are tight, then

`v_i,v_j,v_{j+1},...,v_{i-1},v_i`

is a vertex-simple proper tight cycle: its internal turns lie on `P` and the two seams are exactly the tested turns. Retain any named cyclic break.

Thus two nonidentical exact covers of one pair- or triple-deletion residue manufacture an eligible crossing trimer, selected reversal, reverse trimer, or certified proper-cycle break, with their common source packet retained.

## 5. Internal-puncture three-to-two entry

Let `D_0` have order one or two, let

`C=P|Q`

be an exact two-cover of `H-D_0`, and let `z` be internal on `P`. Deleting `z` splits `P` into two nonempty tight paths `L,R`, so

`L|R|Q`

is a literal three-path cover of

`W=H-(D_0 union {z})`.

Let `T` be an exact two-cover of `W` arising in the same retained prepayment comparison.

Some selected edge of `T` joins two distinct components among `L,R,Q`; otherwise the two connected `T`-rails would each remain inside one inherited component and could not span all three nonempty components. Let `xy` be such a crossing.

If one crossed component is nontrivial, choose a crossing endpoint `x` in that component and an inherited `C`-neighbor `h` of `x` lying in the same component. Boundary antisymmetry on `{h,x,y}` gives exactly one of

`(h,x,y)`, `(y,x,h)`

tight. This is a proper trimer whose two ordinary edges are the inherited source-cover adjacency and the selected `T`-crossing, so its birth is literal and source-labelled.

It remains only the corner in which both crossed components are singletons. The untouched rail `Q` must then be nontrivial. If `|D_0|=2` and `Q={q}` were singleton, the three vertices `D_0 union {q}` admit a tight trimer by boundary antisymmetry; together with `P` this would two-cover `H`. If `|D_0|=1`, say `D_0={d}`, and `Q={q}`, then `P` would be a Hamilton path of `H-{d,q}`; together with the dimer `(d,q)` it would again two-cover `H`. Both are impossible.

Hence the two crossed singleton components must be exactly `L` and `R`. Thus `P` had order three, say

`P=(ell,z,r)`

up to reversal, and the `T`-crossing joins `ell` to `r`. Use either inherited source edge `ell z` or `z r` together with that crossing. One boundary-antisymmetry test on the three physical vertices gives a proper tight trimer born directly from the punctured source packet.

Therefore every internal-puncture three-to-two component drop, including the length-three split-rail corner, manufactures an eligible source-labelled proper-path birth. No payment theorem and no abstract balanced-pair inference is used.

## 6. Direct globally-longest-path terminal-wall entrance

There is also a direct entrance to the terminal-wall interface that does not require a preceding phase-one source packet.

Choose a globally longest tight path `A` of `H`. If `A` spans `H`, then `H` is Hamiltonian and hence certainly has a spanning two-cover. Otherwise `H-V(A)` is a proper induced subsystem. By minimality it has a path cover by at most two tight paths, and it cannot be Hamiltonian, because a Hamilton path of the complement together with `A` would already two-cover `H`. Thus

`H-V(A)=B|C`

is an exact two-cover, and `A|B|C` is a literal spanning three-path forest.

This forest is already a no-slide terminal wall with marked rail `A`. Indeed, any successful inward endpoint transfer from `B` or `C` to either end of `A` would produce a tight path on `|A|+1` vertices, contradicting the global maximality of `A`; and any successful concatenation of `A` with an entire donor rail would directly give a spanning two-cover with the remaining rail. Hence none of the marked-rail growth moves of Section 2 is available unless `H` is already two-covered.

Therefore, in a hypothetical counterexample, `A|B|C` is a genuine terminal wall. Section 2 shows its marked rail has order at least three, and Section 3 applies verbatim: if `|A|>=4`, the two reverse boundary dimers form the certified opposite-polarity `2+2` birth; if `|A|=3`, deleting the hinge and comparing the inherited four-cover with an exact two-cover produces the certified opposite-polarity `1+2` birth. Retaining one fixed turn `J` of `A`, we obtain the same phase-zero entrance checkpoint

`Phi=(A|B|C,J,C_wall)`.

Thus every hypothetical smallest counterexample has a certified E9003 terminal-wall phase-zero interface obtained directly from a globally longest tight path. This statement is interface existence only: it does not assert descent from a preceding phase-one parent and does not weaken the no-phase-zero-replay restriction of the main theorem.

## 7. Exact scope and exported interface

The load-bearing hypothesis of the main descent theorem is *retained birth ancestry*. A path or cycle qualifies only when it is an actual output of the named phase-one prepayment analysis, with the participating source checkpoints and the physical event producing the object carried forward. This includes the crossing, exact-cover-disagreement, and internal-puncture constructions proved above, as well as any later mechanism that directly produces the same source-labelled proper-path or certified-cycle-break interface.

Several superficially similar objects do not qualify by themselves. An arbitrary proper path already present somewhere in `H` is not an input to the descent theorem merely because it exists. A balanced dimer pair, a support change, a fresh maximum three-forest, or an exact-cardinality matching is not such an input unless the retained analysis actually converts it into the required packet-born proper path or cycle break. In particular, a balanced pair born away from the certified terminal marked-rail wall does not by itself trigger the phase-zero transition used in the descent proof. The direct globally-longest-path construction of Section 6 is a separate terminal-wall interface theorem, not an exception to this ancestry requirement.

The parent phase is equally essential. All participating sources in the descent theorem are phase-one checkpoints. If the only retained parent is already phase zero, returning later to a maximum three-forest gives a state with leading coordinate one, and comparison with the old phase-one ceiling no longer proves descent below that phase-zero parent. Thus the theorem makes no recursive claim about a later phase-zero replay of the same static geometry.

Finally, strict phased-rank descent is an interface, not global extinction. The argument stops once it has produced either a spanning two-cover or a checkpoint strictly below every participating phase-one source. Likewise, the direct construction of Section 6 stops once it has produced the certified terminal-wall phase-zero interface. What happens after a phase-zero entrance, or after any other strictly lower checkpoint, belongs to the downstream phase mechanism and is not asserted here.

Accordingly the reusable first-source descent interface is

`retained finite phase-one source packet + certificate-preserving prepayment proper-path/cycle birth`

`=> TWO-COVER or strict phased-rank descent below every participating source`,

while the direct terminal-wall entrance is

`hypothetical smallest counterexample + globally longest tight path`

`=> TWO-COVER or certified terminal-wall phase-zero interface`.

The proof establishes these interfaces from smallest-counterexample minimality and boundary antisymmetry alone: it currentizes a packet-born path when present, grows a marked largest rail against the old packet ceiling, constructs the certified `2+2` or `1+2` terminal-wall birth that legitimately enters phase zero, proves the standard exact-cover and puncture entry portals, and observes that a globally longest path makes the terminal wall available immediately. No quarantined small-order inputs are used. ∎
