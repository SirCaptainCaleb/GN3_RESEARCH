# Double far-edge contact on a canonical 4445 entrance rail is confined to its first two edges

## Statement

In the 4445 setting let R_b=(h_1,h_2,h_3) be a canonical three-edge entrance path for f_b={b,v,u_b}. Suppose the rank-five edge e5 meets R_b in both of its non-v vertices d,w. Then one of d,w lies in h_1 and the other lies in h_2.

Equivalently, the contact-position sets {h_1,h_3} and {h_2,h_3} are impossible. Hence every double-contact canonical rail has the normal form in which h_1,h_2,e5 form a linear 3-cycle, with h_3 as the stem from h_2 to the low entrance b.

## Body

Because e5 is distinct from every edge of R_b and H is linear, d and w cannot lie in the same h_i. Thus the two contacts occupy one of the three pairs {h_1,h_2}, {h_1,h_3}, {h_2,h_3}.

If they occupy h_2 and h_3, then e5 is disjoint from h_1. Also f_b meets R_b only at b∈h_3, so f_b is disjoint from h_1,h_2. Therefore
  (h_1,h_2,e5,f_b)
is a four-edge linear path. It ends in f_b and enters through e5∩f_b={v}, contradicting that the nonspecial rank-four edge f_b has unique rank-four entrance b.

If the contacts occupy h_1 and h_3, then e5 is disjoint from h_2. Again f_b is disjoint from h_1,h_2. Hence
  (h_2,h_1,e5,f_b)
is a four-edge linear path ending in f_b through v, the same contradiction.

Thus only {h_1,h_2} remains. Since h_1,h_2 are consecutive and e5 meets each in a distinct vertex d,w, while h_1∩h_2 is a third vertex, the three edges h_1,h_2,e5 form a linear 3-cycle. The edge h_3 is adjacent to h_2 and contains the endpoint b, giving the stated cycle-plus-stem normal form.
