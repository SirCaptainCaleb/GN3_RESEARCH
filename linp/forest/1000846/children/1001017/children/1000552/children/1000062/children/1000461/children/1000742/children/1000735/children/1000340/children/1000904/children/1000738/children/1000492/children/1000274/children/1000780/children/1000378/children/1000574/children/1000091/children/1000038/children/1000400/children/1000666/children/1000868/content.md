# Entrance-label blockers on a flat gap-one cycle immediately create balanced rail lenses

## Statement

In a flat rank-(p-1) terminal cycle with private entrance labels x_i of potential p-2 and maximum entrance rails R_i ending at x_i, suppose R_i contains x_j for some j!=i. Then R_i and R_j have at least two common vertices and therefore contain a balanced elementary lens. Consequently, in any flat-cycle state with no balanced lens between distinct entrance rails, every forced foreign-cycle contact of every R_i must occur at a terminal vertex of the contacted cycle edge, hence at a vertex of potential p.

## Body

Both R_i and R_j are maximum endpoint paths of length p-2, ending at x_i and x_j respectively. They share x_j because x_j lies on R_i and is the endpoint of R_j. If x_j were their unique common vertex, the universal unique-intersection theorem 5854d853a44b would force x_j to be an internal same-index joint of R_j, impossible because x_j is its endpoint. Thus they share a second vertex. Choosing consecutive common vertices gives an elementary lens. Since the two host paths have equal length and are both maximum endpoint paths, the usual two replacement inequalities force the two lens sides to have equal length. Therefore every entrance-label blocker creates a balanced rail lens. The contrapositive yields the terminal-only blocker conclusion; terminals of a flat rank-(p-1) edge have potential p by definition of the flat cycle.
