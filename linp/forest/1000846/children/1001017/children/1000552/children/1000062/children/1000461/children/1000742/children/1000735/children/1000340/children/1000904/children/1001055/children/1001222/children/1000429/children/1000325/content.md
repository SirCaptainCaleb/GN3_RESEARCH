# Single-blocker cells yield two top-potential rotation endpoints

## Statement

Let P=(g_1,...,g_p) be a maximum p-edge linear path ending at v, and let F be a family of distinct edges f through v such that each f has exactly one contact vertex with V(P)\V(g_p). Then, apart from O(1) boundary contacts, every occupied two-slot cell
  C_i={b_i,z_i},   z_i=g_i∩g_{i+1},
forces two distinct vertices of endpoint potential at least p:
  b_{i+2}  and  z_{i+1}=g_{i+1}∩g_{i+2}.
Consequently there are at least
  |F|-O(1)
distinct vertices w with phi(w)>=p that occur as endpoints of p-edge rotations of P generated by members of F.

Applied to the switching family of b032348c1a8a at an active misaligned vertex v of potential p, if eta_v=o(p), then the chosen maximum p-path supports at least
  (5/8-o(1))p
distinct vertices of potential at least p arising from switching rotations. In the gap-one notation p=q+1 with local slack delta, the packet has size at least
  (5/8)q-O(delta+1),
doubling the previous 5/16 coefficient from 5f8d96bf566a.

## Body

For 1<=i<=p-2 let b_i be the private vertex of g_i and z_i=g_i∩g_{i+1}, and put C_i={b_i,z_i}. As in 5f8d96bf566a, if the unique precursor contact c of f lies in C_i, omitting g_{i+1} and inserting f gives the p-edge rotation
  g_1,...,g_i,f,g_p,g_{p-1},...,g_{i+2}.              (1)
The last edge of (1) is g_{i+2}. Its predecessor in (1) is g_{i+3} when i<=p-3 (with the evident endpoint interpretation at i=p-2). Therefore every vertex of g_{i+2} not lying in that predecessor may be chosen as the last vertex of the rotated p-edge path.

Two such vertices are always available:
- the private vertex b_{i+2};
- the backward joint z_{i+1}=g_{i+1}∩g_{i+2}.

The edge g_{i+1} was omitted from (1), so z_{i+1} does not occur in the preceding path through its old occurrence. By linearity of the original path it occurs in no other retained path edge. It also does not lie in f: the only precursor contact of f is c in C_i, while z_{i+1} is distinct from both b_i and z_i, and f∩g_p={v}. Thus z_{i+1} is a legitimate last vertex of (1). Hence
  phi(b_{i+2})>=p,
  phi(z_{i+1})>=p.                                    (2)

For distinct cells C_i,C_j, the output pairs
  {b_{i+2},z_{i+1}}
and
  {b_{j+2},z_{j+1}}
are disjoint: private vertices of different path edges are distinct, path joints at different indices are distinct, and no private vertex equals a path joint. Thus every occupied interior cell contributes two distinct p-potential vertices.

Each cell C_i contains only two possible contact vertices, and distinct edges through v have distinct non-v contact vertices by linearity. Therefore if s_int members of F have contacts in the interior cells, at least ceil(s_int/2) cells are occupied. By (2) these cells yield at least
  2 ceil(s_int/2) >= s_int
distinct vertices of potential at least p.

Only O(1) contacts lie in the boundary positions not covered by these cells, so the total number of distinct p-potential rotation endpoints is at least |F|-O(1).

For a switching family at an active misaligned center, b032348c1a8a gives
  |F| >= beta(p)-eta_v-ceil((3q(v)-4)/4)
      >= (5/8)p-eta_v-O(1).
Hence eta_v=o(p) gives a packet of (5/8-o(1))p distinct vertices of potential at least p.

In the gap-one notation p=q+1 and delta controls the switching loss exactly as in fecba48a3ffd, giving
  |F| >= (5/8)q-O(delta+1).
The same doubled-endpoint argument applies.
