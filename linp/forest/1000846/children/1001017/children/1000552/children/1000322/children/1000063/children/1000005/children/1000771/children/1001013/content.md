# Every matching deletion of cyclic STS(13) is all-special

## Statement

Let S be the cyclic STS(13) generated by the translates of {0,1,4} and {0,2,7}. For every matching M of blocks, S\M has minimum degree at least 5, is P_7^(3)-free, and every surviving edge is special of edge rank 6.

## Body


Let S be the cyclic STS(13), with block orbits
A_i={i,i+1,i+4},  B_i={i,i+2,i+7}
in Z_13, and let M be any matching.

By aad44f4c4df5, S\M has minimum degree at least 5 and is automatically P_7-free. By fb007f7c4b89, every surviving block has edge rank 6. It remains only to prove specialness.

By translation symmetry it suffices to treat a surviving A_0 and a surviving B_0.

For A_0, db0744b66eb3 proves that the six explicit A_0-ending spanning paths already exhibit two distinct entrance labels unless M contains one of two stabilizer-orbits of exceptional disjoint A-pairs. Under the stabilizer f(x)=3x+1 we may take the exceptional pair to be either
{A_2,A_7} or {A_5,A_7}.

Consider the mixed spanning path
C = A_6, B_5, A_11, B_9, B_1, A_0.
Its consecutive joints are 7,12,11,3,1, so it ends in A_0 through entrance 1.

If M contains {A_2,A_7}, every nonterminal block of C meets A_2 or A_7:
A_6 meets A_2 at 6;
B_5 meets A_7 at 7;
A_11 meets A_2 at 2 and A_7 at 11;
B_9 meets A_2 at 3 and A_7 at 11;
B_1 meets A_2 at 3 and A_7 at 8.
Since M is a matching, none of these five blocks can be deleted. Hence C survives. In this exceptional case the six-path family has the path S1 surviving, with entrance 4. Thus entrances 1 and 4 both occur on rank-six paths ending at A_0.

If M contains {A_5,A_7}, again every nonterminal block of C meets A_5 or A_7:
A_6 meets both;
B_5 meets both;
A_11 meets A_7;
B_9 meets both;
B_1 meets A_7.
Thus C survives. Here the six-path family has T2 surviving, with entrance 4. Again A_0 has two distinct longest-path entrance labels.

Applying f and f^2 handles all six exceptional A-pairs. Therefore every surviving A-block is special.

For B_0, db0744b66eb3 similarly reduces the exceptional pairs, under the stabilizer h(x)=9x+2, to
{B_1,B_4} or {B_1,B_10}.

Now use the mixed spanning path
D = A_4, A_8, B_9, A_10, A_6, B_0.
Its consecutive joints are 8,9,11,10,7, so it ends in B_0 through entrance 7.

If M contains {B_1,B_4}, every nonterminal block of D meets B_1 or B_4:
A_4 meets both;
A_8 meets B_1;
B_9 meets both;
A_10 meets both;
A_6 meets B_4.
Hence D survives. The six-path family has S2' surviving with entrance 0, so B_0 has entrances 7 and 0.

If M contains {B_1,B_10}, every nonterminal block of D again meets one of the deleted pair:
A_4 meets both;
A_8 meets both;
B_9 meets B_1;
A_10 meets both;
A_6 meets B_10.
Thus D survives. The six-path family has T1' surviving with entrance 2, giving entrances 7 and 2.

Applying h and h^2 handles all exceptional B-pairs. Therefore every surviving B-block is special.

Hence every edge of S\M is special and has edge rank 6 for every matching M. Together with the elementary minimum-degree and vertex-count observations, this replaces the exhaustive matching-deletion specialness computation by explicit human mathematics.
