# Exceptional Y/Z single blockers cannot occupy the final cell

## Statement

In the saturated boundary setting q=δ with ascending nonspecial e={x,y,z}, maximum entrance path Q=(g_1,...,g_{q-1}), and no safe rotation, a Y-type or Z-type exceptional single blocker cannot use a blocker vertex in the final cell C_{q-1}=g_{q-1}\g_{q-2}.

## Body

Suppose f is a Y-type exceptional single blocker through the opposite last vertex a of Q, so f contains y and has exactly one blocker w on Q. If w lies in the final cell C_{q-1}, then the sequence g_{q-2},g_{q-3},...,g_1,f,e is a q-edge linear path ending in e and entering e through y. The reversed prefix is linear, g_1 meets f only at a, f meets e only at y, and the only other Q-contact of f is w in the omitted edge g_{q-1}. This gives a longest q-edge path into e with entrance y, contradicting that e is nonspecial with unique entrance x. The same proof applies to a Z-type exceptional single blocker. Hence neither Y nor Z can occupy C_{q-1}. In particular, in the S=2 case with exceptional types Y and Z, both vertices of the final cell must be used by double blockers; in the S=3 case the X-defect occupies one final-cell vertex, while the other is either the unique uncovered vertex or belongs to a double blocker.
