# A first-joint-only rank-five competitor forces both far-left free vertices to potential at least five

## Statement

In the p=5 pure rank-five setup
  P=(g1,g2,g3,g4,e4),
put r=g1∩g2. Let f be another rank-five charged edge through v whose only precursor vertex is r.

Then both vertices of g1\\{r} have endpoint potential at least five.

## Body

Because r is the only precursor vertex of f, the edge f is disjoint from g_3 and g_4, and its other non-v vertex lies outside the precursor. It meets e_4 exactly at v. Consider (g_3,g_4,e_4,f,g_1). Consecutive intersections are g_3∩g_4, g_4∩e_4, e_4∩f={v}, f∩g_1={r}. All nonconsecutive pairs are disjoint: g_3 is disjoint from e_4 and g_1 by the original path P, and from f by the only-precursor-vertex hypothesis; g_4 is disjoint from f and g_1; e_4 is disjoint from g_1. Hence this is a five-edge linear path ending in g_1 through r. Either of the two vertices of g_1\{r} can be chosen as the last vertex. Therefore each has endpoint potential at least five.
