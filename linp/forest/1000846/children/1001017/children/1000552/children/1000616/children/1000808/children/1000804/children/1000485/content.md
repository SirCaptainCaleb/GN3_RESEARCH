# Reciprocal-tail uncrossing for charged spacing

## Statement

For a potential-charged ascending edge e={x,v,u}, compare a maximum φ(v)-edge path ending at v with a maximum φ(u)-edge path ending at u. The terminal-tail lemma forces {x,u} into the final q-2 precursor edges of the first path and {x,v} into the final q-2 precursor edges of the second. Use this reciprocal pair of tail constraints as the basic state for proving charged four-edge spacing or a direct sublinear charged-degree bound.

## Body

The unrestricted spacing counterexamples use a second terminal contact on the largest common-terminal witness path to block the one-path splice. Under potential charging, that second terminal has endpoint potential at least φ(v), hence comes with its own equally long or longer endpoint path. The new tail lemma forces the original common terminal or the low-potential entrance back into the final q-2 tail of that second path. Thus every cross-blocker that defeats the old one-path spacing proof creates a reciprocal tail obstruction rather than terminating the argument. The proposed Minty/Pósa lift uses ordered pairs of endpoint paths as states: a forward move follows a charged cross-contact to the opposite-terminal path, while a backward/repair move uses the forced reciprocal tail contact. A positive closed walk should correspond to a forbidden rank gain or a second longest entrance.
