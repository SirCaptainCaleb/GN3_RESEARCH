# Opposite endpoints amplify the forced equality charge by two

## Statement

Let k_v=3d-d_H(v) in a P_ell-free exact-density equality obstruction and kappa=3d-2ell+3. Then max_v k_v>=kappa+2. Indeed zero-sum charge gives a negative-charge vertex v; its degree exceeds 3d, so phi(v)<=ell-2. The opposite endpoint z of a maximum phi(v)-path has degree at most 2phi(v)-1<=2ell-5, hence k_z>=3d-2ell+5=kappa+2. Thus max charge is at least 5,3,4 in residues 0,1,2.

## Body


Let H be a P_ell-free exact-density equality obstruction, with
  d=floor(2ell/3),
leave charges
  k_v=3d-d_H(v),
and
  kappa=3d-2ell+3∈{3,1,2}.

By the basic minimum-degree/path argument, max_v k_v>=kappa>0. Since
  sum_v k_v=0,
there exists at least one vertex v with negative charge:
  k_v<0.
Then
  d_H(v)=3d-k_v>3d.

Let L be the global maximum path length. Since H is P_ell-free,
  L<=ell-1.
If phi(v)=L, choose a globally longest path ending at v. The standard terminal blocker count for a globally longest path gives
  d_H(v)<=2L-1<=2ell-3.
But 3d>2ell-3 in every residue class, contradiction. Therefore
  p:=phi(v)<=L-1<=ell-2.                            (1)

Choose a maximum p-edge path
  P=(e_1,...,e_p)
ending at v, and let z be the endpoint vertex of e_1 at the opposite end of P. The certified proof of 6a4d9b21f0c3 gives
  d_H(z)-1<=2p-2,
hence
  d_H(z)<=2p-1<=2ell-5.                             (2)

Therefore
  k_z=3d-d_H(z)
      >=3d-(2ell-5)
      =3d-2ell+5
      =kappa+2.                                     (3)

Thus every exact-density P_ell-free obstruction satisfies
  max_v k_v>=kappa+2.

Explicitly:
- ell≡0 mod3: max k>=5;
- ell≡1 mod3: max k>=3;
- ell≡2 mod3: max k>=4.

The amplification is generated by a negative-charge high-degree vertex and realized at the opposite endpoint of one of its maximum endpoint paths.
