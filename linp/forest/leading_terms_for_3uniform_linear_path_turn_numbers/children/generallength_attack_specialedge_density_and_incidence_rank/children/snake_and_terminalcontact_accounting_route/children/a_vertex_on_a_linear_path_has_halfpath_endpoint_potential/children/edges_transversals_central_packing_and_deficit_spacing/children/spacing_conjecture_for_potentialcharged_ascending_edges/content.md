# Four-edge spacing conjecture for potential-charged ascending edges

## Statement

Fix a vertex v and let e_i={x_i,v,u_i}, i=1,2,3,4, be four ascending nonspecial edges for which v is terminal and φ(u_i)>=φ(v). Order q_i=φ(e_i) so q_1<=q_2<=q_3<=q_4. Then 2q_2>=q_1+q_4+1.

## Body

The unrestricted four-edge spacing statement is false, but all currently known counterexamples exploit edges whose opposite terminal has potential below the common terminal. The charged hypothesis excludes exactly that downhill mechanism. The conjecture is therefore a targeted replacement: every edge is oriented toward a terminal v from which its opposite terminal is nondecreasing in endpoint potential. The central-window packing theorem already puts all four ranks in the upper half of φ(v), and the two-point transversal theorem gives four pairwise-disjoint blockers of every maximum v-ending path. The remaining obligation is to turn those charged transversals into the stated deficit-doubling inequality.
