# Rainbow-shadow formulations for 3-uniform linear paths

## Statement

A dedicated research document for translating the 3-uniform linear-path problem into properly edge-colored graph problems. It records both the global A/B shadow reduction and the rank-layer ascending-edge formulation, together with two distinct minimum-degree normalizations: one in the original 3-uniform hypergraph and one in the derived properly edge-colored shadow graph.

## Body

PURPOSE. This document is the durable home for the rainbow-path viewpoint motivating the general 3-uniform linear-path problem. It deliberately keeps two different degree reductions available rather than replacing one by the other.\n\nGLOBAL SHADOW FORMULATION. For a linear 3-graph H, color each shadow pair yz by the unique third vertex x such that xyz is a hyperedge. This coloring is proper. For a bipartition V(H)=A union B, retain on A only pairs whose color lies in B. A rainbow graph path lifts to a linear hypergraph path because path vertices lie in A, colors lie in B, colors are distinct, and linearity rules out additional intersections. The certified object f1a36c8d92be contains the basic reduction and its generic black-box ceiling.\n\nASCENDING-LAYER FORMULATION. Write a(v) for the maximum length of a linear path ending at physical vertex v. If e is nonspecial of global rank t and x is its unique longest-path entrance vertex, then a(x)>=t-1 while the two terminal vertices y,z satisfy a(y),a(z)>=t. The extremal one-level case a(x)=t-1 is called ascending. For each k, encode every ascending edge xyz with a(x)=k and t=k+1 as the graph edge yz colored x. This gives a properly edge-colored graph J_k whose graph vertices have a>=k+1 and whose colors have a=k. A rainbow (k+1)-edge path in J_k would lift to a (k+1)-edge linear hypergraph path ending at an endpoint-edge color x, contradicting a(x)=k. Thus J_k has no rainbow path of length k+1.\n\nDEGREE DISCIPLINE. There are two useful and distinct minimum-degree operations. First, before any shadow construction, one may pass from H to a density-maximizing vertex-induced subhypergraph H0, preserving linearity and P_ell-freeness, with delta(H0)>=|E(H0)|/|V(H0)|>=|E(H)|/|V(H)|. This is the original-hypergraph minimum degree and is useful for dense-core/special-edge arguments. Second, after constructing a properly edge-colored shadow graph J, one may pass to a density-maximizing graph subgraph J0 with delta(J0)>=|E(J0)|/|V(J0)|=d_avg(J0)/2. Rainbow-path minimum-degree theorems apply to J0, not to H0. Both degree reductions may and should be used when useful; they are not interchangeable.\n\nFor the random A/B construction applied after the hypergraph density-core step, some cut satisfies |E(J)|/|A| >= (3/4)|E(H0)|/|V(H0)|, and hence a shadow subgraph J0 exists with delta(J0)>= (3/4)|E(H0)|/|V(H0)|.\n\nRESEARCH USE. The global shadow formulation connects the project to general rainbow-path theorems; the ascending-layer formulation isolates a more structured family where graph vertices and colors occupy adjacent path-rank levels. Any new rainbow-path theorem should state exactly whether its degree hypothesis is average degree or minimum degree and on which derived graph it is applied.
