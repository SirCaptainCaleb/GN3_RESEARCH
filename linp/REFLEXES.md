# Research Reflexes


These are recurring self-checks, not separate tasks.

**Theorem reflex.** Keep the actual theorem target separate from the current sufficient condition. Prefer work that can change the theorem, expose structure, or unlock a route; do not spend substantial effort polishing a local refinement that cannot matter downstream. When a route stalls, return to the earliest step where the needed strength was lost and ask what information was discarded there. Do not replace a proof by an unbounded hand-case ladder: case analysis is appropriate only when the complete configuration space is proved uniformly finite over the theorem class.

**Elevation reflex.** For every worthwhile result, identify the mechanism that really proves it and seek its strongest natural formulation. Test which hypotheses can be weakened or removed, useful generalizations, and stronger consequences. If the result belongs to a predecessor chain, try to apply it as early as possible without importing all predecessor steps; identify the minimum additional conditions needed there and ask whether they can themselves be proved or forced. If a result materially changes a Main Line and the new synthesis is already clear, include that Main Line update in the same publication batch.

**Crystallization reflex.** Treat the hot chunk as a subsection being written, not an append-only log. Rewrite it as understanding improves. Crystallize only at a natural subsection boundary, after the local mathematical story is stable enough to stand on its own. A mature Research Line should be roughly section-sized before its content is rewritten and compressed into an existing or new Main Line.

**Stewardship reflex.** Put mathematics in its canonical home rather than creating parallel versions. Durable non-self-contained claims must record the premises actually used; self-contained claims should explicitly declare none. Integrate stronger results, supersede genuinely obsolete formulations, close or promote Brainstorms when their status is clear, and compress accumulated work when synthesis can replace clutter. Preserve genuinely different approaches when neither supersedes the other. Leave the shared research representation cleaner and more useful to the next researcher.
