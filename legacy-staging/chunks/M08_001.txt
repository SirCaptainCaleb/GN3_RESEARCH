## 1. The exact dominance rule

Represent a theorem as a record with three parts:

- its hypothesis set;
- its mathematical conclusion set; and
- its retained-information set: representative, selected states, orientations, witnesses, marker identities, roles, exact complements, ancestry, simultaneous-versus-descendant status, and cycle debt.

Suppose an old theorem says that assumptions `A` imply conclusions `B`, and a later theorem says that assumptions `A-prime` imply conclusions `B-prime`.

The old theorem may be dropped as a theorem node only when:

1. every `A-prime` assumption is already implied by `A`, so the new theorem has no stronger requirements in the old scope;
2. every conclusion in `B` is implied by `B-prime`; and
3. every item from the old retained-information set that any live consumer uses is reproduced by the new theorem.

If conditions 1 and 2 hold but condition 3 fails, the old theorem is a **retained specialization**. If only its proof method remains useful, the theorem statement can be omitted from the live bank but its method belongs in the technique atlas.

This is stricter than comparing theorem names or final component counts.

## 4. Strategic demotion versus logical subsumption

A theorem is **strategically demoted** when a shorter route reaches similar progress but the theorem is not logically dominated. Examples:

- source routers were demoted after fan reduction, but retain fixed-skeleton source history;
- fan production was demoted after prescribed-path completion, but first-loss fans retain ancestry;
- capacity maxima were demoted after root collision production, but retain quiet-reservoir bounds;
- pair envelopes were demoted for first pair birth, but retain canonical complements;
- R28 through R34 were demoted from mandatory front doors, but remain unique ancestry consumers;
- pivot-specific refund was demoted by split-free root pairs, but pivot fold ancestry remains strictly richer;
- transfer-edge local restorations do not dominate old return lineages because they fix different roots and markers.

Do not label strategic demotion as theorem deletion.

## 5. Major correction ledger

### Rootedness

**Incorrect strengthening:** every prescribed head or source can be the endpoint of a two-cover.

**Correction:** only the unrooted Strong two-cover conjecture remains. Minimality does not prescribe endpoint roles.

### Raw gain

**Incorrect rule:** inserted states minus blockers is the path-cover improvement.

**Correction:** subtract one for every physical tight-cycle component created.

### Tight cycles

**Incorrect rule:** a physical tight cycle is contradictory.

**Correction:** it is constructive; open one selected state and count the debt.

### Auxiliary cycles

**Incorrect rule:** a Hall, matching, compatibility, implication, or exchange cycle is a physical tight cycle.

**Correction:** reconstruct and test the physical vertex sequence.

### Old-port preservation

**Incorrect rule:** local deletion and recompletion preserve prescribed old endpoint ports.

**Correction:** role can be lost; use explicit retention, restoration, or first-loss theorems.

### Endpoint-only Hamilton moves

**Incorrect rule:** moving one vertex or reversing one segment generates every useful Hamilton reconfiguration.

**Correction:** explicit finite witnesses show these moves are incomplete.

### Matching independence

**Incorrect rule:** separate alternating components can be toggled independently.

**Correction:** physical incidence and cycle interactions can couple them.

### Monotone insertion profiles

**Incorrect rule:** insertion depth, capacity, marker generation, shell depth, or partition excess is globally monotone.

**Correction:** these quantities are representative- or object-relative unless a fixed universe and reset theorem are proved.

### Marker reversal

**Incorrect rule:** reorienting a physical marker for actualization changes its old selected orientation.

**Correction:** actualization orientation and source selected orientation are separate.

### Capture and protection

**Incorrect rule:** historical capture makes an anchor permanently current or protected.

**Correction:** protection requires a designated continuation and lasts only until the named target or first contact.

### Boundary pinning and endpoint exposure

**Incorrect rule:** a support known to lie at a historical boundary is current endpoint-exposed.

**Correction:** current endpoint degree and representative must be proved.

### Same-candidate splice

**Incorrect rule:** a current historical seam state is automatically the candidate splice turn in static assembly.

**Correction:** certify the complete ordered turn in the exact displayed candidate.

### Witness-order collision

**Incorrect rule:** cyclic permutations of one certified tight witness turn remain certified.

**Correction:** test the exact order. This repaired the terminal-collision spend ledger and two-peel decoupling.

### Four-vertex classification

**Incorrect rule:** every no-Hamilton four-set has cyclic matching-block quotient.

**Correction:** the transitive quotient also exists. Cyclic fifth-vertex extension eliminates only the cyclic case.

### Four-deletion exactness

**Discarded proof:** classify the four deleted vertices by the cyclic quotient and force a Hamilton extension.

**Accepted proof:** turn three deleted vertices into a tight trimer and contradict the one-vertex deletion rail-order-four floor.

### One-hole order-six currentization

**Incorrect statement:** the canonical one-hole construction always currentizes through short-rail order six.

**Correction:** orders four and five currentize; at order six a transitive matching-block residue survives with a labelled three-pattern signature on each side.

### Pair-shadow side dichotomy

**Incorrect scope:** attachment-side polarization across an entire transfer component.

**Correction:** the crossed-versus-side-polarized dichotomy holds inside one fixed root, moved-endpoint, common-shadow attachment fiber.

### Fan reduction

**Overclaim:** producing a fan consumes a fan-saturated obstruction.

**Correction:** Three-Cover Fan Reduction is Strong-safe but nonconsuming on an already saturated representative.

### Order-ten longest-six attribution

**Potential historical misstatement:** later longest-five compression first closes the longest-six branch.

**Correction:** R15 first closed the order-ten longest-six branch by mate-fork forcing; R16 audited it and supplied cyclic quotient compression.

### Transfer component extrapolation

**Excluded claims:** every extremal good edge forces a two-vertex continuation; every order-ten transfer component is edgeless.

**Correction:** neither is accepted. Component extremality gives directional shielding and at least one surviving opposite transfer in the stated scope.

## 6. Failed or insufficient routes

The items below are not all false mathematical statements. Some are insufficient proof strategies whose output was repeatedly mistaken for progress.

### F1. Arbitrary prescribed-head completion

False. The unrooted conjecture does not imply a requested final endpoint.

### F2. Old-port preservation after local recompletion

False without a retention theorem. Recompletion may change which vertices are endpoints.

### F3. Endpoint-only reconfiguration is complete

False. Internal grafts, multi-block moves, and explicit finite witnesses escape that move set.

### F4. Single relocation plus segment reversal gives complete Hamilton descent

False by explicit examples.

### F5. Raw exchange gain proves Strong improvement

False until physical cycle debt is subtracted.

### F6. A physical tight cycle is a contradiction

False. Open it constructively.

### F7. An auxiliary cycle is physical

False in general.

### F8. A fan, blossom, threshold square, double block, or leafless Hall core must be impossible

False or insufficient. These objects often exist and merely route to another representation.

### F9. A scalar fixed-side atom automatically closes

Insufficient. The other side can absorb the change.

### F10. Bounded component count gives bounded physical support

False. A few path components can contain arbitrarily long support.

### F11. Matching components toggle independently

False without physical compatibility checks.

### F12. Unconditional monotone insertion profile

False across arbitrary recompletions.

### F13. Generic symmetric double-bad lifting

Overbroad. Several proposed four-vertex symmetric lifts lacked a certified turn or common frame.

### F14. Repeated pair reminting is progress

Insufficient. Use fixed-anchor interaction or a monotone consumer.

### F15. Signed dimer plus witness singleton gives a floor

False. Actualization and payment are separate theorems.

### F16. Truncate a longer signed support to a dimer

False without a tight subpath and polarity theorem.

### F17. A selected state survives representative change

False without literal retention or transport.

### F18. Marker reorientation changes the old current state

False; physical and selected orientation differ.

### F19. Capture implies permanent current anchor

False.

### F20. Boundary pin implies endpoint exposure

False.

### F21. Independent left/right deletion covers can be identified

False without an exact common shadow or synchronization theorem.

### F22. Proper cycle output is global improvement

Insufficient. It may be a legal constructive object with debt.

### F23. Capacity is a global monotone

False across marker or representative resets.

### F24. Pair-envelope payment to minimum mass gives canonical fragmentation

False without a theorem preserving the envelope complement.

### F25. Crossing physicalization follows from an auxiliary strongly connected boundary

Insufficient. The physical tight turns must be built.

### F26. R28/R29 are mandatory to produce a first physical exit

Strategically false. Shorter puncture/graft parents produce exits. R28/R29 remain for simultaneous common-carrier ancestry.

### F27. R31/R32 are mandatory to produce a terminal sign or capture

Strategically false. Generic signs and adaptive capture are cheaper. R31/R32 retain boundary and seam ancestry.

### F28. R33 concentration at an internal witness is a graph-level obstruction

False. It is one exact-frame alternative and can be bypassed by adaptive witness choice.

### F29. More anonymous collisions will close the proof

Insufficient. Collision production is already cheap; a consumer must preserve missing assembly information.

### F30. More transfer coordinates will close the proof

Insufficient unless a new coordinate preserves or consumes seam, capture, or final-candidate identity.

### F31. Several graph-intrinsic alternatives are simultaneously current

False. Descendants must be separated.

### F32. A transfer component is one common representative

False. Only a fixed edge or attachment fiber has the proved common shadow.

### F33. Root rotation is a descent

False in general. Terminal rotations can reverse exactly; internal reverse excess is representative-relative.

### F34. Restoring historical seams enters static assembly

Insufficient until they are certified as the exact same-candidate splice turns with current endpoint roles.
