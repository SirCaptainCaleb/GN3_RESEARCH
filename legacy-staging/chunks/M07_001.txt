## How to use this volume

This is the project's technique shelf. It deliberately retains methods from dominated, abandoned, or closed-walk-free branches when the method can be transplanted.

Each entry records:

- what is represented;
- what move is legal;
- what information the method preserves;
- whether it is Strong-safe;
- the common failure mode; and
- where to look for exact certificates.

Do not promote a historical method into a theorem without rechecking its hypotheses and physical realization.

## 1. Representation cards

### Ordered-pair state graph

**Represents:** every oriented physical pair as a state; a legal tight extension as a directed arc.

**Makes visible:** sources, sinks, extension sequences, first failed extension, and—in the CW-free setting—topological height.

**Strong status:** the graph exists, but need not be acyclic. Source/height conclusions are CW-specific.

**Reusable move:** encode a long sequence of local path extensions as one state walk, then translate the first obstruction back to a physical turn.

**Failure mode:** treating an auxiliary directed cycle as a physical tight cycle, or importing height into Strong work.

### Conflict graph and path-forest test

**Represents:** proposed and selected states with conflicts for incidence, predecessor/successor uniqueness, orientation, component joins, and physical cycles.

**Makes visible:** the exact complete blocker set and whether a candidate is a path forest.

**Strong status:** Strong-safe when physical cycles are separately detected and opened.

**Reusable move:** prove a local surgery by listing every blocker rather than arguing informally that “nothing else changes.”

**Failure mode:** counting raw state surplus without cycle debt.

### Matching and alternating structure

**Represents:** predecessor or successor assignments as matchings; cover changes as alternating paths or cycles.

**Makes visible:** neutral exchanges, dependency components, and common blockers.

**Strong status:** combinatorially safe, but physical tightness and cycle debt need separate proof.

**Reusable move:** use symmetric difference to factor a complicated cover comparison into atomic changes.

**Failure mode:** assuming matching components toggle independently or that an alternating cycle is physical.

### Frozen skeleton or fiber

**Represents:** several covers sharing exactly the same unaffected rails and vertices, with only a bounded local packet varying.

**Makes visible:** which states are truly common across representatives and which coordinate pays for each change.

**Strong status:** Strong-safe when every cell is an actual exact cover.

**Reusable move:** before comparing covers, freeze the physical skeleton and name the allowed local coordinates.

**Failure mode:** calling visually similar covers one fiber without literal common-state verification.

### Promotion system

**Represents:** exact moves from a higher-component cover to a minimum cover, indexed by inserted source states and their blockers.

**Makes visible:** minimal dependent candidate families, common bases, promotion collisions, and square coordinates.

**Strong status:** individual exact moves may be Strong-safe; source height and CW termination are not.

**Reusable move:** compare two minimal exchanges from one common base to expose their incompatible blocker ownership.

**Failure mode:** treating a formal coordinate toggle as an exact cover without checking physical cycles.

### Fixed-skeleton router

**Represents:** a finite family of exact covers over one physical rail skeleton, with boundary phases as coordinates.

**Makes visible:** endpoint completeness, one-bad stars, rank lifts, and common-cut interfaces.

**Strong status:** safe if all cells are certified without CW height.

**Reusable move:** replace a sprawling local case tree by one finite coordinate object.

**Failure mode:** continuing to enlarge the router after the needed physical packet is already cheap.

### Hall dependency structure

**Represents:** candidates on one side, blockers on the other, and dependencies under alternating reachability.

**Makes visible:** deficiency, omitted candidates, release ears, canonical hulls, leaf cores, and minimal support cycles.

**Strong status:** Hall combinatorics is safe; physical realization and cycle debt are separate.

**Reusable move:** extract a minimal deficient physical core after parking verified leaves.

**Failure mode:** treating a dependency path as a legal exchange or an auxiliary cycle as physical.

### Fan, double fan, and shell

**Represents:** many tight contacts from one or two path ends, sometimes nested through first-loss history.

**Makes visible:** boundary saturation, transfer directions, common centers, first lost arms, and fixed-marker recurrence.

**Strong status:** the physical fan theorems are Strong-safe in audited scope; some lex or height derivations are historical.

**Reusable move:** choose a fan extremum, delete one boundary, and compare the first transferred or lost arm.

**Failure mode:** calling fan production progress after the representative is already saturated.

### Hub and blossom

**Represents:** a bounded physical core left after fan or Hall reduction: either many arms around a center or an odd cyclic support.

**Makes visible:** common anchors, parity, rotations, and exits from a minimal dependency core.

**Strong status:** physical turns are usable; any CW-derived impossibility is not.

**Reusable move:** route a large auxiliary obstruction into one bounded physical core before doing local turn tests.

**Failure mode:** assuming the hub or blossom is impossible merely because it is highly constrained.

### Flow, broom, and shuttle

**Represents:** a chain of neutral one-block transfers, its extremal blocked endpoint, or its pure reversible corridor.

**Makes visible:** first and last unavailable moves, parity, clean gates, and bounded endpoint obstructions.

**Strong status:** the old potential used CW height; the corridor factorization itself can be reused if a new Strong-safe potential or finite termination proof is supplied.

**Reusable move:** compress an unbounded exchange process to its extremal endpoint packet.

**Failure mode:** assuming neutral movement is progress or that reverse height exists in Strong work.

### Common shadow

**Represents:** the literal residual graph and rails obtained after deleting all vertices on which two representatives differ.

**Makes visible:** graph-intrinsic facts that can be compared across those representatives.

**Strong status:** Strong-safe and central.

**Reusable move:** replace an illicit cross-cover conjunction by deletion to an exact common shadow.

**Failure mode:** using common physical vertex sets without identical rail orientations or exactness.

### Pair envelope and capacity system

**Represents:** a signed pair together with its carrier, complement, witnesses, and maximum quiet reservoir.

**Makes visible:** canonical complement, repeated anchor use, bounded quiet witnesses, and payment ancestry.

**Strong status:** audited Strong modules exist; capacity is representative-relative.

**Reusable move:** prevent pair reminting from forgetting the physical pair and anchor that generated it.

**Failure mode:** treating capacity or mass as a global monotone across resets.

### Fixed-root transfer graph

**Represents:** exact two-covers after deleting one fixed vertex; an edge moves one named endpoint between rails.

**Makes visible:** connected components, rail-gap extrema, reversibility, attachment fibers, and exact common pair shadows.

**Strong status:** Strong-safe.

**Reusable move:** study cover reconfiguration globally while retaining one root and one moved endpoint.

**Failure mode:** importing selected states across a component or assuming every useful edge continues.

### Root rotation

**Represents:** an exact return in which the old marker and a surviving path piece coalesce into a new global-longest root.

**Makes visible:** marker-role transitions, reversible terminal rotations, internal reverse excess, and re-entry into the root theorem package.

**Strong status:** Strong-safe in the accepted order-ten equality scope.

**Reusable move:** treat a return as a legitimate new root and compare its exact reverse to the original representative.

**Failure mode:** calling recurrence a descent without a reset-safe quantity.

## 2. Proof-method cards

### Complete blocker accounting

**Procedure**

1. State the current representative.
2. List the proposed inserted states.
3. At every affected vertex, list selected predecessor and successor incidences.
4. Identify component joins and any physical cycle.
5. Delete the complete blocker set.
6. Open one state per physical cycle.
7. Write the exact resulting components.

**Why it matters:** this method repaired most false local exchanges.

### Literal survivor before minimality

**Procedure**

1. Delete the named component or packet from the displayed cover.
2. Write every surviving rail literally.
3. Determine whether they already give the required exact cover.
4. Invoke minimality only for the uncovered residual, if any.

**Why it matters:** componentwise re-rooting, small-deletion shadows, and several seam restorations were found this way.

### First-failure conversion

**Procedure**

1. Order a proposed neutral transport or extension sequence.
2. Choose the first failed turn, first lost arm, first changed owner, or first contact.
3. Apply boundary antisymmetry to that exact ordered test.
4. Retain all earlier successful states and their representative.

**Why it matters:** the first event carries more ancestry than an arbitrary later collision.

### Common-shadow comparison

**Procedure**

1. Name two representatives and all vertices whose placements differ.
2. Delete those vertices.
3. Verify literal identity and orientation of the residual rails.
4. Prove the residual cover exact.
5. Compare attachment tests or signs over that one shadow.

**Why it matters:** this is the correct way to harvest information from multiple covers without illicit selected-state transfer.

### Adjoin the undersized rail

**Procedure**

1. Assume an exact small-deletion cover has a rail below the desired floor.
2. Add that rail's vertices to the deletion set.
3. Use exactness at the larger deletion size.
4. Contradict the existence of a Hamilton complement or an earlier rail floor.

**Why it matters:** the small-deletion staircase replaced many finite classifications.

### Extremal neutral sequence

**Procedure**

1. Choose a potential justified in the exact fixed universe: height, overlap, rail gap, owner rank, or marker component size.
2. Follow neutral moves that improve it.
3. At an extremum, classify forbidden outgoing moves.
4. Convert each forbidden move to a blocker or reverse turn.

**Strong caution:** the potential must survive cycles and must be defined on one fixed universe. CW height and representative-relative quantities are not global Strong potentials.

### Equality coalescence

**Procedure**

1. Partition the current deletion graph into physical classes with known intrinsic path-cover costs.
2. Prove independent lower bounds requiring transitions across two cuts.
3. Apply the partition identity.
4. On equality, show one transition must satisfy both cut requirements.
5. Read the exact concatenated current rails.

**Why it matters:** this method turned an anonymous minimum count into root rotation and corrected a false two-contact interpretation.

### Marker-fold generation

**Procedure**

1. Start with two global-longest paths sharing an exact marker-deletion shadow.
2. Test the two orientations through one marker vertex.
3. Use reverse stars to build a new five-path in the selected fold type.
4. Apply the five-exterior theorem to that fold path.
5. Intersect its reverse-star system with the original longest path's system.

**Why it matters:** one local antisymmetric choice becomes a large common-witness sign family.

### Fixed-trimer anchoring

**Procedure**

1. Select a fixed trimer disjoint from a path or sign family.
2. Pair one target sign with one terminal trimer sign.
3. Pair the opposite target sign with the other terminal trimer sign.
4. Record the common middle anchor.
5. Invoke the no-quiet-remint theorem on the second birth.

**Why it matters:** it converts abundant signs into nonquiet historical interaction without another marker construction.

### Adaptive witness selection

**Procedure**

1. List every admissible witness and polarity for one physical support.
2. Fix the selected marker in one deletion cover.
3. Choose a witness not lying on that marker.
4. Apply same-frame actualization and orientation-selective capture.

**Why it matters:** it eliminates concentration one crossing at a time while preserving the source cover.

### Static-core transparency

**Procedure**

1. Separate the graph-intrinsic static package from the currently selected pair.
2. Perform the accepted pair excursion or floor descent.
3. Carry the static package only as truth and ancestry.
4. Record which objects cease to be current.

**Why it matters:** it permits proof-tree compression without false simultaneous selection.

### Protected first contact

**Procedure**

1. Choose one actual capture descendant.
2. Choose the designated quiet continuation licensed by the protection theorem.
3. Identify the first later event touching the anchor.
4. Stop there and record the contact output before any further recompletion.

**Why it matters:** it prevents a captured support from being silently reused as an unrelated new epoch.

### Same-candidate audit

**Procedure**

1. Write the final proposed two-path candidate explicitly.
2. Name every seam turn in that candidate.
3. For each turn, identify the representative and theorem that certify it.
4. Reject any certificate that proves only one historical state or comes from another candidate.
5. Verify the complement and component count after all blockers are removed.

**Why it matters:** this is the controlling correction at the final assembly boundary.

## 4. Methods that require a new proof before Strong use

The following are not banned ideas; their old theorems depend on closed-walk-freeness or another lost hypothesis.

- global source/sink induction in the ordered-state graph;
- topological state height and reverse height;
- termination because every auxiliary walk strictly changes height;
- contradiction from a directed auxiliary cycle;
- independent toggling of matching or Hall components;
- scalar insertion profiles assumed monotone under arbitrary recompletion;
- any theorem whose proof says a physical tight cycle cannot exist.

A future Strong version may use condensation into strongly connected components, a cycle-adjusted potential, or constructive cycle opening. It must prove those replacements explicitly.

## 5. Techniques worth re-testing against the current frontier

These are not research instructions or accepted theorems. They are retained creative options with clear prerequisites.

### Common-shadow lift of protected continuation

Try to delete exactly the vertices on which the return representative and protected continuation differ. If their residual rails become one exact common shadow, current seam geometry and first-contact ancestry might be compared without transporting selected states directly.

### Cycle-adjusted potential on a fixed transfer component

The transfer graph supplies a finite fixed universe absent from the old flow program. A potential combining rail gap, preserved seam count, and explicit cycle debt may recover part of the broom/shuttle extremal method without CW height.

### Fixed-trimer anchoring of restored seams

Far return restores two seams and retains one selected crossing. If a fixed trimer can be kept disjoint in the same frame, its two terminal signs may turn later seam use into a named anchored interaction rather than an anonymous first contact.

### Hall hull over a common pair shadow

The common shadow fixes residual rails across four attachment representatives. A Hall dependency system built only from attachment tests over that shadow would avoid the old cross-cover ambiguity. Physical realization and cycle debt would still need proof.

### First-loss analysis inside a transfer component

The old shell method tracked the first lost fan arm. A modern analogue could track the first lost historical seam or first change in capture role along a transfer path. This would require a theorem that the relevant history is well-defined across each edge.
