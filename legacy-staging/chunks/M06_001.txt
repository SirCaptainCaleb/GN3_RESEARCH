## Purpose

Finite-order work served three roles:

1. close the smallest possible counterexample orders;
2. test stronger lemmas and find counterexamples to them; and
3. reveal rigid size profiles that later became arbitrary-order parents.

The current order-ten proof is not one uninterrupted case analysis. It is the convergence of several older lines: longest paths, pair deletion, one-hole mates, signed refunds, transfer edges, exact small deletion, fixed shadows, and return/capture.

## 2. Early finite experiments in the CW-free era

### 2.1 Order-eight rooted regression

The unrooted source-return self-contained handoff retained an order-eight example showing that a prescribed rooted two-cover need not exist even when an unrooted two-cover does. This killed the rooted strengthening and forced later work to treat endpoint role as a resource rather than part of minimality.

### 2.2 Order-ten rotations and hidden middle states

The 2026-08-13 order-ten files studied a global-longest path and a small exterior set. Two themes appeared early:

- rotating a longest path through an exterior marker could preserve order while changing which middle pair was exposed;
- top/bottom boundary tests could bypass one local obstruction but leave an internal hidden state.

These arguments used CW-era height and are not current certificates. Their physical configurations anticipated the later middle-marker, fold, and root-rotation systems.

### 2.3 Orders thirteen and fifteen

The order-thirteen and order-fifteen files explored the first orders at which five-exterior return, lone four-vertex residuals, pure visible returns, and two-coordinate interior cuts could coexist.

Their durable contributions are negative-space examples:

- a local return can recreate the same type of obstruction on a different physical carrier;
- an apparent coordinate descent may reset when the marker changes;
- one visible interface may hide a second internal cut; and
- bounded residual order does not imply a bounded list of global histories.

They should be consulted when proposing a recurrence monotone or a theorem transporting visibility through root changes.

## 3. Strong cleanup through order eight

### 3.1 Pair-deletion rigidity

For any deleted physical pair, the complement has an exact two-cover with two nontrivial rails. Combined with pairwise seam polarity and four-interface normalization, this constrained every very small counterexample.

### 3.2 Universal four-exterior theorem

A proper tight path cannot have only four exterior vertices in a smallest Strong counterexample. The proof forced three turns through every exterior vertex, used the local tournament on the exterior set, and compared dual maximum-path rotations to obtain incompatible source/sink requirements.

This yielded the Strong two-cover theorem through order eight and a smallest-counterexample order floor.

### 3.3 Later subsumption

Universal small-deletion exactness and the proper-path exterior order-five floor now give a shorter parent-level route to the same size exclusion. The historical four-exterior proof remains a useful independent extremal method and a source of pairwise interface orientation data.

## 4. Order-nine closure

### 4.1 Equality frame

The order-nine analysis reached a global-longest path with a four-vertex exterior quotient. The no-Hamilton four-set has cyclic or transitive matching-block type.

### 4.2 Cyclic quotient exclusion

The cyclic case was not locally impossible. Its exclusion required a long chain of one-hole six-path forcing, alternate longest paths, reverse stars, and mate quartets. Head-shell success, tail-shell success, and the all-defect state were each eliminated.

This distinction remains important: “cyclic quotient” is not itself a contradiction; the surrounding longest-path and shell frame is load-bearing.

### 4.3 Transitive middle signature

For the transitive quotient, a five-vertex middle-matching signature theorem constrained four labelled turns. The two endpoint five-sets were no-Hamilton and had to carry the same signature. That common signature forced alternate longest paths and ultimately an explicit spanning two-cover.

### 4.4 Closure and retained techniques

The cyclic exclusion and transitive signature agreement closed the Strong order-nine case.

Reusable techniques:

- classify the physical quotient only after fixing the exterior frame;
- use no-Hamilton five-sets to derive labelled signature constraints;
- compare signatures at both endpoints rather than treating them independently; and
- turn each missing turn into a one-hole longest-path mate.

The exact order-nine shell-position tables are not global theorems and should not be imported elsewhere.

## 5. Historical order-ten split by longest-path order

Before the modern small-deletion theorem, order ten was divided by the maximum tight-path order.

### 5.1 Longest path of order six

Take a six-vertex longest path and four exterior vertices. Reverse stars and a central rectangle produced an inward relay and several endpoint/feedback turns.

R15 proved a **mate-fork forcing lemma**: when a one-hole candidate has two possible missing turns, mate constructions force one of two new turns. Applying successive forks forced four named turns and gave an explicit spanning two-cover.

This directly closed the order-ten longest-six branch.

R16 independently audited the closure. It also proved a cyclic matching-block four-set fifth-vertex extension theorem: two selected arcs on one cyclic face force a Hamilton five-path. This gives a shorter exclusion of the cyclic exterior quotient, but the direct mate-fork closure already covers the entire longest-six branch, including the transitive residue.

Controlling attribution:

- **R15:** first complete longest-six closure;
- **R16:** independent audit and additional cyclic quotient compression.

### 5.2 Longest path of order five

The old longest-five branch began with a five-vertex path and five exterior vertices. A middle physical pair could be used as a marker. Central positive turns were excluded through repeated longest-path and one-hole forcing. The obstruction became marker productivity rather than path production.

The first major packages were:

- long-rail joint lock;
- universal middle-pair marker;

- head/tail/double-negative central state classification;
- a five-plus-two refund that returned mass to the longest path;
- an exact five-plus-three retained-longest pair frame;
- a second-generation marker created without recompletion; and
- gap-one rotation involutions with bi-end locks and collision portals.

These were genuine advances but still generated new markers and portals rather than final assembly.

## 6. Historical longest-five portal line

### 6.1 Middle-marker refund

A middle marker on the five-path created a one-hole overlong-path clause. When the clause could not close directly, it refunded a three-vertex packet and left a residual dimer. Deleting that dimer gave an exact five-plus-three frame retaining the original longest path.

The information ledger was rich: original deletion frame, reverse-wrap turns, full exterior stars, marker bit, refund trimer, residual dimer, exact pair frame, and second-generation marker.

### 6.2 Gap-one rotation involution

In a five-plus-four or related near-equal frame, a one-core rotation could be reversed exactly. This closed one recurrence obligation but also showed why rotation alone is not descent.

The order-ten specialization produced pair-deletion bi-end locks, two endpoint collisions between reverse wraps and shell mates, and a third shifted collision portal.

### 6.3 Double exterior refund and closed reverse-wrap cells

Later R17/R18 work coupled both exterior sides, refunded boundary supports, and transported a shell across a closed reverse-wrap cell. Same-deletion exterior rotations and triple markers increased the amount of simultaneous graph-intrinsic structure.

### 6.4 Cross-rail endcap dualization

The endcap line compared two physical ends of the five-path, dualized their attachment packets, and built exact local transfers. This is an ancestor of the modern endpoint-transfer edge and cross-endcap absorption packages.

### 6.5 Why the line was compressed

The old marker/refund line required a chosen longest path and a chain of specific local states. Universal small-deletion exactness later forced the same five-plus-four and five-plus-three profiles for every root and exterior pair. The modern transfer and gap parents then reproduced much of the bare geometry with weaker hypotheses.

The old line remains valuable for its refund stack, original reverse-wrap turns, retained-longest pair frame, and multi-generation marker ancestry.

## 7. Modern order-ten root

### 7.1 Universal five-plus-four root

Delete any prescribed vertex. Every exact cover has rail orders five and four. The five-path is global-longest, the four-path plus the deleted vertex is the full exterior witness set, and every root has the two reverse-star systems.

This removes the need to choose a special lex-max or marker-produced root.

### 7.2 Small-deletion staircase

Deleting two, three, or four vertices gives the exact profile table recorded in the current theorem bank. In particular:

- pair deletion gives five-plus-three or four-plus-four;
- three deletion gives five-plus-two or four-plus-three; and
- four deletion gives five-plus-one, four-plus-two, or three-plus-three.

This single theorem replaces many finite complement-rigidity branches.

### 7.3 Two endpoint trimer alternatives

In a five-plus-four root, each endpoint of the four-path can be combined with the deleted root and one endpoint of the five-path. The two direct joins cannot both be tight because that would form a six-path. Boundary antisymmetry therefore gives a tight trimer, whose residual four-plus-three complement is exact.

The two endpoint trimers coexist graph-intrinsically in the original root, but their exact complements are separate descendants.

## 8. Transfer-edge branch

### 8.1 Crossed edges

Every order-ten transfer edge moves an endpoint from a five-path to a four-path. Deleting the root and moved endpoint gives an exact four-plus-four common pair shadow.

The two attachment placements must be crossed. Same-side placements concatenate one cover's reverse-star turn with the other's attachment into a forbidden six-path.

### 8.2 Pair-shadow resources

One crossed edge supplies:

- fixed root and moved endpoint;
- exact four-plus-four common shadow;
- two attachment placements;
- both physical dimer orientations;
- bi-end locking of both shadow rails;
- opposite signed polarity at the attachment boundaries; and
- local reversible retractions and endcap restorations.

### 8.3 What remains open

An edge gives a rich fixed fiber but does not force a two-vertex continuation. A transfer component need not be edgeless. Component extremality yields shielding and a surviving opposite transfer, not closure.

## 9. No-edge branch

If the fixed-root transfer graph is edgeless, every exact root cover is completely endpoint-shielded. The eight-splice and complete-shielding parents provide cross-endcap absorptions, bad-pivot terminal collisions, and root-mate alternatives.

The universal reverse-four-path and exterior-pair packets lie above this split, so the no-edge basin is no longer devoid of structure. It remains unclosed because shielding, collisions, and reverse paths still need a same-representative consumer.

## 10. Universal gap and pivot line

### 10.1 Root gap-one reverse four-path

The five-plus-four root has order gap one. Testing both opposite bridge interfaces forces double blocking at at least one end and therefore a reverse four-path. This applies in both transfer basins.

### 10.2 Exterior-pair gap-two pivot

Delete any exterior physical pair from a five-path. The exact five-plus-three frame has order gap two. Either a reverse four-path appears or two endpoint transfers create a second global-longest five-path while preserving the marker.

### 10.3 Four-cover square and exact three-plus-three shadow

In the double-transfer face, the two endpoint moves commute. Four exact covers form a square. Deleting the marker and both old endpoints leaves one exact three-plus-three shadow common to all corners.

### 10.4 Marker folds

Each of the two marker vertices folds through one of the two global-longest paths. One fold creates a new global-longest five-path and a four-witness bidirectional terminal-dimer system. Equal fold types saturate one side by all five exterior vertices; opposite fold types give both systems.

## 11. Root-common shadow and anchor compression

### 11.1 Three shadows without a pivot

Four-deletion exactness shows that every consecutive trimer window of the root five-path forms an exact three-plus-three complement with the fixed exterior trimer. The equality pivot is not needed.

This converts one central shadow into a three-cell family.

### 11.2 Three-anchor interaction

Each five-path trimer window and the fixed trimer give a double-central-anchor interaction. The anchors are the three internal five-path vertices paired with the fixed trimer's middle vertex.

### 11.3 Four return routes

Each adjacent five-path dimer can be paired with an opposite sign from the fixed trimer and passed through R15/R16 floor descent. Every exact return to that dimer deletion regenerates a signed support through the old middle anchor.

These four routes are alternative histories, not one floor paying four dimers.

### 11.4 Split-free floor gateway

Before deciding whether the root is double-blocked, pivoted, transfer-rich, or shielded, one can choose a direct root pair and descend to a mass-two floor carrying the root, three shadows, three-anchor interactions, and one named return route as historical truth.

This unifies bare floor access but does not erase branch-specific refund, transfer, fold, or capture ancestry.

## 12. Return, rotation, and capture

### 12.1 Exact return profiles

For an adjacent five-path dimer deletion, every exact return has the size and marker-role normal form from the current theorem bank. A current turn always crosses the old exterior marker cut.

### 12.2 Equality rotation

On minimum transition count, the old-marker cut transition and R15/R16 return crossing are one state. The old marker becomes a terminal dimer of a new global-longest path. The old fixed trimer remains.

The entire root package can be applied again with the deleted dimer as new marker.

### 12.3 Reverse-excess pattern

Terminal dimer rotations reverse through the old root with zero excess; internal dimer rotations return to that same representative with excess one. This explains why root rotation is not a global monotone.

### 12.4 Crossingwise capture

Every selected parent crossing in an internal-dimer return avoids at least one of the two natural dimer witnesses and can capture its carrier-side endpoint in the same return frame. Inner cut-side anchors immediately interact with old terminal signs; quiet residues are concentrated at the two historical global endpoints.

### 12.5 Near and far seam alignment

- Near the adjacent global endpoint, the crossing is the unique seam blocker and can be exchanged for the historical seam.
- At the far global endpoint, one forced cross-gap chord and parent chain restore both historical seams while the captured crossing survives.

This is the newest accepted order-ten reduction.
