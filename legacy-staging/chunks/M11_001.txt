## Purpose and status

This document is neither a theorem bank nor a work plan. It records durable observations suggested by comparing the current proof state with the full sequence of older approaches. Its purpose is to preserve mathematical perspective that can disappear when a stronger theorem replaces a weaker headline result.

Each entry has three parts:

- **Observation:** the cross-era pattern.
- **Motivation:** the established results or repeated historical behavior behind it.
- **Limit:** what the observation does not prove.

An observation may motivate a new theorem, a finite computation, or a change of representation, but it is not itself promoted to accepted mathematics. Historical methods mentioned here require a new proof before use outside their certified scope.

Only a small working vocabulary is needed:

- A **representative** is a particular selected exact path cover, with its path orientations and current endpoint roles.
- A fact is **current** when it is selected in that representative; it is **graph-intrinsic** when it is true in the graph but need not be selected there.
- A **common shadow** is the exact residual path cover left after deleting the same physical vertices from several representatives.
- **Capture** records how a crossing or anchor arose. **Protection** additionally controls how later exchanges may affect it.
- A **neutral exchange** replaces the same number of path components as it removes. It has no cycle debt merely from the component count, though its path and exactness claims still need proof.

These distinctions are part of the mathematics, not editorial ceremony.

## I. What the reductions reveal about the problem

### 1. The scarce object is synchronized typed information

**Observation.** The project rarely lacks a fan, collision, mate, reverse path, exact complement, or short deletion cover. What repeatedly remains scarce is one representative carrying several facts in the roles needed by a downstream theorem: a current endpoint, the correct physical seam, a selected crossing, its capture ancestry, the required witness orientation, and an exact complement, all at once.

**Motivation.** Successive lines made each local object cheap. Boundary antisymmetry creates reverse turns; completion creates exact covers; the root packets create collisions and caps; transfer fibers create several representatives over one shadow; the order-ten return line creates crossings and restored historical seams. Yet the static assembly line still distinguishes a seam in its final displayed candidate from a turn selected in some other minimum-three representative. This same obstruction appeared much earlier in frozen-fiber and fixed-turn work.

**Limit.** This does not identify which synchronized packet suffices. It says only that producing another anonymous local object is unlikely to address the recurrent loss.

### 2. A representative is mathematical state, not presentation

**Observation.** Two exact covers of the same graph can carry materially different usable information even when they display the same graph-intrinsic tight turns.

**Motivation.** Current endpoints, rail orientation, component identity, prescribed-root role, same-candidate splice identity, and cycle debt all depend on the chosen cover. Transfer edges, root rotations, mate currentizations, and quiet-return exchanges repeatedly preserve some of these coordinates and discard others. Several false historical conjunctions came from treating covers of the same graph as interchangeable.

**Limit.** A common graph does not license combining selected states. Such a combination needs literal survival, a common shadow, or another explicit graph-intrinsic argument.

### 3. Theorem strength is a partial order with several coordinates

**Observation.** A theorem with a broader hypothesis or a stronger numerical conclusion need not replace a narrower theorem that preserves ancestry, orientation, a carrier, or a same-frame relation.

**Motivation.** Generic completion subsumes many existence conclusions but not old-port preservation. Universal grafting subsumes local internal-vertex constructions but may not retain the same rail roles. Later crossing parents make collision production easier but do not reproduce the common-carrier information of R28/R29 or the inherited-seam information of R31/R32. This is why the exact dominance rule must compare both hypotheses and every conclusion coordinate.

**Limit.** Historical age is not evidence of nonredundancy. A narrower result should be retained only when its full implication record contains information absent from the parent.

### 4. Exact deletion frames are the most stable interface in the corpus

**Observation.** The most successful transitions between proof languages occur after deleting a named physical set and proving that the residue is exactly two paths.

**Motivation.** Prescribed-path completion, componentwise re-rooting, small-deletion exactness, pair shadows, singleton complements, root-common three-plus-three shadows, capture frames, and exterior-pair pivots all use this format. Once the deleted set and residual rails are literal, one can compare orientations, attachments, blockers, and descendants without pretending that two whole representatives coincide.

**Limit.** Exactness of a residual cover does not select any particular attachment of the deleted vertices, nor does it preserve ancestry unless that preservation is separately proved.

### 5. A common shadow is the lawful substitute for cross-representative conjunction

**Observation.** When two representatives differ only on a small named set, their shared exact residue can convert selected statements into comparable statements without merging the representatives.

**Motivation.** The endpoint-transfer edge is reversible precisely because deleting the moved endpoint leaves identical rails. The fixed pair-shadow attachment fiber supports four minimum-three representatives, opposite dimer orientations, and signed attachment-side comparisons over one fixed residue. Earlier capture and fixed-fiber arguments used the same idea less explicitly.

**Limit.** Membership in one transfer component is not enough. The common shadow must be proved for the particular compared representatives, with orientations and moved vertices named.

### 6. Neutral exchange is often more valuable than fresh completion

**Observation.** A literal component-for-component exchange can preserve typed information that a stronger existential completion theorem forgets.

**Motivation.** Boundary transfers, root-mate retractions, seam restorations, far-return exchanges, and fixed-fiber wraps retain untouched rails or selected crossings state for state. Universal completion may produce a better-looking cover but ordinarily supplies no relation between the new rails and the old marker, seam, crossing, or ancestry.

**Limit.** Neutrality controls component budget, not tightness or exactness. Those must be checked in every displayed exchange.

### 7. Equality faces can coalesce obligations

**Observation.** An extremal equality case is often useful because two requirements become the same physical turn or the same rail, not merely because a numerical bound is sharp.

**Motivation.** Gap equality creates dual globally longest rails; root-return equality coalesces marker and boundary roles; five-exterior equality turns a local five-path into a global-longest path with a full reverse star; several transfer-lattice corners make two source splice turns literally current together. The real gain is state identification.

**Limit.** Numerical equality alone does not prove coalescence. The physical vertices, orientations, and selected components must be matched explicitly.

### 8. First failure, first loss, and first contact are reset-resistant clocks

**Observation.** The most dependable chronology in the archive is not “the next cover is better,” but “this is the first step at which a named protected property fails or is touched.”

**Motivation.** Signed-ear arguments convert the first failed extension into a reverse turn. Protected owner descent keeps a selected family intact until the first owner hit. Captured-anchor descent stops at the first contact. Fixed-fiber and quiet-return arguments identify the first blocker of a seam. These statements survive neutral wandering because they are defined relative to a fixed protected object.

**Limit.** First-event reasoning requires a finite sequence and a property whose preservation has been proved at every earlier step.

### 9. Endpoint, seam, crossing, capture, and protection are separate resources

**Observation.** The history supports a strict hierarchy: a vertex may lie on a boundary without being a current endpoint; a crossing may be selected without being captured; a captured crossing may cease to be actively protected; a historical seam may be tight without being the seam of a current final candidate.

**Motivation.** Many corrections in the archive are exactly failures of one implication in this hierarchy. The late return line is valuable because it records several levels simultaneously, while protected continuation is valuable for a different level. Static assembly consumes still another level.

**Limit.** None of these roles should be transported through an exchange by terminology alone.

### 10. Physical cycles and auxiliary cycles belong to different ledgers

**Observation.** A cycle among abstract states is not a tight cycle in the graph; a tight cycle in the graph is not automatically a contradiction. Both become meaningful only through the physical path-cover budget.

**Motivation.** Early state-digraph and Hall-circuit arguments repeatedly needed physicalization. Later work corrected the belief that a proper tight cycle by itself proves improvement: the gain-minus-cycle-debt calculation decides whether a cover actually improves. The same distinction governs proposed transfer-cell consistency arguments.

**Limit.** An auxiliary cycle may still be useful, but only after a theorem maps it to physical exchanges and accounts for every cycle debt.

### 11. Reappearance of the same pattern is not descent

**Observation.** A packet can recur isomorphically around a new root, marker, or carrier without any global progress.

**Motivation.** Root rotations and the order-thirteen and order-fifteen recurrence searches showed that a locally attractive scalar may reset when the physical object changes. Earlier shuttle, promotion, and fixed-side atom arguments encountered the same difficulty in other language.

**Limit.** A recurrence becomes descent only when a well-founded quantity is defined on the whole transition system or an explicit reset theorem compares the old and new physical objects.

### 12. A credible potential needs a fixed universe

**Observation.** Numerical quantities are most trustworthy inside a fixed fiber, fixed transfer component, fixed root, fixed carrier, or fixed protected family.

**Motivation.** Lexicographic cover order, overlap in a frozen fiber, owner depth, transfer-component rail gap, and capacity of a fixed pair envelope can all be compared without changing their referents. Global claims failed when the optimized rail, root, skeleton, or envelope was silently replaced.

**Limit.** Fixed-universe monotonicity does not automatically survive an exit from that universe.

## II. Historical methods that may have been demoted too far

### 13. The hard fixed-fiber wrap line preserved exactly the kind of state now scarce

**Observation.** The old quiet fixed-fiber program should be remembered as a state-transport method, even though its original closure strategy was overtaken.

**Motivation.** In its certified scope, a successful wrap retained a complete internal excursion and the untouched rail, then transported the state into an explicit final candidate with reserve-contact turns. The hard residue became either a one-hole candidate or an explicit reverse four-path. This is unusually close to the later need to carry a selected crossing or seam into a final-candidate frame.

**Limit.** The old scope, vocabulary, and hypotheses do not automatically apply to an order-ten return. What survives is the proof architecture: freeze a fiber, alter one controlled region, preserve the untouched state literally, and identify the complete blocker set.

### 14. Frozen-skeleton routers are better viewed as synchronization devices

**Observation.** Routers need not produce a new local object to be useful; their distinctive value is controlled rewriting while a skeleton remains fixed.

**Motivation.** The router line tracked coordinate changes, seam rewriting, one-bad stars, promotion squares, blocker ownership, and owner descent inside a fixed path skeleton. Later universal theorems made many outputs cheaper, but not the fixed-skeleton relation between successive outputs.

**Limit.** Old router graphs sometimes relied on auxiliary-state edges whose physical realization was incomplete. Only explicitly realized moves can be reused.

### 15. Protected-family owner descent is a general transport pattern

**Observation.** One of the archive's most flexible ideas is to protect a distributed family of states, descend neutrally within one descendant, and stop at the first owner that touches the family.

**Motivation.** The first-loss and multi-corridor results preserved every protected state before the first hit and converted the hit into a boundary and polarity certificate. The protected family could be spread across several corridors rather than concentrated in one local packet. That is structurally suited to carrying a crossing, a seam, and perhaps one marker turn together.

**Limit.** The historical descent was proved for particular owner moves. A new family and a new exchange system require a fresh preservation proof.

### 16. One selected directed state plus endpoints can freeze an entire rail

**Observation.** Preserving a whole path may be unnecessary when a stationary-state uniqueness theorem is available.

**Motivation.** The stationary end-window line proved that two tight paths containing the same selected directed state and diverging immediately yield productive same-polarity output. Outside that output, successor and predecessor are unique; coterminal paths sharing one state are identical. Thus endpoints plus a single directed state can determine the rail.

**Limit.** This conclusion is conditional on excluding the productive divergence alternatives and on matching the path orientation exactly.

### 17. Multi-corridor contraction can make long histories finite without erasing ports

**Observation.** Long untouched portions of rails can sometimes be compressed to weighted corridors whose boundary ports and protected states remain explicit.

**Motivation.** The multi-corridor first-loss work contracted stable interiors and kept only port identities, weights, and first-contact ownership. This retained the data needed to interpret the eventual hit while removing irrelevant path length.

**Limit.** A contraction is sound only for moves proved not to inspect the suppressed interior. It cannot be used when a later turn depends on an internal vertex or order.

### 18. A Hall hull over a fixed exact shadow may avoid the old Hall ambiguity

**Observation.** The canonical successor-closed Hall hull remains an appealing way to collect all dependencies forced by a fixed boundary state, especially when built over one exact common shadow.

**Motivation.** The Hall-hull line obtained root independence, closure under hull union, a pure rectangular sector, and an exact switch carrying ancestry. Its historical weakness was the gap between auxiliary dependency structure and physical covers. A common shadow supplies a fixed physical base on which every dependency edge could be required to have an explicit cover witness.

**Limit.** This is a motivated reformulation, not an existing theorem. Root independence and closure must be re-established under the physical witness requirement.

### 19. Root-funded wings isolate where a mirrored comparison really pays

**Observation.** When two mirror alternatives share one exact residue, their difference may be compressible into local one-debt paths whose only nonlocal issue is endpoint funding.

**Motivation.** The root-funded wing analysis showed that generic interiors could be quotiented away while side wings needed funding from a root, unless a unique common mirror blocker paid instead. This separates bulk symmetry from the small set of endpoint obligations.

**Limit.** The original state graph contained context-specific assumptions. The reusable content is the accounting pattern, not the old theorem statement wholesale.

### 20. Pair envelopes remember canonical complements that floor descent forgets

**Observation.** A pair envelope can be valuable even when it does not by itself pay a floor, because it records which exact complement and which fragment boundaries belong to a physical pair.

**Motivation.** Generic signed-pair payment often reaches a numerical mass conclusion while discarding how the pair sat in its original cover. Envelope and capacity work retained canonical fragment data and repeated-remint history. Those are precisely the coordinates needed when later assembly is sensitive to the original seam or carrier.

**Limit.** Capacity was not proved to be a global monotone, and payment to minimum mass did not force a canonical fragmentation in full generality.

### 21. Capacity is more plausible as a bounded-quietness invariant than as a global potential

**Observation.** The natural use of capacity may be to bound how long one fixed envelope or fiber can remain quiet before a named event, rather than to compare arbitrary representatives.

**Motivation.** Historical failures arose when capacity was carried across reminting or a change of canonical complement. Within a fixed pair, fixed shadow, or fixed owner chain, its referent does not move and first-loss reasoning becomes available.

**Limit.** Even local boundedness needs a proof that every quiet move changes or preserves the same capacity in a controlled way.

### 22. R28/R29 retain a rare two-boundary relation on one carrier

**Observation.** Their enduring value is not merely the production of exits or crossings, but the coexistence of two boundary structures on the same physical carrier.
